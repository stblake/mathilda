# Changelog: week of 2026-10-05 (Mon) – 2026-10-11 (Sun)

## Residue `Integrate`: principal-value keyhole-log + hyperbolic rectangular reduction ; v0.275

**Outcome: `Method -> "Residue"` now handles a simple pole of the rational
factor sitting on the keyhole-log branch cut `(0, ∞)` as a Cauchy principal
value, and reduces `x^k/Sinh x`-type whole-line integrals to that same
keyhole-log core.  Two probe-set declines close:**
- `Integrate[Log[x]/(x^3 - 1), {x, 0, Infinity}] = 4π²/27` (Case 18) — the pole
  at `x = 1` is removable (`Log 1 = 0`), so no option is needed.
- `Integrate[x/Sinh[x], {x, -Infinity, Infinity}] = π²/2` (Case 7) — via
  `w = Exp[x]`.

### `PrincipalValue` threaded into the residue method (`integrate.c`, `integrate_residue.{c,h}`)

`Integrate`'s already-parsed `PrincipalValue -> True` option was passed only to
Newton–Leibniz.  It is now threaded into `integrate_residue_try` (a new seventh
parameter, carried through a file-scope `g_pv` that is saved/restored per frame
so a nested call cannot leak it) and consulted by the keyhole-log family.

### Principal-value axis pole in the keyhole-log family (`a0_keyhole_log`)

A simple pole `z₀` of `R` on the positive real axis — previously an automatic
decline — is admitted when the caller asked for `PrincipalValue -> True`, **or**
when it is *removable*: a simple pole at `z₀ = 1` under a `Log` factor, whose
zero (`Log 1 = 0`) cancels the pole so the ordinary integral equals its
principal value.  The contour indents around the pole above and below the cut,
so its contribution to the residue sum `S_k` is the average of the two
keyhole-branch residues, `½ Res(R, z₀)[(Log z₀)^k + (Log z₀ + 2πi)^k]`.  A
higher-order axis pole (whose principal value diverges) still declines.  Derived
and cross-checked: `∫₀^∞ Log[x]/(x²−4) dx = π²/8` with `PrincipalValue -> True`,
matching a symmetric-exclusion Cauchy numeric.

### Hyperbolic reduction in the rectangular family (`residue_family_rectangular`)

The `w = Exp[x]` substitution that reduces `Exp[c x] R(Exp[x])` to the Mellin
integrand now also closes `x^k/Sinh x` and its relatives: a `Sinh`/`Cosh`/`Tanh`/
`Csch`/`Sech`/`Coth` of `Log w` rationalises under `TrigToExp` + `Together`
(`Sinh[Log w] = (w − 1/w)/2`), while a polynomial `x^k` factor becomes
`(Log w)^k`.  The resulting `(Log w)^k R(w)` integrand is handed to the
keyhole-log family, whose removable `w = 1` (`x = 0`) pole is exactly the
principal-value case above.  The pure-`Exp` strip path is unchanged (no
hyperbolic head → no `TrigToExp`), so the existing rectangular family is
unaffected.

## Residue `Integrate`: non-integer-power keyhole-with-log + exact `Arg` on roots of unity ; v0.270

**Outcome: `Method -> "Residue"` now evaluates the log-keyhole with a
NON-integer branch power (`∫₀^∞ x^p (Log x)^m R(x) dx`, `p ∉ ℤ`), e.g.
`Integrate[Sqrt[x] Log[x]/(x^2+1)^2, …] = π(π−4)/(8√2)`.  The integer-power
log-keyhole (Cases 4/4b from the probe set) now returns the clean closed form
instead of silently declining: `Integrate[Log[x]/(1+x^6), …] = −√3 π²/18`,
`Integrate[Log[x]/(1+x^4), …] = −√2 π²/16`.** The enabling fix is a general
`Arg` improvement that lands far beyond the residue method.

### `Arg` of root-of-unity-like constants (`src/complex.c`)

`Arg[(-1)^(1/6)]` and friends were left **unevaluated**: the generic `a + b I`
re/im split has no `(-1)^r` to decompose, so it fell through to a symbolic
`Arg[(-1)^(1/6)]`.  Every pole of `1 + x^n` off the Gaussian lattice is exactly
such a constant, so any residue sum over those poles produced an `Arg`-salad no
simplifier could collapse.

`Arg[z]` now recovers the exact rational multiple of `Pi` for a product of
real-rational powers and the imaginary unit — `(-1)^(1/6) → Pi/6`,
`-(-1)^(5/6) → -Pi/6`, `(-8)^(1/3) → Pi/3`, `I (-1)^(1/4) → 3 Pi/4` — by adding
arguments mod `2 Pi` across the factors (`arg = ([c<0] + Σ eᵢ[bᵢ<0]) Pi`) and
reducing into `(-Pi, Pi]`.  Purely structural and provably correct: a free
symbol, an inexact real, or a non-root-of-unity Gaussian (`Sqrt[3] + I`) declines
and falls through to the existing `ArcTan`/unevaluated path untouched.

### Non-integer-power keyhole-with-log (`src/calculus/integrate_residue.c`)

For `p ∉ ℤ` the branch factor `z^p` jumps across the cut, so `1 − e^{2πi p} ≠ 0`
and the keyhole of `z^p (Log z)^m R(z)` gives the lower-triangular system

```
(1 − e^{2πi p}) I_k = 2πi T_k + e^{2πi p} Σ_{j<k} C(k,j) (2πi)^{k−j} I_j,
T_k = Σ_l Res[z^p (Log z)^k R, z_l]   (keyhole branch arg ∈ [0,2π)),
```

solved upward from `I_0` (the `k=0` row is the plain keyhole/Mellin value).  New
helpers `keyhole_log_general_a` and `klog_branch_pole_contribution` (the
order-≥2 shifted form, carrying both `(1+w/z)^p` and the shifted log);
`residue_family_mellin_log` dispatches integer `p ≥ 0` to the existing `a=0`
`(Log z)^{k+1}` contour and non-integer `p` to the new system.

### Keyhole-log closing (`a0_keyhole_log`)

The integer-power family computed the right value but closed with
`Simplify[RootReduce[Re[…]]]`, which turned the nice `(-1)^(1/6)` poles into
`Root[…]` objects whose `Arg` never simplified — leaving an unresolved
`Re[Root…]` that failed the real-scalar gate, so Cases 4/4b **declined** (and
their tests passed only vacuously through the `N[]` → `NIntegrate` fallback).
The triangular solve now keeps each `I_k` as its exact complex form and closes
once with `Simplify[ComplexExpand[…]]`; with `Arg` reducing on the roots of
unity the poles live at, the result collapses to the clean algebraic multiple of
`π^{m+1}`.

### Tests

- `tests/test_core.c::test_arg` — exact `Arg` on `(-1)^(1/6)`, `(-1)^(5/6)`,
  `-(-1)^(1/6)`, `(-8)^(1/3)`, `I (-1)^(1/4)`, plus the decline cases
  (`2^(1/2) → 0`, `x^(1/3)` unevaluated).
- `tests/test_integrate_residue.c::test_keyhole_log` — the pins are rewritten to
  assert BOTH `FreeQ[result, Integrate]` (so a decline + numeric fallback can no
  longer pass) AND numeric agreement, and a Case 14 non-integer-power pin
  (`Sqrt[x] Log[x]/(x^2+1)^2 = π(π−4)/(8√2)`) is added.

## Residue `Integrate`: parametrized-contour family + essential-singularity residues ; v0.271

**Outcome: `Method -> "Residue"` now evaluates a parametrized contour integral
over a full period, `Integrate[g(c Exp[I t]) (I c Exp[I t]), {t, 0, 2 Pi}]`,
including integrands with an essential singularity.** Examples now solved:
`Integrate[Exp[Exp[-I t]] Sin[Exp[-I t]] I Exp[I t], {t,0,2Pi}] = 2 Pi I`
(Case 9/19, essential singularity at 0);
`Integrate[(Exp[2Exp[I t]]/(Exp[4 I t](Exp[I t]-1/2))) I Exp[I t], …]
= 2 Pi I (16 E - 128/3)` (Case 20, order-4 + simple pole);
`Integrate[(2Exp[I t])^5 Cos[1/(2Exp[I t])^2] 2 I Exp[I t], …] = 0` (Case 22).

### `residue_family_contour_param` + `res_ess0` (`src/calculus/integrate_residue.c`)

The substitution `t -> -I Log[u]` (so `Exp[I t] -> u`, `Exp[-I t] -> 1/u`) turns
the whole `t`-integral into the contour integral of `G(u) = integrand/(I u)`
around `|u| = 1`, hence `2 Pi i Sum_{|u_k|<1} Res[G, u_k]` -- the same transform
`residue_family_trig` uses, but for a transcendental `G` (an essential
singularity, or an Exp/trig-of-(1/u) kernel) that is not rational in `u`, which
the trig family declines on.  Wired after `residue_family_trig` in the
full-period branch, gated on the integrand carrying `Exp` (stored `Power[E,.]`)
so the common rational-trig path is untouched.

`res_ess0` computes the residue at an essential singularity at `0` as the `w^1`
coefficient of `Series[G /. u -> 1/w, {w, 0, 1}]` (the Laurent `u^{-1}`
coefficient); for an ordinary pole at `0` of an entire-in-`u` integrand
(`Exp[2u]/u^4`) the ordinary `residue_compute` runs first (the `1/w` substitution
would turn `Exp[2u]` into the essential `Exp[2/w]`).  `u = 0` is always a
candidate pole (the `1/u` kernels put a singularity there that is not a
polynomial-denominator root); a denominator root equal to `0` is de-duplicated
against it (it was otherwise double-counted -- caught as `16 E - 256/3` instead
of `16 E - 128/3`).  The contour value is genuinely complex, so the finite-scalar
gate applies, not the real gate.

Case 9's literal `z \[Element] Circle[...]` contour spelling remains out of
scope (no named-character / region-iterator parsing); its mathematics is
delivered through the parametrized spelling (Case 19), which is identical.

### Tests

`tests/test_integrate_residue.c::test_contour_param` pins Cases 19, 20, 22 with
`{FreeQ[r, Integrate], Chop[N[r - ref]]} == {True, 0}` (non-vacuous: a decline
leaves an `Integrate` head and fails), plus a regression that the ordinary
`Integrate[1/(2 + Cos[t]), {t,0,2Pi}] = 2 Pi/Sqrt[3]` trig path is unaffected.

## Residue `Integrate`: unit-circle trig with symbolic parameters (Case 16) ; v0.272

**Outcome: `Method -> "Residue"` now evaluates `Integrate[R(Cos x, Sin x), {x,0,2π},
Assumptions -> ...]` with symbolic parameters, including a higher-order pole and a
coupled assumption.** `Integrate[1/(a+b Cos[x])^3, …, a>b>0] =
π(2a²+b²)/(a²−b²)^(5/2)` (Case 16); `1/(a+b Cos[x]) → 2π/√(a²−b²)`.

### Coupled-assumption instantiation (`build_instantiation`, `src/calculus/integrate_residue.c`)

The symbolic-parameter mode reads pole signs at a representative point of the
assumption region.  `absorb_fact` reduces only INDEPENDENT numeric bounds, so a
coupled constraint like `a > b > 0` (where `a` is bounded by `b`, not a number)
left `a` "unconstrained" and declined.  When that happens, `build_instantiation`
now falls back to `FindInstance[assumptions, {params}]` for one consistent point
(e.g. `{a->1, b->1/2}`); the per-parameter intervals are then unknown, so the
mellin/sector families (which gate on them) still decline, while the unit-circle
family — which only classifies poles at the point — proceeds.  This alone fixes
the order-1 symbolic case.

### Analytic-part residue for a parametric higher-order pole (`residue_family_trig`)

`solve_roots` de-duplicates a multiple root to one representative, and the
generic residue engine's Laurent series is pathologically slow for a symbolic
RADICAL pole — `Residue` of the order-3 pole at `(-a+√(a²-b²))/b` is >30s and its
cost explodes with the radical's surface form (`√(4a²-4b²)` ~30× slower than
`√(a²-b²)` for the same value).  The symbolic branch instead:
- recovers each pole's order with `pole_order` (counting the leading vanishing
  derivatives of the denominator at the instantiated root);
- takes the residue by the analytic-part derivative
  `Res = (1/(m-1)!) d^{m-1}/dz^{m-1}[ N / (lead ∏_{r≠z₀}(z-r)^{m_r}) ]|_{z₀}`,
  building the pole-free denominator from the distinct roots and their orders
  (~0.1s, no Series);
- simplifies each residue, sums, and closes with `Simplify[2πi S]` (real under
  the assumptions).

The numeric trig family is unchanged (its `solve_roots`/`residue_compute` path
is already correct and cheap), and a `1/(2+Cos[x])^2 = 4π/(3√3)` regression pins it.

### Tests

`tests/test_integrate_residue.c::test_trig_symbolic` pins the order-1 and order-3
symbolic cases with `{FreeQ[r, Integrate], Simplify[r - ref] == 0} == {True, True}`
(a decline can no longer pass); `test_family_trig` gains the order-2 numeric pin.

## Residue `Integrate`: Mellin–Barnes vertical line + directed-infinity Plus fix (Case 11) ; v0.273

**Outcome: `Integrate[Gamma[s] x^(-s), {s, 1/2 - I Infinity, 1/2 + I Infinity}]`
now returns `2 Pi I e^(-x)` — it was a silent WRONG `0`.** This is the inverse
Mellin transform / Bromwich integral, the last of the probe set's two wrong
answers.

### Directed infinity survives `Plus` (`src/plus.c`)

`1/2 - I Infinity` evaluated to a real `Infinity`: `classify_plus_term` treated
`Times[Complex[0,-1], Infinity]` as a real `+Infinity` (reading
`expr_numeric_sign` of the complex coefficient), so `Plus[1/2, that]` collapsed
to `Infinity` and both vertical-line endpoints became `Infinity` before
`Integrate` (not HoldAll) ever saw them.  A `Times[c, Infinity]` with a NON-REAL
coefficient is now classified as an ordinary term, so `1/2 ± I Infinity` stays
`Plus[1/2, Times[Complex[0,∓1], Infinity]]` — the imaginary direction is
preserved.  Real `±Infinity` arithmetic and the `Infinity + (-Infinity) ->
Indeterminate` checks are unchanged.

### Mellin–Barnes family (`residue_family_mellin_barnes`, `src/calculus/integrate_residue.c`)

`is_vertical_line` recognises `{s, c-iInf, c+iInf}` (same real offset `c`,
opposite imaginary directions) and its orientation.  For an integrand
`const · Gamma[A + B s] · X^(P + Q s)` with `B` a positive numeric and `c` to the
right of the rightmost pole, the contour closes to the left and the value is
`orient · 2 Pi i · Sum_{k>=0} Res[F, s=-(A+k)/B]`, with
`Res[Gamma[A+Bs]] = (1/B)(-1)^k/k!`; the residue series is handed to `Sum`, which
closes it to the inverse-Mellin closed form (`Gamma[s] x^-s -> 2 Pi i e^-x`,
`Gamma[2s] x^-s -> i Pi e^-Sqrt[x]`).  Wired as the first branch of
`integrate_residue_try`.  Declines (stays unevaluated) when the integrand is not
of this shape or the series does not close.

### Tests

`test_mellin_barnes` (Case 11, the `Gamma[2s]` variant, reversed orientation,
and a `1/s` negative control that must stay unevaluated); `test_core.c::
test_directed_infinity` pins the `Plus` behaviour (`1/2 ∓ I Infinity` stays a
directed-infinity `Plus`, while `Infinity + 3 -> Infinity` and `2 - 3 Infinity ->
-Infinity` are unchanged).

## Residue `Integrate`: Chebyshev-weight finite interval (Case 8) ; v0.274

**Outcome: `Integrate[1/((1+x^2) Sqrt[1-x^2]), {x, -1, 1}]` now returns the
correct `Pi/Sqrt[2]` -- Newton-Leibniz alone returned the WRONG SIGN `-Pi/Sqrt[2]`
(the `Sqrt[1-x^2]` antiderivative's branch).**

`residue_family_chebyshev_weight` recognises `R(x)/Sqrt[1-x^2]` on `(-1,1)` (R
rational, detected by `h = f Sqrt[1-x^2]` being rational), substitutes `x=Cos[t]`
to `(1/2) Integrate[R(Cos t), {t,0,2Pi}]`, and reuses the unit-circle trig family.
Because it runs in the residue stage (before the FTC branch) it pre-empts the
wrong-sign antiderivative.  `∫₋₁¹ dx/√(1−x²) = π`, `∫₋₁¹ dx/((2+x²)√(1−x²)) =
π/√6`.  A non-weight integrand (no `1/Sqrt[1-x^2]`) declines and Newton-Leibniz
keeps it.  `test_chebyshev_weight` pins these plus the decline control.
