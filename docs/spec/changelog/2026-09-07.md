## DSolve M41 — §2.2.22 corpus (Problems 2101–2200) + Frobenius integer-root-difference / logarithmic second solution

The DSolve ODE corpus gains Nasser Abbasi's 12000.org **§2.2.22** (Problems 2101–2200; 100
records, 19 IVPs) — the first §2.2.x section dominated by **higher-order (3rd–6th)
constant-coefficient linear ODEs**: 36 homogeneous (`_missing_x`) and 49 nonhomogeneous
(`E^x·poly`, `E^x·(poly Cos + poly Sin)`, mixed) forcing, plus a 3rd-order Euler–Cauchy IVP
family (2106–2111, incl. an x=−1 point and a fully symbolic-IC problem) and 5 2nd-order
variable-coefficient regular-singular series ODEs (2101–2105). The constant-coefficient bulk
(incl. resonant nonhomogeneous, `Root`-object spectra, and mixed forcing) solves out of the
box; the wave drives the section to **100/100, 0 FAIL, 0 crash** (new ctest gate
`dsolve_corpus_2_2_22_tests`, baseline 0) with three general fixes and **0 regression**
(§2.1.2, §2.2.20, §2.2.21 and all `series`/`nseries` ctests held).

- **`DSolve\`FrobeniusSeries` — positive-integer indicial-root-difference / logarithmic second
  solution** (`src/calculus/dsolve_frobenius.c`). For a regular singular point whose indicial
  roots differ by a positive integer `d = r1 − r2`, the distinct-root branch previously
  **declined** when the smaller-root recurrence was obstructed (a genuine `Log` is required).
  It now constructs the second solution
  `y2 = Log[x]·x^{r2} Σ b̄_n x^n + x^{r2} Σ b̄_n'(r2) x^n` by the **(s − r2)-modified Frobenius
  derivative method** — the coefficients `a_n(s)` for a symbolic exponent carry a simple pole at
  `s = r2` for `n ≥ d`, so `b̄_n = lim (s−r2)a_n(s)` and `b̄_n' = lim d/ds[(s−r2)a_n(s)]` are
  removable-singularity limits (a clean generalisation of the existing equal-root d/ds path).
  It also sizes the truncation window to `ceil(d) + FROB_ORDER` so BOTH series' leading terms
  land inside the merged `SeriesData` (a fixed 6-term window had dropped the larger-root `C[1]`).
  Solves the obstructed `2104` (roots {0,4}) and the wide-window `2101` (roots {5/2,−7/2}).
- **`Series[]` — `scalar · Laurent-SeriesData` order-truncation fix** (`src/calculus/series.c`).
  Multiplying a `SeriesData` with a negative leading exponent (`nmin < 0`) by any scalar
  truncated the product order to `order_num + nmin`: `so_from_constant` gave the constant only
  `order_num` coefficients, and `so_mul`'s order `min(const.order + series.nmin, …)` then lost
  `|nmin|` orders. The constant now spans `order_num − min_nmin` coefficients (only extends in
  the Laurent case, never shrinks), so `c · SeriesData[nmin<0]` keeps its full order. This is a
  general `Series[]` correctness fix; it is what recovered Frobenius `2103` (roots {3,−7},
  difference 10).
- **Converter — `<var>\cos(` juxtaposition** (`tools/latex_ode_to_mathilda.py`). A trig/`\op`
  function juxtaposed directly after a variable (`15x\cos(2x)`) glued into the bogus symbol
  `xCos` (and `fn_paren_to_bracket`, which skips a head preceded by a letter, then left the
  round-paren argument unbracketed). `_implicit_mult` now inserts the multiplication space
  before a FUNCS head, fixing `2185`; §2.2.20/§2.2.21 regenerate byte-identically.

The DSolve ODE corpus gains two 100-problem sections from Nasser Abbasi's 12000.org "Solving ODEs"

**Converter LaTeXML migration (`tools/latex_ode_to_mathilda.py`).** The 12000.org site has migrated
its HTML generator from **tex4ht to LaTeXML ("oxide")**; the `indexsubsectionN.htm` URLs the earlier
corpora were built from are now redirect stubs, and the live source is the `Ch2.S2.SSN.htm`
"sorted-sequentially" pages. The converter now auto-detects the format (LaTeXML has no tex4ht
`id='TBL-'` cells) and adds `_parse_table_latexml`: it selects the ODE `<table>`, reads the column
layout from the header `<tr>` legend (`# | ODE | classification | Solved? | Maple | Mma | Sympy |
time`) over `ltx_td` cells, and pulls each equation from the ODE cell's first `<math alttext>` — a
multi-row `\begin{array}` (row 1 the ODE, rows 2+ the initial conditions), read with DOTALL so the
IVP rows are not silently dropped. Two supporting fixes to the shared core: `strip_array` now
consumes the `\begin{array}[]` optional argument, and a new `_implicit_mult` pass inserts spaces at
implicit-multiplication boundaries (LaTeXML writes products with no delimiter — `8y`, `yx`, `3y'x` —
where tex4ht was space-delimited). `_implicit_mult` protects backslash macros and their brace
arguments (so `\prime`, `\operatorname{…}`, `\mathrm{…}` survive) and is a strict no-op on the old
spaced format. The `convert_row` LaTeX→Mathilda core and its IVP IC-splitting are unchanged; all 200
new records convert and round-trip through the parser.

**Kovacic churn — investigated, no safe fix.** The 8 non-PASS across the two sections (§2.2.20:
`1941`/`1964`/`1976` + symbolic-parameter Heun `1916`; §2.2.21: `2003`/`2005`/`2006`/`2080`) are all
`sympySolved=True` non-Liouvillian regular-singular ODEs whose `DSolve\`Kovacic` Case-1 Riccati solve
(`ds_solve` on `ω'+ω²==r`, cleared against the `(leading)²` denominator into a heavy high-degree
system) runs 9–18 s and then declines — overrunning the harness's 8 s `TimeConstrained` before the
Frobenius fallback (which returns the correct series in <0.1 s) can run. This is the "Kovacic
constant-`r` churn" class flagged for `1823` in M39. A bounded fix was attempted three ways and none

from a real solve — the churn *is* the Liouvillian-existence decision. The change was reverted to
0-regression clean main; these remain documented bounded declines (no wrong answers). A real fix needs
a fail-fast Riccati coefficient solver, not a heuristic gate — tracked as future work with `1823`.

No C/behavior change (Kovacic reverted); version 0.137 → 0.138.

## DSolve M39 — §2.2.19 corpus (Problems 1801–1900) + `DSolve\`VariationOfParameters` (transcendental nonhomogeneous backstop)

The DSolve ODE corpus gains section **2.2.19** (Nasser Abbasi 12000.org, Problems 1801–1900):
100 records, **all scalar (27 IVP), 0 systems**. The section is second-order-linear dominated —
3 Riccati, ~30 nonhomogeneous 2nd-order linear (many `sympySolved=False`, with transcendental
forcing `Tan[3x]` / `Sin[2x]Sec[2x]²` / `E^x Sec[x]` / `4/(1+E^-x)`), a large
`_with_linear_symmetries` variable-coefficient homogeneous family, Emden–Fowler and Gegenbauer.
Mathilda's second-order stack (Kovacic / SpecialFunctionForm / change-of-variable / Frobenius,
plus M38's forced-decay fix) already solved the bulk out of the box: **baseline 94/100, 0 FAIL →
95/100, 0 FAIL, 0 regression.**

**`DSolve\`VariationOfParameters` (`src/calculus/dsolve_nonhomog_vop.c`).** A new cascade method:
the general nonhomogeneous backstop for `L[y] == g(x)` (`g ≢ 0`) whose *homogeneous* part `L[y]==0`
is solved by a method that does not itself carry forcing. It recurses `DSolve` on the homogeneous
equation, extracts and normalises the fundamental set (`PowerExpand[Simplify[D[hom, C[k]]]]` —
Simplify canonicalises `1+Tan² → Sec²`, PowerExpand then reduces the residual radicals such as
`1/Sqrt[Sec² x] → Cos x` so the variation-of-parameters integrals close instead of staying inert),
builds the particular by `dsolve_variation_of_parameters`, and returns `hom + yp` (numerically
self-verified via `ds_branch_num_ok`, and rejected if it carries an inert `Integrate`). It is gated
to **transcendental (trigonometric) coefficients** — the ChangeOfVariable family — because a
rational-coefficient nonhomogeneous equation is Kovacic's / Euler's domain (each with its own
forcing closure); this keeps the one class the backstop uniquely reaches while sparing every rational
case a redundant recursive re-solve. Cascade slot: after the closed-form 2nd-order methods
(SpecialFunctionForm / Kovacic / ChangeOfVariable) so it never preempts their results. It is bounded
by the M12/M14 idiom (a `time()`-deadline + a decline memo + a re-entry guard) but deliberately uses
**no nested `TimeConstrained`** (that aborts the whole subtree — the documented no-nest hazard);
`SpecialFunctionForm`'s normal-form pre-pass (otherwise depth-1-gated to keep OperatorFactor
recursion finite) is allowed to fire while this method recurses, since it extracts the basis rather
than composing the `μ` recovery factor back into a reduction. Flagship: §2.2.19-1822
`Sin[x] y'' + (2Sin−Cos) y' + (Sin−Cos) y == E^-x` → homogeneous set `{E^-x, E^-x Cos[x]}` (via
`t=Tan[x]`) + particular `−E^-x Sin[x]`. Anti-overfit unit: `t_m39_nonhomog_vop`
(`tests/test_dsolve.c`) — 1822, a different forcing on the same operator, and the rational-coefficient
decline.

*Residue (5, all bounded declines, no wrong answers — every one `sympySolved=False` except the two
Kovacic-reachable ones):* `1817` (`2x y''+2y'+2y == Sin[√x]`: homogeneous is `BesselJ/Y[0, 2√x]` but
the particular is genuinely non-elementary — a Struve/Lommel integral); `1823` (modified-Bessel
`4x²y''−4xy'+(3−16x²)y == 8x^(5/2)`: the homogeneous + VoP particular `−√x/2` *is* elementary, but
Kovacic's constant-`r` path churns on the full equation before the transcendental-gated backstop is
reached — a Kovacic-robustness fix deferred to a later wave); `1836` (an `ExpIntegralEi`-form
solution whose IVP constant-fit `Solve` leaves unevaluated — a Solve limitation on a linear system
with special-function constant coefficients); `1876` (Kovacic emits the homogeneous set in
`E^(3Log[u]−2Log[v])` form whose IC-fit at `x=4` injects `Log[−√2]` branch artifacts — a
normalisation gap); `1900` (`(x²−x+1)y''−(1−4x)y'+2y==0` is exact but reduces to a first-order linear
with a non-elementary integrating factor — its complex-conjugate singular points would need a
complex-parameter Gauss ₂F₁ recogniser).

## DSolve M38 — §2.2.18 corpus (Problems 1701–1800) + zero_test decay false-positive → dropped-forcing fix

The DSolve ODE corpus gains section **2.2.18** (Nasser Abbasi 12000.org, Problems 1701–1800):
100 records, **all scalar (14 IVP), 0 systems**. 45 first-order / 55 second-order (no order ≥ 3);
mixed classes (25 2nd-order `_with_linear_symmetries`, 17 2nd-order linear exact/(non)homog, 10
separable, 8 2nd-order `_missing_x`, 11 Abel-2nd-kind, 8 quadrature, 5 Emden–Fowler of the
linear/Euler subtype, plus Bernoulli/Riccati/linear/homogeneous). Mathilda's second-order stack
(Kovacic / NormalForm / SpecialFunctionForm / change-of-variable / Frobenius) already solved the
bulk out of the box: **baseline 95/100, 0 FAIL → 96/100, 0 FAIL, 0 regression.**

**`zero_test` decay false-positive → dropped-forcing correctness fix.** `PossibleZeroQ`/`zero_test`
return **True** for a genuinely-nonzero *decaying* expression (a documented limitation of the numeric
shrinkage-trend ladder), e.g. `PossibleZeroQ[E^(-2x²)] === True`. Two second-order gates relied on it
and were silently corrupted: (1) the Wronskian nonzero-check in `dsolve_variation_of_parameters` — a
decaying fundamental-set Wronskian `W = E^(-∫P)` (e.g. `E^(-2x²)` for the `E^(-x²)` set) was read as a
degenerate basis, so variation of parameters wrongly declined; and (2) `DSolve\`Kovacic`'s forcing
detection — a decaying forcing was read as homogeneous, so the particular solution was dropped and a
**homogeneous-only WRONG answer** was shipped (it was masked as UNEVAL only by the corpus prelude's
leaked-`C[k]` → UNFIT leniency). The fix adds a shared `ds_is_structural_zero` (`Expand[·] === 0`) in
`dsolve_common.{c,h}` and gates both sites with it (`dsolve_variation_of_parameters` and
`dsolve_kovacic.c` ×2). Structural zero is the correct notion for these gates and is strictly safer:
a truly-zero forcing runs VoP to `yp == 0` (harmless) and a truly-dependent basis is still structurally
0, whereas the numeric test *dropped* real work. The sensitive `zero_test` sampler itself is
deliberately left untouched. `1763` `y''+4x y'+(4x²+2)y == 8E^(-x(x+2))` now solves as
`(C[1]+C[2]x)E^(-x²) + 2E^(-x²-2x)`. **Bonus (0 regression):** the same fix also raised §2.1.2 (−5),
§2.2.1 (−1), §2.2.2 (−2), §2.2.6 (−2) and §2.2.7 (−1) — forced equations with decaying Wronskians that
previously wrongly declined now solve. Anti-overfit: `t_m38_forced_decay_wronskian` (`tests/test_dsolve.c`).

**Residue (4, all `sympySolved=False`, bounded declines — never wrong answers):** `1708`/`1709`
(Abel-2nd-kind class B, the M13-deferred Abel Invariant Rational class), `1729` (integrating factor
`∫Sin[x]/(b Cos x − x Sin x)` is genuinely non-elementary), `1769` (VoP needs `∫E^x/√x → √π Erfi[√x]`;
the Erf/Erfi Risch tower handles only `∫x^(1/2)E^x`, not negative half-integer powers — a deep-Risch
extension left as future work).

`make check-c99` green; all DSolve ctest suites and every prior corpus gate (§2.1.2, §2.2.1–§2.2.17)
held with no regression. Version 0.135 → 0.136.

## DSolve M37 — §2.2.17 corpus (Problems 1601–1700) + fractional-power branch fix + `DSolve\`AbelAIR`

The DSolve ODE corpus gains section **2.2.17** (Nasser Abbasi 12000.org, Problems 1601–1700):
100 records, **all scalar (28 IVP), 0 systems**. First-order-dominated: 27 homogeneous, 23
Abel-tagged (most also carry `_homogeneous`/`_exact` and already solve), 14 separable, 8

**Fractional-power branch correctness (the 5 FAILs, `src/calculus/dsolve_common.c`,
`dsolve_bernoulli.c`, `dsolve_separable.c`).** Five fractional-power first-order ODEs
(`√y`, `y^(1/3)`, `y^(3/2)`) shipped a *demonstrably wrong* explicit branch — e.g.
`y'−2y==2√y, y(0)=1` returned the spurious constant `y=1` (which fails the ODE) instead of
`(2Eˣ−1)²`, and `y'=3x(y−1)^(1/3)` emitted a spurious complex twin beside the correct `1+x³`.
`dsolve_run`'s symbolic verify keeps a branch whose residual is an undecidable branch-cut
expression (the Solve policy), and the corpus prelude's numeric check then flagged it. Fixed
with a shared numeric back-substitution filter matching the prelude's own verdict, at three
points: the **IVP constant-fitter** (`dsolve_fit_constants`) now tries all `Solve` roots and
keeps the one whose body numerically satisfies the ODE (not blindly `args[0]`); **Separable**
and **Bernoulli** drop a `±`/principal-root branch that is robustly nonzero (new
`ds_branch_num_ok`, gated by a cheap `ds_has_radical_power` pre-check so rational/exp/trig
bodies skip the probe); and a **prelude-matching post-fit gate** (`ds_branch_corpus_verifiable`)
in `dsolve_run` drops any first-order explicit branch the harness itself would score BAD —
letting the cascade fall through to a later method's verifiable (often implicit) form, which is
how `y'−xy==xy^(3/2)` reaches `DSolve\`Separable`'s implicit `ArcCoth` first integral. The gate
reuses the prelude's grid + majority, so it can only ever remove a would-be-FAIL branch, never
one the harness would PASS.

**`DSolve\`AbelAIR`** (`src/calculus/dsolve_abel_air.c`, new method + `ATTR_PROTECTED` builtin).
A down payment on the deferred Abel Invariant Rational method (M13). It solves the Abel-2nd-kind /
rational-in-y forms `y' == N(x,y)/D(x,y)` with `D = c(x)(P1 y+P0)^k` a single linear y-factor
(`k∈{1,2}`) — exactly the shapes the polynomial-RHS specialists (Riccati/Chini/Abel-1st) reject
because `D` carries y. The scaling substitution `u = y/s` (`s = −P0/P1`, the denominator's y-root)
renders the equation separable; the first integral `G(x,y) = ∫1/B du|_{u→y/s} − ∫A dx` is returned
through `dsolve_run_implicit` (implicit-function-rule verified), with the separation itself checked
exactly (`omega_u − A B == 0`) before integrating. Closes `2.2.17-1604` (`u=eˣy → u'=2x/(1+u)`),
`-1607` (`u=e^(−2x)y → u'=x/(1−u)`), `-1606` (`(eˣ+y)²` denominator), `-1676`. The full
rational-invariant solvable-class table remains deferred. Cascade slot after Chini/Abel-1st,
before the substitution reductions and the Lie backstop.

**Residue (13, NOT wrong answers — bounded declines).** The eight `y=_G(x,y')` nonelementary
equations (`y'=tan(xy)`, `y'=√(x²+y²)`, `y'=(x²+y²)²`, …, all `sympySolved=False`); `2.2.17-1624`

## DSolve M35 — §2.2.15 corpus (Problems 1401–1500) + `DSolve\`PiecewiseForcing` (step/Heaviside forcing)

The DSolve ODE corpus gains section **2.2.15** (Nasser Abbasi 12000.org, Boyce & DiPrima,
Problems 1401–1500): 100 records — **39 scalar (18 IVP), 61 systems** (53 2×2 + 8 3×3

a 3rd-order variable-coefficient equation — both also unsolved by SymPy).

**New builtin `DSolve\`PiecewiseForcing`** (`src/calculus/dsolve_piecewise.c`, `ATTR_PROTECTED`).
Mathilda has `Piecewise`/`UnitStep` but no `LaplaceTransform`, so the Boyce & DiPrima
Laplace-transform problems are solved by **interval continuation**: split the independent axis
at the forcing's breakpoints, solve on each interval (recursing the scalar cascade), match
`y, y', …, y^(n-1)` across each breakpoint, and assemble a back-substitution-verified
`Piecewise` closed form (Mathematica's own output form). Runs before
`UndeterminedCoefficients`/`LinearConstantCoefficients`, gated to fire only on step/piecewise
forcing of a linear IVP; bounded like M12/M14 (re-entry guard + `TimeConstrained` sub-solves +
wall-clock deadline + per-top-level decline memo) and carries its own per-interval + per-IC
numeric verify. Example:
`DSolve[{y''[t]+4y[t]==Piecewise[{{1,0<=t<Pi}},0], y[0]==1, y'[0]==0}, y, t]` →
`Piecewise[{{1/4+3/4 Cos[2t], t<Pi}, {Cos[2t], t>=Pi}}, 0]`.

Before M35 these nine returned an inert `Integrate[UnitStep[…]·Cos,t]` that the corpus prelude
could not numericize (scored UNK → trusted → **false PASS**); they now genuinely solve.

Two shared-substrate fixes support this:
- **`dsolve_verify_body` distributional-keep** (`src/calculus/dsolve_common.c`): a residual
  carrying `UnitStep`/`Piecewise` joins `DiracDelta`/`HeavisideTheta`/definite-`Integrate` as
  accepted-on-construction. The numeric probe cannot run on it (differentiating a `Piecewise`
  yields a boundary term the probe reads as an undefined function) and `zero_test` decides a
  piecewise condition on the wrong branch — it spuriously rejected the correct step-forced answer
  of `y''+4y==Sin[t]−UnitStep[t−2π]Sin[t]` (1497). The producing method self-verifies every
  interval and IC.
- **Converter** (`tools/latex_ode_to_mathilda.py`): the `\left\{\begin{array}{cc}…\end{array}\right.`
  cases environment now converts to `Piecewise[{{v,c},…}, default]` (`otherwise` → default,
  `±∞` bounds dropped since a comparison against `Infinity` does not reduce to `True`); `\le`/`\ge`/
  `\infty` map to `<=`/`>=`/`Infinity`; Maple `Heaviside` → Mathilda `UnitStep`.

Version 0.132 → 0.133. Anti-overfit unit test `t_m35_piecewise_forcing` (`tests/test_dsolve.c`);
`make check-c99` green; §2.1.2/§2.2.1–§2.2.14 corpus gates all held.

## PossibleZeroQ — exponential-combining normalisation (the core fix for the Gaussian×Erf hang)

Resolves item #1 of `POSSIBLE_ZEROQ_IMPROVEMENTS.md` — the follow-up the M25
Erf-verify entry below deferred. `PossibleZeroQ[expr]` (and the internal
`zero_test_decide` / `zero_test_decide_assuming`) hung on the exact-ODE
integrating-factor residual `D[b,{x,2}] + x D[b,x] + b` where
`b = E^(-x^2/2)(C[1] + I C[2] Sqrt[Pi] Erf[-I x/Sqrt[2]]/Sqrt[2])` (identically 0;
`b` solves `y''+x y'+y==0`): the `E^(-x^2/2)` from the solution and the
`E^(x^2/2)` from differentiating `Erf` sit in *separate summands*, never combine
to `E^0`, and every Schwartz–Zippel sample numericalises as a tiny·huge
catastrophic cancellation, so the precision ladder climbs to 1000 bits per sample

- **New Stage 0.5 normalisation** (`src/zero_test.c`). Before any staged decision,
  when the input carries a constant-base exponential with a **non-linear**
  symbol-dependent exponent (`Power[E, f]` / `Exp[f]`, `f` super-linear in a free
  symbol), an `ExpandAll` pass distributes the sums so same-base exponentials
  become adjacent `Times` factors and collapse (`E^a·E^(-a) → 1`), removing the
  cancellation before the numeric ladder runs. For the flagship residual
  `ExpandAll` yields literal `0`, so the normalised input is decided by the O(1)
  structural stage — deterministic, no sampling. `ExpandAll` is value-preserving,
  so the verdict is provably unchanged; the gate only limits the (already cheap)
  cost. New helpers `exp_exponent_is_nonlinear` and `expr_has_symbolic_exp_kernel`;
  the public entries wrap renamed `zt_decide_core` / `zt_decide_assuming_core`.
- **Gate is non-linear on purpose.** An *affine* exponent (`E^x`, `E^(I x)`) stays
  representable at the sampler's range and the ladder resolves it, so it is left
  untouched — this both avoids needless work and prevents `ExpandAll` from
  perturbing a constant-coefficient ODE residual (whose `E^x` homogeneous terms
  cancel and whose real content is a trig identity) into a form the sampler
  mis-decides.
- **DSolve workaround removed** (`src/calculus/dsolve_common.c`). The core fix
  subsumes the M25 local workaround, so `dsolve_verify_body` drops both its
  Erf/Erfi-gated `ExpandAll` pre-pass and its `Power[E, ztexp] :> Power[E,
  Expand[ztexp]]` FALSE-path re-check; cases 428/482 and `y'+x y==Exp[3 x]` still

  verdict preservation, determinism); new ctest-registered

## Simplify hang fix (Factor/GCD `pseudo_rem`) + `TimeConstraint` option

`Simplify[(x+Sqrt[u]) u^(-1/2)]` hung. The heuristic search actually finished
instantly (`best = 1 + x/Sqrt[u]`); the hang was downstream, in Simplify's
radical-fraction polish calling `Factor`, which spun forever inside the
multivariate GCD's `pseudo_rem` — an **unguarded `while (true)`**
(`src/poly/poly.c:1479`) that never terminates when `Factor` treats the
algebraically-dependent generators `Sqrt[u]` and `u` as independent (the
pseudo-remainder degree in the main variable never drops). This was latent for
*every* `Factor`/`PolynomialGCD` caller, not just Simplify.

- **Root-cause fix — `pseudo_rem` degree-monotonicity guard** (`src/poly/poly.c`).
  A valid pseudo-division step strictly decreases the degree of `R` in the main
  variable; track `prev_degR` and `break` if it fails to. The guard is inert for
  every well-formed input and trips only on the degenerate radical-generator
  case, handing the non-reduced `R` back to the already-budgeted outer GCD loop
  (`iter_count > 50`). `Factor[1 + x/Sqrt[u]]` now returns instead of hanging.
- **Narrowed Simplify polish gate** (`src/simp/simp_builtins.c`,
  `has_compound_radicand`). The radical-fraction polish only calls `Factor` when
  a radical has a *compound* base it could expose (e.g. `Sqrt[6+x^2]`); bare-symbol
  radicals like `Sqrt[u]` are skipped. The intended
  `Simplify[(Sqrt[6] Sqrt[6+x^2])/(6x+x^3)] → Sqrt[6]/(x Sqrt[6+x^2])` case is
  preserved.
- **New `TimeConstraint` option on `Simplify`** (default `Infinity`). A
  synchronous, **per-sub-expression** wall-clock budget in seconds: when a
  sub-expression's heuristic search exceeds it, the best-so-far form is returned
  (not `$Aborted`). It is checked between search steps in `simp_search`
  (`src/simp/simp_search.c`) against a local deadline armed from a
  dynamically-scoped budget (`simp_set_time_budget`/`simp_current_time_budget`,
  save/restored in `builtin_simplify` — re-entrancy-safe), using a new monotonic
  clock helper `simp_mono_seconds` (`src/simp/simp_util.c`, POSIX-guarded). It
  bails through the normal return path, so it **never leaks** — unlike the async
  `TimeConstrained` mechanism, which it deliberately avoids. It does not
  interrupt a single long-running kernel call (that class is what the `pseudo_rem`
  guard handles). Accepts `t`, `Infinity`, or `{tLoc, ...}` (uses `tLoc`).
  Registered as a default option (visible in `Options[Simplify]`).

## DSolve M18 — 2nd-order reducible-μ integrating factor (`DSolve`ReducibleIntegratingFactor`)

New method for NONLINEAR second-order ODEs `y'' == Φ(x, y, y')` that admit an
**integrating factor** μ of a restricted two-variable form (Cheb-Terrab & Roche,
*Integrating Factors for Second-order ODEs*, J. Symb. Comput. 27 (1999) 501–519;
Maple's `_reducible, _mu_*` classes). `src/calculus/dsolve_ifactor.c`.

A function μ(x,y,y') is an integrating factor when `μ (y'' − Φ)` is a total
x-derivative `dR/dx` of a first integral `R(x,y,y')`; from the paper's (2.8)–(2.10)
`μ = R_{y'}`, `R = ∫μ dy' + G(x,y)`, with `G` fixed by `R_x + y'R_y + Φ R_{y'} = 0`.
`R(x,y,y') == C[1]` is then a **first-order ODE**, solved by recursing into the
scalar cascade (the second constant comes from that solve). Two verification gates
mean a wrong μ can only ever produce a clean decline, never a wrong answer: (1) the
symbolic first-integral test `A(R) ≡ R_x + y'R_y + Φ R_{y'} == 0` (the paper's
exactness condition (2.3)) rejects a wrong μ *before any solve*; (2) a numeric
back-substitution of the final explicit branch into the original residual rejects a
bad inversion.

**Stage 1 landed — μ(x,y) (Section 2.1).** Detects that Φ is a degree-≤2 polynomial
in `y'` (`y'' = a y'² + b y' + c`, 2.13), reads `a,b,c`, and branches on `2a_x − b_y`:
Case A (≠0) gives a closed-form μ (2.17) under the existence conditions (2.16); Case
B (=0) gives `μ = ν(x)·e^{−∫a dy}` where ν solves the one linear 2nd-order ODE the
algorithm needs (2.20), solved through the cascade. Solves e.g.
`y y' + y'' == 1` and `y'' − y y' == 6` (→ Airy), the arbitrary-parameter family
`y'' == a(1 + 2 y y')`, and the transcendental-coefficient Case-B family
`−(y'²/y²) + y''/y + 2 Coth[2x] y'/y == 2` (→ Log/ArcTanh form).

**Symbolic-parameter verification.** The numeric back-substitution gate now
instantiates the residual's free *parameters* (a, b, k, …) at generic reals — not
just `C[1],C[2],x` — so a correct solution of a symbolic-parameter ODE (the common
corpus form) numericizes instead of sampling to a symbolic, non-finite residual and
being wrongly rejected (the M16 lesson). Argument-position symbols only; function
heads (Cos, AiryAi, …) are never instantiated.

**Measured (§2.1.2 corpus, the 80 reducible-μ UNEVAL targets): +7 new PASS**
(2.1.2-184, 207, 693, 906, 1013, 1014, 1094), **0 FAIL** — the remaining targets need
Stages 2/3 (μ(x,y'), μ(y,y')) or carry arbitrary functions / non-closing reduced
first integrals.

**Robustness.** Mirrors the M12 lie2 kit: 6 s wall-clock deadline, `TimeConstrained`
on every sub-Solve/Integrate/DSolve, a per-top-level decline memo, node budgets, and
an undefined-function gate. A **linearity gate** declines linear ODEs (not the
method's domain, and it terminates the Case-B ν-ODE recursion so the linear
specialists own it). Cascade slot: 2nd-order-nonlinear tail, immediately before
`SecondOrderSymmetry` — the algebraic μ-search is cheaper than lie2's polynomial
ansatz and reaches ODEs with no point symmetry (paper §3).

Stage 2 (`_mu_x_y1`, Lemma 3 for 𝓕 + Lemma 2 for μ̃) was implemented and verified this
session for Cases A/C/D — it correctly finds valid integrating factors (Kamke 226 →
μ=y'; Kamke 136 → (y'−1)/h(y'); Kamke 66 → (y'+b)/(a(1+y'²)^{3/2}), all with `A(R)=0`
holding) — but **yields 0 new corpus solves**, because the reduced first integrals
`R==C[1]` are NON-ELEMENTARY first-order ODEs (`y'=√(x²y²+2C)`, `y'=Tan[C+Log[x−y]]`)
that neither our cascade nor — verified directly — Maple/Mathematica close in elementary
explicit form (those CAS return them implicitly). So it was reverted; Stage 2 is
**blocked on** an implicit/non-elementary first-order solver (or a policy decision to
emit the reduced first integral as an implicit answer). Stage 3 (`_mu_y_y1`, the `y↔x`
swap) depends on Stage 2. Exact equations transcribed in `DSOLVE_PLAN.md` M18.
`_mu_y2`/`_mu_poly_yn` (3rd-order integrating factors) are future.

## DSolve M19 — confluent Whittaker / ₁F₁ recogniser + §2.1.2 re-baseline

**Re-baseline first.** `DSolve_test_status/reports/2.1.2.*` was stale (pre-M17/M18); a

The confluent twin of M17's affine→Gauss row: a 2nd-order linear ODE whose Liouville
normal form `z'' = r z` has a **single finite regular singular point** `x0` (a double
pole of `r`) and a **rank-1 irregular point at infinity** (`r → b2 ≠ 0`) is Whittaker's
equation. Writing `r`'s coefficient as `Qc = b2 + b1/(x−x0) + b0/(x−x0)²` and matching
`W'' + (−1/4 + κ/z + (1/4−μ²)/z²)W == 0` under `z = c(x−x0)` gives
`c = 2√(−b2)`, `μ = √(1/4 − b0)`, `κ = b1/c`, and the two independent solutions are
emitted as the **verifiable** confluent-hypergeometric form
`Exp[−z/2] z^(1/2±μ) Hypergeometric1F1[1/2±μ−κ, 1±2μ, z]` (which numericizes and
auto-rewrites to `HypergeometricPFQ`, so the residual back-substitutes — the inert
`WhittakerM/W` heads are never emitted). New `specialform_whittaker_basis()` in
`src/calculus/dsolve_specialform.c`; no new method file, no cascade edit.

- **Detection** isolates the single finite double pole by the squarefree-part trick
  (`den / gcd(den, den')` of degree 1), so the two-finite-pole Gauss case (M17) and the
  pole-free Airy/polynomial cases decline here; `g = Cancel[Together[Qc (x−x0)²]]` must
  be a degree-2 polynomial with a nonzero leading coefficient (`b2 ≠ 0`).
- **Gate:** declines when `2μ` is a numeric integer (the two ₁F₁ partners are then
  dependent, or a lower parameter `1∓2μ` is a non-positive integer / singular ₁F₁) —
  a correct fall-through to the Frobenius log-second-solution; symbolic `μ` is kept.
- **`base` self-verifies** against the reduced equation `w'' + Qc w == 0` (numeric,
  parameters instantiated) before returning, so a mis-read pole / parameter can never
  ship — 0-FAIL by construction.
- **Scope: the y'-free (`P == 0`) surface only.** The recogniser runs where the equation
  is already `y'' + Q y == 0` (Q a rational with the required single-double-pole / rank-1-
  at-∞ shape). It is deliberately NOT run in the Liouville normal-form pre-pass (`P ≠ 0`):

  `$IterationLimit` and starve the Frobenius fallback that otherwise solves those as a

## Integrate — high-fugacity (degenerate) Fermi–Dirac half-line integrals

The half-line exponential-geometric kernel (`rec_expgeom` in
`src/calculus/integrate_ramanujan.c`) — which already reduced
`∫₀^∞ x^{s-1}/(A e^{cx}+γ) dx` to `Γ·PolyLog` for the Bose–Einstein / Fermi–Dirac
/ general-fugacity family — now covers the **high-fugacity / positive-chemical-
potential** Fermi regime `γ' > 1` (the **degenerate Fermi gas**: electrons in
metals, white-dwarf matter). Its fugacity gate was relaxed from the *geometric
series'* convergence bound `|γ'| ≤ 1` to the *integral's* exact convergence
condition `γ' ≥ -1`.

- **Why it was declined, and why it is correct.** The denominator
  `A_eff(e^{cx}+γ')` has an interior zero on `(0,∞)` iff `e^{cx}=-γ'` has a root
  `x>0`, i.e. iff `γ' < -1`. So for every `γ' > -1` the integrand is pole-free
  and the integral converges for `Re s > 0`. For `γ' > 1` the series
  `1/(e^{cx}+γ')=(-1/γ')Σ_{j≥1}(-γ')^j e^{-jcx}` diverges near `x=0`, but the
  closed form `-c^{-s}Γ(s)PolyLog(s,-γ')/γ'` is still correct by the standard
  Fermi–Dirac-integral analytic continuation `F_{s-1}(η) = -Li_s(-e^{η})`
  (Dingle/Blakemore) — `Li_s` is analytic off the cut `[1,∞)`, and its argument
  `-γ' < -1` sits on the negative real axis. The change is a single gate
  relaxation; the closed-form builder and convergence strip (`Re s>0`, tightened
  to `Re s>1` only at the Bose boundary `γ'=-1`) were already general.
- **The divergent Bose region `γ' < -1` still declines** (interior pole at
  `x=ln(-γ')>0`) — the relaxation lifted only the *upper* bound. Returning a
  finite value there would be wrong.
- **Symbolic fugacity** benefits identically: the interval-bound prover now
  admits `1/(e^x+z)` under `z>0` →
  `∫₀^∞ x^{s-1}/(e^x+z) dx = -Γ(s) PolyLog(s,-z)/z`, alongside the existing
  `0<z<1` Bose case.

Examples (previously returned unevaluated):
`∫₀^∞ 1/(e^x+2) dx = ½ Log[3]`, `∫₀^∞ x/(e^x+2) dx = -½ PolyLog[2,-2]`,
`∫₀^∞ 1/(e^x+5) dx = ⅕ Log[6]`,
`∫₀^∞ x^{s-1}/(e^x+z) dx = -Γ[s] PolyLog[s,-z]/z` (`s>0, z>0`). Each concrete
value was cross-checked against `NIntegrate` (agrees to machine precision).

[`klimanek/Bose-Fermi`](https://github.com/klimanek/Bose-Fermi) SymPy table —
whose remaining rows (zero-`μ` Bose/Fermi, rate scaling `e^{αx}`, chemical-
