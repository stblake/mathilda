# Changelog: week of 2026-09-28 (Mon) – 2026-10-04 (Sun)

## `Integrate` — `ParallelMixedTower`: the real form of the logarithmic part (`logrewrite.m`)

The Mathilda port of the research campaign of 2026-09-28 (`logrewrite.{py,wl,mac}`): the
logarithmic part of an antiderivative comes out of the residue analysis and the ansatz as
`Sum c_k Log[u_k]` with algebraic constants, and a real integrand gives its non-real terms in
conjugate pairs. The new module `src/internal/mixed/logrewrite.m` (a post-processing layer, not
part of the parallel algorithm; one function per function of `logrewrite.wl`) collapses every
pair `(c, u), (conj c, conj u)` to a real logarithm and arctangents (Rioboo's `LogToAtan`: a sum
of arctangents of polynomials in the top generator when the radical is absent, one arctangent of
a polynomial in the radical over the generators otherwise), every pair conjugate under
`Sqrt[p] -> -Sqrt[p]` to a real logarithm and a hyperbolic arctangent when its argument is a
polynomial, and, for a real problem, averages an unpaired non-real term with its formal
conjugate. `Integrate`ParallelMixedTower[1/(x (Log[x]^2 + 1)), x]` is now `ArcTan[Log[x]]`,
`1/(x^2 - 2)` gives `-ArcTanh[x/Sqrt[2]]/Sqrt[2]`, `Sqrt[x]/(1 + x)` gives
`2 Sqrt[x] - 2 ArcTan[Sqrt[x]]`; the trace line `real form: N pair(s) of logarithms collapsed
(Rioboo): k to arctan, l to artanh` is the same in the four ports. `.m` files only, no C change.

- **Loading.** `ParallelMixed.m` ends with `LoadModule["mixed/logrewrite.m"]` inside its private
  context (the loader resolves `src/internal/` like the lazy load of the package; `$InputFileName`
  and `DirectoryName`, which `ParallelMixed.wl` uses to find its sibling, do not exist here, A22).
  A default `LogToReal` that keeps the logarithms as they come is defined first, so a missing
  module file degrades to the previous output with one printed line instead of an unevaluated
  call in the result.
- **Host adaptations** (all catalogued with repros in `MATHILDA_DIVERGENCES.md`, A19–A24, B7,
  section D): every test for I uses `_Complex` (A19: `FreeQ[1 + 2 I, Complex]` is True here);
  constants are put in rectangular form by rules, `RectPow`, with `ComplexExpand` applied to
  `(-1)^k` only (A20: `ComplexExpand[Sqrt[2 + Sqrt[3]]]` gives `Cos[Arg[...]]` terms and
  `Arg[1 - Sqrt[5]]` stays unevaluated); `SturmCount` counts real roots (A21: no `CountRoots`);
  `RootFree`/`RootRadicals` replace Root objects before any `NumericQ` test and write a quartic
  root through its biquadratic or palindromic form when `ToRadicals` gives the Ferrari form
  (A23; Charlwood P4's constants); the module canonicalises with `CanRaw` and writes the m = 2
  quotient with the conjugate formula (A24: the number-field detour of `Can` returned a
  `Dot[{}, Inverse[{}], {}]` coefficient on Charlwood A40 after P4 in one kernel, and spent the
  30 s rewrite budget on A40 alone); a square root of a non-real number with real algebraic parts
  and a rational squared modulus counts as tame and equal logands are recognised coefficient by
  coefficient with `RootReduce`, since `Can` leaves nested radicals in non-canonical forms; the
  `Do` iterators are lr-prefixed (A18, still open on v0.222); the rewrite's own 30 s
  `TimeConstrained` cannot be cut short by the package budget (B7).
- **A defect of the campaign found and fixed in all four ports** (`YQuot` / `_y_quot` /
  `pm_y_quot`): over the radical the arctangent argument `A/B` was returned as a plain quotient,
  and when `B` was the radical itself the surface layer -- which rewrites the positive powers of
  the radical only -- read `q^(-1/2)` on the principal branch: Charlwood P5
  (`Cos[x]^2/Sqrt[Cos[x]^4 + Cos[x]^2 + 1]`) was wrong wherever `Tan[x] < 0` in Mathematica and
  was rejected by the verify gate here. The quotient is now a polynomial in the radical over the
  generators (the division of the tower coordinates).
- **Tests.** `tests/test_parallelmixedtower.c`: `test_method_real_form` (ArcTan[Log[x]],

## `FileSize` (v0.221)

Added `FileSize`, a filesystem-size query alongside the other `File*` builtins in
`src/files.c`.

- **`FileSize["name"]`** — the size of the file `"name"` in bytes, returned as a
  plain `Integer` (deliberately **not** a `Quantity`, unlike Wolfram's
  `FileByteCount`). `"name"` is interpreted relative to the current working
  directory; `$Path` is not searched.

Implemented with `stat()`, so symbolic links are followed and the target file's
size is reported. A path that cannot be `stat`'d (most often a missing file)
emits a `FileSize::nffil` message and returns `$Failed`; the message is routed
through a `Quiet[]`/`Check[]`-aware helper (mirroring `dt_msg` in
`src/datetime.c`), so `Quiet[FileSize[...]]` silences it while `Check[]` still
sees it. A non-string argument or wrong arity leaves the call unevaluated, so
symbolic arguments flow through unchanged. Attribute `Protected`; `SYM_FileSize`
interned in `src/sym_names.c`; docstring in `src/info.c`.

## `UnixTime` (v0.220)

Added `UnixTime`, the seconds-since-1970 companion to `AbsoluteTime` (seconds
since 1900). It is `AbsoluteTime` shifted by the fixed 1900→1970 epoch offset
`days_since_1900[1970,1,1] * 86400 == 2208988800`, and **always returns an
integer** (the nearest whole second — a deliberate difference from `AbsoluteTime`,
which keeps a real for fractional seconds).

- **`UnixTime[]`** — the current time as the true POSIX epoch second, from
  `time()` (GMT), matching the operating-system clock / `date +%s`.
- **`UnixTime[time]`** — a number taken as an `AbsoluteTime` (seconds since 1900),
  shifted to the Unix epoch: `UnixTime[3849984000] == 1640995200`.
- **`UnixTime[{y, m, d, h, m, s}]`** — a date-list spec, elided from the right and
  normalized exactly as `DateList`/`AbsoluteTime`: `UnixTime[{1970,1,1,0,0,0}]`
  → `0`, `UnixTime[{2022,1,1,0,0,0}]` → `1640995200`,
  `UnixTime[{2022,2,31}] == UnixTime[{2022,3,3}] == 1646265600`. Year and month
  must be integers — otherwise `UnixTime::arg` and the input is left unevaluated.
- **`UnixTime["string"]`** and **`UnixTime[{"string", {e1, ...}}]`** — the same
  date-string / format-element specs `DateList` accepts, reusing the `DateList`
  parser verbatim so the two heads always agree on interpretation.

**Reuse.** The date-list form shares `datelist_parts_to_abstime` with
`AbsoluteTime`; the string forms call `datelist_from_string` /
`datelist_from_format` and read the resulting list back to seconds via a new
`datelist_result_to_abstime` helper, then subtract the epoch offset and round.
Attribute `Protected`. Implemented in `src/datetime.c`; `SYM_UnixTime` interned
in `src/sym_names.c`; docstring in `src/info.c`.

Tests: `tests/test_datetime.c` gains epoch-zero, known-instant, elision,
out-of-range normalization, numeric-arg, the `AbsoluteTime` relation, string /
format parity, fractional-second rounding, current-time range, unevaluated /
bad-month, and the `Protected` attribute. No leaks; `make check-c99` clean.

## `DateList` (v0.219)

Added `DateList`, the broken-down date/time accessor and inverse of
`AbsoluteTime`. It returns `{year, month, day, hour, minute, second}` with the
first five as integers and the second as a real.

- **`DateList[]`** — the current local date and time, with sub-second precision
  from `clock_gettime(CLOCK_REALTIME)` (falling back to `time()`).
- **`DateList[time]`** — inverts an `AbsoluteTime` number (seconds since 1900).
- **`DateList[{y, m, d, h, m, s}]`** — a date-list spec, elided from the right
  (`{y}` ≡ `{y,1,1,0,0,0}`) and normalized: out-of-range and fractional fields
  reduce exactly as `AbsoluteTime` does (`DateList[{2022,0}]` →
  `{2021,12,1,0,0,0.}`, `DateList[{2022,1,0}]` → `{2021,12,31,0,0,0.}`,
  `DateList[{2022,3,15.5}]` → `{2022,3,15,12,0,0.}`). Year and month must be
  integers — a non-integer month issues `DateList::arg` and leaves the input
  unevaluated.
- **`DateList["string"]`** — heuristic string parse: month names (full or
  3-letter), a four-digit year, and `AM`/`PM`. A purely numeric string with no
  four-digit year is order-ambiguous, so the US `Month/Day/Year` order is taken
  and `DateList::ambig` is issued (`DateList["05/10/1"]` → `{2001,5,10,0,0,0.}`);
  a leading four-digit token reads as ISO `Year/Month/Day`.
- **`DateList[{"string", {e1, ...}}]`** — format-element parse. Elements
  `"Year"`, `"YearShort"`, `"Quarter"`, `"Month"`, `"MonthName"`, `"Day"`,
  `"DayName"`, `"Hour"`, `"Hour12"`, `"AMPM"`, `"Minute"`, `"Second"`,
  `"Millisecond"` are read in order; non-element strings between them are
  separators. Unfilled fields default to `{current year, 1, 1, 0, 0, 0}`.
  `DateList[{"09/28/26",{"Day","Month","YearShort"}}]` → `{2028,4,9,0,0,0.}`.

**One normalization backend.** Every form reduces to a raw `{y,m,d,h,mi,s}`
parts array, is converted to absolute seconds by the same
`datelist_parts_to_abstime` (reusing `days_since_1900`, factored out of
`builtin_absolute_time` so the two heads agree bit-for-bit), and inverted by a
new `gregorian_from_abstime` (inverse Fliegel & Van Flandern plus an intraday
split). Attribute `Protected`. Implemented in `src/datetime.c`; `SYM_DateList`
interned in `src/sym_names.c`; docstring in `src/info.c`.

Tests: `tests/test_datetime.c` gains structural coverage for absolute-time
inversion, elision, out-of-range/fractional normalization, the string and
format-element parses (including explicit separators, `YearShort`, current-year
fill, and the ambiguity path), the `DateList::arg` non-evaluation path, the
`DateList[]` shape, and the `Protected` attribute — and the `datetime_tests`
target is now registered with ctest (previously built but never run).

## `Pause`, `SessionTime`, `TimeUsed`, `$TimeUnit` (v0.218)

Added `Pause[n]`, which blocks for at least `n` seconds of wall-clock time and
returns `Null`, together with the three symbols its documentation references.

- **`Pause[n]`** — sleeps on `nanosleep` (a wall-clock timer that consumes no
  CPU), resuming across signal interruptions so the full duration is always
  observed. The argument is coerced to a machine double via a robust path that
  accepts integers, reals, bignums, rationals (`Pause[1/4]`), MPFR reals, and
  any `NumericQ` symbolic form (`Pause[Pi]`); zero/negative returns immediately
  and a non-numeric argument leaves `Pause[x]` unevaluated. Attribute
  `Protected`. Implemented in `src/datetime.c`; `SYM_Pause` interned in
  `src/sym_names.c`.
- **`SessionTime[]`** — wall-clock seconds since kernel start-up, from the same
  monotonic clock (`clock_gettime(CLOCK_MONOTONIC)`) used by `AbsoluteTiming`,
  measured against a baseline captured in `datetime_init()`.
- **`TimeUsed[]`** — CPU seconds used so far this session, via `clock()`.
- **`$TimeUnit`** — read-only real constant, `1 / CLOCKS_PER_SEC`, the
  resolution of the `clock()`-based timers and the granularity `Pause`
  documents itself against. Registered in `system_constants_init()`
  (`src/core.c`).

**The counted/not-counted semantic falls out of the existing two-clock design.**
`Timing`/`TimeUsed` read CPU time (`clock()`), which does not advance during a
`nanosleep`, so `Pause` is invisible to them (`Timing[Pause[1]]` ≈ `{0, Null}`).
`AbsoluteTiming`/`SessionTime` read the monotonic wall clock, so `Pause` is fully
counted (`AbsoluteTiming[Pause[1]]` ≈ `{1.0, Null}`). No special-casing is
needed; the two clock sources do the accounting.

Tests: `tests/test_datetime.c` gains coverage for the `Null` return, the
"at least `n` seconds" wall-clock guarantee, `Protected` attribute, symbolic
non-evaluation, the CPU-invisible / wall-counted split, and the three helpers.

## Message routing: every diagnostic now supports `Quiet[]` and `Check[]` (v0.222–v0.227)

`Quiet[]` (suppression) and `Check[]` (detection) rely on a two-step convention
each emission site must uphold — `mth_msg_note_fired()` so `Check` sees the
firing, plus a `mth_msg_suppressed()` guard so `Quiet` silences the print — but
there was no single choke-point, and an audit found **~294 diagnostics** emitted
as raw `fprintf(stderr, "Head::tag: …")` that upheld neither. Each was a wrong
answer for `Check[Head[bad], fallback]` (it returned `Head[bad]`, not the
fallback) and a leak past `Quiet[]` — `MatrixPower` escaped both; `Power::infy` /
`Infinity::indet` were invisible to `Check`.

- **The funnel.** New `mth_message(head, tag, fmt, …)` in `src/message.{c,h}`
  does both steps once; `mth_message_gated(extra_mute, …)` adds a subsystem
  probe-mute (`g_arith_warnings_muted`, `g_fm_quiet`) that suppresses a
  diagnostic **entirely — neither printed nor noted**, so an internal probe's
  sampling noise stays invisible to `Check` (e.g. `Check[Limit[Sin[x]/x, x -> 0],
  bad]` returns `1`, not `bad`), while `Quiet` still notes so `Quiet[Check[…]]`
  works. `mth_message_v` is the shared core the ~9 subsystem warn-helpers now
  delegate to; `arith_warn` matches the same contract.
- **Migration.** All ~294 sites across `linalg/`, `poly/`, `numerical_calculus/`,
  `numerical_roots/`, `calculus/`, `solve/`, `special_functions/`,
  `numbertheory/`, `strings/`, `list/`, `product/`, `bitwise/` and top-level
  `src/` were routed. `Integrate::nonelem` and the `RischTranscendental`
  advisory, previously a *deliberate* raw fprintf, are now under `Quiet`/`Check`
  too (their test is updated).
- **Enforcement.** New `make check-messages` (`tools/check_message_routing.py`,
  wired into the Linux CI job) reads the raw `Head::tag` stderr writes out of the
  source and diffs them against an `EXEMPT` (funnel body, parser, REPL, OOM) plus
  `BASELINE`; the backlog was burned to empty, so the gate is now assert-empty.
  See `docs/design/message_routing.md`.

## `Series` resolves a held series variable that names a symbol (v0.229)

`Series` and `SeriesCoefficient` are `HoldAll`, so the series *variable* arrives
unevaluated. Mathematica nevertheless resolves one that carries an OwnValue
naming another symbol —

```
Module[{w}, w = Unique["w"]; Normal[Series[Sqrt[1 + w^4], {w, 0, 4}]]]
   Mathematica:  1 + w11^4/2
   Mathilda was: Sqrt[1 + w11^4]        (* the input, silently *)
```

— and a `.m` routine that expands in a freshly generated symbol does exactly
that. Without the resolution the held symbol does not occur in the (evaluated)
expression at all, so the expression is *constant* in it and the "series" is the
input itself, returned with no message. `series_resolve_spec_vars`
(`src/calculus/series.c`) now resolves such a variable before parsing the spec,
for both heads. A value that is not a symbol is left alone, matching
Mathematica: a numeric value leaves the call unevaluated, an expression value is
treated as a variable the input is constant in.

This was silently crippling `ParallelMixed.m`: `RealiseClass` built the rows of
its exact linear algebra from `Series[Sqrt[q(1/w) w^(2h)/lc], {w, 0, n}]` in a
`Unique["w"]`, so the unbalanced configurations at infinity were solved against a
matrix carrying a square root of a polynomial where a rational number belonged,
and never realised a class. On the 371-integral review corpus:
**304 correct / 67 gap → 329 correct / 42 gap, 0 wrong**, kernel time 582 s → 249 s
(the binomial family 130 → 153 correct).

## `Integrate` — `ParallelMixedTower`: the Charlwood fifty at 1.8x the speed (v0.229)

Cost work only — the method computes the same things, and the suite still returns
49/50 verified antiderivatives (A39 the same honest `failed` as every system).
Kernel time over Charlwood's fifty, one process per integral: **23.53 s → 13.10 s**
(1.80×), against 9.34 s for Maxima and 17.18 s for Mathematica measured the same
way on the same day. Mathilda is now faster than Mathematica on the suite as a
whole, and on four rows it is faster than *both*: P8 0.64 s against 1.36 / 1.56,
P4 1.24 against 1.73 / 2.27, A33 and A17; A28 and A38 sit on the target. Biggest
single-row wins: A28 3.45 s → 0.23 (14.9×), P8 1.70 → 0.64, P4 2.01 → 1.24.

Kernel:

- **`Cancel`'s extension option is asked for only when it is needed.** The
  package's canonical form `CanRaw` was `Cancel[Together[e], Extension -> Automatic]`
  unconditionally. Measured on this kernel, the plain one-argument `Cancel`
  detects a radical coefficient field from the operands themselves and reduces
  identically (`x^2 - 2` over `x - Sqrt[2]`, a common factor over `Q(Sqrt[5])`, a
  nested `Sqrt[1 + Sqrt[5]]`), while the option re-derives the splitting field on
  every call — 51 ms against 1.9 ms on one nested-radical fraction. The one case
  that genuinely needs it is `Complex`. Charlwood P8 1.62 s → 0.59 s, A40 1.40 →
  1.11, the suite −1.4 s.
- **`replace_bindings` builds its capture-avoidance set once, lazily**
  (`src/match.c`). It recursed into arguments through the public entry point, so
  the set — a full walk of every binding value — was rebuilt at *every node* of
  every rule RHS; and it is needed only when the RHS actually contains a
  `Module`/`With`/`Table`-style binder. Interned names also make the `strcmp`
  fallback dead. This is most of the small-row regression the A11 capture fix
  introduced in v0.212.
- **`expr_compare`'s degree tier consults the persistent per-node symbol-set
  cache outside an Orderless sort** (`src/sort.c`). It was consulted only from
  `symmemo_lookup`, i.e. only *during* a sort, so a bare comparison — `Plus`'s and
  `Times`' already-sorted pre-check on every re-evaluation — re-walked both whole
  subtrees each time.
- **`AlgebraicNumber` is a constant, not a polynomial variable.**
  `collect_variables` (`src/poly/poly.c`) enrolled each distinct
  `AlgebraicNumber[theta, coords]` as an independent generator — and `Plus`/`Times`
  fold a rational scalar into the coordinate vector, so `2 a` is a structurally
  distinct atom from `a`, i.e. two "independent" variables that are Q-linearly
  dependent. `Variables[AlgebraicNumber[Sqrt[2], {0,1}] + x]` is now `{x}`, as in
  Mathematica. `autodetect_walk` (`src/poly/qafactor.c`) likewise no longer mines
  the field's label `theta` out of an `AlgebraicNumber` as a tower generator —
  which made `PolynomialGCD[(x+a)(x+1), (x+a)(x+2), Extension -> Automatic]`
  answer `1`; it now answers `x + a`.

Package (`src/internal/mixed/`), each verified against the suite and the review
corpus:

- **`VanishOrder` by exact arithmetic.** `ord_P(a + b y)` at an unramified place
  is the multiplicity of `rho` in the norm `a^2 - q b^2` once the common factor
  `(g - rho)` is divided out of `a` and `b`. It was the order of vanishing of a
  `SeriesCoefficient` of `Sqrt[q(rho + e)]` with algebraic constants — 3.3 s of
  A28's 3.4 s. A28 3.24 s → 0.22 s (now at Maxima's 0.23 s); checked to agree with
  the series on every call of the suite.
- **The assembly without quadratic primitives.** `AppendTo` rebuilds the whole
  list on every call in this kernel (3,000 appends 61 ms against 1.6 ms for
  `Reap`/`Sow`), and an element assignment into a matrix copies a row per
  element; `parts` and the monomial columns are now built with `Table`/`Join`,
  the 10^4 equation entries with `Reap`/`Sow`, and the augmented matrix row by
  row (158 ms → 15 ms on a 300 x 140 fill). The 1-part and the y-part of each
  monomial column also share the derivative contraction, which was computed
  twice, identically.
- **`Can`'s field detour drops `Extension -> Automatic`** (the coefficients are
  already `AlgebraicNumber` in one field, so the option only re-derives that
  field), `FieldData` **declines** when the monomials in the atoms do not span
  the field instead of returning the `Dot[{}, Inverse[{}], {}]` garbage of
  divergence A24, and its memo is shared with `AnsatzSystem`.
- **Rung-invariant work is cached.** The unit/S'-unit/special logand columns do
  not depend on the degree bounds or the special exponents, so the retry ladder
  (five rungs on P4, A2, A3, A16) no longer re-derives them.
- **Smaller cuts**: the residual `f - Sum tau Du/u` runs one `Can` per logand
  instead of two; `RealiseClass` tries the cheapest configuration at infinity
  first (A27 −0.15 s); `KGcd` takes the number-field gcd without the extension
  option when the input is univariate; `RootRadicals`, `RealZeroFreeQ` and
  `ClassifyPrime` are memoised; the tower-specials scan canonicalises each
  derivation component once rather than twice.
