# Changelog: week of 2026-11-23 (Mon) – 2026-11-29 (Sun)

## PossibleZeroQ past the 1000-bit rung; three slow Bronstein cases (Campaign D6, v0.6699)

**PossibleZeroQ (G-pzq-1).** Integrate's Euler-substitution answer for 257 `cM-3-152`,
`((1 + E^x^2)(1 + E^x^2 + E^(2 x^2))^(3/2) x)/E^x^2`, was correct but read `False`. Its
`E^(x^2) ~ 2^90` sits under nested `(2 Sqrt[q] - 2 u - 1)^k` denominators, and at a sample
point near `x = -7.9` the cancellation is about 1300 bits deep: the residual reads `2^274` at
200, 500 and 1000 bits, `2^-331` at 2000 and `2^-2329` at 4000. Three changes to the
huge-scale path in `src/zero_test.c`:

- On an exact point whose residual stays flat over the whole ladder, the climb now doubles
  past 1000 bits (up to 8000, `ZT_HUGE_EXTEND_MAX_BITS`). It decides on the ladder's own
  evidence: a zero falls by at least half the bits added, and a genuine value holds.
- The rung-1 plateau test no longer compares against the machine residual, which a deep
  cancellation under-reads.
- A residual still falling by half the added bits at the top rung counts as a zero, for the
  same reason, on the point path and in the constant's re-read.

`Exp[300] + 1 - Exp[300]`, `Exp[10^4] (Sin[Pi/7]^2 + Cos[Pi/7]^2 - 1) + 1` and both
perturbations of the cM-3-152 residual (`+ x E^(x^2)/10^6` and `+ 10^-20`) stay `False`.

**The kernel substitution is verified in u.** f = h(theta) D theta by construction, so
`D[G, u] = h` (antiderivativeQ and PossibleZeroQ in u), together with an exact check of that
construction, proves `G(theta)` by the chain rule. The test in u is small; the test in x was
the one that met G-pzq-1.

**Three slow cases:**

| case | before | after | what |
|---|---|---|---|
| 257 `cM-3-152` | 7.5 s | 0.06 s | the kernel substitution's answer is accepted (above) |
| 260 `two-elem-49` | 2.9 s | 0.10 s | `powerBasisInverse` reads a monomial `c y^j` structurally |
| 257 `cM-4-306` | 3.3 s | 2.2 s | exponential-kernel budget 1.5 s → 0.6 s |

- **`two-elem-49`.** The monomial test ran on `Expand[q]`, which distributed
  `2 y^5 (1 + x^3)^2 (3 + x^4)^3` into many terms. The denominator then went to the general
  6 × 6 symbolic system, which was 2.8 s of the 2.9 s.
- **`cM-4-306`.** Every cM success closes within 0.31 s. This case, where Trager in u needs
  2.5 s, now falls to the 1.8 s tower sooner. Its remaining cost is the principal-divisor
  generator (`kRowEchelon`).

`cB-2-1053` (1.8 s) was left alone. `Automatic` already closes it at once in the
QuadraticRadicals stage. The 1.8 s is the `Method -> "Trager"` column, which measures
Trager's algorithm itself. Sending that method to the Euler substitution first would have
changed its answer form (`ArcSinh[x]` for `Log[x + Sqrt[1 + x^2]]`) and misreported what the
column measures.

## Trager's normalisation at infinity in C; two PossibleZeroQ false negatives (Campaign D6, v0.6700)

**The divisor-generator step.** `principalGenerator` decides whether a divisor is
principal by normalising the basis of its ideal at infinity ([T] Ch 2 Sec 3).
`normalizeAtInfinity` did this in .m, one unimodular step per pass. On 257 `cM-4-306`
(Trager in u = E^(2x) over a cube-root curve), each pass ran over Q(ω) on a 3 × 3 matrix of
degree 24. That took 35 passes and 0.5 s per divisor, and two divisors made 1.0 s of the
1.76 s.

The normalisation is a shifted weak Popov form with shift δ:
- the key of entry j, `deg c_j + δ_j`, is minus its order at infinity plus `deg den`;
- the kernel breaks a tie on the first column of greatest key, so distinct leading columns put
  the leading vectors in echelon form, which makes them independent.

So `normalizeAtInfinity` now calls the existing C kernel ``FLINT`PolynomialWeakPopovForm``
for every field it supports (Q, GF(p), Q(θ)). The .m loop stays as the fallback: for
an unsupported field, a non-integer shift, or a zero row (a rank-deficient module, still an
internal error).

| case | v0.6699 | v0.6700 |
|---|---|---|
| 257 `cM-4-306`, `Method -> "Bronstein"` | 1.89 s | 1.14 s |
| its kernel in u, `Method -> "Trager"` | 1.76 s | 0.73 s |

The generator can differ from the .m loop's by a constant factor, and the log terms can take
a different form. Two such forms exposed two PossibleZeroQ false negatives (G-pzq-1) in the
verifier. Both answers were correct, and both are fixed in `src/zero_test.c`:

- **A FALSE needs two rungs that agree in value.** The ArcTan form of the antiderivative of
  `D[x + Log[-E^x + (1 + E^(3 x))^(1/3)], x]` (paper example E3-6) has an exactly zero
  residual. At `x = 63.7477` its terms are ratios of sums of size `2^560`, and it reads
  1.6, 2.98 and 2.975 at 200, 500 and 1000 bits, then 0 from 2000 bits. The top two rungs had
  the same magnitude, which the old rule read as a settled value.
  - `zt_resolve_machine_nonzero` now keeps the values of its last two rungs and confirms a
    FALSE only when they agree to `2^-32` relative (`ZT_RESOLVED_GAP_BITS`).
  - Otherwise it doubles the precision up to 8000 bits, deciding on the ladder's evidence: a
    zero falls by half the bits added, and a value reads the same twice.
  - A sample point still unresolved at the cap is re-drawn from a smaller shell, while one is
    left.
  - Genuine values agree at once and cost one extra subtraction.
- **A signed-zero imaginary part on a cut.** Bronstein's answer to
  `Sqrt[Tan[x]] + Sqrt[Cot[x]]` (paper example E6d-4) holds
  `Sqrt[(-I - I E^(-2 I x))/(-1 + E^(-2 I x))]`, which is `Sqrt[Cot[x]]`. At `x = -3.26342`
  the radicand reads `-8.17 + 0.*^-60 I`, and the sign of that zero picks the side of the
  cut. The unresolvable-cut test now counts an inexact zero imaginary part, and it reads the
  exact point, where a machine-real coordinate had kept machine noise at every rung. A
  radicand computed as a real is still evidence:
  `Sqrt[(1 - x)/(1 + x)] (1 + x) - Sqrt[1 - x^2]` stays `False`. That answer was also
  wrong-signed at sampled points in v0.6699, and the old generator passed only because the
  sampler drew other points.

Tests:
- `tests/test_zero_test.c`: `test_unresolved_rungs_climb` and `test_signed_zero_cut_redraw`
  (both residuals, perturbations, genuine non-zero controls).
- `tests/test_trager_normalize_ideal.c`: the native and .m normalisations agree on the orders
  at infinity over Q(√2) and GF(5).

## CI repairs: the no-MPFR build and the packed-aware audit (v0.6701)

- **No-MPFR build.** `make USE_MPFR=0` stopped compiling at v0.6681: the
  piecewise-linear interpolant splitting in `NIntegrate`
  (`src/numerical_calculus/nint.c`) is a machine-precision path, but it called
  `ni_free_of_var` and `ni_numeric_poly_degree`, which lived inside the
  `USE_MPFR` block of the contour-rotation code. The two structural helpers (and
  `NI_SING_MAX_DEGREE`) now sit above that block, with the `EXPR_MPFR` case
  label guarded.
- **`make check-packed-aware`.** The Dispatch rule index (v0.6672) reads
  `is_packed_list` in `src/replace.c` so a packed-list left-hand side, or a
  packed node met while walking, hashes with its plain-List twin. That is
  representation tolerance, not a buffer kernel, so `ReplaceAll` and
  `ReplaceRepeated` are recorded in the audit's `EXEMPT` list with that reason
  rather than added to `AWARE`.

## PossibleZeroQ reads a cut point at its principal value; ArcTan on its lower cut at arbitrary precision (v0.6702)

Investigating the two v0.6700 PossibleZeroQ fixes found that one of them had caused a
wrong `True`, along with an older wrong `True`.

- **The two v0.6700 cases.** Both answers were correct.
  - E3-6: the residual is exactly 0 but cancels about 1100 bits at `x = 63.75`. That is
    two cancellations of about 554 bits each (`12 x log2(e)`), so the 1000-bit rung was
    genuinely too shallow.
  - E6d-4: with its radicands replaced by the exact identities `Cot[x]` and `Tan[x]`, the
    residual is 0 at points with `Tan < 0` as well as `Tan > 0`. The nonzero machine
    readings were branch-cut noise.
- **Re-drawing a cut point hid an interval.** v0.6700 re-drew a point where a radicand is
  real by value but computed through complex arithmetic. On a whole interval every point is
  such a point, so a residual wrong only there went unseen. With `ztC`, `ztT` the exponential
  forms of `Cot[x]`, `Tan[x]`:
  - `PossibleZeroQ[(Sqrt[ztC] Sqrt[ztT] - 1) UnitStep[-Tan[x]]]` was `True` at v0.6700
    (`False` before);
  - `PossibleZeroQ[Sqrt[ztC] Sqrt[ztT] - 1]` was `True` on both, through correlated noise.

  Both are `False` now. The point is read at its principal value instead of being re-drawn:
  - the argument's noisy component is dropped once it falls between 200 and 400 bits on the
    exact point (`b -> Re[b]` for cuts on the real axis, `b -> I Im[b]` for `ArcTan`,
    `ArcCot`, `ArcSinh` and `ArcCsch`);
  - the rewrite goes on the sample tree, so nodes not on a cut read as before;
  - every cut function is projected: projecting `Log` alone broke the noise correlation with
    `ArcTan`, and read `D[F] - ArcTan[Sqrt[x]]` (Bronstein's answer) as `Pi` at `x < -1`.
- **`ArcTan[0 - 2 I]` at MPFR precision read `+Pi/2 - 0.549 I`.** The exact value and the
  machine kernel give `-Pi/2 - 0.549 I`. `mpfr_complex_atan` negated a zero real part into
  `-0`, which put `Log` on the wrong side of its cut. `ArcCot[+-I/2]` had the same error,
  through `ArcTan` of the reciprocal. A sweep of the inverse trig and hyperbolic functions
  plus `Log` and `Sqrt` over eleven cut points now shows no differences between the exact,
  MPFR and machine values.

The gated cost (an expression with a complex constant and a cut function) is a few ms per
call, and ztR6d drops from 1.24 s to 0.32 s because fewer points are re-drawn. Tests: the
`test_signed_zero_cut_redraw` additions in `tests/test_zero_test.c`, and
`test_arctan_mpfr_lower_cut` in `tests/test_trig.c`.

## The branch-cut pre-read and the good-prime search (Campaign D6, v0.6703)

- **PossibleZeroQ's cut test reads in doubles first.** v0.6702 read every cut
  function's argument at 200 bits at every sample point of an expression with a
  complex constant. On Trager's answer for 257 `cM-4-306`, 207 cut functions, the
  verification went from 0.57 s to 0.85 s. A machine-precision read now skips any
  argument more than 2^-30 (relative) from its cut line: such an argument cannot be
  rounding noise on the line. A real machine value is skipped only when it is off
  the half-line or the imaginary axis. `1 + I Sqrt[-3.77]` is exactly real in
  doubles and noisy at 200 bits, so it still has to be read at 200 bits. The
  check now takes 0.40 s.
- **Trager's torsion screen tests each prime with the data computed once.**
  `goodPrime` recomputed the squarefree part of G, the discriminant of
  `Gs Bp`, the resultant of `Bp` and `Ep`, and the residue field's discriminant
  for every candidate prime, about 98 primes on `cM-4-306`.
  `goodPrimeInvariants` computes them once per component (cached on the data).
  Each prime is first tested for a degree-1 prime of the residue field, the
  commonest failure. The search went from 0.086 s to 0.038 s.

`cM-4-306` in u, `Method -> "Trager"`: 0.84 s to 0.79 s. The rest is the answer
check (about 0.4 s), the torsion screen's Jacobian arithmetic (about 0.1 s,
already in C kernels) and the residue ideals (about 0.1 s). A C port of the mod-p
order loop could save about 0.15 s more.

## Elementary functions agree on every surface, on and off the branch cuts (v0.6704)

An audit of every circular and hyperbolic function and its inverse, `Log`, `Sqrt`, `Exp`
and `Power` with rational exponents compared the exact value `N[f[p], n]` with MPFR at 30,
60 and 200 digits (each against the exact value at the same rounded input, so only the
algorithm's error counts), machine scalar, packed, NDArray and `Compile[]` (scalar and
rank 1). Points: both sides of every cut, the branch points, `10^-20` and `10^-100` off
the cuts, signed zeros, `10^-30`, `10^30` and `10^100` along the axes and diagonals, and
generic points. Before: 563 mismatches; after: none (the remaining unevaluated cases are the
singular points). Odd symmetry on the cuts, and the exact values against the
principal-branch definitions, hold on every surface.

- **Machine kernels and `Compile[]` on the imaginary cuts.** `ArcTan`, `ArcCot`, `ArcSinh`
  and `ArcCsch` of a packed list, an NDArray or a compiled argument took C99's side of the
  imaginary-axis cut: `ArcTan[{-2. I}]` was `{1.5708 - 0.549 I}` where the scalar, the exact
  value and MPFR give `-1.5708 - 0.549 I`; `ArcCot[{0.5 I}]`, `ArcSinh[{-2. I}]`,
  `ArcCsch[{0.5 I}]` likewise. `catan`/`casinh` read the side from the sign of the zero
  real part; `mth_machine_elem.h` now passes the zero whose sign gives the definition's
  side, so all four surfaces share one answer. The scalar `ArcCsch` now calls the same
  helper.
- **MPFR complex inverses lost digits, or everything.** The log forms
  (`ArcSin = -I Log[I z + Sqrt[1 - z^2]]`, ...) cancelled on half the plane:
  `ArcSin[SetPrecision[-10^30, 40]]` was `Overflow[]`, at 60 digits it had six correct;
  `ArcTan` of `10^-30 I` at 30 digits had six; `ArcCot` at `I + 10^-20` 23 of 30. They are
  now Kahan's cancellation-free forms, internally at 32 guard bits, with the same values on
  the cuts. The reciprocal functions take `(z -+ 1)/z` as `(z -+ 1) conj(z)/|z|^2`: Smith's
  division had rounded the `10^-200` imaginary part of `(z - 1)/z` at `z = -10^100 + I` to
  an exact 0, which put `ArcSech` on its cut, with the wrong sign of `Pi/2 I`. The real MPFR
  `ArcSec`, `ArcCsc`, `ArcCoth`, `ArcSech` no longer round `1/x` before a function that is
  infinitely sensitive at `x = +-1` (`ArcSec[1 + 10^-25]` at 60 digits had 41 correct).
- **MPFR `Log` near `|z| = 1`.** `Log[SetPrecision[1 + 10^-20 I, 30]]` had `Re = 0`
  (`log(hypot)` of a number rounding to 1); it is `Log1p[(a - 1)(a + 1) + b^2]/2` there.
- **MPFR forward functions at a huge imaginary part.** `Tan`, `Cot`, `Sec`, `Csc`, `Tanh`,
  `Coth`, `Sech`, `Csch` overflowed `Cosh^2` and returned `Indeterminate`
  (`Tan[SetPrecision[10^30 I, 30]]`, even `Tan[SetPrecision[10^9 I, 30]]`); they are
  divided through by `Cosh^2`, so `Tan[...] = I`, `Sec[...] = 0`. `Sin`/`Cos`/`Exp` of a
  zero part times an overflowed factor give an exact 0 part (`Overflow[]`, not
  `Indeterminate`).
- **`N` of `E^z` for complex `z`.** The generic path computed `exp(z log e)` and multiplied
  the rounding of `log e` by `|z|`: `N[Exp[I d], 30]` for `d` the 100-digit integer value of
  `1.*^100` was `0.644 + 0.765 I` (the right value is `0.925 - 0.381 I`). A complex exponent
  now goes to one MPFR complex `exp`, as a real one already did.

Not changed: at a singular point the machine value is `Overflow[]` where the exact value is
a directed infinity (`ArcTan[1. I]`, `ArcTanh[1.]`).

Tests: `test_inverse_imag_cut_all_surfaces`, `test_mpfr_complex_inverse_accuracy` and
`test_mpfr_complex_forward_huge` in `tests/test_trig.c`.
