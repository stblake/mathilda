# Changelog: week of 2026-11-23 (Mon) – 2026-11-29 (Sun)

## PossibleZeroQ past the 1000-bit rung; three slow Bronstein cases (Campaign D6, v0.6699)

**PossibleZeroQ (G-pzq-1).** Integrate's Euler-substitution answer for 257 `cM-3-152`,
`((1 + E^x^2)(1 + E^x^2 + E^(2 x^2))^(3/2) x)/E^x^2`, was correct but read `False`. Its
`E^(x^2) ~ 2^90` sits under nested `(2 Sqrt[q] - 2 u - 1)^k` denominators, and at a sample
point near `x = -7.9` the cancellation is about 1300 bits deep: the residual reads `2^274` at
200, 500 and 1000 bits, `2^-331` at 2000 and `2^-2329` at 4000. Three changes to the
huge-scale path in `src/zero_test.c`:

- On an exact point whose residual stays flat over the whole ladder, the climb now doubles
  past 1000 bits (up to 8000, `ZT_HUGE_EXTEND_MAX_BITS`). It decides on the ladder's own
  evidence: a zero falls by at least half the bits added, and a genuine value holds.
- The rung-1 plateau test no longer compares against the machine residual, which a deep
  cancellation under-reads.
- A residual still falling by half the added bits at the top rung counts as a zero, for the
  same reason, on the point path and in the constant's re-read.

`Exp[300] + 1 - Exp[300]`, `Exp[10^4] (Sin[Pi/7]^2 + Cos[Pi/7]^2 - 1) + 1` and both
perturbations of the cM-3-152 residual (`+ x E^(x^2)/10^6` and `+ 10^-20`) stay `False`.

**The kernel substitution is verified in u.** f = h(theta) D theta by construction, so
`D[G, u] = h` (antiderivativeQ and PossibleZeroQ in u), together with an exact check of that
construction, proves `G(theta)` by the chain rule. The test in u is small; the test in x was
the one that met G-pzq-1.

**Three slow cases:**

| case | before | after | what |
|---|---|---|---|
| 257 `cM-3-152` | 7.5 s | 0.06 s | the kernel substitution's answer is accepted (above) |
| 260 `two-elem-49` | 2.9 s | 0.10 s | `powerBasisInverse` reads a monomial `c y^j` structurally |
| 257 `cM-4-306` | 3.3 s | 2.2 s | exponential-kernel budget 1.5 s → 0.6 s |

- **`two-elem-49`.** The monomial test ran on `Expand[q]`, which distributed
  `2 y^5 (1 + x^3)^2 (3 + x^4)^3` into many terms. The denominator then went to the general
  6 × 6 symbolic system, which was 2.8 s of the 2.9 s.
- **`cM-4-306`.** Every cM success closes within 0.31 s. This case, where Trager in u needs
  2.5 s, now falls to the 1.8 s tower sooner. Its remaining cost is the principal-divisor
  generator (`kRowEchelon`).

`cB-2-1053` (1.8 s) was left alone. `Automatic` already closes it at once in the
QuadraticRadicals stage. The 1.8 s is the `Method -> "Trager"` column, which measures
Trager's algorithm itself. Sending that method to the Euler substitution first would have
changed its answer form (`ArcSinh[x]` for `Log[x + Sqrt[1 + x^2]]`) and misreported what the
column measures.

## Trager's normalisation at infinity in C; two PossibleZeroQ false negatives (Campaign D6, v0.6700)

**The divisor-generator step.** `principalGenerator` decides whether a divisor is
principal by normalising the basis of its ideal at infinity ([T] Ch 2 Sec 3).
`normalizeAtInfinity` did this in .m, one unimodular step per pass. On 257 `cM-4-306`
(Trager in u = E^(2x) over a cube-root curve), each pass ran over Q(ω) on a 3 × 3 matrix of
degree 24. That took 35 passes and 0.5 s per divisor, and two divisors made 1.0 s of the
1.76 s.

The normalisation is a shifted weak Popov form with shift δ:
- the key of entry j, `deg c_j + δ_j`, is minus its order at infinity plus `deg den`;
- the kernel breaks a tie on the first column of greatest key, so distinct leading columns put
  the leading vectors in echelon form, which makes them independent.

So `normalizeAtInfinity` now calls the existing C kernel ``FLINT`PolynomialWeakPopovForm``
for every field it supports (Q, GF(p), Q(θ)). The .m loop stays as the fallback: for
an unsupported field, a non-integer shift, or a zero row (a rank-deficient module, still an
internal error).

| case | v0.6699 | v0.6700 |
|---|---|---|
| 257 `cM-4-306`, `Method -> "Bronstein"` | 1.89 s | 1.14 s |
| its kernel in u, `Method -> "Trager"` | 1.76 s | 0.73 s |

The generator can differ from the .m loop's by a constant factor, and the log terms can take
a different form. Two such forms exposed two PossibleZeroQ false negatives (G-pzq-1) in the
verifier. Both answers were correct, and both are fixed in `src/zero_test.c`:

- **A FALSE needs two rungs that agree in value.** The ArcTan form of the antiderivative of
  `D[x + Log[-E^x + (1 + E^(3 x))^(1/3)], x]` (paper example E3-6) has an exactly zero
  residual. At `x = 63.7477` its terms are ratios of sums of size `2^560`, and it reads
  1.6, 2.98 and 2.975 at 200, 500 and 1000 bits, then 0 from 2000 bits. The top two rungs had
  the same magnitude, which the old rule read as a settled value.
  - `zt_resolve_machine_nonzero` now keeps the values of its last two rungs and confirms a
    FALSE only when they agree to `2^-32` relative (`ZT_RESOLVED_GAP_BITS`).
  - Otherwise it doubles the precision up to 8000 bits, deciding on the ladder's evidence: a
    zero falls by half the bits added, and a value reads the same twice.
  - A sample point still unresolved at the cap is re-drawn from a smaller shell, while one is
    left.
  - Genuine values agree at once and cost one extra subtraction.
- **A signed-zero imaginary part on a cut.** Bronstein's answer to
  `Sqrt[Tan[x]] + Sqrt[Cot[x]]` (paper example E6d-4) holds
  `Sqrt[(-I - I E^(-2 I x))/(-1 + E^(-2 I x))]`, which is `Sqrt[Cot[x]]`. At `x = -3.26342`
  the radicand reads `-8.17 + 0.*^-60 I`, and the sign of that zero picks the side of the
  cut. The unresolvable-cut test now counts an inexact zero imaginary part, and it reads the
  exact point, where a machine-real coordinate had kept machine noise at every rung. A
  radicand computed as a real is still evidence:
  `Sqrt[(1 - x)/(1 + x)] (1 + x) - Sqrt[1 - x^2]` stays `False`. That answer was also
  wrong-signed at sampled points in v0.6699, and the old generator passed only because the
  sampler drew other points.

Tests:
- `tests/test_zero_test.c`: `test_unresolved_rungs_climb` and `test_signed_zero_cut_redraw`
  (both residuals, perturbations, genuine non-zero controls).
- `tests/test_trager_normalize_ideal.c`: the native and .m normalisations agree on the orders
  at infinity over Q(√2) and GF(5).

## PossibleZeroQ reads a cut point at its principal value; ArcTan on its lower cut at arbitrary precision (v0.6702)

Investigating the two v0.6700 PossibleZeroQ fixes found that one of them had caused a
wrong `True`, along with an older wrong `True`.

- **The two v0.6700 cases.** Both answers were correct.
  - E3-6: the residual is exactly 0 but cancels about 1100 bits at `x = 63.75`. That is
    two cancellations of about 554 bits each (`12 x log2(e)`), so the 1000-bit rung was
    genuinely too shallow.
  - E6d-4: with its radicands replaced by the exact identities `Cot[x]` and `Tan[x]`, the
    residual is 0 at points with `Tan < 0` as well as `Tan > 0`. The nonzero machine
    readings were branch-cut noise.
- **Re-drawing a cut point hid an interval.** v0.6700 re-drew a point where a radicand is
  real by value but computed through complex arithmetic. On a whole interval every point is
  such a point, so a residual wrong only there went unseen. With `ztC`, `ztT` the exponential
  forms of `Cot[x]`, `Tan[x]`:
  - `PossibleZeroQ[(Sqrt[ztC] Sqrt[ztT] - 1) UnitStep[-Tan[x]]]` was `True` at v0.6700
    (`False` before);
  - `PossibleZeroQ[Sqrt[ztC] Sqrt[ztT] - 1]` was `True` on both, through correlated noise.

  Both are `False` now. The point is read at its principal value instead of being re-drawn:
  - the argument's noisy component is dropped once it falls between 200 and 400 bits on the
    exact point (`b -> Re[b]` for cuts on the real axis, `b -> I Im[b]` for `ArcTan`,
    `ArcCot`, `ArcSinh` and `ArcCsch`);
  - the rewrite goes on the sample tree, so nodes not on a cut read as before;
  - every cut function is projected: projecting `Log` alone broke the noise correlation with
    `ArcTan`, and read `D[F] - ArcTan[Sqrt[x]]` (Bronstein's answer) as `Pi` at `x < -1`.
- **`ArcTan[0 - 2 I]` at MPFR precision read `+Pi/2 - 0.549 I`.** The exact value and the
  machine kernel give `-Pi/2 - 0.549 I`. `mpfr_complex_atan` negated a zero real part into
  `-0`, which put `Log` on the wrong side of its cut. `ArcCot[+-I/2]` had the same error,
  through `ArcTan` of the reciprocal. A sweep of the inverse trig and hyperbolic functions
  plus `Log` and `Sqrt` over eleven cut points now shows no differences between the exact,
  MPFR and machine values.

The gated cost (an expression with a complex constant and a cut function) is a few ms per
call, and ztR6d drops from 1.24 s to 0.32 s because fewer points are re-drawn. Tests: the
`test_signed_zero_cut_redraw` additions in `tests/test_zero_test.c`, and
`test_arctan_mpfr_lower_cut` in `tests/test_trig.c`.

## The branch-cut pre-read and the good-prime search (Campaign D6, v0.6703)

- **PossibleZeroQ's cut test reads in doubles first.** v0.6702 read every cut
  function's argument at 200 bits at every sample point of an expression with a
  complex constant. On Trager's answer for 257 `cM-4-306`, 207 cut functions, the
  verification went from 0.57 s to 0.85 s. A machine-precision read now skips any
  argument more than 2^-30 (relative) from its cut line: such an argument cannot be
  rounding noise on the line. A real machine value is skipped only when it is off
  the half-line or the imaginary axis. `1 + I Sqrt[-3.77]` is exactly real in
  doubles and noisy at 200 bits, so it still has to be read at 200 bits. The
  check now takes 0.40 s.
- **Trager's torsion screen tests each prime with the data computed once.**
  `goodPrime` recomputed the squarefree part of G, the discriminant of
  `Gs Bp`, the resultant of `Bp` and `Ep`, and the residue field's discriminant
  for every candidate prime, about 98 primes on `cM-4-306`.
  `goodPrimeInvariants` computes them once per component (cached on the data).
  Each prime is first tested for a degree-1 prime of the residue field, the
  commonest failure. The search went from 0.086 s to 0.038 s.

`cM-4-306` in u, `Method -> "Trager"`: 0.84 s to 0.79 s. The rest is the answer
check (about 0.4 s), the torsion screen's Jacobian arithmetic (about 0.1 s,
already in C kernels) and the residue ideals (about 0.1 s). A C port of the mod-p
order loop could save about 0.15 s more.

## Elementary functions agree on every surface, on and off the branch cuts (v0.6704)

An audit of every circular and hyperbolic function and its inverse, `Log`, `Sqrt`, `Exp`
and `Power` with rational exponents compared the exact value `N[f[p], n]` with MPFR at 30,
60 and 200 digits (each against the exact value at the same rounded input, so only the
algorithm's error counts), machine scalar, packed, NDArray and `Compile[]` (scalar and
rank 1). Points: both sides of every cut, the branch points, `10^-20` and `10^-100` off
the cuts, signed zeros, `10^-30`, `10^30` and `10^100` along the axes and diagonals, and
generic points. Before: 563 mismatches; after: none (the remaining unevaluated cases are the
singular points). Odd symmetry on the cuts, and the exact values against the
principal-branch definitions, hold on every surface.

- **Machine kernels and `Compile[]` on the imaginary cuts.** `ArcTan`, `ArcCot`, `ArcSinh`
  and `ArcCsch` of a packed list, an NDArray or a compiled argument took C99's side of the
  imaginary-axis cut: `ArcTan[{-2. I}]` was `{1.5708 - 0.549 I}` where the scalar, the exact
  value and MPFR give `-1.5708 - 0.549 I`; `ArcCot[{0.5 I}]`, `ArcSinh[{-2. I}]`,
  `ArcCsch[{0.5 I}]` likewise. `catan`/`casinh` read the side from the sign of the zero
  real part; `mth_machine_elem.h` now passes the zero whose sign gives the definition's
  side, so all four surfaces share one answer. The scalar `ArcCsch` now calls the same
  helper.
- **MPFR complex inverses lost digits, or everything.** The log forms
  (`ArcSin = -I Log[I z + Sqrt[1 - z^2]]`, ...) cancelled on half the plane:
  `ArcSin[SetPrecision[-10^30, 40]]` was `Overflow[]`, at 60 digits it had six correct;
  `ArcTan` of `10^-30 I` at 30 digits had six; `ArcCot` at `I + 10^-20` 23 of 30. They are
  now Kahan's cancellation-free forms, internally at 32 guard bits, with the same values on
  the cuts. The reciprocal functions take `(z -+ 1)/z` as `(z -+ 1) conj(z)/|z|^2`: Smith's
  division had rounded the `10^-200` imaginary part of `(z - 1)/z` at `z = -10^100 + I` to
  an exact 0, which put `ArcSech` on its cut, with the wrong sign of `Pi/2 I`. The real MPFR
  `ArcSec`, `ArcCsc`, `ArcCoth`, `ArcSech` no longer round `1/x` before a function that is
  infinitely sensitive at `x = +-1` (`ArcSec[1 + 10^-25]` at 60 digits had 41 correct).
- **MPFR `Log` near `|z| = 1`.** `Log[SetPrecision[1 + 10^-20 I, 30]]` had `Re = 0`
  (`log(hypot)` of a number rounding to 1); it is `Log1p[(a - 1)(a + 1) + b^2]/2` there.
- **MPFR forward functions at a huge imaginary part.** `Tan`, `Cot`, `Sec`, `Csc`, `Tanh`,
  `Coth`, `Sech`, `Csch` overflowed `Cosh^2` and returned `Indeterminate`
  (`Tan[SetPrecision[10^30 I, 30]]`, even `Tan[SetPrecision[10^9 I, 30]]`); they are
  divided through by `Cosh^2`, so `Tan[...] = I`, `Sec[...] = 0`. `Sin`/`Cos`/`Exp` of a
  zero part times an overflowed factor give an exact 0 part (`Overflow[]`, not
  `Indeterminate`).
- **`N` of `E^z` for complex `z`.** The generic path computed `exp(z log e)` and multiplied
  the rounding of `log e` by `|z|`: `N[Exp[I d], 30]` for `d` the 100-digit integer value of
  `1.*^100` was `0.644 + 0.765 I` (the right value is `0.925 - 0.381 I`). A complex exponent
  now goes to one MPFR complex `exp`, as a real one already did.

Not changed: at a singular point the machine value is `Overflow[]` where the exact value is
a directed infinity (`ArcTan[1. I]`, `ArcTanh[1.]`).

Tests: `test_inverse_imag_cut_all_surfaces`, `test_mpfr_complex_inverse_accuracy` and
`test_mpfr_complex_forward_huge` in `tests/test_trig.c`.

## An abort no longer escapes its TimeConstrained through a stale Simplify memo (EVAL-abort-1, v0.6705)

An abort escaped every `TimeConstrained` and aborted a later computation that had no
time limit. `TimeConstrained[Integrate[f3, x], 30]` followed by an unconstrained
`CheckAbort[Integrate[f7, x], ...]` (f7 = `1/(x (-1 + Log[x]) (2 + Log[x]^2)^(1/3))`)
reached the `CheckAbort` after 1.6 s instead of returning `Integrate::nonelem` after
about 12 s, in about one run in two under load.

- **The cause: a memo that outlived its Simplify.** `Simplify` pushes a
  Factor/Together/Cancel memo (`src/poly/facpoly_memo.inc`) and pops it on return. The
  stack depth was not a `TimeConstrained` unwind counter, so a timeout that
  siglongjmp'd out of a `Simplify` skipped the pop. The memo stayed active for the
  rest of the session, and every `Factor`, `Together` and `Cancel` anywhere read and
  grew it. In the reproduction the jump came from a 0.5 s DerivativeDivides candidate
  budget expiring inside ParallelMixedSpecial's 10 s budget. The memo also held entries
  stored while that timeout was in flight, when the evaluator answers every call with
  the `Abort[]` sentinel. A `Together` whose inner evaluation came back `Abort[]` had
  stored `Abort[] + ...`. Later, f7's `PolynomialExtendedGCD` (in ParallelMixed's
  `InvMod`) got `Abort[]` coefficients from that entry, and `PolynomialRemainder`
  evaluated them with no constraint left to own the abort.
- **Fix.** The memo-stack depth is registered with the unwind-counter registry, and the
  next push into a slot left by an abandoned `Simplify` frees that memo. A memo store
  is now skipped while an abort is due (`eval_abort_raised()` in `src/eval.h`: a
  tripped `TimeConstrained`/`MemoryConstrained` frame, or Ctrl-C outside
  `AbortProtect`). This applies to the Factor/Together/Cancel memo, the evaluator's
  numeric-call memo, the `Root` numericalisation cache (which already skipped
  interrupts), DSolve's autonomous-reduction caches and Integrate's fail-memo. The
  check is O(1), and the evaluator's common path is unchanged.
- **The sentinel no longer becomes data in two evaluator paths.** A head that
  evaluates to `Abort[]`, a `Throw` or a `Goto` now unwinds the call, as an argument
  always did. Before, `s["E"]` with `s` bound to an aborted value was the stable datum
  `Abort[]["E"]`, and `Catch[(Throw[7])[x]]` gave `Throw[7][x]` instead of `7`. An
  initializer of `Module`, `With` or `Block` that aborts now leaves the construct
  without running the body. Before, only a `Throw` did, and the body ran with the
  local bound to `Abort[]`.

The bug depends on timing. To verify the fix, the backstop was forced at every trip and
the candidate budget was swept: 5 escapes in about 66 runs before the fix, none in 60
after, and none in 20 unforced runs of the reproduction. Test:
`tests/test_abort_poison.c`, a new suite that unwinds a pushed memo with a real SIGPROF
jump and checks the store gate, the sentinel head and the initializers.

## Builtin audit, wave 1a: checked user-sized allocations; five crashes (v0.6708)

The 2026-11-14 builtin audit (`review/report.html`, `review/issues/`) confirmed 2,475
defects. `tools/audit_backlog.py` now re-runs every finding against the current binary,
one process each under a time limit and an RSS cap, and classifies it (`CRASH`, `HANG`,
`SAME`, `CHANGED`, `LIKELY-FIXED`) into `review/backlog_status.md`. On v0.6701, 1,924
findings still reproduced exactly, 442 had changed, 73 looked fixed and 36 crashed or hung.
Each fixed finding is pinned by a check in `tests/test_audit_<category>.c`, keyed by its
audit id (`tests/test_audit.h`; the suites are globbed into ctest as
`audit_<category>_tests`).

- **`src/checked_alloc.{c,h}`.** About 25 of the crashes have one shape: a builtin
  multiplies sizes the user chose and allocates the product unchecked. `mth_alloc_ok`
  refuses a negative count, a 64-bit overflow, more bytes than physical memory, or a
  request an active `MemoryConstrained` refuses. `mth_malloc_n` / `mth_calloc_n` check
  and allocate. `mth_nomem_abort` emits `General::nomem` through `mth_message` and
  returns the `Abort[]` sentinel, so the computation ends in `$Aborted` (catchable by
  `CheckAbort`), as in the reference.
- **CompleteGraph#1, StarGraph#1 (critical, high).** `CompleteGraph[10^7]` and
  `StarGraph[10^15]` segfaulted on an unchecked `calloc`. The families in
  `src/graph/generators.c` (`CompleteGraph`, `CycleGraph`, `PathGraph`, `StarGraph`,
  `RandomGraph`) now honour the documented resource limit (10^8 vertices or 5×10^7
  edges: left unevaluated) and allocate through the checked helper. Their 64-bit counts
  are `int64_t` rather than `long`, which is 32 bits on Windows.
- **UnitVector#1 (critical).** `UnitVector[WorkingPrecision -> MachinePrecision]` read
  past the argument array. It now reports `UnitVector::argt`. A length past memory aborts
  with `General::nomem`.
- **Series#1 (critical).** `Series[x, {x, 0, 2^62}]` sized its dense coefficient buffer
  unchecked. An order the machine cannot hold now aborts with `General::nomem`. A constant
  is still returned verbatim at any order.
- **NSeries#1 (critical).** An order of `2^31` wrapped to a negative `int`. Orders above
  `2^24` are refused with `NSeries::ivar`.
- **ComplexExpand#1 (critical).** The `TargetFunctions -> Conjugate` path moved its real
  and imaginary parts into `Plus[re, I im]` and then freed them again. The recycled nodes
  resurfaced inside the caller's expression (`Print[Print[Print[…`) before the kernel
  crashed.

Verified: the five suites (34 checks) pass, along with `graph_tests`, `series_tests`,
`nseries_tests`, `unit_vector_tests` and `complexexpand_tests`. Under valgrind every
fixed path leaves the same `definitely lost` total at 1× and 20× as `Print[1]`
(13,376 bytes / 418 blocks, the fixed start-up baseline), with no invalid reads or
writes. `make check-c99` and `make check-messages` pass.

## Builtin audit wave 1d: five special-function hangs at extreme parameters (v0.6712)

## Core semantics: pure functions, scoping, loop exits, level specs (v0.6719)

- **Function#1, Slot#1.** `#0` is the pure function itself, so `If[# == 0, 1, # #0[# - 1]] &[5]`
  is 120. **SlotSequence#1.** `##n` with no arguments left is the empty sequence.
- **Function#2-3, With#1-2.** Substitution is capture-avoiding. A nested Function, Module or
  With whose local occurs in a value being substituted is alpha-renamed first, so
  `Function[x, Function[y, x + y]][y]` is `Function[y$n, y + y$n]`. A Module or With that
  binds a parameter shadows it in its body but not in its initialisers. The check reads the
  binder summaries and fills them on an immutable value, so applying a function to a stored
  expression does not rescan it at every call.
- **Break#1, Continue#1.** Inside a Do, For or While body, `Break[]` and `Continue[]` are
  unwinds like `Throw`. They reach the nearest enclosing loop from anywhere in the body:
  a function argument, a Map, or a Select, Count or Sort predicate. Outside every loop they
  stay plain values.
- **While#1, For#3.** The compiled numeric loop no longer turns an exact variable inexact.
  An assigned variable that provably stays an integer runs on the int64 VM, beside the
  Real ones, and is written back as an Integer, so `y = y + 1` next to `x = x + 1.` stays
  compiled and exact. An int64 overflow, division or fractional constant bails to the
  interpreter before anything is written. Any other exact variable sends the loop to the
  interpreter.
- **Apply#1, Map#1.** One level-spec parser (`mth_level_spec_parse`) serves Map, Apply, Level,
  Cases, Count, Replace, MapIndexed, FreeQ and Position. `{m, Infinity}`, `All` and negative
  levels work everywhere, and a malformed spec reports `Head::level`.
- **PatternSequence#1** Condition on a PatternSequence matches. **Divide#2** `0./0.` is
  `Indeterminate`. **DivideBy#1, TimesBy#1, Decrement#1** evaluate a Part target once.
  **Abort#1** an abort inside NIntegrate's integrand (every rule, including MonteCarlo and
  WorkingPrecision) aborts instead of being dropped as a non-numeric sample.
  **ConditionalExpression#1** a NumericFunction lifts the condition out. **Inequality#1**
  malformed chains. **ReplaceAt#1, ReplacePart#1** the first matching rule wins.
  **TeXForm#1**. **For#2** is pinned under its own id.

## Assigning a value nested deep through its heads no longer overflows the stack (v0.6724)

## DerivativeDivides inverts an exponential kernel (v0.6731)

`Integrate[E^x Log[E^(2 x) - E^x - 1], x, Method -> "DerivativeDivides"]` came back
unevaluated. The kernel `u = E^x` divides `f`, but `f/u'` is `Log[E^(2 x) - E^x - 1]`, and
this still has `x` in it. That `x` only goes away when it is rewritten in terms of `u`.
`try_direct_kernel` now does that for an exponential kernel `E^(c x + d)`: it substitutes
`x -> (Log[u] - d)/c` and cancels, giving `Log[u^2 - u - 1]`. That reduces to
`-2 E^x + Sqrt[5] ArcTanh[(2 E^x - 1)/Sqrt[5]] - Log[...]/2 + E^x Log[...]`, which
PossibleZeroQ verifies.

The inverse is used only under an explicit `Method -> "DerivativeDivides"` (a depth counter,
`dd_explicit_depth`, registered as a TimeConstrained unwind counter), and only for
exponential kernels. Inverting a linear kernel changed answers on the cascade path. Inverting
a `Log` kernel turns `Log[-u] - Log[u]` into `I Pi`, a branch-dependent constant, and that
gave a wrong answer. New test `test_exponential_kernel_inverse` in
`tests/test_integrate_derivdivides.c`.

## DerivativeDivides: an inverse that only renames x is not a reduction (v0.6732)

The exponential inverse of v0.6731 applied to every `E^x` integrand. The reduced integral
goes back through the whole Integrate cascade, so any integrand in `x` and `E^x` that the
cascade could do came back as a "derivative-divides" close, with `x` merely renamed
`Log[u]`. An example is `(1 + 5 x^2 + E^x (1 + 4 x + x^2))/(2 Sqrt[E^x + x])`, which no
kernel divides. The Eliminate strategy did the same for a `Log[c x + d]` kernel, with
`x = E^u`.

Both are now declined:
- after the inverse substitution, a reduced integrand that still contains `Log[u]`;
- for a Log kernel, an Eliminate branch with an `E^(linear in u)` left in it.

`E^x Log[E^(2 x) - E^x - 1]` still reduces to `Log[u^2 - u - 1]`, and all 80 Integrate,
Risch, Trager and Bronstein suites pass. Two tests added to
`test_exponential_kernel_inverse`. This also corrects the easy-class test for
benchmark 262: there, "dd" means `Method -> "DerivativeDivides"` closes the integrand.

## Simplify fuses a sum of logs only where the identity holds (v0.6733)

`Simplify` fused `Sum[c_i Log[a_i]]` into a single `Log` in two cases where the identity
does not hold for complex variables.

- **Positivity derived with real variables.** The fuser required every `a_i > 0`. Its last
  two positivity tests, the reduction-based entailment and `Refine`, read `a > 0` the way
  `Reduce` does, with every variable real, so with no assumptions at all they proved
  `2 + x^2 > 0`. That made `Simplify[Log[2 + x^2] + Log[3 + x^2]]` give
  `Log[6 + 5 x^2 + x^4]`, which is `2 Pi I` off at `x = 1 + 2 I`. They now decide only when
  every variable of `a_i` is provably real. The structural test, which never assumes a
  variable is real, is unchanged.
- **Complex coefficients.** With positive `a_i` and coefficients that are not integer
  multiples of one another, the fuser built `Log[Product[a_i^c_i]]`. That equals
  `Sum[c_i Log[a_i]]` only for real `c_i`. `x Log[2] + x^2 Log[3]` became
  `Log[2^x 3^x^2]`, and `(40 + 24 x + 3 x^6) Log[2 + x^2]` became
  `Log[(2 + x^2)^(40 + 24 x + 3 x^6)]`. Every `c_i` must now be provably real. The
  integer-ratio form, `c_1 Log[Product[a_i^r_i]]`, needs no such condition, so
  `x Log[2] + 3 x Log[3]` still fuses to `x Log[54]`.

Two `simp_log` tests had encoded the real-variable reading, and they are corrected:
- `Log[E^(3 x)] - 3 Log[E^x]` is 0 only for real `x`. At `x = 9/10 Pi I` it is `-2 Pi I`.
- `Log[Sec[x] - Tan[x]] + Log[Sec[x] + Tan[x]]` is `2 Pi I` at `x = Pi`.

Each is now 0 under `Element[x, Reals]` and `-Pi/2 < x < Pi/2` respectively, and left
alone without an assumption. The integral generator found this: its `compactForm` takes the
smallest of `Together`, `Factor` and `Simplify`, so it was printing benchmark integrands
with merged logs.

## Wave 4 G4 (core evaluation): assignment, rules, scoping, patterns and expression-information fixes (v0.6736)

Audit findings in expression information, functional programming, pattern matching,
assignment and rules, and scoping constructs. Each fix is pinned in
`tests/test_audit_<category>.c` under its audit id.

**Assignment and definitions**
- `Attributes[s] = {...}` sets exactly the listed attributes, and `SubValues[s] = {...}`
  replaces the SubValues. Before, both were refused with `Set::write`.
- `ClearAll` and `Remove` drop every message `s::tag`, not just `s::usage`. `ClearAll` also
  drops `Options[s]`.
- `Protect["xa*"]` and `Unprotect["fa*"]` expand the string as a `Names` pattern, as
  `ClearAll` does. Before, they created a symbol literally named `xa*`.
- `Format[lhs] =.` and `Format[lhs, form] =.` remove the display rules. A
  `Format[lhs] := rhs /; test` rule applies only when the test is `True`. A tagged
  `h /: MakeBoxes[h[x_], fmt_] := boxes` is now used when `MakeBoxes` evaluates.
- `{a, b} =.` unsets each element and returns `{Null, Null}`.
- `+=`, `-=`, `*=` and `/=` (and `++`, `--`) work on indexed l-values: a DownValue
  `f[1]`, or a key `asc["a"]` of an association held in a variable. Each index is evaluated
  once.
- Part assignment to a position that does not exist emits `Set::partw`, and one deeper than
  the expression emits `Set::partd`. The value is left unchanged.
- `SetOptions[{s1, s2}, rule]` is all or nothing. When one symbol refuses the option, the
  symbols already set are restored. `OptionValue[f, {n1, n2}]` gives the list of values.
- `ClearAttributes` treats `HoldAll` and `NHoldAll` as single attributes. Clearing
  `HoldFirst` from a `HoldAll` symbol, or `HoldAll` from a `HoldFirst` one, is a no-op.

**Rules and replacement**
- `Replace`, `ReplaceAll`, `ReplaceRepeated` and `ReplaceAt` emit `::reps` for a spec that
  is not a rule, an association, a list of them, or a list of such lists. The call is left
  unevaluated. Before, the non-rules were dropped silently.
- `ReplacePart` reaches inside a head (`{0, 1} -> z` on `f[a][b]` gives `f[z][b]`). It also
  accepts `Heads -> v` as an option rather than reading it as the positional form.
- New operator forms: `ReplaceRepeated[r][x]`, `ReplaceAt[r, n][x]`, `MatchQ[p][x]`,
  `ReverseSortBy[f][x]`, `Scan[f][x]` and `MapIndexed[f][x]`.

**Scoping**
- A local specification that is neither a symbol nor `sym = v` gives `Module::lvsym` or
  `Block::lvsym`, or `With::lvset` (where `sym := v` is also allowed). The construct is left
  unevaluated.
- Module local names are no longer cut off at 256 characters. Before, two Module calls
  with a long local name could share one symbol.
- `With[{x = a}, {y = f[x]}, ..., body]` nests the lists.
- `Unique["str"]` rejects a string that is not a symbol name, with `Unique::usym`.
  `Unique[x, attrs]` gives the fresh symbol those attributes.

**Functions and patterns**
- `Function[params, body, Orderless]` sorts its arguments. A named-parameter `Function`
  called with too few arguments emits `Function::fpct` and is left unevaluated.
- `MatchQ` no longer evaluates its subject a second time, so
  `MatchQ[Unevaluated[1 + 1], _Plus]` is `True`.
- `Longest` or `Shortest` around a `PatternSequence`, named or not, matches: Longest takes
  the longest run first.
- `OrderlessPatternSequence[...] /; test` matches. The guard is checked once the run is
  bound.
- `Default[Plus]`, `Default[Times]` and `Default[Power, 2]` evaluate to `0`, `1` and `1`.
- Bad counts give a message and leave the call unevaluated: `Select[list, crit, -1]` emits
  `Select::innf`, and a fourth argument to `Position` other than a non-negative integer or
  `Infinity` emits `Position::innf`.
- `Through[expr, h]` applies only when the head of expr is `h[...]`.
- `MaximalBy` and `MinimalBy` compare the f-values numerically, so `Pi < 4`. They also
  take a count `n` or `UpTo[n]`.
- `TakeLargest`, `TakeSmallest` and their `By` forms accept `UpTo[n]`, and exclude
  `Missing[...]` and `Indeterminate`.
- `ComposeList` evaluates each step once and passes it to the next. Before, the work was
  quadratic and deep lists hit `$RecursionLimit`.

**Expression information and printing**
- `AtomQ`, `NumberQ`, `ExactNumberQ`, `InexactNumberQ` and `NumericQ` treat `Rational[a, b]`
  and `Complex[a, b]` as numbers only when their parts are numbers. `NumericQ` is `False`
  for `Infinity`, `ComplexInfinity` and `DirectedInfinity[...]`.
- `PossibleZeroQ` is `False` for an equation, an inequality or a logical combination.
- `Definition` shows an `Attributes[f] = {...}` line first and an `Options[f] = {...}` line
  last. `Information` shows them along with a symbol's rules, or in place of a usage
  message the symbol lacks. A builtin with no rules still shows only its usage.

  rather than `0.09999999999999999`. An arbitrary-precision real shows its decimal
  precision, so `N[10^40, 20]` is `1.e40`. `NumberForm` shows a machine real with at most
  16 digits, so `NumberForm[0.1, 20]` is `0.1`.

  separator that is not a string.
- `(a -> b) -> c` and `(a = b) = c` keep their parentheses when printed, and so does a
  comparison nested in another comparison. `Plus[]` and `Times[]` print as written.
- `Names` takes `IgnoreCase -> True` and general string patterns (`"Sin" | "Cos"`,
  `"ArcSin" ~~ __`).
- `Begin` rejects a context name whose segments are not symbol names, with `Begin::cxt`.
  `Context["`x"]` resolves against `$Context`. `Symbol["`"]` and `Symbol["a`"]` emit
  `Symbol::symname`. `SyntaxQ` is `False` for an unterminated comment.

## The Automatic Integrate cascade resumes Trager and Bronstein when their budget runs out (v0.6744)

`$TragerCascadeBudget` and `$BronsteinCascadeBudget` (10 s) used to turn an expiry
into a decline. Benchmarks 262 and 263 had 36 integrals that Trager or Bronstein
decides in 10-40 s but `Integrate[f, x]` returned unevaluated, for example
`(-2 + 2 x^6)^(5/6)/(1 + x^6)` and `Log[x]/(x^6 (3 + 2 x^6)^(1/6))`.

Simply removing the budget would have been worse. In 29 of the gap cases, Trager
needs from 21 s to over 220 s, while GoursatAlgebraic, the cascade's last elementary
stage, closes the same integral in about a second.

So an expiry is now a deferral:
- the cascade runs its later stages as before;
- if none of them closes the integral, the method is resumed with no budget, before
  the special-function stages.

A decision procedure is never abandoned, and the integrals Goursat closes keep their
time. The resumed run happens only at the outermost `Integrate`, and the user's
`TimeConstrained` bounds it as usual. Test: `test_budgets` in
`tests/test_integrate_cascade_trager_bronstein.c`.

## PossibleZeroQ: a non-zero reading must survive a doubling of the precision; TeXForm writes pure functions as #n and & (v0.6744)

**PossibleZeroQ.** The integration benchmarks now verify every answer by
`PossibleZeroQ[D[F, x] - f]` (below). That exposed false negatives: on 257 alone,
4 of Mathilda's answers, 3 of FriCAS's and 1 of Wolfram's are exactly zero
everywhere, yet were rejected.

All had the same cause. The random sampler drew a point where a cancellation needs
more precision than the 1000-bit top rung:
- `Log[g]`, with g cancelling to `E^(24x)` ~ 2^-1720 at x = -49.6 (257 cM-1-176);
- a derivative of size 2^-622 built from exponentials of 2^627 (cM-2-351);
- `(E^(2x))^6` cancelling inside a Log argument at x = 57 (cM-1-126);
- `1 - Tanh[u]^2` at |u| ~ 57 (259 tanh-elem-13).

Every rung read the same wrong value, and agreement between rungs is what the
ladder takes as a genuine value.

Now a FALSE at a point holding exponentials (including the hyperbolic and circular
functions) is read again:
- first at a precision covering the point's exponential range, then at twice that,
  doubling while the value still moves;
- a residual that falls with the added bits is a zero, one that holds is genuine;
- one still moving at 8000 bits is re-drawn.

The PossibleZeroQ suites (zero_test, stress, expcombine stress, assumptions,
special zeros, trigexp, numeric tower review) pass unchanged. New tests are in
`test_deep_cancellation_point_is_not_evidence`.

**TeXForm** printed pure functions as calls:
`\text{Root}(\text{Function}(\text{Slot}(1)^{3}+2),1)`. Now `Slot[n]` is `\#n`,
`#name` is `\#\text{name}`, `##n` is `\#\#n` and `Function[body]` is `body\&`,
parenthesised inside an operator. So `TeXForm[Root[#^3 + 2 &, 1]]` is
`\text{Root}(\#1^{3}+2\&,1)`. A `Function` with named variables keeps its call
form. Test: `test_texform_pure_functions` in `tests/test_print.c`.

**Numerics.** PolyLog of a negative integer order at |z| > 1 uses the inversion Li_{-n}(1/z) =
(-1)^(n+1) Li_{-n}(z), so a real z > 1 gives a real result. QPochhammer for |q| near 1 uses the
log series instead of millions of factors, and a machine underflow gives the extended-exponent
value, not a subnormal. LogGamma past the double range gives the 53-bit extended real.
ProductLog takes a bignum branch index, and its working precision now carries log2|k| bits:
ProductLog[2^62, 1.] had the wrong imaginary sign. ChebyshevT/U past the recurrence cap, and with
a bignum order, use the closed form with guard bits, so ChebyshevT[10^30, 0.5`50] is right to all
50 places. An exact rational x past the polynomial cap is evaluated exactly by matrix powering.
GegenbauerC's machine recurrence runs to degree 10^7. EulerE past the table is computed by FLINT
(to 200000). BesselJZero/BesselYZero take k up to 10^12 and an exact rational order with an
inexact k. Machine BarnesG goes through arb for every argument (BarnesG[5.] = 12.). Kelvin
functions at an inexact zero, StruveL[-1.5, 0], SphericalBesselJ[0.5, 0.],
Hypergeometric1F1[1, b, 0.] and WhittakerM at z = 0 give their values. A Hypergeometric0F1 past
the MPFR exponent range gives Overflow[]. AppellF1 with a negative integer a and a pole c stays
unevaluated at every precision.

**Integrate.** UnitBox integrates through the step rules. Surd[a x + b, n] and x^m Surd[x, n]
have antiderivatives (Integrate[CubeRoot[x], x] = 3/4 x Surd[x, 3]). A head that is the derivative
of another by definition integrates back to it (WeierstrassPPrime to WeierstrassP; MathieuCPrime,
MathieuSPrime and EllipticThetaPrime likewise).

**Also.** Hypergeometric2F1 at z = 1 with positive parameters and c - a - b <= 0 is Infinity.
InverseJacobiDN at m = 0 gives 0 for v = 1, without leaking Power::infy. MathieuCharacteristicB of
an arbitrary-precision order within 2^-53 of an integer evaluates from the side it lies on. The
ArcSin table search stays in [-Pi/2, Pi/2] now that it compares evaluated values. HermiteH[n, 0]
is 0 for an odd bignum n, and a machine HermiteH / ChebyshevT / ChebyshevU past the double range
is a MachinePrecision 53-bit real, not the recurrence's working precision. The BarnesG spec example
N[BarnesG[13/2], 30] shows the right value. Series of SphericalBesselY at an integer order expands
its elementary form (a pole at 0 included), and Series of ExpIntegralE[n, u] at an integer order
where u vanishes is the logarithmic series. Series of WeierstrassP / WeierstrassPPrime at the
pole is the Laurent series (no Power::infy), and KelvinKer / KelvinKei of order 0 have their
logarithmic series at 0. BesselY's logarithmic series now also serves a nested or composite
BesselY and HankelH1 / HankelH2 (Series[HankelH2[0, x], {x, 0, 1}],
Series[BesselJ[0, x] - I BesselY[0, x], {x, 0, 1}]).

## Wave 6 G3: calculus, algebra, solving, structural manipulation and linear algebra -- the last open audit rows (v0.6755)

- **Integrate.** A rational integrand with numeric real poles and a symbolic bound gives a
  `ConditionalExpression`: the integral is taken with both bounds in each gap between the poles,
  and the answer proved on most gaps (another gap joins when `Simplify` under its condition proves
  the difference 0) is returned with the condition naming those gaps.
  `Integrate[1/x, {x, a, b}]` is `ConditionalExpression[-Log[a] + Log[b], a < 0 && b < 0 || a > 0 && b > 0]`
  (it stayed unevaluated). An antiderivative of an integrand without `I` that came back in complex
  exponentials is rewritten exactly, `E^(p + I q) = E^p (Cos[q] + I Sin[q])`, when every `I`
  cancels: `Integrate[E^x Sin[x], x]` is `1/2 E^x (-Cos[x] + Sin[x])`.
- **The inexact round trip keeps `E^u`.** `common_numericalize_result` and the
  rationalise-then-numericalise round trip (Simplify, Together, ...) numericalise the numbers but
  not the base of `E^u` for a non-numeric `u`: `Simplify[E^(0.5 x) c]` is `c E^(0.5 x)` and
  `Integrate[E^(-0.5 x), x]` is `-2. E^(-0.5 x)` (both had `2.71828^(...)`).
- **DSolve.** `DSolve[y'[x] == 0.5 y[x], y[x], x]` is `{{y[x] -> E^(0.5 x) C[1]}}` (the linear
  first-order solver also drops the machine-zero forcing that gave `0. + C[1]`);
  `GeneratedParameters -> (K[# + 1] &)` names the constants `K[2], K[3], ...`; `y' + y == Sin[x]`
  has a real trigonometric answer.
- **D.** A symbolic order keeps the x-free terms: `D[c, {x, n}]` is `c KroneckerDelta[n]`, so
  `D[x^2 + c, {x, n}] /. n -> 0` is `c + x^2`.
- **Minimize / Maximize.** A radical objective rational in its adjoined radicals takes the rational
  reformulation (`Minimize[(x^2 + 3)/Sqrt[x^2 + 2], x]` is `{3/Sqrt[2], {x -> 0}}`); a univariate
  `Max`/`Min` or continuous two-piece `Piecewise` objective is rewritten in `Abs` and solved by the
  breakpoint method (`Maximize[Max[-x^2, -1], x]`, `Maximize[Piecewise[{{x, x < 1}}, 2 - x], x]`).
- **Power.** A rational power of a negative exact coefficient times known-positive factors splits
  off `(-1)^(p/q)`: `Sqrt[-Pi^2]` is `I Pi`, so `MatrixExp[{{0, -Pi}, {Pi, 0}}]` is
  `{{-1, 0}, {0, -1}}`.
- **MatrixFunction / MatrixExp.** A rational matrix whose characteristic polynomial is squarefree
  with an irreducible factor of degree 3 or more is answered by the resolvent form
  `RootSum[p, f[#] adj(# I - m)_ij / p'[#] &]`: `MatrixFunction[Sin, HilbertMatrix[4]]` takes
  milliseconds (over 120 s).
- **QRDecomposition.** Free symbols may be complex: the Hermitian inner product and `Abs[.]^2`
  norms, with a structural conjugate (over sums, products, integer powers, past the real
  non-negative norms), so q is unitary at any complex values. The exact zero matrix gives the
  reference's one row with `Pivoting -> True` as well.
- **GroebnerBasis over Q(i)**, via `t^2 + 1` adjoined, mapped back, made monic and inter-reduced.
- **PolynomialGCD / PolynomialLCM** read `x^(2a)` as `(x^a)^2` (the exponent generators Factor
  and Cancel already use).
- **PolynomialMod** never divides: the integer part of the quotient toward zero (or the exact
  quotient when it needs no new denominator), `PolynomialMod[x^3, 2x - 1]` is `x^3`. The spec no
  longer claims `Listable` (a List second argument is a set of moduli).
- **MinimalPolynomial** of a `Root` with algebraic coefficients walks the coefficients:
  `MinimalPolynomial[Root[#^2 - Sqrt[2] &, 2], x]` is `-2 + x^4`.
- **NumberFieldIntegralBasis[2^(1/32)]** in about 10 ms (over two minutes): the generator is
  rendered once for the basis, and the canonical Root order decides `|Im|` on rigorous enclosures
  and a conjugate pair without an exact real-part proof.
- **PowerExpand[expr, {vars}]** leaves `Log` of a rational alone (Gruntz splits it itself).
- **Tally** compares with `SameQ` on both surfaces: `Tally[{0.1 + 0.2, 0.3}]` is one class (a sort
  of the distinct values merges neighbours within the tolerance).
- **InputForm** of an `InterpolatingFunction` prints its data, so it reads back.
- **Thread::tdlen** for unequal `SparseArray`s is printed once; a visible `NDArray` opens the
  transparency gate in a session that has packed nothing (`NDArray[{1., 2.}] + {10, 20}` is an
  `NDArray`).

Declined: `Complex[1, 0.]` collapsing (the documented inexact-zero rule), `Sin[N[Pi, 30]]`
precision (no significance arithmetic), `Cos[Pi/8]` (the reference keeps it), `Cancel`'s default
reduction over radicals (documented; `Extension -> None` is the default and must agree),
`FactorList[..., Modulus -> a]` (documented: a symbolic modulus means Q), and

## Wave 6 G5: Mathieu functions near a high-order characteristic value and at a large q (v0.6757)

All four heads (`MathieuC`, `MathieuCPrime`, `MathieuS`, `MathieuSPrime`) share
one plan per `(a, q)`, so each fix below applies to all of them.

- **No false characteristic value.** Hill's integer snap was relative to `n`
  (2e-6 n), while `nu - n ~ (a - a_n)/(2n)`: at `n = 20` every `a` within
  1.6e-3 of `a_20(1) = 400.00125` counted as a characteristic value. The integer
  gluing then failed, and `MathieuCPrime[400., 1., 0.1]` and
  `MathieuCPrime[400, 1, 0.1`20]` stayed unevaluated (audit MathieuCPrime#1).
  For real `a` and `q` the Sturm count of the characteristic matrices now decides
  alone. Hill's snap is kept for complex arguments, and a failed snap falls back
  to the generic exponent.
- **The sign at `Sqrt[a]` near an integer.** The two exponent representatives
  `n -+ d` are equidistant from `Sqrt[a]` to first order, and exactly so at
  `a = n^2`. "Nearest" could pick the one whose `c_0` is a tail coefficient
  (1e-43). That coefficient's sign then flipped the function: `MathieuC` changed
  sign between `a = 399.9` and `a = 400`, and `MathieuC[9., 0.5, 0.1]` was
  `-0.928` for a function close to `Cos[3 z]`. When one of the two candidates puts
  `c_0` on the dominant coefficient, that one is taken. `MathieuC[400., 1., 0.1]`
  is now `-0.412115` and `MathieuCPrime[400., 1., 0.1]` is `-18.2045`.
- **Large q (audit MathieuCPrime#2).** Across an instability band,
  `Sum[c_k^2]` cancels against `Sum[|c_k|^2]` (2.6e-10 at `a = 1`, `q = 800`).
  This had three effects:
  - the machine Newton on the consistency function stalled on its noise floor
    (`MathieuC[1., 800., 0.1]` stayed unevaluated);
  - where it converged, the machine value was wrong by up to 6e-6 (`q = 900`);
  - the ball kernel ran out of bits, and `MathieuC[1, 1000, 0.1`20]` stayed
    unevaluated.

  The changes:
  - The machine plan declines once that cancellation exceeds 4 digits, or Newton
    stalls. It still gives the ball kernel its starting point.
  - The ball kernel adds twice the lost bits to its working precision and raises
    the floor of its Newton ladder to match.
  - The ball kernel's final pass now works on the midpoints of `a` and `q`. As a
    ball, `a = 777/10` had put a 2^-102 relative radius on every coefficient.
  - When Hill's determinant's own rounding estimate exceeds 1e-11, `Cos[Pi nu]`
    comes from the monodromy of the equation, by RK4 to `Pi/2`. At `q = 6000`
    Hill gave `Im nu` = 23.19 for 41.91, and above `q ~ 8000` it could not run.
    `acos` no longer overflows for `|Cos[Pi nu]| > 1e154`.

  Values now hold to `q = 10^4` (`MathieuC[1., 10000., 0.3] = 4.70463*^17`), at
  machine and arbitrary precision.
- **Signs decided by rounding.** Two ties used to fall to rounding noise:
  - The normalization rule `Re(c_0) > 0` tested the real part before checking
    whether `c_0` is purely imaginary (as it is for real `(a, q)` with
    `Re nu = 0`). `MathieuS[-30, 500, 1/10]` therefore had opposite signs at 114
    and 208 bits. The imaginary tie is now checked first.
  - The conjugate tie `n +- i mu` was decided by the sign of a zero imaginary
    part inside `acos`, and by where Newton ended. `MathieuS[1., q, 0.1]`
    changed sign between `q = 300` and `q = 500`. The tie now takes `Im nu < 0`,
    the principal branch of `ArcCos[Cos[Pi nu]]`, which is what most of the old
    values had.
- **Cost.** An instability band at a large `q` now goes through the ball kernel
  at machine precision. That is about 1 ms per point instead of 1 µs, and gives
  16 correct digits where the machine plan had 6 to 12.
- Verified from first principles:
  - coefficients by continued fractions at 80 digits, with `nu` solved to 1e-78;
  - a direct high-precision integration of `y'' + (a - 2 q cos 2z) y = 0` from
    0.1 to 0.6 (agreement to 1e-35);
  - the Wronskian, constant in `z`;
  - the identity `Sum[c_k^2] = 2 (dnu/da) Sum[c_k] Sum[(nu + 2k) c_k]`.
- Pinned in `tests/test_audit_special_functions.c` (`test_wave6_g5`).

## DerivativeDivides no longer depends on a constant factor; Cancel keeps a flattened radicand (v0.6760)

- **What was wrong.** Whether `Method -> "DerivativeDivides"` found a
  substitution could depend on a constant factor of the integrand. With
  `g = (2 Log[x] + 2 x Log[x] + x Log[x]^2)/(x w^(2/3) + x w)` and
  `w = 1 + (1 + x) Log[x]^2`, `g/3` closed and `g`, `2 g` and `-g` did not.
  `E^x` did not close either, while `3 E^x` did. Removing the constant
  factors from the benchmark integrands exposed all three, by moving cases
  from the derivative-divides class to the hard one.
- **Three causes, three fixes:**
  - *The quotient absorbed the kernel.* `Cancel[Together[f/w']]` merged the
    sum `w` into a larger sum, `1/(1 + (1 + x) Log[x]^2 + w^(2/3))`, so `w -> u`
    left `x` behind. With `1/3` the quotient stayed a product, `3 w + 3 w^(2/3)`.
    When `w -> u` leaves `x`, the direct strategy now substitutes first,
    `(f //. w -> u)/(w' //. w -> u)`, and cancels that. It uses `ReplaceRepeated`:
    a sub-sum match stops `ReplaceAll` from descending into that sum's other
    terms, as in WL.
  - *No kernel for `E^x`.* The whole integrand is never a kernel, since `f/f'` is
    rarely simpler. `E^(c x + d)` is now the exception: its quotient is `1/c`.
  - *`Cancel` lost a factor.* The radical generator `g = Sqrt[B]` rewrote
    `B -> g^2` only where `B` occurs as a whole node. Where `B = 2 + y` was
    flattened into a larger sum (`2 (2 + y)/2` is `2 + y`), it stayed, so
    `Cancel[(1 + x)/(2 + y + Sqrt[2 + y] + x Sqrt[2 + y] + x (2 + y))]` kept
    `1 + x`. `poly_subst_radical_to_gen` now rewrites a sum holding every term
    of `B` the same way. The result is `1/(2 + y + Sqrt[2 + y])`.
- Tests: `test_constant_multiple_invariance` in
  `tests/test_integrate_derivdivides.c`, and `test_cancel_flattened_radicand`
  in `tests/test_rat.c`.

## Integrate: non-elementary binomials close in their 2F1, fast and on every branch (v0.6763)

- **What was wrong.** `Integrate[x^3/Sqrt[2 + x^6], x]` took 5.7 s and came back
  unevaluated with `Integrate::nonelem`. Given more time, the Roach stage would
  have returned the 2F1, so the answer depended on the machine's speed:
  `integrate_cascade_trager_bronstein_tests` failed intermittently on loaded CI
  runners. `Integrate[x^(-1/3)/Sqrt[1 - x], x]` took 17 s.
- **Cause.** This is Chebychev's class of binomials `x^p (a x^r + b)^q`, the ones
  the theorem proves non-elementary.

    searched each one and closed nothing.

    integration probes are binomials of the same class. Each probe paid the same

    and gives ParallelMixedSpecial 1 s. Its elliptic-pencil closes take 0.07–0.18 s.

    `x^(p+1)/(p+1) (a x^r + b)^q (1 + a x^r/b)^(-q) 2F1(-q, mu; mu+1; -a x^r/b)`,

## Linear algebra and polynomial division over the number field of algebraic entries

**LinearSolve, Inverse, RowReduce, Det.** A matrix whose entries are constant algebraic
numbers (rationals with radicals of rationals, `I`, `Root` objects) went through the
symbolic fraction-free elimination. That path treats each atom as an opaque indeterminate,
so nothing reduced `Sqrt[3]^2` or `I^2` and the entries grew at every step. Each exact
division and zero test then went through `Together` / `PolynomialGCD` over a multivariate
ring. A 12x12 `LinearSolve` over `Q(I Sqrt[3])` (a goursat ansatz system) did not finish in
40 s, and neither did `Inverse` of the same matrix. `RowReduce` took 2 s.

`src/poly/flint_qqbar.c` (`flint_qqbar_alg_mat_*`) now maps such a matrix once into the
number field `K` its atoms generate. It takes a primitive element by degree, expresses each
atom in it exactly, and evaluates every entry as an antic `nf_elem`. Gauss-Jordan then runs
natively, with exact O(1) zero tests. Results are written in a monomial basis of the
original atoms (`a + b I + c Sqrt[3] + d I Sqrt[3]`), never in a primitive element the user
did not write. The 12x12 solve, inverse and RREF each take about 2 ms. Rectangular,
underdetermined (free variables 0) and inconsistent systems (`LinearSolve::nosol`) take the
same path, and so does a singular `Inverse` (`Inverse::sing`). `RowReduce` keeps the result
only when it is rational, such as a full-rank matrix's identity, because then it is the same
expression on either path. An algebraic non-pivot entry would change form, and NullSpace,
Eigenvectors and SingularValueDecomposition build on those entries. For a cubic `Root`
eigenvalue, the basis form's large coefficients broke the machine-precision reconstruction
check on `SingularValueDecomposition[{{1, 2, 3}, {4, 5, 6}, {7, 8, 10}}]`. A symbolic,
inexact or all-rational matrix declines, as does one holding `GoldenRatio`, which keeps the
reference's forms. `MATHILDA_NO_ALG_FIELD=1` turns the path off for differential testing.

**PolynomialQuotient, PolynomialRemainder, PolynomialQuotientRemainder.** `poly_div_rem`
(`src/poly/poly.c`) ran `Together` + `Cancel` on every coefficient of every step whenever the
quotient coefficient was not an integer. That doubled as its zero test. For a `Root` object
it is neither a zero test nor cheap: nothing reduces `Root[..]^3`, so each step multiplied
the algebraic denominators out. A degree-12 dividend with coefficients rational in
`Root[-1 - 4 #1 + #1^3 &, 1]`, over a monic degree-6 divisor, took more than 20 s. The goursat
original (LeafCount 2724) did not finish in 60 s.

When the divisor's leading coefficient is rational and every coefficient is a constant
algebraic number, a step now only combines like terms (`Expand` of the coefficients it
touches). A coefficient's zero test is exact: `flint_qqbar_alg_field_is_zero`, over the number
field of the coefficients' atoms, built once per division. The strict degree drop never
depended on the simplification; it comes from discarding the `x^degR` term. The synthetic
case takes about 20 ms and the goursat original 12 ms, both checked against `p == Q q + R`
to 200 digits. A free parameter, or a coefficient the field cannot read, keeps the
`Together` / `Cancel` step.

Two simpler versions were tried and rejected. Rendering every coefficient in the field's
basis rewrote harmless divisions such as `(-2)^(2/3) (-1)^(2/3) t^2 / t`, and Integrate's
Goursat method no longer closed. `Expand` without the exact zero test left a zero-equivalent
radical coefficient in a remainder, and a Bronstein integral of
`D[Log[Sqrt[x^3 + 1] + Sqrt[2] x + 1], x]` no longer closed.

Regression tests with 5 s timing gates: `tests/test_linearsolve.c`
(`test_linearsolve_algebraic_field`) and `tests/test_poly.c`
(`test_polynomial_div_rem_algebraic`). Each answer is checked against its defining identity.

  engine for `Q(vars)(Sqrt[b])` with `b` a non-constant polynomial other than a bare
  symbol, and the classical cascade's multivariate gcds took 7-20 s (over 60 s for
  `Sqrt[k^3 + 1]`) per Cancel on a Goursat pull-back by the Hessian roots of
  `t (1 - t)(1 - k t)`. The canonical form `A + B Sqrt[b]` (lowest terms over
  `Q(vars)`; reduce modulo `s^2 - b`, multiply the conjugate through) is now computed
  directly, 10-60 ms, and written as the shorter of that and the reciprocal's
  `1/(A' + B' Sqrt[b])`, so a radical denominator with nothing to cancel stays.
  Non-squarefree radicands (`-3 (k - 1)^2`) are fine: the field only needs the
  squarefree part not to be a square.
- **Several generators.** The common field of radicals of rationals and roots of
  unity (`flint_qqbar_to_number_field_common`, used by the field gcd engine and
  algfield) was a qqbar primitive-element search: sixteen composed resultants per
  atom, each factored, LLL for every image, and the Root index of a primitive
  element whose conjugates tie in real part. It is now exact linear algebra in the
  product algebra `Q(zeta_N) (x) Q(l_1^(1/n_1)) (x) ...` (`kf_number_field_common`):
  the minimal polynomial of `theta` from its powers, the factor vanishing at its
  value, the subfield the atoms actually generate. Over `Q(I, Sqrt[3], 2^(1/3))`
  the first `Cancel[Together[e], Extension -> Automatic]` on a new field fell from
  3.7-5.3 s to 0.1 s of CPU, and `Cancel[Together[(e1 + e2)/(e1 - e2)], ...]` from
  2.2 s to 0.04 s. The qqbar search also stops as soon as a candidate reaches the
  degree bound `deg(alpha) deg(b)`.
- **Still slow:** a multivariate combination over a number field whose input is large
  (a 24k-leaf sum over `Q(I, Sqrt[3])(k, z)` from the cube-root Goursat descent,
  2.2 s for `Together[e, Extension -> Automatic]`, where the same sum with `I` and
  `Sqrt[3]` replaced by symbols combines in 0.04 s). The field is built once; the cost
  is the evaluator-level cascade (the native field engine is univariate). The fix is
  the generator-symbol FLINT combination plus reduction modulo the primitive element's
  minimal polynomial and a modular field gcd.
- Tests: `test_cyclotomic_zero_constants`, `test_cyclotomic_rational_functions`,
  `test_symbolic_radicand_hessian`, `test_multigen_cyclotomic_cube_root_speed` in
  `tests/test_extension_auto_builtins.c` (with timing gates).

### Integrate: the rational log part over Q(Root) runs in the number field

`Integrate[rho, m, Method -> "BronsteinRational"]` (and the default cascade)
did not finish in 60 s on a 92-leaf rational function over
K = Q(r), r = `Root[837/4 + #1^3 &, 1]`. It now takes 0.17 s. The answer has 291
leaves, Root powers below the degree, and `PossibleZeroQ[D[P, m] - rho]` is True.
The hang predates the 2026-11 Root-coefficient merge in `intrat.c`.

Root cause: the Lazard-Rioboo-Trager core (`intrat_log_part_core`) treated a
Root object as an indeterminate. Nothing reduced `r^n` modulo its minimal
polynomial, so the Rothstein-Trager resultant came back with `r^14` and
26-digit coefficients. Reduced, it is `262144 t^6 - 1`. Every gcd and Bezout
cofactor in the residue variable was also computed over Q(r)(t) with r
transcendental. The inverse of `lc(S_1)` modulo `Q_1` (a quintic against a sextic)
never finished as a `PolynomialExtendedGCD`, because the rational functions in r
swell along the Euclidean chain.

Fix (`src/calculus/intrat.c`, `intrat_log_part_core_root`): when the
coefficients of a pure-rational log part span Q(alpha) for a single Root object
alpha, the core runs over K:

- alpha becomes a placeholder z, and every polynomial is reduced modulo its
  minimal polynomial.
- Each operation in t that depends on the field runs natively over the number
  field via `FLINT`PolynomialExtendedGCD` / `FLINT`PolynomialQuotientRemainder`
  with `Extension -> {z, p}`. These operations are the gcds, the exact
  quotients, the inverse modulo Q_i in PrimitivePart_mod, and Yun's squarefree
  split of `lc(s_i)`.
- The residue factors Q_i are certified squarefree and pairwise coprime over K.
  If a kernel declines or the certification fails, the generic path runs as
  before.
- For each Q_i the output keeps whichever spelling is smaller, the reduced one
  or the one as computed. The two are equal in K[t], so `1/(m^4 - r^2)` keeps
  its `(r^-6)^(1/4)` radicand.

Two more integrals that timed out (> 30 s) now finish in 0.05 s:
`1/(m^6 + Root[-5 + #^3 &, 1] m^2 + 1)` and
`(q^2 m + 1)/(m^3 + q m + 1)` with q = `Root[-1 - 4 #1 + #1^3 &, 1]`. Over a
sample of 10 Root-coefficient integrands, every other answer is identical to
the previous output, and several run up to 4x faster.
`Together` / `Cancel` with `Extension -> Automatic` are unchanged and still
leave Root powers unreduced, matching the reference.

Tests: `tests/test_intrat.c` `test_root_coefficient_log_part_in_number_field`.

## Rational-function integration: a review, a benchmark, and four fixes

`Integrate` on rational functions (`src/calculus/intrat.c`, `intsimp.c`) was reviewed
against Bronstein, *Symbolic Integration I*, ch. 2; the findings are in
`tasks/intrat_review.md`. There is a new benchmark, `benchmarks/266-rational-integration`
(`make bench-integrate-rational`): 389 integrals in 15 families, compared with the
reference system and sympy's `ratint`. Every Mathilda answer is checked by
`PossibleZeroQ[D[F, x] - f]` and by a continuity scan.

- **Wrong answers when a coefficient passes 2^63 (fixed).** The sign test used by
  the log part's real conversion read a BigInt as "unknown". A positive
  discriminant then took the complex-pair branch, and the answer was one wrong
  `Log` with huge nested radicals. The baseline benchmark had 17 such wrong
  answers: 5 of 30 non-squarefree integrands and 12 of 25 large-coefficient ones.
  The test now signs Integer, BigInt and Rational exactly. A parameter-free
  algebraic constant gets a certified sign, so `1/(x^2 - (3 - 2 Sqrt[2]))` gives
  `-(1 + Sqrt[2]) ArcTanh[(1 + Sqrt[2]) x]` rather than an `ArcTan` of an imaginary
  argument.
- **Log terms could be silently dropped (fixed).** When the Lazard–Rioboo–Trager
  log part could not form one of its S_i, it skipped that resultant factor and the
  whole RootSum vanished from the answer. It now declines. The exact quotient
  helper no longer returns an inexact quotient (it also wrote a raw stderr line
  that bypassed `Quiet`).
- **Native subresultant chain: up to 750× faster on long chains.** The log part's
  chain came from a pseudo-remainder sequence on expression trees, whose
  coefficients grow exponentially. It is now the subresultant PRS over Z[t][x] on
  FLINT (`src/calculus/intrat_subres.c`) for rational coefficients.
  - Czichowski's degree-14 example: 18.9 s → 0.03 s.
  - `1/(x^30 + 1)`, and irreducible denominators of degree 12, 16 and 20: from
    timeouts (> 30 s) to under a second.
- **More compact output.**
  - The Hermite rational part is one fraction over the squarefree-factored
    denominator, or partial fractions, whichever is smaller. Its numerators are no
    longer split over an expanded denominator: `1/(1 + x^2)^3` →
    `(5 x + 3 x^3)/(8 (1 + x^2)^2) + 3/8 ArcTan[x]`, and `1/(x^2 + 1)^8` goes from
    195 to 52 leaves.
  - Log arguments lose their rational content: `Log[4 - 4 x + 4 x^2 - ...]` →
    `Log[1 - x + x^2 - ...]`.
- A small leak in the palindromic-quartic expander was fixed.

### Together / Cancel over number fields: a native engine, and three wrong answers fixed

**New: `src/poly/nfrat.{c,h}`, rational functions over the number field of the
constant algebraic coefficients.** Together and Cancel now go here first for
any input with constant algebraic atoms: I, `Sqrt[n]`, `n^(1/k)`,
`(-1)^(p/q)` and `Root` objects. This covers one or several generators, any
number of variables, both with and without `Extension -> Automatic`, and with
an explicit `Extension -> alpha | {alpha, ..}`, whose generators join the
input's.

How it works:
- `K = Q(theta)` is built once from the cached primitive-element images.
- The expression tree is converted **directly** into a factored rational
  function `c prod Q^f / (u prod P^e)`. Polynomials are `fmpq_mpoly` over
  `(x.., tau)` reduced modulo `theta`'s minimal polynomial. Denominator pieces
  are interned monic, so a common denominator is an index merge.
- Every numerator piece is cancelled against every denominator piece with the
  certified modular gcd over `K`, which works in any number of variables.
- The denominator is written over a squarefree, gcd-free basis, back in the
  caller's own radicals.

No intermediate goes through the evaluator. The paths it replaces evaluated a
1335-leaf tree, or an expanded num/den with radical coefficients, through
Times' radical canonicaliser.

The output follows the reference's forms, each checked against it:
- A numeric algebraic denominator is never rationalised:
  `Together[x + 1/(1 + Sqrt[2]), Extension -> Automatic]` is
  `(1 + x + Sqrt[2] x)/(1 + Sqrt[2])`.
- A `Root` power is not reduced modulo its polynomial.
- Denominator pieces keep their written form, except that a Gaussian unit is
  normalised away (`2/((-I + x) (I + x))`).
- A negative sign goes into the numerator sum (`(1 - Sqrt[2] x)/x`).
- Cancel works term by term, and a power of a sum is the power of the
  cancelled sum: `Cancel[(1/(x+1) + 1/(x+2))^2]` stays as it is.

Inputs with a nested radical stay with algfield. A denominator-free product
or polynomial is left as written; rewriting it over the field's basis changed
the shape `Integrate[Log[1 + x + x^2]/x, {x, 0, 1}]` reads. `MATHILDA_NFRAT=off`
disables the engine.

Two kernels inside the engine:
- Reduction modulo `M(tau)` is term-wise, from a table of `tau^j mod M`,
  instead of a multivariate division per product.
- Exact division over `K` is by Kronecker substitution into `K[z]`, using
  `gr_poly` over antic. The quotient is certified by multiplying it back.
  This is used when the dense image is small; otherwise the ideal division
  over `{G, M}` is kept.

The Generalised Goursat repro (`tests/data/generalised_goursat/repro/together_slow_qi_sqrt3.m`,
1335 leaves over `Q(I, Sqrt[3])`):

| | before | after | reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Together[e]` | 8.3–17 s, 431 leaves | 7–15 ms warm (26 ms cold), 50 leaves | 25 ms, 63 leaves |
| `Together[e, Extension -> Automatic]` | > 120 s, `Power::infy` | same as `Together[e]` | 11 ms, 63 leaves |
| `Cancel[e]` | unchanged, 1335 leaves | 3 terms, 181 leaves | 3 terms, 171 leaves |

**New: Root objects as independent generators.** The reference keeps
`Root[f, k]^m` with `m >= deg f` unreduced:
`Together[1/(x - r) + r^4/x]` is `(x + x r^4 - r^5)/(x (x - r))`. When such a
power is present, each constant Root becomes a fresh symbol. The call then
goes through the builtin's own dispatch (the factored engine over Q) and the
Roots are put back. Before, these inputs expanded products of Root powers
through the evaluator: a 3268-leaf Together over `Q(Root[#^3 - # - 1 &, 1])`
took 8.5 s and gave 48983 leaves. It now takes 0.018 s and gives 7613 leaves
(the reference: 0.08 s, 8005 leaves). At user level Cancel follows the
reference's structure here: sums term by term, and powers of sums as the power
of the cancelled sum.

**Fixed (wrong answer): `Power::infy` from Together/Cancel over `Q(I, ..)`.**
`ratcanon` built the relation of its I generator over the kernel `I` itself:
`I^2 + 1`, which is 0. So the tower reduction never reduced a power of I, and
the fraction it substituted back divided by zero. Before the fix:
- `Together[e, Extension -> Sqrt[3]]` printed `1/0` messages and returned
  `$Failed`-like garbage after 2.2 s.
- `Together[e, Extension -> Automatic]` did not finish.
- The default `Together[e]` fell through to the slow kernel path, the 8.3 s
  above.

The relation is now written over the generator symbol. The same path takes
1.5 ms.

**Fixed (wrong answer): `Apart` with dependent radicals returned
`Indeterminate`.** Apart abstracted the radicals as independent symbols and
substituted them back unchecked. But `Sqrt[6] = Sqrt[2] Sqrt[3]`, so a
coefficient denominator vanished on the way back:
`Apart[1/((x + y - Sqrt[6]) (Sqrt[2] x + Sqrt[2] y - 2 Sqrt[3])), x]` was
`Indeterminate`, with `Power::infy` printed. The restore now runs with the
arithmetic warnings muted (an internal probe, invisible to `Quiet` and
`Check`). It declines to the ordinary path when a denominator vanished. This
applies at both sites: the radical abstraction and the kernel-to-symbol
abstraction.

**Fixed: `Factor` of a Gaussian polynomial in three or more variables** now
factors over `Q(i)`, as the reference does. A product of four Gaussian factors
in x, y, z came back as two (66 leaves; the reference has 37). Before, the
`Q(i)` route was refused past two variables. It is now taken when it splits
further than the classical factorisation. Where both agree, the classical one
and its orientation are kept: `Factor[-4 (I x - y) z]` is still
`4 (-I x + y) z`.

**Fixed: `Cancel` of nested-radical input under the kernel model** reduced only
a single fraction modulo `g^m - b`. A term-wise sum kept every power of `g`.
Each term is now reduced on its own.

**Faster: the certified field gcd (`fg_modular_gcd`)** memoises the
factorisation of `M mod p`. Cantor–Zassenhaus on the minimal polynomial was
most of every small gcd. This helps every field gcd, Trager's included.

**Probe:** `tools/probe_together_cancel_numberfield.m` generates 101 random
cases over seven fields: `Q(I)`, `Q(Sqrt[2], Sqrt[3])`, `Q(I, Sqrt[3])`,
`Q(2^(1/3))`, `Q(Root[#^3-#-1&,1])`, `Q((-1)^(1/3))`, and a parametric one
over `a`, `k` with `Sqrt[k - 1]`. Cases have 1–3 variables and run from about
100 to 5000 leaves. It runs Together, Cancel, Together/Cancel with Extension,
Apart (with and without Extension), Factor (default, `Extension -> Automatic`,
`Extension -> {gens}`) and FactorList. It checks every result: numerically at
two points to 40 digits, or by an exact Expand for Factor. It compares time,
LeafCount and factor count with the reference's own run of the same cases.

Tests:
- `field_together_cancel_tests`: the repro, the reference's output forms, and
  Apart with dependent radicals.
- `ratfun`, `ratcanon_spec`, `extension_options` and `extension_auto_builtins`
  expectations rebaselined to the reference's factored forms, each checked

## Rational integration: every algorithm of Bronstein ch. 2, selectable, with measured defaults

The rational-function integrator now implements all the algorithms of Bronstein,
*Symbolic Integration I*, ch. 2, natively in C (`src/calculus/intrat_alg.c`, on
FLINT). Each can be selected through Method sub-options:

```
Integrate[f, x, Method -> {"BronsteinRational",
    "Hermite"  -> "Linear" | "Quadratic" | "HorowitzOstrogradsky",
    "LogPart"  -> "LazardRiobooTrager" | "RothsteinTrager" | "Czichowski",
    "RealForm" -> "Rioboo" | "LogToReal" | None}]
```

Each sub-option is optional and defaults to `Automatic`. The same rules work as
trailing options of `Integrate`BronsteinRational`.

- **Hermite reduction.**
  - The quadratic version (§2.2) and Horowitz–Ostrogradsky (§2.3, one exact linear
    system) are new.
  - Mack's linear version is now native too, over the rationals.
- **Log part.**
  - Rothstein–Trager (§2.4): the resultant is factored over Q, then a gcd is taken
    over each residue field.
  - Czichowski (§2.6): the residues and log arguments are read off a lex Gröbner
    basis, computed by the in-tree FLINT engine.
  - These join Lazard–Rioboo–Trager.
- **Real form.** Bronstein's general LogToReal (§2.8) is new. It works for a residue
  polynomial of any degree:
  - it finds the exact roots (FLINT `qqbar`);
  - a real root a gives a Log[S(a, x)];
  - a root u + I v gives u Log[A^2 + B^2] + v LogToAtan(A, B), by Rioboo's
    continuous LogToAtan, so there are no spurious jumps.
  - `RealForm -> None` returns the formal `RootSum` of complex logarithms, in slot
    form.
- **Defaults chosen by measurement.** All 36 combinations were run on the 423
  integrals of benchmark 266, which gained two families, `czichowski` and
  `logtoreal`. Every Automatic and "Rioboo" combination verified on every case. The
  defaults are:
  - Hermite: Mack's linear version (the three variants were within noise).
  - Log part:
    - Rothstein–Trager while every irreducible residue factor has degree ≤ 8;
    - Czichowski otherwise, 4–5× faster than the alternatives on high-degree
      irreducible denominators;
    - Lazard–Rioboo–Trager for parameters, radicals and Root coefficients.
  - Real form: the existing Rioboo shape dispatch. The general LogToReal never gave a
    smaller answer on the corpus.
  - Previous default to new: the median time per integral falls from 7.7 ms to
    about 5.8 ms, and the total from 17.4 s to about 14 s.
- **Benchmark 266 at the new defaults (Automatic), on top of the number-field
  engine:**
  - 423 of 423 integrals verify, with no spurious discontinuities.
  - The median time per integral is 1–30 ms in every family, 0.01–0.34× the
    reference system's.
  - Against the review's starting point: classical 4.0 → 1.7 ms, nonsqfree 51 →
    6 ms, rootsum 65 → 4.7 ms.
  - The worst case, an irreducible degree-20 denominator, takes 1.06 s (Czichowski).
    It timed out before the review.
- **Safety caps.** Exact-root arithmetic cannot be interrupted, so the general
  LogToReal declines when a residue factor has degree > 6 or coefficients beyond 256
  bits (degree > 4 when only radicals are allowed). It then falls back to the shape
  dispatch.
- **Tests.** Bronstein's textbook examples for each algorithm (2.2.1–2.3.1, 2.4.1,
  2.5.1/2.5.2, 2.6.1, 2.8.1/(2.20), Czichowski's degree-14 example) are in
  `tests/test_intrat.c`. All variants agree.

### Integrate: branch-wrong answers off the real axis (benchmark 265)

Seventeen integrands of benchmark 265 (goursat-stress) got answers that were correct on part of the
complex plane and wrong elsewhere. They came from the Goursat stage and the elliptic (Legendre) stage of
ParallelMixedSpecial. Default `Integrate` returned three of the wrong answers itself: the
Sqrt[2]-coefficient pseudo-elliptic integrands and the elliptic residual of `G-s7-sqrt-nonelementary-008`.
All seventeen now give answers that are correct on the whole plane. They pass `PossibleZeroQ[D[F, x] - f]`
with `x` complex, and D[F, x] - f is zero at 40 digits at real and complex points.

- **`Integrate`GoursatAlgebraic`: sound certificate.** The differentiate-back guard used to sample only real
  x > 1. It could not see a branch error, and the answers are written through radicals of the
  Mobius-transformed variable. Sqrt[g(x)], with g = c(x)^2 R(x), equals c Sqrt[R] only up to a sign that
  changes from region to region. Every candidate must now pass `PossibleZeroQ` over the complex plane
  (`diff_back_certified`). The normalised forms are tried first, then the raw form. If none passes, the
  stage declines.
- **`Integrate`GoursatAlgebraic`: re-expression over Sqrt[R].** The rewrite of Sqrt[g] to
  rational * Sqrt[R] refused a square part that carried a constant radical. Examples are
  8 Sqrt[R]/(x - 1 - Sqrt[2])^2, and the Sqrt[-2] of the period-3 reductions. It also used plain
  `Factor`, which cannot find a square part with algebraic coefficients. It now counts only
  x-dependent radicals, never rewrites a constant Sqrt[2], and factors with `Extension -> Automatic`.
  The period-3, V4-quartic and Sqrt[2]-coefficient cases now close, with answers in the integrand's own
  Sqrt[R].
- **ParallelMixedSpecial, cubic Legendre form.** `CubicSign` picked the root ordering whose branch sign
  was one constant on the real line. For Int dx/Sqrt[(x - 1)(x - 3)(x + 3)], that form,
  I Sqrt[2] EllipticF[ArcSin[Sqrt[-2/(x - 3)]], 3], was wrong at x = 1/3 + 2 I/7. The sign is now probed
  at complex points too. When no ordering has one sign, the exact locally constant branch factor
  (`CubicBranchFactor`) multiplies the form. Every answer must also pass `PossibleZeroQ` over the complex
  plane (`ComplexSoundQ`), and `LocalBranchFix` is the fallback.
- **`PossibleZeroQ` with `Element[x, Complexes]`: stratified complex screen.** The complex draws took
  independent log-uniform real and imaginary parts, so about 1 draw in 25 landed in a bounded wrong
  region near the origin. The old Legendre answer for 1/Sqrt[1 + x^3] is wrong near x = -1 - I, and it was
  certified. In addition, the screen's sign pattern gave Re and Im the same bit, so a one-symbol test
  never sampled quadrants II and IV. A Bronstein candidate for Sqrt[1 + Sin[x]] was wrong near
  u = -1/5 + I/10 and passed 3 seeds in 16. The complex visits now cycle the 16 (Re, Im) magnitude-band
  pairs, the Im sign flips with the parity of each pair so every quadrant meets every band, and each
  orthant is visited 32 times instead of 16.
- **`Gamma[a, z]` for large |z|.** Two calls hung: `Gamma[1/3, 27000.]` (mpfr_gamma_inc) and
  `Gamma[1/3, -216000.]` (the lower series at about 3*10^5 guard bits). Both now use the asymptotic
  series (DLMF 8.11.2) once |z| exceeds the working precision and 4(|a| + 1). These hangs had stalled the
  complex-plane certificate on `Integrate[E^-x^3, x]`.

Tests: `test_integrate_goursat.c::test_branch_plane`,
`test_parallelmixedspecial.c::test_cubic_branch_whole_plane` (plus the updated `CubicData` assertions in
`test_cubic_branch_whole_line`), and `test_gamma.c::test_gamma_incomplete_large_argument`.

## N: a branch-cut function at an exact argument on its cut takes the principal side (v0.6765)

- **What was wrong.** `a = -1/48 - 13/(768 c) - c/3` with `c = ((-35/2048 + (9 I/1024) Sqrt[3])/2)^(1/3)`
  is exactly `-1/8` (`RootReduce[a]`), but its numerical value carries a rounding residue
  in the imaginary part: `-0.125 - 6.9*10^-18 I` at machine precision, a residue of
  changing sign at each MPFR precision. Every function with a branch cut picked its side
  by the sign of that residue. `N[Sqrt[a]]` was `-0.353553 I` (the principal value is
  `+I/(2 Sqrt[2])`), `N[Log[a]]` was `-2.07944 - 3.14159 I`, and `N[ArcSin[16 a]]`,
  `N[ArcTan[16 I a]]` and `a^(1/3)` were wrong in the same way. At `N[.., 30]` the sign
  changed from one precision to the next, so the adaptive climb never certified a pass.
  It returned `0.*^-30 + 0.4 I` with `N::meprec`, and `Check[N[Sqrt[a], 30], $Failed]`
  was `$Failed`. A Bronstein antiderivative built from these radicals had a machine
  residual of `0.0222 - 0.168 I` at `u = 3/10`, and its machine value was off by
  `0.33 - 0.08 I`. Raising the precision cannot settle an imaginary part that is exactly zero.
- **Fix** (`src/numeric.c`, `nbc_*`; `flint_qqbar_part_sign` in `src/poly/flint_qqbar.c`).
  After a cut head's arguments are numericalized, N checks each Complex argument. The
  heads are `Power` with a non-integer exponent, `Log`, `ArcSin`, `ArcCos`, `ArcTan`,
  `ArcCot`, `ArcSec`, `ArcCsc`, `ArcSinh`, `ArcCosh`, `ArcTanh`, `ArcCoth`, `ArcSech`
  and `ArcCsch`. The check is one exponent comparison. An argument straddles when its cut
  component is below half the working precision relative to the other component, or has
  cancelled to an exact `0.`, and the other component lies on the cut. For a straddling
  argument whose input was exact, qqbar decides the component's sign exactly:
  - **zero:** the value is re-derived from the canonical exact form (`-1/8`), so the
    function is evaluated at the real (or pure imaginary) number, the same convention
    as at an exact real argument. The rounding residue's cancellation record goes with
    it, so `N[.., p]` certifies.
  - **nonzero:** the residue takes that sign.
  - **non-algebraic argument:** it is re-evaluated alone at rising precision until the
    component separates from 0. If it never does within `$MaxExtraPrecision`, N emits
    `N::meprec` once (routed through `mth_message`, so `Check` and `Quiet` see it) and
    takes the principal side.

  Inexact inputs are untouched. The zero tests' sampler numericalizes constant
  subexpressions through the same path.
- **After.** `N[Sqrt[a]]` is `0.0 + 0.353553 I`. `N[Sqrt[a], 30]` is
  `0.353553390593273762200422181052 I` with no message, and `Check` returns the value.
  `N[Log[a], 30]` has `+3.14159... I`. The antiderivative's machine and 30-digit values
  now agree with the explicit principal-value form. Its difference over `[1/5, 7/10]`
  matches `NIntegrate` to 20 digits, so that output is correct under principal values.
- **Cost.** A typical-N timing loop (10 expressions: machine N x2000, N[., 30] x500,
  3000 substituted points) is unchanged within noise (0.89-0.97 s before, 0.92-0.93 s after).
- Tests: `test_n_branch_cut_exact_straddle` (numeric_tests) and
  `test_branch_cut_straddle_constants` (zero_test_tests).

### Integrate: a complex-coefficient rational function gets the complex log sum over its residues, never Cardano/Ferrari radicals

Take `Integrate[((4 + 2 Sqrt[2]) u^2)/((3 + 2 Sqrt[2]) (I + 5 u^2 + 5 I u^4 + u^6)),
u]`. Its denominator splits over Q(I) into three quadratics:
`(u^2 - I) (u^2 + (-2 + 2 I) u - I) (u^2 + (2 - 2 I) u - I)`. Even so, the default
answer (`Automatic`, `"BronsteinRational"`, every `"LogPart"` and every
`"RealForm"` other than `None`) was 1515 leaves of casus-irreducibilis

### PossibleZeroQ / ParallelMixedSpecial: elliptic residuals verified without re-reading their elliptic integrals

The complex-plane certificate on ParallelMixedSpecial answers (ComplexSoundQ, added with the
cubic branch-soundness fix) made `Integrate`ParallelMixedSpecial[1/((x - 3) Sqrt[1 + x^3]), x]`
take 21 s. The new checks were not the cause. Every check read the answer's EllipticPi, and here
its characteristic term `1 - n Sin[phi]^2 = (x - 3)/(x + 1)` is on the negative real axis for all
`x` in (-1, 3). Carlson duplication is not valid there, so Arb integrates numerically: about
120 ms per call at 53 bits, with an erratic cost at higher precision (0.008 s at 30 digits, 0.9 s
at 40, 85 s at 300). In a correct answer the terms carrying the elliptic integral cancel
identically. The derivative of `c(x) F(phi | m)` holds `F` only through `c'(x) F`, and `c` is a
locally constant branch factor.

- **PossibleZeroQ (zero_test.c, `zt_elliptic_pretest`).** Before the ordinary test, each distinct
  maximal `EllipticF`/`EllipticE`/`EllipticPi`/`EllipticK` call that depends on a free symbol is
  replaced by a fresh symbol. The fresh symbol is sampled over the complex plane, because the
  atoms' values are complex. Only a TRUE of the abstracted expression is taken, since `e' == 0`
  implies `e == 0`. FALSE and UNKNOWN fall through to the unchanged ordinary test, so no FALSE
  or wrong-answer rejection is affected. On the residual above, PossibleZeroQ went from 8.8 s to
  0.45 s on the real line, and from 8.9 s to 0.53 s with `Assumptions -> Element[x, Complexes]`.
- **ParallelMixedSpecial `Num` (VerifyAnswer, FixBranch).** The 30-digit point reading now
  evaluates each elliptic integral once, at 30 digits, and then numericalises the rest around it.
  Before, N's adaptive precision re-read every copy at 51, 80 and 100 digits, chasing a
  cancellation that is exact. **FixBranch** now reads `d` and the kernel once per sample instead
  of once per multiplier.
- **Timings.** `1/((x - 3) Sqrt[1 + x^3])` went from 22 s to 1.2 s and gives the same answer.
  `test_cubic_branch_whole_line` went from about 60 s to 13 s. `parallelmixedspecial_tests` went
  from 112 s to 87 s. Of the remaining 87 s, 56 s is `test_overflow_contained`, which runs the
  stage to its 45 s `$SpecialTimeBudget` by design (unchanged, unrelated).

## Elliptic integrals on their cuts: the side decided exactly, the value fast (v0.6765)

- **What was wrong.** `Integrate`ParallelMixedSpecial[1/((x - 3) Sqrt[1 + x^3]), x]`
  answers with `EllipticPi[4/(1 + (-1)^(1/3)), ArcSin[Sqrt[(1 + (-1)^(1/3))/(1 + x)]],
  (1 - I Sqrt[3])/2]`, where `p = 1 - n Sin[phi]^2 = (x - 3)/(x + 1)` is exactly a
  negative real on `-1 < x < 3`. That is the cut of the Carlson form `R_J(x, y, 1, p)`.
  The kernel recomputed `p` from the rounded arguments, so the side came from the sign
  of a rounding residue. At `x = 3/10`, `N[.., 30]` and `N[.., 60]` gave
  `0.459966 - 1.494599 I`, while the machine value and `N[.., 40]` gave
  `-0.348958 + 1.524344 I`. The two differ by a residue of the integrand. Arb had no
  Carlson path there and integrated numerically next to the pole: 120 ms at machine
  precision and 85 s at 300 digits. The same rounding choice affected `EllipticF` and
  `EllipticE` with `y = 1 - m Sin[phi]^2` on its cut, `EllipticK`, `EllipticE` and
  `EllipticPi[n, m]` at exact complex spellings of `m >= 1` or `n >= 1`, and all three
  incomplete forms at `Re phi = Pi/2` (`EllipticF[ArcSin[2], 1/3]` was
  `1.10577 - 2.02896 I` at 20 digits and `2.36206 + 2.02896 I` at 30). The integrand
  evaluated at the point was right, so `D[F, x] - f` still read zero.
  `N[F /. x -> 3/10, 30]` was `0.604510 + 0.*I` with `N::meprec`; it is now
  `0.604510 + 0.593705 I`.
- **Convention, documented.** The incomplete integrals are Carlson forms in
  `x = Cos[phi]^2`, `y = 1 - m Sin[phi]^2` and `p = 1 - n Sin[phi]^2`. This is the
  defining integral along the straight segment in `v = Sin[t]`. On a cut, the value is the
  limit from `Im > 0` of the quantity on its cut. For real phi this is `m -> m - i0` and
  `n -> n - i0`, the placement real arguments already had
  (`EllipticPi[3/2, 1/2] = -0.45672 - 2.72070 I`, `EllipticK[2.] = 1.31103 - 1.31103 I`).
  At `Re phi = Pi/2 (mod Pi)` the side picks the quasi-period shift. The choice is checked
  against `NIntegrate` of the defining integral moved to that side, to 20 digits, for
  `Pi` with `p` on its cut, `F` and `E` with `y` on its cut, `K` at `m = 3`, and real
  `n > 1` (path indented below the pole).
- **Fix 1: side decided exactly** (`src/numeric.c`, `nbc_elliptic`). This extends this
  week's exact-argument straddle rule. When N evaluates an incomplete elliptic integral
  with exact arguments and a numerical `x`, `y` or `p` within the noise of its cut, it
  builds the quantity from the exact arguments and takes the sign of its imaginary part
  from qqbar. A non-algebraic quantity goes through a precision climb up to
  `$MaxExtraPrecision`, then `N::meprec` via `mth_message`. The kernel is then called
  with that side. The complete heads join `nbc_cut` (`m >= 1`, `n >= 1`).
- **Fix 2: Carlson assembly with sides and fast paths** (`src/flint_num_bridge.c`,
  `nb_ell_*`, new `flint_num_elliptic_inc_sided`).
  - `F`, `E` and `Pi` are now assembled from `R_F`, `R_D` and `R_J`. This is Arb's own
    construction, bit-identical on 3000 random complex triples, so values off the cuts
    are unchanged.
  - A quantity on its cut has its imaginary part dropped, giving the limit from above.
    The limit from below is the conjugate at the conjugated arguments.
  - `R_J` uses Carlson's duplication directly in the proven cases: real `x` and `y`, or a
    conjugate pair `y = conj(x)` with `z > 0`. A pair conjugate to within `2^-(inb - 8)`
    is symmetrised first; the near-pair duplication was measured wrong 1 time in 600.
  - With `p` exactly on `(-oo, 0)`, `R_J` is the Cauchy principal value from DLMF
    19.20.14 minus the half residue `3 Pi I/(2 Sqrt[(x - p)(y - p)(z - p)])`. This
    matches the duplication at `p + 10^-70 I` to 70 digits.
  - Arb's numerical integration is left only for the general complex case.
- **After.** All eleven audited cut cases agree across machine, 20, 30, 40 and 60 digits.
  Timings for the example:

  - 300 digits: 85 s to 10 ms. 1000 digits: 61 ms.

    (was 1.6 s).
  - Wrong-antiderivative residuals: 0.20 s to 0.015 s and 0.12 s to 0.024 s.
- **Remaining gap.** `R_J` with `y` (not `p`) exactly on its cut and `x` neither real nor

## Simplify: the radical function-field normal form; Together/Cancel combine radicals next to constant algebraic numbers (v0.6766)

**The report.** `D[Integrate`GeneralisedGoursat[(x - 1 - Sqrt[3] I)/((x + 1 + Sqrt[3] I)
(x^3 + 8)^(1/3)), x], x] // Simplify` took 3.3 s and returned about 15 unsimplified
fractions in `(8 + x^3)^(k/3)` over the constants `(-1)^(1/3)`, `2^(1/3)`, `(-2)^(1/3)`,
`(-1/2)^(1/3)`, `Sqrt[3]`. `Together` left the sum uncombined and `Cancel` changed nothing.
Now `Simplify` returns `(-2 (-1)^(1/3) + x)/((2 (-1)^(1/3) + x) (8 + x^3)^(1/3))`, the
integrand (`2 (-1)^(1/3) = 1 + I Sqrt[3]`), in 0.15 s; `Together` gives one fraction in
0.08 s and `Cancel` of it one fraction in 6 ms.

**Simplify: the radical function-field normal form** (`nfrat_radical_normal_form`,
`src/poly/nfrat.c`; first in `simp_algebraic_field`, `src/simp/radrat.c`). An expression
rational in variables, constant algebraic numbers and radicals of non-constant radicands is
an element of `L = K(x)[h_1..h_r]/(h_i^Q_i - R_i)`, `K` the number field of the constants
(the nfrat machinery: primitive element, reduction modulo its minimal polynomial, certified
modular gcds, the render-back into the caller's radicals). The algorithm is general, not
pattern-based:
- *Tower.* One generator per radicand class, a class being a radicand up to a positive
  rational factor (`(2 x)^(1/3) = 2^(1/3) x^(1/3)` is principal-branch valid). The index is
  the lcm of the class's indices and `B^(p/q) = g^(p Q/q)` (an integer power of one
  principal radical is always exact: `(8 + x^3)^(5/3) = (8 + x^3) g^2`). A rational
  radicand `N/D` is rescaled to the integral `h = D g`. A bare-symbol radicand is eliminated,
  `s = t^Q`. No other radicals are combined (`Sqrt[a] Sqrt[b] -> Sqrt[a b]` is not valid).
- *Inverse by the norm.* Per generator, the traces `Tr(A^k) = Q [h^0] A^k` give the
  characteristic polynomial of multiplication by `A` through Newton's identities, and
  Cayley-Hamilton gives `A adj = Norm(A)`, generator-free; exact in any commutative ring, so
  a zero divisor (`(x^2)^(1/2) - x`) declines rather than guesses.
- *Canonical form and zero test.* The content is cancelled over `K`. Kummer theory decides
  whether `L` is a field of full degree (each radicand has a squarefree factor of
  multiplicity prime to its index, coprime to the other radicands); then the form is
  canonical. A zero normal form is a proof of zero in every case.
- *Read-back.* `Sum c_m(x) y^m`, each `c_m` factored and written in the caller's constants,
  a radicand factor absorbed into its radical when that shortens the term; the sum, its
  one-fraction form and the dual `1/nf(1/nf)` are scored and the best kept only when it
  beats the input. The generator engine (nested radicals, transcendental atoms) remains the
  fallback.

Branch traps are untouched: `Sqrt[x] Sqrt[1 + x] - Sqrt[x + x^2]`, `Sqrt[-x] - I Sqrt[x]`,
`(x^3)^(1/3) - x`, `Sqrt[x^2] - x`, `(-8 - x^3)^(1/3) + (8 + x^3)^(1/3)` and
`Sqrt[1/x] - 1/Sqrt[x]` stay as they are.

**Together / Cancel** (`nfrat_radical_kernels`, wired in `rat_nfrat`, `src/rat.c`). A sum
over radicals of non-constant radicands with constant algebraic coefficients is one
fraction, as in the reference: each radical is an independent kernel over `K` (one
generator per radicand as written, integer powers of `B^(1/Q)`, a bare-symbol radicand
eliminated), and nothing is rationalised. `Together[1/(1 + y) + 1/(2^(1/3) - y)]` with
`y = (8 + x^3)^(1/3)` is `(-1 - 2^(1/3))/((1 + y) (-2^(1/3) + y))` (it was returned as
given). The documented semantics, checked against the reference, are unchanged: a numeric
algebraic denominator is not rationalised and a `Root` power is not reduced.

**Cost gates.** An a-priori bound on `[K:Q]` (prime factors of rational radicands,
root-of-unity orders, the index of each distinct nested radical) declines a constant field
past degree 32, whose primitive element is the expensive part (degree 54, `2^(1/3)`,
`3^(1/6)`, `37^(1/3)`: ~25 s). When the field takes an input of more than 1000 leaves the
generator engine is skipped, and on a result of more than 150 leaves neither its re-dispatch
polish nor `simp_dispatch`'s second pass over a kernel-free result runs (`src/simp/simp_search.c`);
on an 89k-leaf Trager derivative those were whole searches per sub-node. In Together the
relation-aware zero test (`flint_algebraic_field_normalize`) runs before the kernel reading
(inputs up to 4000 leaves), so an identity over a cube-root tower is still `0`.

**Before / after** (`Simplify[D[F, x]]` returns the integrand or a form no larger; same
machine, both binaries run side by side): 146 `GeneralisedGoursat` answers from benchmark 265,
105 -> 134, total 1216 s -> 238 s; 118 `Trager` answers, 89 -> 96. No case got worse. The
misses are `RootSum` answers (not a radical field), the degree-54 field (where `Simplify`
returns `Indeterminate` in both, a separate wrong answer), and timeouts in both. One very
large Trager derivative (89k leaves) is slower, 1.9 -> 3.5 s.

**Two shared fixes found on the way.**
- The render-back of a number-field answer (`fg_render_init`, `src/poly/flint_bridge.c`)
  chose its atoms greedily and could take one the earlier ones generate (`2^(2/3)` after
  `2^(1/3)`), miss the spanning set `{2^(1/3), (-1)^(2/3), Sqrt[3]}` of degree 12, and make
  the whole nfrat computation decline; more than 8 atoms always declined. A failed greedy

  times the basis so far are independent) over up to 32 atoms, then by a subset search.
- `nr_reduce` (the reduction modulo the primitive element's minimal polynomial, run after
  every product in nfrat) pushed each term as a rational, with a gcd per push. `M` is monic
  and integral, so `tau^j mod M` is integral: the reduction now runs on the integer part
  with one content renormalisation. That alone took the repro's `Together` from 0.23 s to

One expectation rebaselined in `tests/test_radical_simplify.c`:
`Simplify[-1/Sqrt[a] - I y/(a Sqrt[a + I y] - Sqrt[a] (a + I y))]` is now `1/Sqrt[a + I y]`
(with `s = Sqrt[a]`, `u = Sqrt[a + I y]`: `a u - s u^2 = u s (s - u)` and

Tests: `tests/test_radical_field_simplify.c` (`radical_field_simplify_tests`): the repro

## Simplify returned Indeterminate: an internal Expand's Overflow[] entered the arithmetic (v0.6766)

**The report.** `Simplify[D[F, x]]`, with `F` the `GeneralisedGoursat` antiderivative of
benchmark 265's `G-s7-cbrt-symmetric-001`,

    f = (-1512 + 6545 x - 8346 x^2 + 3619 x^3 - 2170 x^4 + 369 x^5 - 128 x^6)/
        ((-2 + 2 x - 2 x^2)^(1/3) (216 - 580 x + 324 x^2 + 108 x^3 - 548 x^4 +
         324 x^5 - 108 x^6 + 32 x^7)),

returned `Indeterminate` after about 20 s. The derivative (1152 leaves, constants
`2^(1/3)`, `3^(1/6)`, `6^(1/3)`, `37^(1/3)`, ... and `Sqrt[3]`) is a regular algebraic
function equal to `f`.

**Root cause.** The `Expand` builtin always ran in its user-facing mode, where an
expansion whose estimated size passes the memory ceiling (5e7 terms) answers
`Overflow[]`. C code that reaches `Expand` through the evaluator from inside another
builtin got that sentinel back as if it were a value. Simplify's trig-rational fast path
(`simp_trig_rational`, `src/simp/trigrat.c`) treats the one constant radical `Sqrt[3]` as a
generator and the other 14 radicals as opaque atoms. It rationalises the denominator by
multiplying it and the numerator by the conjugate. The two products had 530k and 1.7M
leaves and would have expanded to 24M and 212M leaves, so both Expands answered
`Overflow[]`. The pass carried on, its final `num/den` evaluated
`Overflow[]/Overflow[]` to `Indeterminate`, and the strict leaf-count gate accepted that
1-leaf "form" as the simplest. About 70 call sites in `src/simp` alone reach `Expand` the
same way, and none checks for the sentinel.

**Fix** (`src/expand.c`, `expand_with_options`). `Overflow[]` stays the answer of a
user-level `Expand` (documented). A call from inside another builtin
(`eval_builtin_depth() > 1`) whose expansion overflows gets its subject back unexpanded.
That is the same value, so every identity the caller then applies stays an identity, and
the caller's own gates judge the result. `Simplify` of the reported derivative now gives
`(1512 - 6545 x + 8346 x^2 - 3619 x^3 + 2170 x^4 - 369 x^5 + 128 x^6)/(2 (-2 + 2 x -
2 x^2)^(4/3) (54 - 91 x - 64 x^2 + 54 x^3 - 19 x^4 + 8 x^5))` (75 leaves), equal to `f`
to 1e-39 at two complex points, in about 20 s as before.

Tests: `tests/test_expand_internal_overflow.c` (`expand_internal_overflow_tests`):
user-level `Expand[(a + ... + j)^30]` is still `Overflow[]`; the same call at builtin
depth > 1 returns its subject; an internal Expand within the ceiling still expands; the
reported derivative (`tests/data/radical_field/goursat_cbrt_overflow_derivative.m`)

### GeneralisedGoursat package

- **Deferred integration.** In the Integrate stage (`GoursatIntegrate`) `ratInt` records each
  rational integrand and returns a placeholder `ggDeferred[id][m]`; a cubic-field piece is
  deferred likewise. An undecided reduction (a third-kind residual at the last level) integrates
  nothing: both non-elementary cases above spent 25 s and 8 s integrating pieces of second-level
  reductions whose answer was then discarded. The public `GoursatReduce` /
  `GoursatIntegrateAlgebraic` are unchanged (eager).
- **Chain-rule verification before integration.** A placeholder's derivative is `rho(m) m'(t)`
  (ratInt returns a piece only once `P'(m) = rho(m)` is verified), so the derivative of the whole
  answer is an algebraic function of `(t, y)`: `PossibleZeroQ` decides it without logarithms or
  RootSums (k-family: 3.6 s of closed-form verification -> 0.2 s). The closed-form verifier stays
  the fallback.
- **Conjugate pieces.** A rational integrand that is the Galois conjugate of one already
  integrated (complex conjugation, or the sign of one `Sqrt[n]`) takes the conjugate
  antiderivative, checked by `PossibleZeroQ`, instead of a second rational integration. The
  automorphism is applied node by node (a ReplaceAll inside a RootSum's held Function body left
  `Complex[0, -1*-1]` unevaluated).
- **Parameters at the second level.** Over `Q(params)` the multiplication-by-2 chain's
  integrands grow at every level (a 7000-leaf residual at depth 2 for the `p = 2/3` k-family,
  30 s). The second level is first run at two parameter values; when both are undecided the
  parametric chain is not attempted (a decline, never an answer or a certificate; a degenerate
  specialisation is no evidence).
- `gK` reduces numeric algebraic constants (Internal`NumberFieldCanonical). `ratOK` tries
  `PossibleZeroQ` first for a RootSum answer too, under a short budget (2 s over `Q(alg)`, 5 s
  over `Q(params)`), before the exact vector check: a 471-leaf RootSum answer (stress_7
  sqrt-nonelementary #6) took 30 s exactly, 17 ms by PossibleZeroQ. The exact verifier
  (`derivZeroQ`) puts a RootSum answer to PossibleZeroQ first only over `Q(params)` (the vector
  arithmetic over `Q(k)(I Sqrt[3])` ran past a minute, PossibleZeroQ 30 ms); over `Q(alg)` the
  hybrid check stays first, because PossibleZeroQ on the whole form can run far past its
  TimeConstrained budget there (stress_2026 sqrt-nonelementary #7: over 200 s). The exact
  derivative of one RootSum term now has its own 30 s budget, past which the term is left to
  PossibleZeroQ (a degree-9 RootSum over `Q(Sqrt[-51])`: over a minute exactly, 0.5 s by
  PossibleZeroQ). The exact verifier also reads a
  RootSum summand with the root in its denominator modulo the defining polynomial (it passed a
  non-polynomial to CoefficientList and rejected a correct antiderivative), and its
  rationality test `ratInQ` descends into a replaced `Power[den, -1]` (`z^3/(a z^2 + b z^5)` read
  as irrational, and stress_7 cbrt-symmetric #15 fell back to a minute-long PossibleZeroQ:
  77 s -> 2.7 s); `tidy` is skipped
  when it has nothing to do, and `tidyArgs` no longer re-evaluates unchanged nodes; a resolved
  answer whose rational pieces cancel is tidied to its normal form (the non-elementary partial
  of `t^2/Sqrt[(t^2 - 1)(t^2 - 4)]` is `0` again).

### Core

- **Internal`NumberFieldCanonical[e]** (new, `src/poly/nfrat.c`, `src/rat.c`): the canonical form
  of a rational function over the number field of its constant algebraic atoms -- numerator and
  denominator expanded, constants reduced, a numeric denominator inverted over `K`, unique for
  equal functions; `$Failed` otherwise. Together/Cancel keep the reference's behaviour.
- **Apart** (`src/parfrac.c`): coefficients over a number field are reduced in the field (a
  degree-6 denominator over `Q(I, Sqrt[3])`: 110 leaves of 100-digit numbers -> 42 leaves); a
  squarefree denominator with algebraic coefficients takes the residue-formula path (over
  `Q(k)(I Sqrt[3])`: 1.1 s -> 3 ms). A nested radical (`Sqrt[-567 - 324 Sqrt[3]]`) keeps the
  previous paths and spelling: its field's canonical form is written over the powers of the
  nested radical, and the log part built on it grew.
- **LinearSolve** (`src/linalg/linsolve.c`): a system over `Q(t1, ..., tk)` is solved from the
  native `fmpz_mpoly` RREF of `[m | b]`, as RowReduce already was (a 3 x 3 system over `Q(k, x)`,
  degree-12 entries: 26 s -> 7 ms).
- **Rational integration over number fields** (`src/calculus/intrat.c`, `intrat_subres.c`): the
  Lazard-Rioboo-Trager log part runs over `K = Q(theta)` for radical / `I` / Root coefficients
  (algebraic mode: atoms rewritten in a primitive element, answer rendered back in the caller's
  radicals) when the subresultant chain is long (`deg >= 5`) or parameters are present; the
  parameters are passed to the field kernels (they declined before); the subresultant chain is
  computed natively over `Q[t, y1, ...][x]` (`intrat_subres_chain_mv`); the residue factors are
  split squarefree over `K` when the split over `Q[z, t]` is not squarefree over `K`. A degree-12
  denominator over `Q(I Sqrt[3])`: over 60 s -> 4 s; `u/(a + b u^3)` over `Q(k)(I Sqrt[3])`:
  0.56 s -> 0.19 s.
