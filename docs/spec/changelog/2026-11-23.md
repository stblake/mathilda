# Changelog: week of 2026-11-23 (Mon) – 2026-11-29 (Sun)

## PossibleZeroQ past the 1000-bit rung; three slow Bronstein cases (Campaign D6, v0.6699)

**PossibleZeroQ (G-pzq-1).** Integrate's Euler-substitution answer for 257 `cM-3-152`,
`((1 + E^x^2)(1 + E^x^2 + E^(2 x^2))^(3/2) x)/E^x^2`, was correct but read `False`. Its
`E^(x^2) ~ 2^90` sits under nested `(2 Sqrt[q] - 2 u - 1)^k` denominators, and at a sample
point near `x = -7.9` the cancellation is about 1300 bits deep: the residual reads `2^274` at
200, 500 and 1000 bits, `2^-331` at 2000 and `2^-2329` at 4000. Three changes to the
huge-scale path in `src/zero_test.c`:

- On an exact point whose residual stays flat over the whole ladder, the climb now doubles
  past 1000 bits (up to 8000, `ZT_HUGE_EXTEND_MAX_BITS`). It decides on the ladder's own
  evidence: a zero falls by at least half the bits added, and a genuine value holds.
- The rung-1 plateau test no longer compares against the machine residual, which a deep
  cancellation under-reads.
- A residual still falling by half the added bits at the top rung counts as a zero, for the
  same reason, on the point path and in the constant's re-read.

`Exp[300] + 1 - Exp[300]`, `Exp[10^4] (Sin[Pi/7]^2 + Cos[Pi/7]^2 - 1) + 1` and both
perturbations of the cM-3-152 residual (`+ x E^(x^2)/10^6` and `+ 10^-20`) stay `False`.

**The kernel substitution is verified in u.** f = h(theta) D theta by construction, so
`D[G, u] = h` (antiderivativeQ and PossibleZeroQ in u), together with an exact check of that
construction, proves `G(theta)` by the chain rule. The test in u is small; the test in x was
the one that met G-pzq-1.

**Three slow cases:**

| case | before | after | what |
|---|---|---|---|
| 257 `cM-3-152` | 7.5 s | 0.06 s | the kernel substitution's answer is accepted (above) |
| 260 `two-elem-49` | 2.9 s | 0.10 s | `powerBasisInverse` reads a monomial `c y^j` structurally |
| 257 `cM-4-306` | 3.3 s | 2.2 s | exponential-kernel budget 1.5 s → 0.6 s |

- **`two-elem-49`.** The monomial test ran on `Expand[q]`, which distributed
  `2 y^5 (1 + x^3)^2 (3 + x^4)^3` into many terms. The denominator then went to the general
  6 × 6 symbolic system, which was 2.8 s of the 2.9 s.
- **`cM-4-306`.** Every cM success closes within 0.31 s. This case, where Trager in u needs
  2.5 s, now falls to the 1.8 s tower sooner. Its remaining cost is the principal-divisor
  generator (`kRowEchelon`).

`cB-2-1053` (1.8 s) was left alone. `Automatic` already closes it at once in the
QuadraticRadicals stage. The 1.8 s is the `Method -> "Trager"` column, which measures
Trager's algorithm itself. Sending that method to the Euler substitution first would have
changed its answer form (`ArcSinh[x]` for `Log[x + Sqrt[1 + x^2]]`) and misreported what the
column measures.

## Trager's normalisation at infinity in C; two PossibleZeroQ false negatives (Campaign D6, v0.6700)

**The divisor-generator step.** `principalGenerator` decides whether a divisor is
principal by normalising the basis of its ideal at infinity ([T] Ch 2 Sec 3).
`normalizeAtInfinity` did this in .m, one unimodular step per pass. On 257 `cM-4-306`
(Trager in u = E^(2x) over a cube-root curve), each pass ran over Q(ω) on a 3 × 3 matrix of
degree 24. That took 35 passes and 0.5 s per divisor, and two divisors made 1.0 s of the
1.76 s.

The normalisation is a shifted weak Popov form with shift δ:
- the key of entry j, `deg c_j + δ_j`, is minus its order at infinity plus `deg den`;
- the kernel breaks a tie on the first column of greatest key, so distinct leading columns put
  the leading vectors in echelon form, which makes them independent.

So `normalizeAtInfinity` now calls the existing C kernel ``FLINT`PolynomialWeakPopovForm``
for every field it supports (Q, GF(p), Q(θ)). The .m loop stays as the fallback: for
an unsupported field, a non-integer shift, or a zero row (a rank-deficient module, still an
internal error).

| case | v0.6699 | v0.6700 |
|---|---|---|
| 257 `cM-4-306`, `Method -> "Bronstein"` | 1.89 s | 1.14 s |
| its kernel in u, `Method -> "Trager"` | 1.76 s | 0.73 s |

The generator can differ from the .m loop's by a constant factor, and the log terms can take
a different form. Two such forms exposed two PossibleZeroQ false negatives (G-pzq-1) in the
verifier. Both answers were correct, and both are fixed in `src/zero_test.c`:

- **A FALSE needs two rungs that agree in value.** The ArcTan form of the antiderivative of
  `D[x + Log[-E^x + (1 + E^(3 x))^(1/3)], x]` (paper example E3-6) has an exactly zero
  residual. At `x = 63.7477` its terms are ratios of sums of size `2^560`, and it reads
  1.6, 2.98 and 2.975 at 200, 500 and 1000 bits, then 0 from 2000 bits. The top two rungs had
  the same magnitude, which the old rule read as a settled value.
  - `zt_resolve_machine_nonzero` now keeps the values of its last two rungs and confirms a
    FALSE only when they agree to `2^-32` relative (`ZT_RESOLVED_GAP_BITS`).
  - Otherwise it doubles the precision up to 8000 bits, deciding on the ladder's evidence: a
    zero falls by half the bits added, and a value reads the same twice.
  - A sample point still unresolved at the cap is re-drawn from a smaller shell, while one is
    left.
  - Genuine values agree at once and cost one extra subtraction.
- **A signed-zero imaginary part on a cut.** Bronstein's answer to
  `Sqrt[Tan[x]] + Sqrt[Cot[x]]` (paper example E6d-4) holds
  `Sqrt[(-I - I E^(-2 I x))/(-1 + E^(-2 I x))]`, which is `Sqrt[Cot[x]]`. At `x = -3.26342`
  the radicand reads `-8.17 + 0.*^-60 I`, and the sign of that zero picks the side of the
  cut. The unresolvable-cut test now counts an inexact zero imaginary part, and it reads the
  exact point, where a machine-real coordinate had kept machine noise at every rung. A
  radicand computed as a real is still evidence:
  `Sqrt[(1 - x)/(1 + x)] (1 + x) - Sqrt[1 - x^2]` stays `False`. That answer was also
  wrong-signed at sampled points in v0.6699, and the old generator passed only because the
  sampler drew other points.

Tests:
- `tests/test_zero_test.c`: `test_unresolved_rungs_climb` and `test_signed_zero_cut_redraw`
  (both residuals, perturbations, genuine non-zero controls).
- `tests/test_trager_normalize_ideal.c`: the native and .m normalisations agree on the orders
  at infinity over Q(√2) and GF(5).

## PossibleZeroQ reads a cut point at its principal value; ArcTan on its lower cut at arbitrary precision (v0.6702)

Investigating the two v0.6700 PossibleZeroQ fixes found that one of them had caused a
wrong `True`, along with an older wrong `True`.

- **The two v0.6700 cases.** Both answers were correct.
  - E3-6: the residual is exactly 0 but cancels about 1100 bits at `x = 63.75`. That is
    two cancellations of about 554 bits each (`12 x log2(e)`), so the 1000-bit rung was
    genuinely too shallow.
  - E6d-4: with its radicands replaced by the exact identities `Cot[x]` and `Tan[x]`, the
    residual is 0 at points with `Tan < 0` as well as `Tan > 0`. The nonzero machine
    readings were branch-cut noise.
- **Re-drawing a cut point hid an interval.** v0.6700 re-drew a point where a radicand is
  real by value but computed through complex arithmetic. On a whole interval every point is
  such a point, so a residual wrong only there went unseen. With `ztC`, `ztT` the exponential
  forms of `Cot[x]`, `Tan[x]`:
  - `PossibleZeroQ[(Sqrt[ztC] Sqrt[ztT] - 1) UnitStep[-Tan[x]]]` was `True` at v0.6700
    (`False` before);
  - `PossibleZeroQ[Sqrt[ztC] Sqrt[ztT] - 1]` was `True` on both, through correlated noise.

  Both are `False` now. The point is read at its principal value instead of being re-drawn:
  - the argument's noisy component is dropped once it falls between 200 and 400 bits on the
    exact point (`b -> Re[b]` for cuts on the real axis, `b -> I Im[b]` for `ArcTan`,
    `ArcCot`, `ArcSinh` and `ArcCsch`);
  - the rewrite goes on the sample tree, so nodes not on a cut read as before;
  - every cut function is projected: projecting `Log` alone broke the noise correlation with
    `ArcTan`, and read `D[F] - ArcTan[Sqrt[x]]` (Bronstein's answer) as `Pi` at `x < -1`.
- **`ArcTan[0 - 2 I]` at MPFR precision read `+Pi/2 - 0.549 I`.** The exact value and the
  machine kernel give `-Pi/2 - 0.549 I`. `mpfr_complex_atan` negated a zero real part into
  `-0`, which put `Log` on the wrong side of its cut. `ArcCot[+-I/2]` had the same error,
  through `ArcTan` of the reciprocal. A sweep of the inverse trig and hyperbolic functions
  plus `Log` and `Sqrt` over eleven cut points now shows no differences between the exact,
  MPFR and machine values.

The gated cost (an expression with a complex constant and a cut function) is a few ms per
call, and ztR6d drops from 1.24 s to 0.32 s because fewer points are re-drawn. Tests: the
`test_signed_zero_cut_redraw` additions in `tests/test_zero_test.c`, and
`test_arctan_mpfr_lower_cut` in `tests/test_trig.c`.

## The branch-cut pre-read and the good-prime search (Campaign D6, v0.6703)

- **PossibleZeroQ's cut test reads in doubles first.** v0.6702 read every cut
  function's argument at 200 bits at every sample point of an expression with a
  complex constant. On Trager's answer for 257 `cM-4-306`, 207 cut functions, the
  verification went from 0.57 s to 0.85 s. A machine-precision read now skips any
  argument more than 2^-30 (relative) from its cut line: such an argument cannot be
  rounding noise on the line. A real machine value is skipped only when it is off
  the half-line or the imaginary axis. `1 + I Sqrt[-3.77]` is exactly real in
  doubles and noisy at 200 bits, so it still has to be read at 200 bits. The
  check now takes 0.40 s.
- **Trager's torsion screen tests each prime with the data computed once.**
  `goodPrime` recomputed the squarefree part of G, the discriminant of
  `Gs Bp`, the resultant of `Bp` and `Ep`, and the residue field's discriminant
  for every candidate prime, about 98 primes on `cM-4-306`.
  `goodPrimeInvariants` computes them once per component (cached on the data).
  Each prime is first tested for a degree-1 prime of the residue field, the
  commonest failure. The search went from 0.086 s to 0.038 s.

`cM-4-306` in u, `Method -> "Trager"`: 0.84 s to 0.79 s. The rest is the answer
check (about 0.4 s), the torsion screen's Jacobian arithmetic (about 0.1 s,
already in C kernels) and the residue ideals (about 0.1 s). A C port of the mod-p
order loop could save about 0.15 s more.

## Elementary functions agree on every surface, on and off the branch cuts (v0.6704)

An audit of every circular and hyperbolic function and its inverse, `Log`, `Sqrt`, `Exp`
and `Power` with rational exponents compared the exact value `N[f[p], n]` with MPFR at 30,
60 and 200 digits (each against the exact value at the same rounded input, so only the
algorithm's error counts), machine scalar, packed, NDArray and `Compile[]` (scalar and
rank 1). Points: both sides of every cut, the branch points, `10^-20` and `10^-100` off
the cuts, signed zeros, `10^-30`, `10^30` and `10^100` along the axes and diagonals, and
generic points. Before: 563 mismatches; after: none (the remaining unevaluated cases are the
singular points). Odd symmetry on the cuts, and the exact values against the
principal-branch definitions, hold on every surface.

- **Machine kernels and `Compile[]` on the imaginary cuts.** `ArcTan`, `ArcCot`, `ArcSinh`
  and `ArcCsch` of a packed list, an NDArray or a compiled argument took C99's side of the
  imaginary-axis cut: `ArcTan[{-2. I}]` was `{1.5708 - 0.549 I}` where the scalar, the exact
  value and MPFR give `-1.5708 - 0.549 I`; `ArcCot[{0.5 I}]`, `ArcSinh[{-2. I}]`,
  `ArcCsch[{0.5 I}]` likewise. `catan`/`casinh` read the side from the sign of the zero
  real part; `mth_machine_elem.h` now passes the zero whose sign gives the definition's
  side, so all four surfaces share one answer. The scalar `ArcCsch` now calls the same
  helper.
- **MPFR complex inverses lost digits, or everything.** The log forms
  (`ArcSin = -I Log[I z + Sqrt[1 - z^2]]`, ...) cancelled on half the plane:
  `ArcSin[SetPrecision[-10^30, 40]]` was `Overflow[]`, at 60 digits it had six correct;
  `ArcTan` of `10^-30 I` at 30 digits had six; `ArcCot` at `I + 10^-20` 23 of 30. They are
  now Kahan's cancellation-free forms, internally at 32 guard bits, with the same values on
  the cuts. The reciprocal functions take `(z -+ 1)/z` as `(z -+ 1) conj(z)/|z|^2`: Smith's
  division had rounded the `10^-200` imaginary part of `(z - 1)/z` at `z = -10^100 + I` to
  an exact 0, which put `ArcSech` on its cut, with the wrong sign of `Pi/2 I`. The real MPFR
  `ArcSec`, `ArcCsc`, `ArcCoth`, `ArcSech` no longer round `1/x` before a function that is
  infinitely sensitive at `x = +-1` (`ArcSec[1 + 10^-25]` at 60 digits had 41 correct).
- **MPFR `Log` near `|z| = 1`.** `Log[SetPrecision[1 + 10^-20 I, 30]]` had `Re = 0`
  (`log(hypot)` of a number rounding to 1); it is `Log1p[(a - 1)(a + 1) + b^2]/2` there.
- **MPFR forward functions at a huge imaginary part.** `Tan`, `Cot`, `Sec`, `Csc`, `Tanh`,
  `Coth`, `Sech`, `Csch` overflowed `Cosh^2` and returned `Indeterminate`
  (`Tan[SetPrecision[10^30 I, 30]]`, even `Tan[SetPrecision[10^9 I, 30]]`); they are
  divided through by `Cosh^2`, so `Tan[...] = I`, `Sec[...] = 0`. `Sin`/`Cos`/`Exp` of a
  zero part times an overflowed factor give an exact 0 part (`Overflow[]`, not
  `Indeterminate`).
- **`N` of `E^z` for complex `z`.** The generic path computed `exp(z log e)` and multiplied
  the rounding of `log e` by `|z|`: `N[Exp[I d], 30]` for `d` the 100-digit integer value of
  `1.*^100` was `0.644 + 0.765 I` (the right value is `0.925 - 0.381 I`). A complex exponent
  now goes to one MPFR complex `exp`, as a real one already did.

Not changed: at a singular point the machine value is `Overflow[]` where the exact value is
a directed infinity (`ArcTan[1. I]`, `ArcTanh[1.]`).

Tests: `test_inverse_imag_cut_all_surfaces`, `test_mpfr_complex_inverse_accuracy` and
`test_mpfr_complex_forward_huge` in `tests/test_trig.c`.

## An abort no longer escapes its TimeConstrained through a stale Simplify memo (EVAL-abort-1, v0.6705)

An abort escaped every `TimeConstrained` and aborted a later computation that had no
time limit. `TimeConstrained[Integrate[f3, x], 30]` followed by an unconstrained
`CheckAbort[Integrate[f7, x], ...]` (f7 = `1/(x (-1 + Log[x]) (2 + Log[x]^2)^(1/3))`)
reached the `CheckAbort` after 1.6 s instead of returning `Integrate::nonelem` after
about 12 s, in about one run in two under load.

- **The cause: a memo that outlived its Simplify.** `Simplify` pushes a
  Factor/Together/Cancel memo (`src/poly/facpoly_memo.inc`) and pops it on return. The
  stack depth was not a `TimeConstrained` unwind counter, so a timeout that
  siglongjmp'd out of a `Simplify` skipped the pop. The memo stayed active for the
  rest of the session, and every `Factor`, `Together` and `Cancel` anywhere read and
  grew it. In the reproduction the jump came from a 0.5 s DerivativeDivides candidate
  budget expiring inside ParallelMixedSpecial's 10 s budget. The memo also held entries
  stored while that timeout was in flight, when the evaluator answers every call with
  the `Abort[]` sentinel. A `Together` whose inner evaluation came back `Abort[]` had
  stored `Abort[] + ...`. Later, f7's `PolynomialExtendedGCD` (in ParallelMixed's
  `InvMod`) got `Abort[]` coefficients from that entry, and `PolynomialRemainder`
  evaluated them with no constraint left to own the abort.
- **Fix.** The memo-stack depth is registered with the unwind-counter registry, and the
  next push into a slot left by an abandoned `Simplify` frees that memo. A memo store
  is now skipped while an abort is due (`eval_abort_raised()` in `src/eval.h`: a
  tripped `TimeConstrained`/`MemoryConstrained` frame, or Ctrl-C outside
  `AbortProtect`). This applies to the Factor/Together/Cancel memo, the evaluator's
  numeric-call memo, the `Root` numericalisation cache (which already skipped
  interrupts), DSolve's autonomous-reduction caches and Integrate's fail-memo. The
  check is O(1), and the evaluator's common path is unchanged.
- **The sentinel no longer becomes data in two evaluator paths.** A head that
  evaluates to `Abort[]`, a `Throw` or a `Goto` now unwinds the call, as an argument
  always did. Before, `s["E"]` with `s` bound to an aborted value was the stable datum
  `Abort[]["E"]`, and `Catch[(Throw[7])[x]]` gave `Throw[7][x]` instead of `7`. An
  initializer of `Module`, `With` or `Block` that aborts now leaves the construct
  without running the body. Before, only a `Throw` did, and the body ran with the
  local bound to `Abort[]`.

The bug depends on timing. To verify the fix, the backstop was forced at every trip and
the candidate budget was swept: 5 escapes in about 66 runs before the fix, none in 60
after, and none in 20 unforced runs of the reproduction. Test:
`tests/test_abort_poison.c`, a new suite that unwinds a pushed memo with a real SIGPROF
jump and checks the store gate, the sentinel head and the initializers.

## Builtin audit, wave 1a: checked user-sized allocations; five crashes (v0.6708)

The 2026-11-14 builtin audit (`review/report.html`, `review/issues/`) confirmed 2,475
defects. `tools/audit_backlog.py` now re-runs every finding against the current binary,
one process each under a time limit and an RSS cap, and classifies it (`CRASH`, `HANG`,
`SAME`, `CHANGED`, `LIKELY-FIXED`) into `review/backlog_status.md`. On v0.6701, 1,924
findings still reproduced exactly, 442 had changed, 73 looked fixed and 36 crashed or hung.
Each fixed finding is pinned by a check in `tests/test_audit_<category>.c`, keyed by its
audit id (`tests/test_audit.h`; the suites are globbed into ctest as
`audit_<category>_tests`).

- **`src/checked_alloc.{c,h}`.** About 25 of the crashes have one shape: a builtin
  multiplies sizes the user chose and allocates the product unchecked. `mth_alloc_ok`
  refuses a negative count, a 64-bit overflow, more bytes than physical memory, or a
  request an active `MemoryConstrained` refuses. `mth_malloc_n` / `mth_calloc_n` check
  and allocate. `mth_nomem_abort` emits `General::nomem` through `mth_message` and
  returns the `Abort[]` sentinel, so the computation ends in `$Aborted` (catchable by
  `CheckAbort`), as in the reference.
- **CompleteGraph#1, StarGraph#1 (critical, high).** `CompleteGraph[10^7]` and
  `StarGraph[10^15]` segfaulted on an unchecked `calloc`. The families in
  `src/graph/generators.c` (`CompleteGraph`, `CycleGraph`, `PathGraph`, `StarGraph`,
  `RandomGraph`) now honour the documented resource limit (10^8 vertices or 5×10^7
  edges: left unevaluated) and allocate through the checked helper. Their 64-bit counts
  are `int64_t` rather than `long`, which is 32 bits on Windows.
- **UnitVector#1 (critical).** `UnitVector[WorkingPrecision -> MachinePrecision]` read
  past the argument array. It now reports `UnitVector::argt`. A length past memory aborts
  with `General::nomem`.
- **Series#1 (critical).** `Series[x, {x, 0, 2^62}]` sized its dense coefficient buffer
  unchecked. An order the machine cannot hold now aborts with `General::nomem`. A constant
  is still returned verbatim at any order.
- **NSeries#1 (critical).** An order of `2^31` wrapped to a negative `int`. Orders above
  `2^24` are refused with `NSeries::ivar`.
- **ComplexExpand#1 (critical).** The `TargetFunctions -> Conjugate` path moved its real
  and imaginary parts into `Plus[re, I im]` and then freed them again. The recycled nodes
  resurfaced inside the caller's expression (`Print[Print[Print[…`) before the kernel
  crashed.

Verified: the five suites (34 checks) pass, along with `graph_tests`, `series_tests`,
`nseries_tests`, `unit_vector_tests` and `complexexpand_tests`. Under valgrind every
fixed path leaves the same `definitely lost` total at 1× and 20× as `Print[1]`
(13,376 bytes / 418 blocks, the fixed start-up baseline), with no invalid reads or
writes. `make check-c99` and `make check-messages` pass.

## Builtin audit wave 1d: five special-function hangs at extreme parameters (v0.6712)

## Core semantics: pure functions, scoping, loop exits, level specs (v0.6719)

- **Function#1, Slot#1.** `#0` is the pure function itself, so `If[# == 0, 1, # #0[# - 1]] &[5]`
  is 120. **SlotSequence#1.** `##n` with no arguments left is the empty sequence.
- **Function#2-3, With#1-2.** Substitution is capture-avoiding. A nested Function, Module or
  With whose local occurs in a value being substituted is alpha-renamed first, so
  `Function[x, Function[y, x + y]][y]` is `Function[y$n, y + y$n]`. A Module or With that
  binds a parameter shadows it in its body but not in its initialisers. The check reads the
  binder summaries and fills them on an immutable value, so applying a function to a stored
  expression does not rescan it at every call.
- **Break#1, Continue#1.** Inside a Do, For or While body, `Break[]` and `Continue[]` are
  unwinds like `Throw`. They reach the nearest enclosing loop from anywhere in the body:
  a function argument, a Map, or a Select, Count or Sort predicate. Outside every loop they
  stay plain values.
- **While#1, For#3.** The compiled numeric loop no longer turns an exact variable inexact.
  An assigned variable that provably stays an integer runs on the int64 VM, beside the
  Real ones, and is written back as an Integer, so `y = y + 1` next to `x = x + 1.` stays
  compiled and exact. An int64 overflow, division or fractional constant bails to the
  interpreter before anything is written. Any other exact variable sends the loop to the
  interpreter.
- **Apply#1, Map#1.** One level-spec parser (`mth_level_spec_parse`) serves Map, Apply, Level,
  Cases, Count, Replace, MapIndexed, FreeQ and Position. `{m, Infinity}`, `All` and negative
  levels work everywhere, and a malformed spec reports `Head::level`.
- **PatternSequence#1** Condition on a PatternSequence matches. **Divide#2** `0./0.` is
  `Indeterminate`. **DivideBy#1, TimesBy#1, Decrement#1** evaluate a Part target once.
  **Abort#1** an abort inside NIntegrate's integrand (every rule, including MonteCarlo and
  WorkingPrecision) aborts instead of being dropped as a non-numeric sample.
  **ConditionalExpression#1** a NumericFunction lifts the condition out. **Inequality#1**
  malformed chains. **ReplaceAt#1, ReplacePart#1** the first matching rule wins.
  **TeXForm#1**. **For#2** is pinned under its own id.

## Assigning a value nested deep through its heads no longer overflows the stack (v0.6724)

## Nested NSum no longer differentiates at a sample point (v0.6726)

v0.6722 made `D` reject a numeric variable (`D::ivar`, audit D#1). That exposed NSum's
Euler-Maclaurin tail: after a failed evaluation of a derivative, or the contour fallback, the
summation variable could stay bound to a sample point, so the next symbolic derivative was
`D[f, 16.]`. Every nested NSum with a finite inner range, such as
`NSum[1/(i j)^2, {i, 1, Infinity}, {j, 1, 3}]`, flooded `D::ivar`, and `Check` took its failure
branch. The binding is now cleared before every round of symbolic derivatives. The values are
unchanged and agree with the closed forms (`Zeta[2] (1 + 1/4 + 1/9)`). Pinned in
`audit_numerical_calculus_tests`.

## Benchmark 261: compact integrals against FriCAS, Axiom, Maxima and sympy (benchmark, no version bump)

`benchmarks/261-compact-integrals-x4` follows benchmark 257. It has 1550 random
integrands, and the elementary antiderivative of each is more than four times the
integrand in LeafCount. They are weighted to the mixed algebraic and transcendental
classes: R 50, T 250, A 250, M 550, B 450. All of Mathilda's integrators run on them,
and FriCAS, Axiom, Maxima and sympy run as other systems. The run used v0.6714.

## Declared argument counts and option checks (v0.6728)

Audit Wave 3, shared fixes S12 and S13. A builtin can now declare its positional argument
count and whether it takes options (`symtab_set_arity`, `symtab_set_arity_opts`; the rows are

declare (Scan, Replace, the matrix predicates and Eigen heads, NSum, NDSolve, GroebnerBasis, ...).
Pinned by id in the `test_audit_*` suites.

## Indeterminate, infinities and poles in numeric functions (v0.6729)

Audit Wave 3, shared fix S11: one mechanism instead of per-head patches. The pass that already
looks for List arguments of a Listable call now also notes an `Indeterminate` or infinite
argument (`Infinity`, `ComplexInfinity`, `c Infinity`); the few non-Listable numeric heads get
one cheap scan. Only a flagged call reaches `src/infinity_rules.c`, which runs before UpValues,
DownValues and the builtin, so C and `.m` heads agree.

- A non-Flat NumericFunction with an `Indeterminate` argument is `Indeterminate` (`ReIm` and
  `QuotientRemainder` give a pair); `Min`/`Max` too.
- A table gives each head's value at `Infinity`, `-Infinity`, `I Infinity`, `-I Infinity`,
  `ComplexInfinity` and other directions: trig and hyperbolic functions and their inverses
  (branches read off the numerics at |z| = 10^8), Log, Bessel J/Y/K/I, ExpIntegralE,
  EllipticK/E, Erf/Erfc/Erfi (`Indeterminate` at `ComplexInfinity`, an essential singularity),
  `ArcTan[x, y]`, `FractionalPart` (`Interval[{0, 1}]`).
- Abs/Re/Im/ReIm/Sign/Arg/Conjugate act on the direction; Power raises it
  (`Sqrt[-Infinity]` is `I Infinity`); the classical polynomials take their leading term.
- An inexact call landing exactly on a pole (`Cot[0.]`, `ArcTanh[1.]`, `Factorial[-1.]`)
  answers the exact call's infinity instead of `Overflow[]`; a genuine overflow
  (`Exp[1.*^300]`) is still `Overflow[]`.
- `DirectedInfinity[z]` evaluates to the canonical infinity, `ComplexInfinity` absorbs any
  nonzero factor, a direction is kept as a unit number (`2 I Infinity` is `I Infinity`),
  `Pi Infinity` is `Infinity`, and `I Infinity - I Infinity` is `Indeterminate` (was `0`).
  `CountRoots[0, x]` is `Infinity`.

Numeric and Plus/Times loops are unchanged within noise; a symbolic Expand loop is about 2-3%
slower. 148 audit ids, pinned in the `test_audit_*` suites.

## Total takes Method -> "CompensatedSummation" (v0.6730)

`Total[list, Method -> m]` and `Total[list, n, Method -> m]` read the option instead of
handing it to the level-spec parser, which since v0.6727 printed a spurious `Total::level` for
it. `"CompensatedSummation"` sums a flat machine-real list, packed or not, with Kahan

## DerivativeDivides inverts an exponential kernel (v0.6731)

`Integrate[E^x Log[E^(2 x) - E^x - 1], x, Method -> "DerivativeDivides"]` came back
unevaluated. The kernel `u = E^x` divides `f`, but `f/u'` is `Log[E^(2 x) - E^x - 1]`, and
this still has `x` in it. That `x` only goes away when it is rewritten in terms of `u`.
`try_direct_kernel` now does that for an exponential kernel `E^(c x + d)`: it substitutes
`x -> (Log[u] - d)/c` and cancels, giving `Log[u^2 - u - 1]`. That reduces to
`-2 E^x + Sqrt[5] ArcTanh[(2 E^x - 1)/Sqrt[5]] - Log[...]/2 + E^x Log[...]`, which
PossibleZeroQ verifies.

The inverse is used only under an explicit `Method -> "DerivativeDivides"` (a depth counter,
`dd_explicit_depth`, registered as a TimeConstrained unwind counter), and only for
exponential kernels. Inverting a linear kernel changed answers on the cascade path. Inverting
a `Log` kernel turns `Log[-u] - Log[u]` into `I Pi`, a branch-dependent constant, and that
gave a wrong answer. New test `test_exponential_kernel_inverse` in
`tests/test_integrate_derivdivides.c`.

## DerivativeDivides: an inverse that only renames x is not a reduction (v0.6732)

The exponential inverse of v0.6731 applied to every `E^x` integrand. The reduced integral
goes back through the whole Integrate cascade, so any integrand in `x` and `E^x` that the
cascade could do came back as a "derivative-divides" close, with `x` merely renamed
`Log[u]`. An example is `(1 + 5 x^2 + E^x (1 + 4 x + x^2))/(2 Sqrt[E^x + x])`, which no
kernel divides. The Eliminate strategy did the same for a `Log[c x + d]` kernel, with
`x = E^u`.

Both are now declined:
- after the inverse substitution, a reduced integrand that still contains `Log[u]`;
- for a Log kernel, an Eliminate branch with an `E^(linear in u)` left in it.

`E^x Log[E^(2 x) - E^x - 1]` still reduces to `Log[u^2 - u - 1]`, and all 80 Integrate,
Risch, Trager and Bronstein suites pass. Two tests added to
`test_exponential_kernel_inverse`. This also corrects the easy-class test for
benchmark 262: there, "dd" means `Method -> "DerivativeDivides"` closes the integrand.
