# Changelog: week of 2026-11-09 (Mon) – 2026-11-15 (Sun)

## Bronstein completion campaign, Phase 4.1: the constant field Q(i) (v0.6551)

`Method -> "Bronstein"` and `Method -> "Trager"` now take Gaussian-rational
constants. Examples: `Sqrt[x + I]/x`, `E^(I x) Sqrt[1 + E^(I x)]`,
`1/Sqrt[E^(2 I x) + 1]`, `(1 + I) Sqrt[x]/(x^2 + I)`, `Sqrt[Log[x] + I]`.

`I` stays the literal CAS number, so Gaussian arithmetic reduces `I^2 = -1`
everywhere. Three rules keep every algebraic step a genuine field computation
over Q(i); the proof is theory E4.1 in `docs/notes/bronstein_extensions.tex`.
- **Factorisations over Q(i).** `tragerFactorList`, the flag `tragerConstantI`.
- **Residue fields contain i.** An absolute residue field is built with i
  adjoined, and `I` is written in its generator (`fieldSubI` in `kc`/`rc`;
  `brFieldOver`).
- **No reduction mod p with a literal `I`.** That case is refused
  (`TorsionModP:complex`), so positive-genus torsion over Q(i) stays undecided
  instead of being decided wrongly.

The coefficient tests (`constantFieldNumberQ`, `brRationalInQ`) accept Gaussian
rationals.

**Results.**
- A trial with the old declines lifted answered 2 of a 12-integral Q(i) battery.
  With this layer, 10 are answered and verified and 1 is certified
  (`Sqrt[Log[x] + I]`). FriCAS reports "implementation incomplete" on that one,
  so it was checked by hand through the Risch DE.
- Five examples were added to the gap log (35 in all), and there is a new suite
  `tests/test_bronstein_phase4.c`.

## Bronstein completion campaign, Phase 3 (part 4): the log part has no other weights (gap #12); fix: a false E6d certificate (v0.6550)

**Fix: a false certificate released in v0.6549 (E6d), found by the independent review.**
- **The case:** `(1 + E^(x^2))/2 + (1 - E^(x^2))/2 Sqrt[x + 1] Sqrt[1/(x + 1)]`,
  which equals 1, was certified not elementary.
- **The cause:** the sign `Sqrt[x + 1] Sqrt[1/(x + 1)]` is -1 only ON the branch
  cut `x < -1`, never on an open set, and the sign sampler used real points on
  that cut.
- **The fix:** `brSignPatternSeenQ` now counts a sample point only when every
  radicand is off its cut and the same signs hold at four perturbations of size
  `10^-6`. The example now ends `implementation incomplete`, and it is a
  regression test and gap-log entry E6d-5.

The review confirmed E6e and corrected its write-up: a consistent residue
convention, the weight-0 claim, and a weaker hypothesis that holds at the places
over `theta = 0`.


Gap #12 was a log part on an `m >= 3` curve whose derivative has nonzero
coordinates of weight `2..m-1`, where the norm formula for the weight-1
projection would not apply. It is now a theorem that this cannot happen, so the
site is an internal consistency check. The proof is new theory: E6e in
`docs/notes/bronstein_extensions.tex`.
- The residues of `c Y` are `sigma`-equivariant (`sigma: Y -> zeta Y`), so every
  weight-`k` projection of the log part has zero residue at every place.
- A combination of logarithmic derivatives with zero residues vanishes on a
  reduced curve (Rosenlicht).

**Changes.**
- A nonzero coordinate is now an Internal invariant violation
  (`LogPartWeightNonzero`), not a missing branch.
- The exception is the special places of an exponential. There Bronstein's
  residues (Def 2.21) are not ordinary residues, so `LogPart:weights` stays,
  narrowed to that case.
- Empirically the site never fired: not in 120 random `m = 3, 4` components,
  nor in the 36 integrands of E3.

## Bronstein completion campaign, Phase 3 (part 3): one radical up to a sign (v0.6549)

Radicals that equal one radical only up to a locally constant sign are now
decided, for example `Sqrt[x^2 (x + 1)]` beside `Sqrt[x + 1]`, where
`Sqrt[x^2 (x + 1)] = eps x Sqrt[x + 1]` with `eps = +-1`. The proof is new
theory: E6d in `docs/notes/bronstein_extensions.tex`.

- **The rewrite.** Each radical becomes `eps_j R_j y^k_j`, with
  `eps_j = Prod Sqrt[b_i]/s_j` an explicit expression whose values are `+-1`
  (`brCompositumPlan[.., True]`, `brRationalSquareRoot`).
- **The integral.** For each sign pattern the integrand lies in one radical
  extension. The answer is `Sum_s Prod_j (1 + s_j eps_j)/2 F_s`, valid on every
  region (`brIntegrateSignSplit`).
- **Certificates.** A non-elementary pattern is a certificate when a sample point
  shows it (`brSignPatternSeenQ`). Example: `Sqrt[x^2 (x^3 + 1)] - x Sqrt[x^3 + 1]`
  is 0 where `eps = 1` and `-2 x Sqrt[x^3 + 1]` (elliptic) where `eps = -1`.
- **What remains.** Units other than `+-1` (`Sqrt[2 x]` beside `Sqrt[x]`, or a
  root of unity of higher order) need constant field extensions (Phase 4), and
  stay `Tower:cyclic-compositum`.
- The gap log has four new examples (E6d-1 to E6d-4), 29 in all.

## Bronstein completion campaign, Phase 3 (part 2): residues at special places on curves of positive genus (v0.6548)

`Method -> "Bronstein"` now decides components whose log part has residues at
the special places (`theta = infinity` for a logarithm, `theta = 0, infinity`
for an exponential) on a curve of positive genus. This was gap #9
(`LogPart:special-genus`). In a sweep of 36 integrands on `m >= 3` curves it
was the most frequent stop, at 6 of 36.

- **The divisor.** It is written on the shifted frame `theta = a + 1/u`, where
  the special places are finite (`brSpecialDivisorIdeal`). The construction is
  field-generic, so the same code builds it over k, over Q, and over GF(p).
- **The order.** x and the lower monomials are specialised to rationals that
  keep the shape of the curve and of every place, special ones included
  (`brSpecialLocationsGoodQ`). Trager's two-prime method then runs, with the
  special places' good-reduction conditions as a new `goodQ` hook in
  `goodPrime` (`brSpecialTorsionOrder`).
- **The decision.** Specialisation is injective on torsion in characteristic 0,
  so the order found is the only possible one, and `N delta` is tested for
  principality over k. A non-principal `N delta` is a certificate. The proof
  is new theory: E3 in `docs/notes/bronstein_extensions.tex`.
- **Examples.** `Int 1/(x (1 + Log[x]^3)^(1/3))`, `Int E^x/(1 + E^(3 x))^(1/3)`
  and derivatives such as `D[Log[Log[x] + (1 + Log[x] + Log[x]^3)^(1/3)]]` now
  integrate, verified. `(1 + Log[x])/(x (1 + Log[x] + Log[x]^3)^(1/3))` is
  certified non-elementary: its divisor has infinite order.
- **Fix: inconsistent special-place data.** It used to send the prime search
  into an endless loop. The characteristic-0 non-vanishing of every `goodQ`
  condition is now checked first, and a failure throws Internal.
- **Found in review before release.** The first version could issue a false
  certificate. A specialisation that put a residue place on the frame point
  `theta = a` dropped that place from the divisor (`brFrameDegreesKeptQ`). Two
  guards were added:
  - the specialised divisor must have degree 0 (`brFrameIdealDegree`), or the
    result is an Internal error;
  - a curve with no frame root of multiplicity prime to m reports
    `TorsionModP:frame-root` instead of searching primes forever.
- The ratchet loses `LogPart:special-genus` (38 sites).

## Bronstein completion campaign, Phase 3 (part 1): front-end rewrites; fix: a wrong answer for logarithms of powers (v0.6547)

`Method -> "Bronstein"` now decides three classes of integrand it used to report
as `Integrate::incomplete`. Each rewrite is a pointwise identity. The proofs are
new theory, in `docs/notes/bronstein_extensions.tex` (E6a–E6c). That LaTeX note
replaces the earlier Markdown one and holds all the campaign's theory.

- **A cyclic radical compositum is one radical** (`brCompositumPlan`,
  `brCompositumRewrite`, `brCompositumBack`).
  - Example: `Sqrt[x] (x + 1)^(1/3)` is integrated over `y^6 = x^3 (x + 1)^2`,
    with `y = Sqrt[x] (x + 1)^(1/3)` itself.
  - A search over exponents finds a product of the user's radicals that
    generates the field, using only `rho^d = b`. The answer is valid on every
    branch, and Chebyshev's non-elementary cases are now certified.
- **Exponents with a rational log part** (`brSplitLogExponent`,
  `brPruneMonomials`). `E^(x + Log[x]/2) = Sqrt[x] E^x` and
  `E^(Log[a]/3) = a^(1/3)`. A monomial the rewrite leaves unused is dropped from
  the tower.
- **Reducible logarithms** (`brReducibleLogSplit`, `brIntegrateKappaSplit`).
  - `Log[a] = L0 + kappa`, with `kappa` a locally constant branch difference. The
    integrand is expanded in `kappa` and each coefficient integrated.
  - On each region `kappa` is 0 or transcendental (Hermite–Lindemann), so a
    non-elementary coefficient is a certificate (theorem E6a).
- **Fix: a wrong answer.** `Integrate`RischTranscendental[Log[x^2]/x, x]`
  returned `Log[x]^2`, which is wrong for `x < 0`.
  - Cause: its dependent-logarithm retry expanded `Log[x^2] -> 2 Log[x]` and
    called that value-preserving.
  - The retry's answer is now checked by `PossibleZeroQ` against the original
    integrand. The integral is left unevaluated there; the Automatic cascade
    still answers `Log[x^2]^2/4`.
  - Real identities (`Log[2 x] = Log[2] + Log[x]`) still go through.
- `MATHILDA_RDE_CERT_DEBUG=1` traces every uncertain step of the C Risch DE to
  stderr (a developer aid for the completeness ratchet).
- New suite `tests/test_bronstein_phase3.c`. The ratchet loses
  `Tower:exp-of-log-radical`.

## Bronstein completion campaign, Phase 2 (part 1): the tower Risch DE decides its cancellation cases (v0.6546)

The C Risch DE (`src/calculus/integrate_risch_rde.c`) under
`Risch\`RischDECertified`, which the Bronstein package and
`Risch\`ElementaryIntegralQ` rest on, now decides cases it used to leave undecided.
Derivations: `docs/notes/bronstein_extensions.tex`, Part A.

- **WeakNormalizer over a tower** (Bronstein §6.1). A normal simple pole with a
  positive-integer residue is removed instead of making every later "no"
  uncertain; one implementation serves the base field and the tower. Integer
  residues are read off linear factors over Q, with no `Solve`.
- **ParametricLogarithmicDerivative, exact** (`rc_param_log_deriv`). It decides
  `f = m η + Dz/z` by one Q-span over the log-derivatives of the denominators'
  irreducible factors and the exponential monomials. In a valid tower these are
  independent, so the span is unique and integrality decides. Through it:
  `RdeSpecialDenomExp`, `RdeBoundDegreeExp`, the `deg b = deg a` case of
  `RdeBoundDegreePrim`, and the log-derivative branch of `PolyRischDECancel`
  (`q = w ∫ c/w`). With that branch ruled out, a failed top-down pass is a proof.
- **`RdeBoundDegreePrim`, `deg b = deg a − 1`:** limited integration against the
  one primitive, through the field integrator.
- **Soundness: dependent towers.** The tower builder checked exponentials only
  for rational multiples of one exponent. `E^(x + x^2)` beside `E^x` and
  `E^(x^2)` passed as a new monomial, so every "no solution" theorem's premise
  was false. The builder now runs the structure theorem on each Log/Exp monomial
  (`RtTower.dependent`); certificates and certified Risch DE answers on such a
  tower are demoted to undecided. Rewriting the relation into a valid tower is
  the next increment.
- **Bug caught by the property test:** the first `deg b = deg a` bound used
  `max(dc − da, s)`; it must be `max(dc − da + 1, s)`, because cancelling the top
  coefficient leaves degree `dc` reachable. That gave a false `NoSolution` for
  `y' + y/x = 2 Log[x]/x^2` (solution `Log[x]^2/x`).
- **Completeness ratchet:** the uncertain C paths went from 16 to 12.
- **Tests:** `bronstein_rde_tests` gains the decided cases and the dependent
  tower. A seeded property run of `RischDECertified[f, D[y] + f y]` over 195
  pairs gave 0 wrong answers and 0 false `NoSolution` (the field contains y).

## Bronstein completion campaign, Phases 0-1: soundness, "implementation incomplete" (v0.6545)

The campaign makes `Method -> "Bronstein"` a decision procedure for every integrand
in ONE simple radical extension of an elementary tower (plan:
`src/internal/bronstein/BRONSTEIN_PLAN.md`, "2026-11-14 -- Completion campaign").
These two phases make every stop honest before any branch is added.

- **Soundness: a CAS failure is never a certificate.**
  - `Risch\`ElementaryIntegralQ` read every NULL of the field Risch DE as a
    proof of non-elementarity, including one from a step that could not be
    computed (an extended gcd, the weak normaliser, an identity gate). Both
    routes (`rde_base`, `rde_tower`) now run inside a certification scope, and
    only an unmarked NULL raises the certificate; the mark propagates to an
    enclosing scope.
  - `Cos[E^x]`'s exponent `I E^x` reached the base-field solver in surface form,
    because the "free of the tower variables" test looked for the symbol `t0`, not
    `E^x`. The solver could not even form its equation, and its NULL was a
    certificate by accident. The test now reads the exponent in tower variables,
    and `Cos[E^x]` is decided (certified) by the tower solver.
  - `Risch\`RationalSpan`, `LogReducible`, `ExpReducible`, `TanReducible`,
    `ArcTanReducible` and `LogarithmicDerivativeOfRadical` returned `False` on a
    failure (a tower that does not decode, a linear solve that gave up, a
    non-rational particular solution). The Bronstein front end reads `False` as "a
    new monomial", a premise of its certificates. They now return `$Failed` there;
    `False` only as a proof.
- **"Implementation incomplete" (`Integrate::incomplete`).** A stop is now
  NonElementary, Incomplete (outside one simple radical extension), Unimplemented
  (inside it, a branch not written yet), Decline (non-elementary input, a resource
  bound) or Internal. Incomplete and Unimplemented return `{"implementation
  incomplete", reason}` and are reported by `Method -> "Bronstein"`/`"Trager"` and
  by the Automatic cascade when nothing answered
  (`Bronstein\`BronsteinScope[f, x]`); `Quiet` and `Check` work. Several radicals
  generating a cyclic Kummer group (`brKummerCyclicQ`) are one simple radical in
  disguise and count as in scope.
- **Retry on any non-proof.** Callers that try another construction after a
  `"Decline"` (`tragerRetryableQ`) now also do so after Incomplete/Unimplemented;
  `KummerIntegrate` no longer takes an `"implementation incomplete"` list for an
  answer.
- **Specialization search grows.** The torsion specialization points double in
  height every round (`brOption["SpecializationRounds"]` = 8) instead of cycling
  through a fixed set; exhausting the search is an Internal error, not a decline.
- **Completeness ratchet.** `make check-bronstein-completeness` (push-tier ctest
  `bronstein_completeness`) lists 40 in-scope unimplemented sites and 16 uncertain
  steps of the C Risch DE against a baseline that only moves down.
- **Tests:** `bronstein_completeness_tests` (62 assertions); the structure suite
  asserts the tri-state; the Bronstein suites assert the new classes.

## Integrate (#132): differentiation under the integral sign; no ComplexInfinity values (v0.6543)

- **Coefficient dropped terms.** DiffUnderInt's Gaussian, linear-decay and
  partial-fraction readers took an exponent or base apart by `Coefficient`
  without checking its degree: `E^(-2 nu x^2 - mu x^4)` was integrated as the
  Gaussian `E^(-2 nu x^2)` and the result was the first-order Taylor polynomial
  `(16 nu^2 - 3 mu) Sqrt[Pi]/(32 Sqrt[2] nu^(5/2))`; a quartic denominator was
  read as a quadratic (`Sqrt[2]`s in `Integrate[x ArcTan[q x]/(p^2 + x^2)^2, ...]`).
  Each reader now requires the expected polynomial degree, and Apart's constant
  cofactor is split from the base. Fixes 3.469.1, 3.472.2, 4.535.11.
- **Derivative bases at a boundary.** Repeated quadrature took `I'(0)` as the
  integral of `D[f, b]` at `b = 0` (identically 0) although `I'(b) ->
  Pi/(a g^4)` as `b -> 0+`. It now takes the one-sided limit of `J_j(p)`, and
  when that is out of reach confirms the result against a quadrature at a
  generic point. Fixes 4.295.26.
- **A package loop variable captured the user's `g`.** `MeijerConvolution`Split`
  iterated `Do[..., {g, fs}]` with a global `g`; an integrand with a parameter
  `g` recursed to `$RecursionLimit` (a 40 s timeout in the cascade). Fixes
  4.295.25 (and the timeout of 4.295.26).
- **ComplexInfinity husks.** A definite result containing `ComplexInfinity` or
  `Indeterminate` (the G&R record `-Pi (AngerJ[-nu, beta] - BesselJ[-nu, beta])
  Csc[nu Pi]` at integer `nu`, Mathar M7296) is declined, at the stage and after
  the final assumption-aware Refine.

## Integrate (#132): periodic Abs, coinciding frequencies, region-respecting anchors, real Log branch points (v0.6542)

- **Abs of a periodic function on a half-line was dropped.** The piecewise stage
  could not enumerate the infinite root family of `Sin[5 x/8]` on `[0, Infinity)`,
  kept no breakpoints and resolved `Abs` at one test point:

  Fixes 17.13.34, 17.13.35.
- **Refine with an irrelevant fact.** `Refine[Pi/a > 0, a > 0 && s > 0]` stayed
  unevaluated: the entailment went to one multivariate Reduce that a
  transcendental coefficient defeats. The conjunction is now split into groups
  sharing no variable, decided separately.
- **Frequencies that may coincide.** The Meijer-G convolution of two oscillating
  kernels returned `Piecewise[{{G12, q < 1}, {G21, q > 1}}]` with an implicit
  default of 0 at `q = 1`; `Integrate[Sin[a x] Sin[b x]/x^2, {x, 0, Infinity},
  Assumptions -> 0 < a <= b]` was 0 at `a = b`. The boundary `q = 1` is solved for
  a parameter (a branch whose substituted assumptions are satisfiable) and
  integrated as its own integral; a boundary that does not close is excluded by
  `ConditionalExpression[..., q != 1]`. Fixes 3.741.3a, 3.828.2, 6.693.1a,

- **Differentiation under the integral sign anchored outside the region.** The
  base `a = 0` passed `a`'s own bound under `0 < xi < 2 a`, but is in the closure
  of no point of that region; the back-integration crossed `a = xi/2`, where
  the derivative changes formula. A base must lie in the closure of the region

- **A real Log argument changing sign.** Newton-Leibniz now splits at the real
  zeros of `Log`/`Arg`/fractional-power arguments; Limit expands `Ei`/`Ci`/`Chi`
  at a zero of their argument (`Ei` with the log term real on both sides), and

- **Tests:** `test_integrate_gr132.c` (`test_branch_structure`); `test_series.c`

- **One-sided limits at a branch cut.** `Limit[ArcTan[-1, y], y -> 0,
  Direction -> "FromBelow"]` was `Pi` (`-Pi`); `Limit[Log[-1 + I y], ...]` was
  `I Pi` (`-I Pi`). New first Limit layer: a `Log`, `Arg`, fractional power or
  two-argument `ArcTan` on its cut at the point, approached by a non-real
  argument, is replaced by the branch continuous on the approach side (the
  side sign from the leading term of `Im g`); two-sided limits must agree.
- **Antiderivatives that jump.** Newton-Leibniz split only at the integrand's
  poles, so `F(b) - F(a)` telescoped across the 2 Pi jump of
  `ArcTan[Cos[2 x], -Sin[2 x]]` at `Pi/2`. The zeros of `v` of every
  `ArcTan[u, v]` in `F` (of `Im g` for a non-real `Log`/power argument) are now
  breakpoints, evaluated by one-sided limits, as are finite endpoints of such
  an `F`. Fixes 3.613.8a, 4.397.11.
- **Residue families: parameters of undetermined sign.** The closure was read
  at one representative point and returned for the whole region, so
  `Element[xi, Reals]` gave the `xi < 0` answer everywhere. The region is now cut
  into the sign orthants of each real parameter whose sign is open; per-orthant
  forms are verified at two generic points each and assembled into a
  `Piecewise` (conditions minimised; a non-closing boundary `p = 0` is cut out by
  `ConditionalExpression`). Fixes 3.354.5, 17.23.14, 17.23.15.
- **Tests:** `tests/test_integrate_gr132.c` (new; every fixed row of #132 at
  every instantiation, plus the rows already fixed earlier).

### Lean integration: adversarial-review soundness fixes (branch v0.6528)

- **Unsupported Sum iterators are no longer "proved".** A claim
  `Sum[t, {k, a, u}] == C` whose upper limit `u` is not a variable
  (`{k, 1, n - 2}`, `{k, 1, n + 1}`, `{k, 1, 2 n}`, `{k, n, 2 n}`) fell to the
  identity generator, which evaluated both sides, so Lean proved a tautology
  of Mathilda's own `Sum` output with no trusted step:

  (false at `n = 0`), and `Certify[Sum[k, {k, 1, n + 1}] == (n + 1) (n + 2)/2]`
  gave `Verified -> True`. Any held `Sum` in an `Equal` the closed-form sum
  generator does not take is now `Certify::nocert`; `Conjecture` reports
  `"Tested"`. The same root cause gave `Refute` wrong witnesses (`n -> -3`
  where the claim holds, `n -> -1/2`); those claims are now refused too.
- **Every verdict key is authenticated.** `"Status"`, `"Refuted"`,
  `"Counterexample"` and `"RefutationTheorem"` of an edited or hand-made

- **A claim variable `K`** no longer collides with the generic type variable
  of ordered-field / char-0 statements (`Refute[ForAll[K, K^2 >= K + 1/10]]`
  failed in Lean): the type variable is `K1`, `K2`, ... when the claim uses
  `K`, and the refutation's `(K1 := Rat)` follows it.
- **Assumptions:** `Refute`'s Domain/Assumptions restriction splits only a
  top-level `And`; `Not[y > 0 && y > 1/2]` was rewritten into `Not[{...}]`

### Conjecture; Fibonacci sum certificates (branch v0.6527)

loop. `expr` is evaluated exactly at 12 fit points and 40 test points
(`"FitPoints"`, `"TestPoints"`, `"Start"`); the guessers are `"Polynomial"`

### Certify: counterexamples and kernel-checked refutations; Refute (branch v0.6526)

### Certify: the Exact engine and multi-engine consensus (branch v0.6525)

## Lean 4 integration: Certify, ProofObject, LeanVerify, LeanForm and the lean/ tactics (v0.6536; PR #134, developed as v0.6514–v0.6523)

Mathilda can hand its answers to the Lean 4 kernel for checking, and Lean can
ask Mathilda for certificates. Mathilda is the untrusted oracle and the Lean
kernel is the judge. Reference:
[`builtins/formal-verification.md`](../builtins/formal-verification.md); design,
trust base and hardening history: `docs/design/lean_integration.md`.

**Mathilda → Lean**
- **`Certify[comp]`**, **`Certify[a == b]`**, **`Certify[{c1, c2, …}]`** and
  **`ProofObject[comp, opts]`** compute a result, write a self-contained Lean 4
  file stating it as a theorem with a proof script, and run Lean on it. The
  result is a `ProofObject` with `"Verified"`, `"Theorem"`, `"LeanTheorem"`,
  `"LeanSource"`, `"Certificate"`, `"Hypotheses"`, `"TrustedSteps"`,
  `"Axioms"`, `"Logs"`, `"Timing"`, `"Cached"`, … . `"Verified" -> True` means
  Lean accepted the theorem with no `sorry`, only the standard axioms
  (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`), and a kernel replay of the
  compiled file in a fresh process confirmed it. `"TrustedSteps"` lists what
  Lean did not check; Mathilda's arithmetic normalisation of the claim
  (Plus / Times / Power canonical form) is also part of the trust base.
- **Certificate kinds** (Tier 0, core Lean ≥ 4.35, no Mathlib):
  polynomial and rational-function identities (`Expand`, `Together`, `Apart`,
  `Cancel`, `Simplify`, `FullSimplify`, `Collect`; `Factor` / `FactorTerms`
  with the irreducibility trusted step); antiderivatives of `f ∈ ℚ(x)` built
  from rational parts, `Log`, `ArcTan`, `ArcTanh` and quadratic surds (the
  cleared chain-rule identity), and claims `Integrate[f, x] == F`, certified
  as `D[F, x] == f`; `PolynomialExtendedGCD` (Bezout); `PolynomialReduce` and
  `Implies[p1 == q1 && …, p == q]` (ideal membership); `Solve` (completeness
  `lhs = rhs ↔ x = r1 ∨ …` for a univariate polynomial that splits over ℚ,
  soundness otherwise); `Inverse`, `Det`, `LinearSolve`, `NullSpace` over ℚ;
  `ForAll[vars, p >= q]` (sum of squares, with hypotheses); `Minimize` /
  `Maximize` (KKT multipliers plus SOS; the LP dual certificate);
  `FactorInteger` and `PrimeQ` (Pratt certificates; Lucas's theorem is the
  trusted step); `Sum[t, {k, a, n}]` and `Sum[t, {k, n}]` with polynomial or
  polynomial·c^k terms (induction).
- **Tier 1** (opt-in, a Lake project with Mathlib via `$LeanProject` /
  `MATHILDA_LEAN_PROJECT`): `Certify[Integrate[f, x], Tier -> 1]` proves
  `∀ x : ℝ, hyps → HasDerivAt (fun x => F) f x`. A statement whose hypotheses
  have no common real witness point under `Real.log` semantics is refused as
  vacuous (`Certify::vacuous`).
- **Claims are certified as written.** `Certify` is `HoldAll`; the claim is
  walked held. Only arithmetic, relations and connectives are kept, and
  value-preserving rational-function heads are read through. A computation
  inside a claim (`Simplify`, `Refine`, `Evaluate`, a user function, …) is
  `Certify::eval`; a symbol with a value or an evaluation-changing attribute
  is `Certify::value`. Hypotheses are stated over every field of
  characteristic 0, or every ordered field when every variable is real.
- **Options**: `"Engine"` (`"Lean"`, or `"Mathilda"`, a heuristic check that
  never reports `True`), `"Tier"`, `"Domain"` (`Automatic`, `Complexes`,
  `Reals`, `Rationals`, `Integers`, each with one stated meaning), `Method` (a
  plain tactic script, checked), `TimeConstraint` (mapped to `maxHeartbeats`),
  `Assumptions`, `"Verify"` (`False` generates the source only),
  `"LeanExecutable"`, `"LeanProject"`. Option names are matched by their
  text: `Engine -> v` and `"Engine" -> v` are the same option, and the package
  creates no option-name symbols, so a user's `Engine`, `Domain`, `Verify`, …
  stay ordinary `Global`` symbols. Unknown names are `Certify::optx`.
- **Batches and caching.** A batch runs Lean once per tier, with a verdict per
  item; verdicts are cached for the session by theorem text.
- **Authenticated results.** `obj["Verified"]` reports a verdict only for an
  object `Certify` produced in this session.

**Raw Lean and printing**
- **`LeanVerify[src]`** checks a Lean 4 source string and returns
  `"Success"`, the messages, the axioms and the kernel-replay result. It runs
  only sources without user-defined code: a minimal lexical surface (no

## Integrate (Bronstein): the log part one Galois orbit at a time (v0.6534)

- **The problem.** Bronstein's log part built the field of *all* residues. For
  `P(z) = Q(z^3)`, with Q quadratic, that is a degree-36 splitting field, and
  four benchmark-257 integrals took more than 200 s each:
  - `(2 E^(2x) (1 + E^(2x)) (2 + E^(2x))^(1/3))/(2 + E^(2x) + E^(4x))`;
  - and three like it.
- **The fix.** `brOrbitLogPart` now works one Galois orbit at a time, over
  `Q(γ)` (degree 6 here). This is Trager's per-orbit construction.
  - The orbit divisor is `m Δ(γ) − κ (θ − c)₀`.
  - The automorphism `Y → ωY` maps its places over the roots of a polynomial
    defined over `Q(γ)`, so the norm needs no root of unity.
  - The answer is a `RootSum` over q.
  - An orbit divisor of infinite order is not a certificate; the old
    construction decides then.
- **Results.**
  - Those four integrals now take 0.3–0.7 s.
  - Benchmark 257: Automatic closes 1000/1000 (was 996), Bronstein 419/450 (was
    409).
  - There are no wrong answers, false certificates or AUTO-GAP cases.

## Sum / Product: poles and zeros on the range (soundness campaign, Phase 1) (v0.6531)

- **Sum: a pole inside the range is no longer summed over.** Before the
  closed-form cascade, the dispatcher reads the summand's integer poles off the
  linear factors of its denominator; every integer pole of a rational function is
  one of these. With a concrete integer lower bound:
  - a pole the range reaches makes the sum that term's value, `ComplexInfinity`
    with its `Power::infy` message;
  - a pole that a symbolic upper bound may or may not reach leaves the `Sum` held,
    as the reference does.

  Fourteen wrong answers of this kind are gone, among them
  `Sum[1/k^2, {k, 0, Infinity}] = Pi^2/6`,
  `Sum[1/(k - 3)^2, {k, 1, Infinity}] = 5/4 + Pi^2/6`,
  `Sum[(-1)^k/(k - 3), {k, 1, Infinity}] = -1/2 + Log[2]` and
  `Sum[1/(k (k + 1)), {k, -1, n}] = -1 - 1/(1 + n)`. The scan costs under 1 ms on
  a degree-9 rational summand.
- **`Product`Telescoping` declines a zero or pole on the range.** Then
  `Product`Rational` gives the Pochhammer form, which is right for every `n`.
  - `Product[(k - 3)/(k + 1), {k, 1, n}]` was `0` and is
    `Pochhammer[-2, n]/Pochhammer[2, n]`.
  - `Product[k/(k - 2), {k, 1, n}]` was `Indeterminate` and is
    `n!/Pochhammer[-1, n]`.
- **`Sum`Hypergeometric` treats a `Binomial` with a negative-integer lower index
  as zero when looking for the first term.** `Binomial[n, -2]` stays symbolic, as
  in the reference, so the zero test also looks at the factorial expansion.
  `Sum[Binomial[n, k], {k, -2, n}]` was 0 at every `n` and is now `2^n`.
- **Two triage entries were not bugs.** Both were checked against the reference.
  - `Sum[k, {k}] = (k - 1) k/2`: `{c}`, with `c` the summand's own symbol, is the
    indefinite sum.
  - `Product[k, {k, 1/2, n}] = Pochhammer[1/2, 1/2 + n]`: a symbolic `n` is
    assumed to lie on `imin + Integers`.

  The oracle's `finite` kind gained an optional sampling offset for such bounds.
- **Oracle.** The baseline is down from 88 to 71. Five records were added: a pole
  on a 10^7-term range, a pole that a symbolic upper bound reaches, the
  half-integer sum, the `{n}` copy count, and the symbolic-lower-bound Hurwitz gap
  (baselined).

## Sum / Product: five silent wrong answers (soundness campaign, Phase 0.5) (v0.6530)

- **`Sum`Polynomial` at degree ≥ 20.** The `1/(k+1)!` weights were accumulated in
  a machine integer, which overflows from `21!` on. So `Sum[i^25, {i, 1, n}]` had a
  leading coefficient of `-3698160658676859375/374203567546` instead of `1/26`. The
  factorial is now exact.
- **Hypergeometric series cut off at an interior zero.**
  `Sum[(k - 3)^2/k!, {k, 0, Infinity}]` was `27/2`, the three terms before the zero;
  it is now `5 E`.
  - Cause: a nonpositive-integer upper parameter is accepted as termination only
    when the summand vanishes from there on, as `Binomial[5, k]` does. Otherwise the
    head is summed explicitly and the series restarts after the zero.
  - A finite symbolic range with such a zero now stays unevaluated instead of being
    truncated.
- **Gamma ratios that are `Indeterminate` at every integer bound.**
  `Sum[Binomial[n, k]/Binomial[2 n, k], {k, 0, n}]` is now `(1 + 2 n)/(1 + n)`. The
  balanced-pole regularisation, previously applied only for a symbolic lower bound,
  now also runs for a symbolic upper bound. Dixon-type forms with half-integer
  slopes, such as `Gamma[1 - n/2]`, stay open for Phase 5.
- **`Product`QProduct` divided by zero.** A pure power factor `q^k` was read as
  `alpha + beta q^k` with `alpha = 0`. `Product[q^k (1 - q^k), {k, 1, n}]` gave
  `0^n QPochhammer[q ComplexInfinity, q, n]`; it is now
  `q^(n (1 + n)/2) QPochhammer[q, q, n]`.
- **`Product`EulerPrime` ignored the lower bound.**
  - `Product[1/(1 - Prime[k]^-2), {k, 2, Infinity}]` was `Pi^2/6`, the product over
    all primes; it is now `Pi^2/8`.
  - The `chi_4` body `(-1)^((p-1)/2)` is `I` at `p = 2`, so `Pi/4` is the product
    over the odd primes, and from `k = 1` the old `Pi/4` was also wrong. It is now
    `(1/5 + I/10) Pi`.
  - Each template now carries its base index, and any other lower bound divides
    out, or multiplies in, the leading factors.
- **Tests.** The oracle baseline shrinks from 95 to 88. Eight records now pass, and
  seven new regression records were added: the `chi_4` product from 1, 2 and 3, the
  zeta product from 4, Dobinski, a genuinely terminating sum, and the finite
  interior-zero decline, which is baselined. `product_stress_tests` (nightly) had
  failed since v0.6525. Its telescoping family built `h(k+1)` as `h /. k -> k + 1`,
  which is a no-op on a bound iterator, so the enumerated reference was 1. The
  shifted factor is now written out.

## Rules substitute into Table / Do / Sum / Product bodies as written (v0.6525)

`g[f_] := Sum[f, {k, 1, 3}]; g[1/k]` gave `3/k`; the reference gives `11/6`.
When a rule's right-hand side held one of the iterator heads, the pattern
substitution treated it as a lexical scoping construct and alpha-renamed the
iterator (`Sum[1/k, {k$2, 1, 3}]`), so the argument's `k` was never bound by
the sum. `Table`, `Do` and `Product` did the same (`h[f_] := Table[f, {k, 3}];
h[k^2]` was `{k^2, k^2, k^2}`, not `{1, 4, 9}`), and so did a delayed rule
applied by `ReplaceAll`.

- The iterator heads scope dynamically, as `Block` does, so they are no longer
  on the scoping-construct list in `src/modular.c` (-120 lines): rule

## NProduct: infinite products with factors near 1 are accurate; messages say NProduct (v0.6515)
- **Before:** `NProduct[1 + 1/k^2, {k, 10^6 + 1, Infinity}]` was `1 + 8e-11`
  (true `1 + 1e-6`), with `NSum::ncvg` and `NSum::accgl`. From `k = 1` the
  value was `3.6760778772` against `Sinh[Pi]/Pi = 3.6760779104`, with
  `NSum::accgl`. `1 - 2/k^2`, `(k^2 + 1)/k^2`, `Exp[1/k^2]` and
  `1 + 1/k^1.5` from `10^5` were wrong in the 8th to 3rd digit in the same way.
- **Cause:** NProduct sums `Log[f]`. For a factor `f = 1 + g` with small `g`,
  machine `Log[1 + g]` keeps only the digits of `g` that survive the addition,
  and it is an exact `0` once `g < 1e-16`. NSum's tail integral samples the
  summand up to eight decades past the head, where it saw a summand that stops.
  The tail model failed, and Wynn extrapolated a sequence that converges too
  slowly. The infinite NSum itself was fine: `NSum[1/k^2, {k, 10^6+1,
  Infinity}]`, `1/k^3` from `10^4`, `1/k^1.5` from `10^5`, `(-1)^k/k` from
  `10^6` and `Exp[-k/10^5]` all agree with their closed forms.
- **Now:** for an infinite range the summand is `2 ArcTanh[g/(2 + g)]`, which is
  exactly `Log[1 + g]` on the principal branch, with `g = f - 1` formed
  symbolically (`Together` when the `1` does not cancel by itself). The
  Euler–Maclaurin corrections differentiate the equal `Log[f]`. That is a new
  derivative twin passed to NSum through `nsum_eval_for_nproduct`. Without it,
  the ArcTanh derivatives balloon onto the contour, about 10x slower at
  `WorkingPrecision -> 30`. A factor `E^h` sums `h`. A factor of exactly `-1`,
  where the ArcTanh form is singular, can only be a leading one. The leading
  factors are probed, and those before the settled run are multiplied out

## HarmonicNumber: fast at large n, exact and numeric (v0.6514)
- **Before:** an integer `n <= 10^5` was expanded into an `n`-term `Plus` of

## Sum / Product: single-element iterators; Product evaluates each factor once; balanced multiplication (v0.6509)
- **`Sum[f, {n}]` and `Product[f, {n}]`** used to stay unevaluated. They now
  follow the reference (`iter_count_spec_for_sum` in `src/iter.c`, shared by
  both):
  - A real `n` means `Floor[n]` copies of the body, each evaluated separately.
    `Sum[a, {3}]` is `3 a`, `Product[a, {3}]` is `a^3`, and
    `Block[{k = 0}, Sum[k++, {4}]]` is `6`. `{Pi}` gives 3 copies, `{2.5}`
    gives 2, and `n <= 0` gives the empty sum or product.
  - A free symbol means the indefinite form. `Sum[n^2, {n}]` is
    `Sum[n^2, n]`, and `Product[a, {n}]` is `a^(-1 + n)`, the reference's
    indefinite-product convention.
  - Any other `n` (a string, `-n`, a non-real number) gives `::vloc`, or
    `::iterb` for a non-real number, and the call stays unevaluated.
  - A bare `Sum[f, 3]` without braces stays unevaluated, as in the reference.
  - The numeric case is rewritten to the range `{k, 1, n}` over a private dummy
    iterator, so it reuses the existing enumeration and closed forms.
    `Sum[a, {10^9}]` is `1000000000 a`; the reference gives `Sum::vloc` there.
- **Product evaluates each factor exactly once.** Before,
  `k = 0; Product[k++; 2, {i, 4}]` left `k == 3`: the compiled path's probe
  and the closed-form cascade both evaluated the body. Now the probe is reused
  as the first factor, and a range of at most 10^4 factors is enumerated before
  the cascade, as `Sum` already did up to 10^6 terms. The printed forms are
  unchanged.
- **Balanced multiplication.** `fold_times` multiplies the enumerated factors
  by binary splitting instead of in one flat `Times`, which was quadratic in
  the size of the running product. `Product[1 + 1/k^2, {k, 1, 9000}]` went from
  11.4 s to 0.046 s, with the same result.
- **Tests.** `tests/test_sum.c` and `tests/test_product.c` cover the
  single-element forms, side-effect counts, per-term `RandomInteger`
  evaluation, the messages under `Check`/`Quiet`, that the private dummy never
  leaks into an answer, and the 9000-factor timing.

## Integrate: complex-coefficient rational functions no longer give wrong answers (v0.6508)
- **The bug.** `Integrate[1/(x^2 + I), x]` and `Integrate[1/(x^2 - I), x]`
  were `0`. `1/(x^2 + I x + 1)`, `1/(x^3 + I)` and
  `(x + I)/(x^2 + 2 x + 2 + I)` returned wrong nonzero answers. Across 158
  random Gaussian-coefficient integrands, 53 were wrong. They are now all
  correct (checked numerically, including at a point off the real axis), with
  no timeouts.
- **Cause.** Rioboo's `LogToReal` is valid only over a real coefficient field:
  the conjugate of a residue must again be a residue, and
  `S(conj a, x) = conj S(a, x)`. With `I` in the integrand, its
  conjugate-pair branch cancelled against itself.
- **Fix.**
  - The rational path (`try_lrt_close`) no longer calls `LogToReal` for a
    complex-coefficient integrand. It uses the plain complex log sum instead:
    first the linear closer, then `NaiveLogPart` with explicit roots. So
    `Integrate[1/(x^2 + I), x]` is `-(2 ArcTanh[(2 x)/Sqrt[-4*I]])/Sqrt[-4*I]`.
  - `NaiveLogPart`'s root expansion now accepts symbolic parameters for a
    complex linear or quadratic factor, so `1/(x^2 + I a)` keeps a closed form.
  - The transcendental callers, which use `t = E^(I x)` for trig integrands,
    keep `LogToReal`. There `I` is part of the real reconstruction; the first
    version of this guard broke `RischTranscendental[1/(Sin[x] + Cos[x]), x]`.
- **Tests.** `tests/test_intrat.c` has `test_complex_coefficient_log_part`: the
  cases above, the symbolic-parameter case, and a seeded random
  Gaussian-coefficient family.
- **Known gap.** `N` does not yet evaluate `Root` objects with non-real
  coefficients. A held `RootSum` answer over an irreducible complex cubic is
  correct, but it cannot be checked numerically; for example,
  `N[Root[#^2 + I &, 1]]` stays unevaluated.

## Together, Cancel, Simplify: nested constant radicals among the coefficients (v0.6507, D2)
- A 4639-leaf rational function of `x` over `Q(I, Sqrt[2], q)`, `q = Sqrt[3/4 - 1/Sqrt[2]]`
  (from issue #133, `tasks/polygcd_together_deficiencies/`), ran past 20 s in
  `Together`, `Cancel` and `Simplify`, with or without `Extension -> Automatic`. It
  is `1 + Sqrt[2] x`. Now:
  - `Together[e, Extension -> Automatic]` and `Cancel[e, Extension -> Automatic]`
    are `1 + Sqrt[2] x` in 9 ms;
  - `Simplify[e]` and `FullSimplify[e]` are `1 + Sqrt[2] x` in 0.08 s;
  - `Together[e]` takes 0.09 s (an equal-valued LeafCount-163 form that keeps `q`).
- **Cause.** The generator model reads every radical as an indeterminate tied to
  its radicand. A nested radical's relation has algebraic coefficients and may be
  reducible (`q = 1/Sqrt[2] - 1/2`), so the relations among `I`, `Sqrt[2]`, `q` and
  `q^3` were invisible and the multivariate gcds in `cancel_recursive` swelled.
- **Fix** (`src/poly/algfield.c`), gated on one linear walk for a radical with a
  constant, non-rational algebraic base:
  - *Default*: once the fast exact paths decline, just before the classical
    fallback, each nested base takes its Together form and each remaining nested
    radical is an opaque generator `g = b^(1/m)`, reduced modulo `g^m - b`. There
    is no denesting, as in the reference and in Maxima's `ratsimp` and FriCAS's
    `normalize`. A call with no symbolic variable keeps its former path.
  - *`Extension -> Automatic`*: the computation runs over the number field of the
    coefficients (one qqbar primitive element). The answer is written over a basis
    of radical monomials in the atoms, simplest first, so a denestable radical drops
    out: `Together[(x^2 - q^2)/(x - q), Extension -> Automatic]` is
    `(-1 + Sqrt[2] + 2 x)/2`, `Together[(x^2 - 1 - Sqrt[2])/(x - Sqrt[1 + Sqrt[2]]),
    Extension -> Automatic]` is `Sqrt[1 + Sqrt[2]] + x`.
  - *Simplify* takes that field normal form when it scores lower.
- Independent of the v0.6505 `PolynomialGCD` fix: after it, `Together` on this
  input still timed out in the pipeline around ~5300 gcds; the paths here never
  issue them.
- Changed expectations (value-equal): `Together[1/(5 + 2 Sqrt[6])^(3/2) + (Sqrt[2] -
  Sqrt[3])^3, Extension -> Automatic]` is `0`, and `Integrate[1/(x^2 - 3 - 2 Sqrt[2]), x]`
  is `(1 - Sqrt[2]) ArcTanh[(-1 + Sqrt[2]) x]`, the reference's form denested.
- Reference comparison table (Wolfram Language 14.0, Maxima 5.49.0, FriCAS 1.3.6):
  `tasks/polygcd_together_deficiencies/README.md`. Tests: `field_together_cancel_tests`
  (fixture `tests/together_nested_radical_d2.m`).

## Integrate: algebraic constants in a rational antiderivative are in normal form (v0.6506)
- The rational integrator's log part computes with algebraic constants and
  nothing rationalises them, so answers carried unreduced quotients:
  `Integrate[1/(x^2 + 3 + 2 Sqrt[2]), x]` was
  `(4 ArcTan[(3 x + 2 Sqrt[2] x)/(7 + 5 Sqrt[2])])/(12 + 8 Sqrt[2]) + (4 Sqrt[2] ArcTan[(3 x + 2 Sqrt[2] x)/(7 + 5 Sqrt[2])])/(12 + 8 Sqrt[2])`
  (leaf count 82) and is now `(-1 + Sqrt[2]) ArcTan[(-1 + Sqrt[2]) x]` (18).
- **The pass** (`intsimp_reduce_algebraic_constants`, run last in
  `BronsteinRational` before the sign normalisation) does three things:
  - It writes each `Log`/`ArcTan`/`ArcTanh` argument as a polynomial in x, or
    as a ratio with a monic denominator.
  - It puts each coefficient in normal form, and merges terms that share a
    kernel.
  - In a quadratic field the normal form is `RootReduce`'s. Past degree 2
    `RootReduce` answers a `Root` object, so the pass converts that back with
    `ToRadicals` and denests the quadratic surds.
- **Examples:**

  | Integrand | Before | After |
  |---|---|---|
  | `1/(x^2 + 5 - 2 Sqrt[6])` | leaf count 99 | `(Sqrt[2] + Sqrt[3]) ArcTan[(Sqrt[2] + Sqrt[3]) x]` |
  | `1/(x^2 + 1 + Sqrt[2])` | leaf count 74 | `Sqrt[-1 + Sqrt[2]] ArcTan[Sqrt[-1 + Sqrt[2]] x]` |
  | `1/(x^2 - 3 - 2 Sqrt[2])` | leaf count 78 | `(-1 + Sqrt[2]) ArcTanh[(1 - Sqrt[2]) x]` |
  | `(x^2 + x + 1)/(x^4 + 1)` (#133) | 4 `ArcTan` terms | `(1/2 + 1/Sqrt[2]) ArcTan[-1 + Sqrt[2] x] + (-1/2 + 1/Sqrt[2]) ArcTan[1 + Sqrt[2] x]` |

- **Safety.**
  - Every rewrite is an exact identity.
  - A coefficient is replaced only by a radical (never `Root`) form that is no
    larger, and the answer is kept only if it does not grow.
  - Rational, symbolic and inexact coefficients are untouched.
- **Cost.** About 2% on the 108-case biquadratic-quartic sweep. `Simplify` per
  constant finds the same forms but doubled that sweep's time.
- **Tests.** `tests/test_intrat.c`:
  - `test_algebraic_constant_normal_form` pins the forms above. Each is also
    certified by `PossibleZeroQ` on the derivative.
  - The same test has guards showing rational, symbolic and inexact
    coefficients stay unchanged.
  - The #133 tests' nested-radical checks now use `Power[_Plus, _Rational]`,
    so a radical in a denominator is seen.
- **Known gap (pre-existing, not changed here).** `Integrate[1/(x^2 + I), x]`
  and `Integrate[1/(x^2 - I), x]` return `0`, a wrong answer. Complex
  coefficients reach `LogToReal`, which assumes real ones. The v0.6503 code
  gives the same answers.

## PolynomialGCD: dependent algebraic atoms no longer give a gcd of 1 (v0.6505)
- With `q = Sqrt[3/4 - 1/Sqrt[2]]` and `d1 = 7 + 5 Sqrt[2] - 4 x q`,
  `PolynomialGCD[Expand[d1 (3 - Sqrt[2] x + q^3)], Expand[d1^2]]` was `1`, with or
  without `Extension -> Automatic`. That is a wrong answer. It is now
  `-17/2 - 6 Sqrt[2] + x`, which is `d1` up to a unit. Results are also right for
  powers of one nested radical (`x - q^3`), for a denestable `Sqrt[3 + 2 Sqrt[2]]`,
  and for the non-denestable `Sqrt[1 + Sqrt[2]]` with a planted factor. Each of
  these was `1` before.
- **Cause.** The certified number-field gcd engine (`flint_field_gcd`) declined
  every univariate input up front. The univariate engines that ran instead take
  only `Sqrt[n]` and roots of unity, so they declined too. The classical path then
  treated `q`, `q^3`, `(3/4 - 1/Sqrt[2])^2` and `Sqrt[2]` as independent
  indeterminates. `Expand` applies only some of the relations between these atoms,
  so the common factor was invisible.
- **Fix.** A univariate input now goes to the field engine when its atoms are
  dependent, meaning the product of their degrees exceeds the degree of the field
  they generate. The verdict is cached with the field images. Inputs with a single
  atom or with independent atoms, and all rational inputs, keep their existing
  route. The gate covers `PolynomialGCD` and every caller of `poly_gcd_internal`
  (`Factor`, `PolynomialLCM`, `Cancel`/`Together`).
- **Speed.** The engine's prime scan re-factored `M mod p` for every scanned prime
  on every call, which was about 3/4 of the cost on small operands. Split counts
  are now memoised per minimal polynomial. A univariate gcd over
  `{2^(1/3), Sqrt[2], 2^(5/6)}` went from 1.7 ms (old classical path) to 1.35 ms
  (certified field path).
- **Leak.** Each `Extension -> Automatic` call over a nested radical leaked the expanded
  radicand in `qa_resolve_nested_radical`, because `evaluate` borrows its argument and the
  expanded tree was never freed. It is now freed.
- **Not fixed.** `Together` on the 4639-leaf input of
  `tasks/polygcd_together_deficiencies/` (D2) still runs past 20 s. The gcds are
  no longer the bottleneck; see that README.
## Evaluator: Module-local reclamation inside a long dispatch frame is linear (v0.6504)

- **The intermittent `dsolve_tests` timeout (CI since v0.6494).** When a Module
  local is reclaimed, it is removed from the dependency log that backs the
  dependency-recorded fixed points. That removal (`eval_dep_forget`) scanned the
  whole log. The log lives as long as the outermost dispatch frame, so a long
  computation under one frame that reclaims n locals cost O(n²): a rule
  condition reclaiming 80 000 locals took 1.42 s, against 0.22 s now (doubling
  ratio 3.6, now 2.2). A DSolve cascade is such a computation. With a log grown
  by the ~100 earlier tests, `t_m26_general_forcing` spent 93% of its time in
  this scan and passed the suite's 400 s alarm whenever the machine was loaded.
  That is why the hang depended on state and timing. Six parallel `dsolve_tests`
  runs, which all timed out before, now pass in 152 s each.
- **How removal works now.** Each log entry links to the previous entry for the
  same symbol. A symbol is logged once per nested frame that reads it while its
  mark is tentative. Removal follows only that symbol's chain and checks every
  link, so stale links left from an earlier log are ignored.
- **A latent use-after-free, fixed by the same change.** A binding bump on a
  committed symbol resets its mark to 0 while its log entries are still live.
  Reclamation removed a local from the log only when its mark was nonzero, so
  such a local was freed with its entries still in the log, and the next commit
  or release read the freed node. Removal is now unconditional, which the chain
  makes cheap.
- Regression test: `test_dep_log_reclaim_linear` in
  `tests/test_eval_timestamps.c`, a doubling-ratio gate (< 3.0).

## Integrate: a biquadratic Rothstein-Trager resultant no longer hangs (v0.6503, issue #133)
- `Integrate[(x^2 + x + 1)/(x^4 + 1), x]` never returned, by default or with
  `Method -> "BronsteinRational"`; each summand alone took milliseconds. It now
  takes ~5 ms:
  `1/2 ArcTan[(-Sqrt[2] + 2 x)/Sqrt[2]] + ArcTan[(-Sqrt[2] + 2 x)/Sqrt[2]]/Sqrt[2] - 1/2 ArcTan[(Sqrt[2] + 2 x)/Sqrt[2]] + ArcTan[(Sqrt[2] + 2 x)/Sqrt[2]]/Sqrt[2]`.
  All four residues `±I (Sqrt[2] ± 1)/4` are purely imaginary, so the answer has
  no `Log` terms.
- **Cause.** `intrat_primitive` (the rational integrator's `p / content[p, x]`)
  used `Expand[p/c]`. When the content `c` is a polynomial in the residue variable
  `t` (here `16 t^2 + 64 t^3`, the content of the degree-1 subresultant), `Expand`
  only distributes `1/c` and leaves every term an uncancelled fraction in `t`. The
  log-part polynomial `S(t, x)` therefore reached `LogToReal` as a rational
  function of `t`. There the nested-radical residues `±I Sqrt[3/4 ∓ 1/Sqrt[2]]`
  were substituted into its denominators, and `Together` / `PolynomialGCD` over
  that field did not finish.
  - Other numerators over `x^4 + 1` have a numeric content, which explains why
    they worked.
  - It now divides coefficient-wise with `Cancel` when the content is not a
    number.
- **Denesting.** `intsimp_pos_sqrt` (the integrator's positive-branch `Sqrt`)
  rewrites `Sqrt[a + b Sqrt[c]]`, for rationals `a > 0`, `b` and an integer
  `c > 1`, as `Sqrt[(a + d)/2] + Sign[b] Sqrt[(a - d)/2]` when
  `d = Sqrt[a^2 - b^2 c]` is rational. So `Sqrt[3/4 - 1/Sqrt[2]]` becomes
  `1/Sqrt[2] - 1/2`, and the biquadratic branch's answers live in `Q(Sqrt[2])`
  instead of carrying `Sqrt[(4 + 3 Sqrt[2])/Sqrt[2]]` towers.
- **Deficiencies recorded, not fixed.**
  - `PolynomialGCD` returns `1` for a non-trivial gcd over a nested-radical field
    (a wrong answer).
  - `Together` / `Simplify` do not finish on the rational function the old
    integrator built.
  - Both are documented under their builtins, with reproducers in
    `tasks/polygcd_together_deficiencies/`.
- **Tests.** `tests/test_intrat.c` `test_issue133_biquadratic_resultant`: four
  biquadratic-resultant numerators over `x^4 + 1`, each under `TimeConstrained`.
  Each answer is checked by `PossibleZeroQ` of the derivative residual and must be
  free of `Sqrt[_Plus]`. Also checked: `BronsteinRational` agrees with the default
  route, and the integral over `(-Infinity, Infinity)` is `Sqrt[2] Pi`.
- **Sweep tests** (follow-up, no behaviour change). Seven more `intrat_tests`
  cases cover the bug class, which is a subresultant content that is a polynomial
  in `t`. They were chosen from a 15,500-case sweep: 30 denominators, numerators
  with small coefficients, both routes. All of it is clean now. Against the
  pre-fix code the same sweep flagged only the `x^4 + 1` family.
  - The full 80-numerator `x^4 + 1` grid, by both routes.
  - A 108-case slice over twelve other quartics.
  - Hermite, mixed-factor, sextic, octic and quintic denominators.
  - Parametric numerators and denominators.
  - Two direct checks: `IntRationalLogPart`'s `S_i` are polynomials in `t` and
    `x`, and `Integrate`Helpers`Primitive` divides a polynomial content exactly.
    The pre-fix code fails the first on 13 of 25 integrands, including some it

  - Quadratic-surd denesting through `Integrate`, plus a non-denestable case

  - The suite runs in about 7 s, and every call is under `TimeConstrained`.

## DSolve: the condition fit no longer integrates with respect to a number (v0.6502)
- The shared constant fit `dsolve_fit_constants` evaluates the general solution
  at each condition point by substituting `x -> x0`. That also rewrote the
  integration variable of every unevaluated indefinite integral, so
  `DSolve[{y''[t] + 4 y[t] == f[t], y[0] == 0, y'[0] == 0}, y, t]` returned
  `Integrate[(-I/4) f[0], 0]`, an integral with respect to `0`.
  - The test that covered it checked only the ODE residual. Those terms are
    constants times homogeneous solutions, so the residual vanished while the
    conditions were never met.
  - Individual methods had worked around the hazard by declining or by building
    the convolution themselves. Any method without such a guard reached the fit
    with an indefinite integral.
- The fit now **rebases** each unevaluated `Integrate[g(x), x]` at the first
  condition point, as `Integrate[g(K), {K, x0, x}]`. That changes the
  antiderivative only by a constant, which the `C[k]` absorb, and the answer
  becomes the Duhamel form `Integrate[G(t, K) f(K), {K, 0, t}]`. Conditions at
  other points (a BVP) evaluate the rebased integrals there.
- While the fit and the real-form pass run, the rebased integrals are inert, over
  a private dummy. They are activated once afterwards, and only those: a method's
  own deliberate `Inactive[Integrate]` stays as it is. Active integrals made the
  real-form pass re-run the definite-integration cascade on every rewrite, so a
  forced `y'' + y == f` IVP or BVP took 15–30 s once earlier calls had loaded
  that cascade's later stages.
- `tests/test_dsolve.c` (`t_m26_general_forcing`) now also checks the
  conditions: zero and nonzero initial values, and a BVP with conditions at two
  points.

## DSolve: no integral with respect to an expression (v0.6499)
- `DSolve`FirstOrderSubstitution`'s implicit entry substituted `W -> y + r x`
  into an unevaluated `Integrate[g(W), W]`. That produced
  `Integrate[1/(1 + f[x + y[x]]), x + y[x]]`, an integral with respect to an
  expression, which has no meaning and which D cannot differentiate.
  - The ODE `y' == f[a x + b y + c]` was affected, and so was every PDE built on
    its first integral: `w_x + a Sin[x + λ y]^k w_y == 0` and its siblings,
    pde-615 … pde-804, nine corpus records scored FAIL.
- The antiderivative is now the definite inert integral
  `Inactive[Integrate][g(K), {K, 1, y + r x}]`. Its lower limit is absorbed by
  `C[1]`, and D applies the Leibniz rule to it. The dummy is a symbol, because D
  does not yet handle an indexed `K[1]` (tasks/todo.md).

## DSolve: forced wave and heat equations no longer get the homogeneous answer (v0.6498)
- `DSolve`WaveDAlembert` and `DSolve`HeatKernel` read the coefficients of the
  terms they name (`u_tt`, `u_xx`, `u_x`, `u`, …) but never checked that nothing
  else was left. So a forcing term or a higher space derivative was silently
  dropped:
  - `u_tt == 4 u_xx + Cos[t]` got the homogeneous d'Alembert solution;
  - `u_t == u_xx + Sin[x]` and `u_t == u_xx + u_xxxx` got the heat kernel.
- Neither method's verifier could see the problem. d'Alembert rebuilds its formula
  with test data, and the heat method checks the kernel, not the user's PDE.
- Both now require the residual to be exactly the model operator, and decline
  otherwise. The PDE corpus found these wrong answers (pde-381, pde-391). The
  forced problems are W7 (Duhamel) in `DSOLVE_PDE_PLAN.md`.

## D: the one-element derivative spec `D[f, {x}]` (v0.6497)
- `D[f, {x}]` is the first derivative, `D[f, x]`. It used to emit `D::dvar` and
  stay unevaluated, which made `DSolve` see a non-equation. The PDE-in-CAS corpus
  (below) writes its PDEs this way throughout, starting with record 1,
  `D[u[x, t], {t}] + D[u[x, t], {x}] == 0`. `{x, y, z}` and `{}` are still
  malformed specs. The test is `test_single_element_spec` in
  `tests/test_deriv_array.c`.

## DSolve: a PDE initial-value layer (v0.6496)
- **Parsing.** `DSolve[{...}, u[x, t], {x, t}]` now parses. A dependent
  function applied to all the independent variables used to fail, so every PDE
  method declined on the usual spelling. `{x, y, t}` is read as three variables
  when the unknown is applied to them; `{x, xmin, xmax}` is still a range.
- **Heat kernel.** For concrete initial data, `DSolve`HeatKernel` now evaluates
  its convolution, under τ > 0 and a real space variable, with a 10 s budget:
  - `u[x,0] == E^(-x^2)` gives `E^(-x^2/(1 + 4 t))/Sqrt[1 + 4 t]`;
  - `Sin[x]` data gives `E^-t Sin[x]`.
- **New `DSolve`PDEInitialValue`** (src/calculus/dsolve_pde_ivp.c), in the PDE
  cascade before the d'Alembert and heat-kernel runners. It solves an
  evolution PDE with initial data u(x, t0) = u0 (and u_t for second order in
  t) by an invariant family of the data. Substituting the family, the spatial
  dependence must cancel, leaving an ODE in t for the family's parameters:
  - **amplitude θ(t) u0(x):** the Riemann simple wave gives `x/(1 + t)`;
    Taylor–Green gives `2 E^(-2 nu t) Sin[x] Sin[y]`;
  - **shift u0(x − c t):** c is solved in the moving coordinate, or read from
    u_t(x, 0) when the equation is second order in t. This covers KdV,
    Fisher–KPP (c = 5/√6), Burgers with tanh data and the sine-Gordon kink;
  - **exponential polynomial c0 Exp[Σ θ_m(t) x^m]:** the monomials come from
    the data's exponent, and the coefficient groups are solved as a
    triangular chain of scalar IVPs. This covers the heat and Schrödinger
    Gaussians, the 2-D heat equation and Lamb–Oseen (the radial 1/r term).
- **Half-line boundary problems.** With no assumptions, the parameters and
  variables are taken positive (the generic, well-posed case):
  - **similarity reduction** u = F((y − y0)/√(t − t0)) for constant data, with
    F(0) from the boundary value and F(∞) from the initial value. Stokes I
    gives `U - U Erf[y/(2 Sqrt[nu] Sqrt[t])]`;
  - **time-harmonic boundary data,** the periodic steady state
    Re[e^(iωt) F(y)] with the decaying branch. Stokes II gives
    `U Cos[omega t - y Sqrt[omega/(2 nu)]] E^(-y Sqrt[omega/(2 nu)])`.
- **Liouville's equation** `a u_xy + b E^(m u) == 0` has the general solution
  `(1/m) Log[2 C[1]'[x] C[2]'[y]/(lam m (C[1][x] + C[2][y])^2)]`, lam = −b/a.
- **Verification.** Every answer is back-substituted into the PDE and every
  condition. A condition at a point where the answer is singular (Stokes I at
  t = 0) is checked as the one-sided limit.
- **ODE cascade.** A second-order equation missing y is now reduced by
  ReductionOfOrder before SpecialFunctionForm: `F'' + s F'/(2 nu) == 0` gives
  `C[2] + C[1] Sqrt[Pi] Erf[...]`, not ₁F₁ forms.
- **Benchmark checker.** An arbitrary function's derivative (Liouville's
  `C[1]'[x]`) is sampled as the sample function's derivative.
- **Benchmark:** all 13 PDE problems are solved.

## Radical denesting, Root objects in radicals, Gauss periods (v0.6494)
All three are native C (`src/simp/simp_kummer.c`, `functionexpand.c`).
- **Simplify denests radicals over their Kummer field.**
  - **The test.** K is the field Q(p_i^(1/m_i)) of the radicals inside r,
    with the monomials as a basis. A radical r^(1/n), r > 0, denests exactly
    when r^(1/n) = c^(1/n) u with c ∈ Q and u ∈ K.
  - **Candidates for c.** These are the products of the generator primes and
    of 2, 3, 5 dividing n·∏m_i, with exponents below n. They are pruned by
    the norm condition v_p(N(r)) ≡ D·v_p(c) (mod n).
  - **Finding and proving u.** For each surviving c, u is an integer relation
    (FLINT `fmpz_lll`) between its 320-bit value and the basis values. It is
    then proved exactly, as c uⁿ = r in rational arithmetic on the basis.
  - **Output form.** One rational times one integer radical per term. A
    second LLL on {(B, u B)} offers a short quotient A/B, with the common
    monomial cancelled, when that has fewer leaves.
  - **Results** (1.5–35 ms each):
    - `(2^(1/3) - 1)^(1/3) = (3^(1/3) - 6^(1/3) + 12^(1/3))/3`
    - `Sqrt[5^(1/3) - 2^(2/3)] = (2^(1/3) - 5^(2/3) + 20^(1/3))/3`
    - `Sqrt[28^(1/3) - 3] = (-1 - 28^(1/3) + 98^(1/3))/3`
    - `(7 20^(1/3) - 19)^(1/6) = (45^(1/3) - 18^(1/3))/3`
    - `((3 + 2 5^(1/4))/(3 - 2 5^(1/4)))^(1/4) = (1 + 5^(1/4))/(5^(1/4) - 1)`
  - **Scope.** Only pure radical constants are touched; the result is
    Simplify's answer, under any assumptions.
- **Root objects in radicals.** For `Root[q(x^m) &, k]` with q of degree 2,
  or a cubic that the Tschirnhaus shift makes binomial, Simplify returns the
  radical form, matched to the Root's value. Ramanujan's
  `Cos[2 Pi/7]^(1/3) - (-Cos[4 Pi/7])^(1/3) - (-Cos[8 Pi/7])^(1/3)` is
  `-(-5/2 + 3/2 7^(1/3))^(1/3)`.
- **FunctionExpand: Gauss periods.** Cos[a Pi/p] and Cos[a Pi/(2p)] are given
  in radicals for the Fermat primes 17 and 257.
  - **The descent.** The periods halve through the cyclic Galois group. Each
    child is (η_parent ± √Δ)/2, where Δ = (η_a − η_b)² is an integer
    combination of the parent level's periods, read off by counting exponent
    sums, so no radicals are multiplied.
  - **Normalisation.** Each radicand is cleared to integer coefficients by a
    square.
  - **Results.** `FunctionExpand[Cos[2 Pi/17]]` is Gauss's form exactly
    (77 leaves). The 257-gon takes 0.2 s.
- Benchmark: all five Ramanujan denestings, the cosine cube roots and the
  heptadecagon are solved.

## Famous-problem triage, phase 2: summation theorems, Gamma limits, LaplaceTransform (v0.6491)
All the stages below are native C. The matching (parameter roles, factor
classes, linear coefficients) is structural and exact. Builtins such as
Expand, D, Series, Integrate and Factor are called only for genuine sub-problems.
The v0.6490 stages that were written as embedded templates (the Lambert-W peel in
Solve, the DSolve singular-point fit, the pFq shift pair, the Integrate exponent
split) were rewritten the same way. Their outputs are unchanged.
- **HypergeometricPFQ at z = ±1: the classical theorems.** Gauss 2F1(1),
  Kummer 2F1(−1), Pfaff–Saalschütz (terminating, including a symbolic degree −n),
  Dixon 3F2(1), and Dougall's very-well-poised 5F4(1). Parameters are matched up
  to permutation by exact comparison of the linear relations, O(p²q) differences.
  The previous template ran p!·q! `Simplify` calls and mis-assigned Kummer's pair
  when the upper parameters were given as `{b, a}`. A numeric parameter set
  outside the region of convergence is declined: the all-½ Dougall series gave
  `2 ComplexInfinity/Pi^2` and now stays unevaluated.
- **Sum.**
  - Pochhammer terms are rewritten as Gamma.
  - A symbolic upper bound must terminate the series.
  - A symbolic lower bound is shifted to 0.
  - The |z| = 1 convergence gate is z = 1: σ < −1; otherwise σ < 0.
  - Gamma pole pairs from the bounds are regularised by reflection. The sign
    exponent is reduced mod 2, so Dixon's `Sum[(-1)^k Binomial[2n, n+k]^3, {k, -n, n}]`
    is `(3n)!/(n!)^3`, where v0.6490 printed `(-1)^(2n)`.
  - New stage ``Sum`Lambert``: `Sum[n^s/(E^(2 Pi n) - 1), {n, 1, Infinity}]` for
    s ≡ 1 (mod 4) is B_{s+1}/(2(s+1)) (Eisenstein series at τ = i), and
    1/24 − 1/(8π) for s = 1.
- **Product.** ``Product`RationalInfiniteReal`` accepts all-real roots after the
  limit route: Wallis `Product[4k^2/(4k^2 - 1), {k, 1, Infinity}] = Pi/2`.
- **Limit.** Factorial, Pochhammer and Binomial of the limit variable are
  rewritten as Gamma ratios and the limit is re-run. This covers Stirling,
  Lalescu (1/e) and Gauss's `n! n^z/Pochhammer[z, n+1] → Gamma[z]`.
- **MinimalPolynomial** of Cos, Sin and Tan of a rational multiple of π, through
  T_q(t) = (−1)^p and the factor that vanishes: Wantzel's 8x³ − 6x − 1 and the
  heptagon cubic.
- **Simplify** folds an inverse-trig combination that is a nonzero rational
  multiple of π (Machin, Størmer). The candidate p/q (q ≤ 360) comes from the
  machine value and is folded only when the existing inverse-function decider
  *proves* the difference zero.
- **FunctionExpand.**
  - Li₂ at the four golden-ratio points (Landen).
  - EllipticK at the singular moduli k₃² and k₂² and their complements
    (Chowla–Selberg).
  - Each table is screened against a double value, then checked exactly with
    `RootReduce`. `EllipticK[(Sqrt[2] - 1)^2]` (a `Power` argument) is now
    recognised too.
- **Integrate.** ∫ over [0, π/2] (also [0, π] and [0, 2π]) of
  c (A + B Sin²)^(±1/2) is EllipticK/EllipticE in m = −B/A, for A > 0. This
  covers Gauss's lemniscate AGM integral and the ellipse perimeter. A constant
  factor is now pulled out of the recogniser's input.
- **LaplaceTransform** (new). The algorithm:
  - Linearity, and threading over lists.
  - The exponential shift E^(at+b) g → E^b G(s − a).
  - A t-free remainder becomes c/s.
  - Otherwise the defining integral under s > 0.
  Lipschitz: `LaplaceTransform[BesselJ[0, t], t, s] = 1/Sqrt[1 + s^2]`.
- **Reduce with Lambert breakpoints.** Since v0.6490 Solve gives the exact real
  roots of `E^x == 3 x` (−W₀(−1/3), −W₋₁(−1/3)), and `Reduce[E^x > 3 x, x, Reals]`
  had stopped answering. Two fixes:
  - The reality test now knows `ProductLog[k, u]`: W₀ is real for u ≥ −1/e,
    W₋₁ for −1/e ≤ u < 0, and the other branches never.
  - The sign diagram confirms a residual within tolerance of 0 at an exact
    breakpoint with the exact zero test.
  The answer is now `x < -ProductLog[-1/3] || x > -ProductLog[-1, -1/3]`.
- **Greater/Less on exact operands** that are numerically within 1e-10 of each
  other check the exact zero test before committing to an order.
  `E^w - 3 w > 0` at w = −W(−1/3) was `True` (the machine residue is ~1e-35); it is
  now `False`, as Equal already decided.
- **ProductLog** accepts an integer-valued inexact branch index only in a wholly

  `ProductLog::bidx`, restoring the pre-v0.6490 behaviour.

## Build: MacPorts dependencies are detected
- The makefile and the CMake test build now find libraries installed with
  MacPorts. The prefix comes from the `port` command on `PATH` (usually
  `/opt/local`), or from an explicit `MACPORTS=<prefix>`. Its `include`, `lib`
  and `lib/pkgconfig` directories are added to every dependency probe
  (gmp/mpfr, GMP-ECM, SuiteSparse, ARPACK, and the pkg-config probes for
  FLINT, PCRE2, FFTW and Raylib).
- MacPorts' GNU Readline is used in preference to Apple's libedit shim.
- The MacPorts GCC names `gcc-mp-16` … `gcc-mp-13` are tried after Homebrew's
  `gcc-NN`.
- When both are installed, the one that provides GMP owns the build, so two
  GMPs never meet in one link. If Homebrew has `gmp`, MacPorts is not
  auto-detected, and existing Homebrew builds get exactly the same flags as
  before. If Homebrew lacks `gmp`, MacPorts is added. (A first version of this
  change ignored MacPorts whenever any `brew` existed. That broke a
  MacPorts-based Mac which also had a Homebrew holding only fftw.)
  Mathilda installs its own GMP allocator (`mp_set_memory_functions`), which
  only one copy of GMP would get, so mixing the two is not merely untidy.
- MacPorts is never skipped silently. When it is installed but ignored because
  Homebrew provides GMP, `make` prints a notice with the switch to use it
  instead: `make HOMEBREW= SYSTEM_PREFIX=`. On an Intel Mac Homebrew is
  `/usr/local`, which is also `SYSTEM_PREFIX`, so both must be cleared.
  `MACPORTS=` (empty) opts out and silences the notice. No version bump (build
  tooling only).

## AppellF1, AppellF2, AppellF3, AppellF4 (v0.6485)
- New heads (Listable, NumericFunction, Protected; docstrings; spec entry in
  `special-functions.md`): the four Appell functions of two variables, in the
  reference system's argument order. Files `src/special_functions/appell.c`
  (builtins, exact rules, machine kernel, the expansion planner) and
  `appell_arb.c` (ball arithmetic), `flint_num_appell` / `flint_appell_d` on the
  shared precision ladder.
- Exact rules: `x = 0`, `y = 0` and vanishing parameters to `Hypergeometric2F1`;
  F1 also `x = y`, `a = c`, `c = b1 + b2`, `x = 1`, `y = 1`, `a = 0` and the
  polynomial for a negative integer `a`. `D` in `x` and `y` by the
  contiguous-parameter formulas; parameter derivatives stay inert.
- Numerics: one outer series whose terms carry the analytically continued Gauss
  function of the other variable; F1's five Euler substitutions (two valid on one
  side of an extra ray only, the sector test checked against the integral) and
  F2's three Pfaff transformations pick the smallest outer ratio; the inner
  functions by a three-term recurrence in `m` in its stable direction (four
  recurrences, derived and checked numerically), one by one when its roots are
  close. F1 with no convergent expansion is its Euler integral (endpoint series
  plus `acb_calc_integrate`), cuts from below.
- Surfaces: n-ary ND kernels (scalar == packed == `NDArray` == `Compile[]` scalar
  and rank 1, real and complex, bit for bit); rows in the arbitrary-precision
  compile registry; `compile_coverage.py` shapes and precision bodies. All of
  `check-c99`, `check-messages`, `check-packed-aware`, `check-array-exactness`,
  `check-nd-surfaces`, `check-compile-coverage` (both passes) green.
- Found on the way: FLINT 3.6's `acb_hypgeom_2f1` returns an indeterminate ball for
  `2F1(-0.0721, 1.15; 102.33; 0.514 - 0.614 i)` (a large `c` inside the unit disk);
  the Appell start values therefore use their own direct series there. Complex
  ball products wrap (up to √2 a step), so long products run on midpoints with an
  explicit bound. mpmath's `appellf3`/`appellf4` lose digits near the region

  value is 1.0248673980), and the reference system's F1 at `(10., -20.)` is wrong

- Accuracy vs mpmath (400 points): machine median 0.4-1.0 ulp, max 5-45 ulp; 30
  digits within `1.5*10^-35`. Timings: scalar 3-5 µs (wolframscript 0.9-7 ms,
  mpmath 1.5-6 ms); 50 digits 0.5-1.1 ms (wolframscript 1.5-5.2 ms, mpmath
  8-23 ms); 10^6 packed 1.3-5.4 s (F3 38 s on its arb route). Open: F2/F3/F4

- Tests: `tests/test_appell.c` (7 groups; 28 reference points, reductions,

## RandomChoice: batched draws (v0.6468)
- The packed `RandomChoice[list, n]` / `RandomChoice[w -> list, n]` producer

## NSum: step-function and digit-length summands (v0.6467)
- A summand defined through `Round[n]` (or `Floor`/`Ceiling`) is recognised as a
  step function of the index and no longer goes to Euler–Maclaurin:
  `NSum[h[n], {n, 1, Infinity}]` with `h[n_?NumericQ] := 1/Round[n]^2` is now
  π²/6 to machine precision (was 1.62533), `Σ 1/(n IntegerLength[n]^2)` is
  4.325970846720485 (was 3.32572, no warning).
- New integer-series engine (`src/numerical_calculus/nsum_blocks.c`) for
  integer-only summands: Levin u from the best start for a smooth head; for a
  head smooth on geometric blocks `b^(k-1) <= n < b^k` (digit/bit-length
  summands) the sum becomes the series of block sums, each by Euler–Maclaurin
  in `log n` over interpolated integer samples, summed recursively.  Exact to
  the target at machine precision and `WorkingPrecision -> 60` for
  `1/(n IntegerLength[n]^2)`, `1/(n BitLength[n]^2)`, `1/(n IntegerLength[n]!)`,
  `(-1)^BitLength[n]/(n BitLength[n])`, `1/f(n)` with `f(n) = n^2 f(BitLength[n])`.
- Structures nested past any reachable index return with an `NSum::accgl`
  error that brackets the true sum instead of a confident wrong value.

## Fold / FoldList: compiled rows, vector states and exact folds (v0.6464)
- The automatic compiled fold loop now covers four more shapes. The result
  is bit-identical to the interpreter's in each case.
  - **Elements that are rows**, read through constant parts (`#2[[j]]`). On
    10⁶ pairs, `FoldList[#2[[1]] + #2[[2]]/#1 &, 1., pairs]` takes 34 ms
    (was 1.33 s).
  - **Exact rows with a `Real` accumulator**, when every value the body
    computes carries the accumulator.
  - **Vector accumulators** `{x1, ..., xA}` with a `List` body. In doubles, a
    pair state over 10⁶ steps takes 55 ms (was 1.67 s), and the history is a
    packed `(n+1) × A` array.
  - **Exact-integer folds**, scalar or vector, in GMP. A pair recurrence over
    10⁴ steps reaching 4,739 digits takes 3.1 ms (was 33 ms).
- SIAM D10 (random-recurrence constants) moves from 3–13× slower than scipy
  to faster than it at machine precision, and within 1.3× at 50 digits.

## GroebnerBasis: Method -> "F4", Method -> "Buchberger" (v0.6463)
- `Method -> "F4"` computes every basis over Q from F4 at each prime of the
  multi-modular routes, skipping the direct run.
  - The primes are combined by CRT and rational reconstruction, then
    certified.
  - If F4 does not cover the case (for example
    `CoefficientDomain -> Integers` or `MonomialOrder -> EliminationOrder`),
    you get the Buchberger basis with a `GroebnerBasis::f4na` note.
- `Method -> "Buchberger"` now switches F4 off. Before, it ran the same
  engines as `Automatic`.
- `Automatic` is unchanged: F4 for every basis over GF(p), Buchberger
  elsewhere.
- The docstring now names all four methods and states that F5 is not
  implemented. It also corrects two stale claims, about the default method
  and about `Modulus` falling back to the rational basis.

## Element: exact constants in Integers, Rationals, Algebraics (v0.6461)
- `Element[x, dom]` for an exact constant `x` and `dom` one of `Integers`,
  `Rationals` or `Algebraics`:
  - **Algebraic constants** decide from their canonical algebraic (qqbar)
    form. `Element[Sqrt[2], Rationals]` is `False`;
    `Element[(1 + Sqrt[2])^2 - 2 Sqrt[2], Integers]` is `True`.
  - **One-atom transcendentals** are `False` in all three domains: any
    `c0 + c1 T^k` with algebraic coefficients and `T` one of `Pi`, `E`,
    `Log[r]` or `E^r`. Examples: `Element[Log[2], Rationals]`,
    `Element[1 + 3 Pi^2, Algebraics]`.
  - **Anything else** is left unevaluated, because no proof is implemented:
    `Pi + E`, `E Pi`, `EulerGamma`.
- This was found by SIAM holdout B (bd20 wp50), where a
  `Which[Element[Log[2], Rationals], ...]` branch never decided.

## D through Sum / Product; applied derivatives of built-ins; Bessel order derivative (v0.6471)
- **`D` threads over `Sum` / `Product`** (`deriv.c`). `D[Sum[x^k/k, {k, 1, n}], x]`

## Integrate: wrong answers from a missed square and from PossibleZeroQ sample aborts (v0.6473)
- **`Integrate[1/(Sqrt[b^2 + c^2] + b Cos[x] + c Sin[x]), x]` was `0`** (G&R
  2.558.4d), after `Power::infy` / `Infinity::indet`. Through `u = Tan[x/2]`
  the denominator is `(A - b) u^2 + 2 c u + A + b`, `A = Sqrt[b^2 + c^2]`, a
  perfect square only through `A^2 = b^2 + c^2`. Hermite reduction's gcd
  (`PolynomialGCD`, which treats `A` as a free variable) missed it, and
  Rothstein-Trager divided by the zero discriminant. With a symbolic radical in
  the coefficients the Hermite gcd is now Euclid's algorithm with every
  remainder put over a common denominator, so `Sqrt[p]^2 -> p` decides
  (`intrat.c`). The answer is `-2 Cos[x/2]/(c Cos[x/2] + (Sqrt[b^2 + c^2] - b) Sin[x/2])`
  after Simplify, i.e. `-2/((A - b) Tan[x/2] + c)`.
- **PossibleZeroQ reported `True` when its first sample was uninformative**
  (`zero_test.c`). A point whose value is `Indeterminate` only by rounding
  (`Coth[63.9]` is exactly `1.0`, so `(-1 + Coth[x])^(3/2)` in a denominator is
  `1/0`) or an `Overflow[]` residue aborted the sampler to UNKNOWN, which the
  builtin reports as `True`. Both are now re-drawn like an IEEE overflow. That
  is how wrong antiderivatives passed Integrate's differentiate-back verifier:
  `Integrate[Cosh[x] Coth[x]^2, x]` came out as the NEGATIVE of the true
  antiderivative (G&R 2.423.39; now the correct `Sinh - Csch`, still in a
  `Coth`-radical form), and `Integrate[Sinh[x]/Sqrt[Sinh[x]^2 - 49/16], x]`
  (G&R 2.462.3b instance) was wrong on `x > ArcSinh[7/4]`. The G&R 2.462.10
  instances (`Cosh/Sqrt[a^2 + Cosh^2]`) now differentiate back at the corpus
  points, and the 2.619 `Sqrt[-Cos[2 a x]]` instances are left unevaluated
  rather than returned with a spurious factor `I`.
- Tests: `tests/test_intrat.c: test_square_through_symbolic_radical`,
  `tests/test_zero_test.c: test_uninformative_points_are_redrawn`.
- Open (B9): `Integrate[1/(Sqrt[a + b x] (alpha + beta x)), x, Assumptions -> beta (a beta - b alpha) > 0]`
  ignores the assumption when choosing `ArcTan` vs `ArcTanh`, so it returns the
  `ArcTan` form with `Sqrt[16 alpha b beta - 16 a beta^2]` of a negative radicand
  (analytically correct, not real-form); the LogToReal sign decision

## Compile / auto-compilation: every special function at arbitrary precision (v0.6482)
- `Compile[..., WorkingPrecision -> n]`, and the arbitrary-precision
  auto-compilation behind `NIntegrate` / `NSum` / `FindRoot` / `NDSolve` at
  `WorkingPrecision -> n`, now lower every special function — 142 heads (Erf,
  the Bessel / Hankel / Airy / Kelvin / Struve / Anger–Weber families, Gamma and
  its relatives, Beta(Regularized), the hypergeometric functions, MeijerG, the
  elliptic integrals, Jacobi elliptic, EllipticTheta, Weierstrass and Mathieu
  families, Zeta / HurwitzZeta / PolyLog / LerchPhi / DirichletL, the orthogonal
  polynomials, ProductLog, the Riemann functions, ...) — for real AND complex
  arguments. Before, any of them sent the WHOLE high-precision body to the
  interpreter at every sample (`MATHILDA_COMPILE_DIAG=1`: "no machine lowering
  for this head at these argument types"). Measured on v0.6481: 23 of 151
  numeric heads lowered for a real argument, 11 for a complex one; now 151 and
  145 (the 6 real-only heads have no complex evaluator to dispatch to).
- Mechanism: one table-driven opcode, `OP_MG_SPECIAL`, and a registry
  (`src/compile/compile_mgd_special.c`: head, arity range, real / complex /
  complex-valued-on-the-real-line). The call hands its MPFR / ncpx operands to
  the head's OWN arbitrary-precision evaluator — the builtin `N[f[...], n]`
  reaches — so compiled == uncompiled by construction and no numerics were
  added. Closed parameters stay exact (the `2` of `BesselJ[2, x]`, the parameter
  Lists of `HypergeometricPFQ` / `MeijerG` / `WeierstrassP`); a closed call
  (`BesselJZero[0, 3]`, `Gamma[1/3]`) folds to a constant at the working
  precision; `HankelH1` & co. of a real argument compile to an MPFR complex
  result. The registry also covers the complex domain of the elementary
  functions the mpfr / ncpx opcode set lacks (`Sinh`, `ArcSin`, `Log2`, ... of
  a complex), and `Arg`, `Log[b, x]`, `ArcTan[x, y]`, `ArcSec`, `ArcCsc`,
  `ArcCoth`, `Re` / `Im` / `Conjugate`. A point the evaluator declines (a
  complex value in a real program, a non-finite value) is interpreted, as
  before.
- Introspection: ``Internal`CompileArbitraryPrecisionHeads[]`` (the registry)
  and ``Internal`CompileArbitraryPrecisionStats[]`` (compiled special-function
  calls and declines, arbitrary-precision compiled calls that fell back,
  auto-compilations that bailed, auto-compiled points that declined).
- Measured (wall time, a loaded machine; same results to every printed digit):
  one special function alone runs at about the interpreter's speed (compiled
  `Erf` at 30 digits 22.4 µs/call vs 19.6 µs interpreted; `BesselK[1/3, x]`
  433 vs 402 µs) — the arb evaluation dominates, plus a CompiledFunction call
  and the boxing; a body with arithmetic around it
  (`Erf[x] x^2 + Sin[x] Exp[-x] + Sqrt[1 + x^2] - Log[2 + x]`) 43 vs 67 µs
  (1.55×). `NIntegrate` at `WorkingPrecision -> 30 / 50` over seven
  special-function integrands on [1/10, 1]: 0.9–1.4×, typically within noise,
  because those integrands converge in a few dozen samples whose cost is the
  special functions themselves. Where arithmetic surrounds the special
  function, at 30 digits: `NIntegrate[Sin[3 x] Erf[x] Exp[-x] (1 + x^2)/(2 + x),
  {x, 0, 1}]` 13.1 → 5.5 ms (2.4×), `NDSolve` of
  `y' == -Erf[t] y + Sin[t] y^2/(1 + t^2)` on [0, 1] 67 → 49 ms (1.4×),
  `FindRoot[Erf[x] BesselJ[0, x] - x/10, {x, 2}]` 1.25 → 0.70 ms (1.8×);
  `NSum[Erfc[k/3]/k^2 + Sin[k]/k^3, {k, 1, Infinity}]` 1.50 → 1.48 s (its cost
  is the acceleration machinery).
- Tests: `tests/test_compile_mpfr_special.c` (new suite, 1784 checks): every
  registry head, real and complex, at 30 and 50 digits — lowers, runs without a
  decline, agrees with the interpreter to the requested precision — mpmath
  reference values, composite bodies, and NIntegrate / NSum / FindRoot / NDSolve
  end to end (no auto-compile bail, == uncompiled, == mpmath to 25 digits).

## Integrate: the Gradshteyn & Ryzhik table stage (v0.6493)
- **New:** `Method -> "GRTable"`. It is also the LAST stage of both automatic
  cascades (indefinite: after the Roach Meijer G tail; definite: after the
  late Roach slot; not under `PrincipalValue`).
  - It answers from 3,745 formulas of G&R 7th ed., chapters 2–7 and 17:
    432 antiderivatives and 3,313 definite integrals.
  - Every formula was verified numerically by the G&R oracle and is not
    contradicted by the Python cross-check.
  - The table holds only formulas the baseline `Integrate` (run with an empty
    table) did not close.
- **Engine** (`src/internal/GRTable.m`, records in `src/internal/GRTables/`,
  generated by `tools/gr/build_table.py`):
  - Matching is unification: each maximal `x`-free subterm becomes a slot,
    and the record's parameters are solved from the slot equations.
  - The book's conditions are settled by `Refine` under the Assumptions.
  - Every answer is self-checked numerically: `NIntegrate` for a definite
    integral with no free symbols, differentiation at generic values for an
    antiderivative.
  - The table loads lazily, per interval class, and records compile on first
    use.
- **Soundness rules found while building it:**
  - A power slot solves to several roots (`a^4` gives `±a`, `±I a`), and the
    book's conditions often cannot tell them apart. The principal root is
    tried first, and among the unconditional answers of all records in the
    bucket the most principal solution wins. This fixed 19 sign-flipped
    elliptic answers (G&R 3.16x) and the 3.915.4a/b branch.
  - A `ConditionalExpression` from the table is a decline in the automatic
    cascade. Its open condition is usually a sibling formula's case split
    (a parity, or `p^2 <= 1` under `p^2 >= 1`).
  - Record parameter symbols live in `GRTable`R``, apart from the engine.
    The engine's `Do` iterator `s` was the same symbol as G&R 17.13.30's
    parameter `s`, and the recursion that caused left the engine half
    evaluated.
- **Convention:** `Integrate[1/x, x]` is `Log[x]`, so the book's
  `Log[Abs[u]]` antiderivatives are stored as `Log[u]`
  (`tools/gr/logconv.py`).
- **Book errors** are corrected from first principles or removed:
  - The book check (`build_corpus.py --book`) found no formula wrong as
    stated. Its failures were three Mathilda bugs, fixed in v0.6487–v0.6489.
  - The new condition audit (`tools/gr/condaudit.py`) samples points that the
    stated conditions admit and compares each value with `NIntegrate`.
  - 267 definite formulas disagreed there and are left out
    (`tools/gr/audit/condaudit_wrong.tsv`).
  - Two were corrected by hand in `tools/gr/cond_fixes.tsv`: 3.197.7 needs
    `mu > 1/2`, and 3.165.2 needs `a > 0`.
- **Tests:**
  - `tests/test_grtable.c`.
  - `tests/test_integrate_newton_leibniz.c` now loads `init.m`, the production
    environment. Without it the cascade declined an antiderivative that the
    table, being last, then supplied in a `Gamma` form.
- **Status and next steps:** `docs/design/gr_integrate_triage.md`.
