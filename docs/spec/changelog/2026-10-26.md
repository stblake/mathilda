# Changelog: week of 2026-10-26 (Mon) – 2026-11-01 (Sun)

## PossibleZeroQ over complex x visits every orthant's real slice; the DDI harness checks more points (v0.6100)

- **The defect.** The full ParallelMixedTower re-run at v0.6099 produced one WRONG answer, accepted by
  `PossibleZeroQ[D[F] - f, Assumptions -> Element[x, Complexes]]`: for
  `(x^4 - a b)/((d x^4 - (a d + b d - c) x^2 + a b d) Sqrt[x^2 - a] Sqrt[x^2 - b])` the answer through the Euler
  parameter of `Sqrt[x^2 - a]` is `-f` on parts of the real interval `x^2 < a` when `a, b, c, d > 0`. That region is
  a real-axis branch cut in 2 sign orthants of 32 and holds ~15% of their real points; the screen gave each orthant
  ~3 visits and ~1 real draw, so it was missed about 3 times in 4.
- **Fix.** When a symbol is complex-domain, the Stage-3 screen visits every orthant at least 16 times
  (`ZT_CPLX_ORTHANT_VISITS`, at most `ZT_CPLX_SCREEN_MAX` = 2048 points), and the complex-domain symbols are real on
  every other visit instead of on a random third of draws. Screen points are machine precision (~0.5 ms); the
  5-symbol identity above costs 0.43 s instead of 0.13 s. Real-domain `PossibleZeroQ` (and so Simplify, DSolve) is
  unchanged.
- **Corpus.** Re-verifying every stored DDI answer with the new screen: Trager's 351 all pass; three PMT answers fail
  (`multiple_radicals_0` (i), `multiple radicals` introduction 1, `nested_17` (i), the last wrong on ~10% of the
  complex plane). Re-run, two decline and `nested_17` (i) gets an answer that passes. PMT: 0 wrong / branch.
- **Harness.** `tools/ddi_stress.py` labels an answer PossibleZeroQ rejects by numerics only. With three complex
  points and fourteen real ones those wrong answers read as PZQ-FN ("zero at every sample"); it now uses ten complex
  points and 22 real ones, denser near 0 and 1.
- Test: `test_complex_screen_catches_real_slice_cut` (tests/test_parallelmixedtower.c).

## ParallelMixedTower integrates products of flat radicals piece by piece (v0.6099)

- **The gap.** 41 of the DDI declines carry several flat radicands (`Sqrt[a x^2 + b]/Sqrt[c/x^2 + d]`,
  `(x^3 - 1)^(1/3) (x^3 + 1)^(1/3)`, `Sqrt[b - a x] Sqrt[b + a x] Sqrt[b^4 - a^4 x^4]`), which BuildTower cannot fold
  into its one radical; Trager declines them as "a radical compositum".
- **The split (`KummerSplit`).** When the tower cannot be built, each radicand `b` becomes one symbol `b^(1/q)`, and
  the integrand is written as `Sum g M`, `g` rational, `M` a radical monomial. Monomials are grouped by their Kummer
  class, the exponent vector over the irreducible factors of the radicands mod 1: two monomials share a class iff
  their quotient is rational up to a locally constant root of unity. A class with two different monomials, a nested
  radical or a radical sum in the denominator is not split.
- **Each piece (`KummerIntegrate`).** With `M^L = R` rational, `g P` is integrated with the single radical
  `P = sq sf^(1/L)/den` (`sf` L-th-power-free). `M = kappa P` with `kappa` a locally constant root of unity, so the
  antiderivative is rewritten through `M` (`sf^(1/L) -> M den/sq`, exact), or else as `(M/P) F_P`. Each candidate
  must pass `PossibleZeroQ[D[F] - g M]` over complex x.
- **Certificates.** A piece proven non-elementary makes the integrand non-elementary: the projection onto that
  piece's Kummer character commutes with `D` and keeps Liouville's form.
- **Effect.** 22 of the 41 decided (21 antiderivatives, 1 certificate agreeing with the book and FriCAS:
  `Sqrt[2 x^2 + 3]/Sqrt[5 x^4 + 7]`), none wrong; every one is an integral Trager declines. The parametric chebychev 5
  cases reduce to `Int Sqrt[(b + a u) (c + d u)]/(c + d u) du`, which declines on bounds (a separate gap).
- `BuildTower::radicals` is now quiet (the failure is in the returned tuple).
- Tests: `test_kummer_split_flat_radicals`; the eulerStep leak test now requires a verified answer for
  `Sqrt[2 x^2 + 3]/Sqrt[5/x^2 + 7]` instead of a decline.

## ParallelMixedTower parametrises a conic before normalising a root nested on it (v0.6097)

- **The defect.** Every one of PMT's 151 "tower construction failed" declines on the DDI corpus is
  `BuildTower::radicals` (a second radical BuildTower cannot fold into the tower). 70 of them are a root nested on a
  square root of a quadratic, `(A + B Sqrt[q])^(1/m)` with `deg q <= 2`, where Euler's substitution leaves one pure
  radical. Two things stopped that:
  - **Order.** `rootNormalize` split a generator factor off the outer base before the Euler step could run:
    `Sqrt[x (2 x + y)] -> Sqrt[x] Sqrt[2 x + y]`, and `Sqrt[x]` was flattened as `x = u^2`, turning the conic
    `y^2 = 4 x^2 + 3` into the quartic `4 u^4 + 3`. Now a root whose base involves the tower radical `y` of a conic
    runs the Euler step first; the root is then re-examined over `Q(w)`.
  - **Parameters.** The Euler step needed `Sqrt[a2]` rational, and `Sqrt[a^2]` does not evaluate to `a`.
    `RationalSqrt` (`PowerExpand[Sqrt[Factor[e]]]`, checked by `r^2 == e`) supplies `a`, `a + b`, `2 a b/3`; it also
    finds the rational point `(0, b)` of `y^2 = a x^2 + b^2` in `RationalPoint`.
- **Effect.** 56 of the 151 towers now build. 19 new PMT answers, all on integrals Trager declines (nested
  radicals): nested 7, 10, 12, 14, 15, 16 families, potpourri p8 (v)/(vii), the inverse-function introduction.
  None wrong. The rest of the 56 now reach the later stages (18 time out, 19 decline on bounds, verification or
  torsion).
- **Still unbuildable.** Conics with no rational point (`2 x^2 - 3 = y^2`: nested 13, 17) need the Euler step through
  an algebraic point; flat radical products (41) and roots nested on a non-conic (40) are separate work.
- Test: `test_euler_before_root_split` (tests/test_parallelmixedtower.c).

## ParallelMixedTower writes answers through the integrand's own radical; PossibleZeroQ covers every sign orthant (v0.6096)

- **ParallelMixedTower: a second back-substitution candidate.**
  - **What BuildTower records.** When BuildTower rewrites one of the integrand's radicals as `c = A sf^(+-1/m)` (A
    rational, its signs chosen at one sample point), it records `Yown = c/A`. Then `Yown^m = sf` exactly.
  - **How the gate uses it.** If the principal surface (formal y -> `sf^(1/m)`) fails the all-complex-x gate, the
    same surface written through `Yown` is tried. Both are checked by PossibleZeroQ for complex x.
  - **Example.** `Sqrt[(1-x)/(1+x)]` -> `(1+x) Sqrt[(1-x)/(1+x)] - ArcTan[...]`, valid for all complex x, where the
    principal `Sqrt[1-x^2]` form was valid only for -1 < x.
  - **Why it is a fallback, not the default.** The real-form rewrite deliberately produces principal roots, so the
    own radical can be wrong where the principal one is right: an ArcSin case became wrong at x = -3. Both are tried
    in turn.
  - **DDI corpus.** 32 ParallelMixedTower answers recovered, 0 lost: warmup 8 and 11, power_expand, product_rule 5,
    nested 1/3/12, multiple_radicals 2, timofeev 1, ...
- **PossibleZeroQ sampling** (`src/zero_test.c`).
  - **Orthants.** Screen trial t gives symbol i the sign of bit i of t, so every sign orthant of up to 5 symbols is
    visited. The screen also scales with the symbol count (24 per symbol, at most 96).
  - **Why.** An answer wrong only where a > 0, b > 0 and x^2 < Min[a, b] (multiple_radicals_0 (iv)) is reached by
    under 1% of random-sign draws.
- **Harness.** A probe whose value depends on the working precision sits on a branch cut of an intermediate radicand
  and is now ignored (`na`). Three apparent "PossibleZeroQ false positives" were such probes.
- **Test fix.** The `x + 3^(1/3)` decline in `test_trager_phase0` became a rational function over Q(3^(1/3)) in
  v0.6094, and the test now says so.
- **Corpus.**
  - Trager: 351 antiderivatives and 15 proofs.
  - ParallelMixedTower: 260 antiderivatives and 21 proofs.
  - 0 wrong, 0 branch-wrong.

## Trager and ParallelMixedTower reduce f = x^(k-1) g(x^k) by u = x^k (v0.6095)

- **The reduction.** Both algebraic methods first test whether `f/x^(k-1)` mentions x only through powers
  `x^(j k)`, for k = 6..2 (`xPowerReduce` / `XPowerReduce`). If so, they integrate `g(u)` with the same method and
  return `G(x^k)/k`.
  - **Validity.** This is an exact identity, valid for all complex x because `u = x^k` is entire.
  - **Effect.** It lowers the radical's degree, as in `x^3/Sqrt[x^8 + 1]` or
    `1/(x Sqrt[x^8 + 1]) -> 1/8 Log[(2 + x^8 - 2 Sqrt[1 + x^8])/x^8]`.
  - **Checks.** The result is verified by PossibleZeroQ for complex x, and the normal path runs if the reduced
    integrand is not solved.
- **Scope.** It applies only to integrands with a radical in x. A rational one (`1/x -> 1/u -> ...`) would recurse
  without end, and the corpus run caught that before this commit.
- **ParallelMixedTower.** An internal error in the reduced sub-call ends the outer call, because a recursion overflow
  leaves the evaluator degraded.
- **Corpus effect.** No DDI integrand is newly decided by this alone: the eligible ones still have several or nested
  radicals after the reduction. It is general capability, not a corpus fix.
- **Tests.** The Trager and ParallelMixedTower suites pass (two expectations relaxed: the [B] Ex. 4 answer is still a
  single logarithm, and the forced-overflow direct call must fail, whatever the reason).

## PossibleZeroQ survives deep cancellation; the DDI harness verifies by PossibleZeroQ alone, for all complex x (v0.6091)

- **Why.** PossibleZeroQ is now the sole verifier of an antiderivative: no numeric fallback anywhere, and a false
  negative is a PossibleZeroQ bug. It rejected 24 correct Trager antiderivatives on the DDI corpus. The cancellation
  in their Log arguments, for example `Log[1 + 2x^4 + 2x^3 y + 2x^2 Sqrt[1+x^4] + 2x y^3]` at negative x, runs
  deeper than the top-level operand scale the "obvious non-zero" gate measures against.
- **Fixes** (`src/zero_test.c`):
  - **A machine-precision non-zero** must survive the ladder (`zt_resolve_machine_nonzero`): two consecutive MPFR
    rungs must agree to 2^-20. A collapse below the deep-zero floor, or by 2^60, is a zero. The machine rung never
    vouches for itself, because an artifact can survive unchanged to 200 bits (x = -39.45: 6.98, 6.98, then 2e-207).
  - **A machine-precision Infinity** (a denominator rounding to exactly 0.0) defers to the next rung. A genuine pole
    stays infinite and `False`.
  - **Result.** All 24 now return `True`; every non-zero control stays `False`.
- **What did not change.** Points on a real-axis branch cut remain evidence: principal values there count, so
  `Sqrt[(1-x)/(1+x)] (1+x) - Sqrt[1-x^2]` (it fails on x < -1) is still `False`. An attempt to discount cut points
  let a branch-wrong DSolve answer through `dsolve_tests` and was reverted.
- **Harness** (`tools/ddi_stress.py`).
  - **ELEM means PossibleZeroQ True.** Numerics only label the rest: PZQ-FN (zero at every real and complex probe,
    a PossibleZeroQ bug), BRANCH (+f/-f, or real-only agreement) or WRONG.
  - **Probes.** 3 complex points, plus a fixed spread of real points covering every real component, including
    intervals where f is complex-valued. Probes run with `$MaxExtraPrecision = 2000`.
  - **`--reverify`** re-checks every stored answer without re-running the integrators.
- **Corpus now.**
  - Trager: 338 antiderivatives, all confirmed by PossibleZeroQ, none wrong or branch-wrong.
  - ParallelMixedTower: 232 confirmed, 50 branch-wrong, 29 wrong. Its branch-wrong and wrong answers are the next fix (answers valid for all
    complex x).
- **Tests.** `test_deep_cancellation_zero_is_true` in `test_zero_test.c`. The unit suite is 563/565; the 2 known
  failures are in-tree build artefacts.

## A FLINT exception no longer kills the kernel; to_qqbar declines a zero base to a negative power (v0.6090)

- **Root cause.** `to_qqbar` (`src/poly/flint_qqbar.c`, behind RootReduce, ToNumberField and the AlgebraicNumber
  functions) passed a base that is zero only after expansion, such as `(Sqrt[2]+Sqrt[3])^2 - 5 - 2 Sqrt[6]`, to
  `qqbar_pow_si` with a negative exponent. FLINT threw `qqbar_pow_fmpq: division by zero` and its default handler
  aborted the process. ParallelMixedTower hit this on a DDI integrand.
  - **Fix.** That case now declines: `RootReduce` gives `ComplexInfinity` and `ToNumberField` stays unevaluated.
- **Defence in depth.** FLINT's `flint_throw` is now routed to the innermost recovery point (`tc_flint_throw`) and
  reported as `FLINT::exc`. The `Quiet` of the throw site is honoured and `Check` sees the message.
  - **Inside `TimeConstrained`:** `failexpr` or `$Aborted`.
  - **Outside it:** every top-level `evaluate()` now runs under `tc_evaluate_fault_guarded`, a recovery point with no
    signal-mask save, so a top-level input becomes `$Aborted` and the session survives. The guard costs nothing
    measurable: 200k top-level evaluations take 0.28 s against 0.27 s.
  - **Inside heavy methods:** the Trager and ParallelMixedTower method builtins evaluate their package call under
    their own recovery point, so a fault is a decline (`{"failed", "internal error: FLINT exception"}`) and
    `Integrate` moves on.
- **Tests** (`test_rootreduce.c`):
  - `test_qqbar_zero_negative_power`.
  - `test_flint_exception_recovery`, which uses a test-only builtin calling `flint_throw` and checks TimeConstrained,
    top-level, Check and session survival.
  - Unit suite: 563 of 565 pass. The 2 failures (multi-argument `HeavisideTheta` in `mixed_numericalize_tests`, and

## ParallelMixedTower accepts an answer only on positive evidence; a recursion overflow is never an answer (v0.6089)

- **Why.** A `$RecursionLimit` overflow does not abort. The evaluator wraps the call in `Hold` and degrades every
  enclosing fixed-point loop to one rewrite, so the package's decline branches could be skipped and whatever was in
  hand fell out. In v0.6086 that was the unassigned Module local `ParallelMixed`Private`r$NNN`, returned as the
  "antiderivative".
- **The package gate** (`ParallelMixed.m`, 2-arg entry):
  - It accepts a surface only if all of these hold: a verified unit `fac` ∈ {1, -1, I, -I}, which is now a Module
    local (it was a global, so a stale value could pass); dependence on x; no `Hold`; and no `Temporary` symbol other
    than a `Function`'s bound variable.
  - The body runs under `Check[..., {"failed", "internal error: $RecursionLimit exceeded"}, {$RecursionLimit::reclim}]`.
  - The pair form's `r` starts as a failure tuple.
- **The C side** (`integrate.c`):
  - `builtin_integrate_pmt` turns any result produced while an overflow fired (`eval_overflow_fired()`, new in
    `eval.h`) into a failure tuple.
  - `pmt_leaks_symbol` no longer exempts `Temporary` Module locals; it tracks `Function`-bound variables instead.
- **Tests.**
  - `test_recursion_overflow_declines` forces a real overflow inside the entry and requires a failure from the
    package, from the qualified symbol and from the Method form.
  - The PMT, PMT-special and Integrate suites pass.

## Series reaches the requested order at high-order poles; no more false ParallelMixedTower certificates through it (v0.6088)

- **`Series` came back short at high-order poles.**
  - **Cause.** `do_series_single` expanded every subexpression to one fixed padded order (user order + 12). Each
    order of pole lost one order of relative precision: an `Apart`-merged `e^7 + e^8` through `so_inv`, or Laurent
    `so_mul`. A pole of order ≥ 7 therefore came back short of the requested O-term, and nothing noticed.
  - **Symptoms.** `Series[Sqrt[(1+e)^2+e^6]/(e^7 (1+e)), {e,0,0}]` gave `1/e^7 + O[e]^-1`. `Exp[e]/e^14` and
    `Sqrt[1+e]/e^20` were short the same way. `SeriesCoefficient` read the missing coefficient as 0.
  - **Fix.** The shortfall is measured and the expansion is redone with the pad widened by exactly that much, for
    numeric centres and at most 4 rounds. The single-term `so_inv` no longer overstates precision.
- **Consequence for ParallelMixedTower.**
  - **What happened.** At the order-9 pole of `Sqrt[1-x] Sqrt[1+x] Sqrt[1-x^4]` its deep residues came out as
    {0, 0, 0, 0} instead of ±5/4. The holomorphic-remainder argument then "proved" the integrand non-elementary,
    though it is (1 - x^2) Sqrt[1 + x^2] on the reals. multiple_radicals_4 (iii) failed the same way.
  - **Now.** Both decline ("needs torsion realisation"), and the DDI corpus has 0 disagreements.
- **Tests.**
  - `test_series_high_order_pole_precision`: the reproducer, O-term positions for k = 7 at n = 0, 3, 6, `e^-14`,
    `e^-20`, and the residue −5/4.
  - `test_no_false_certificate_series_precision`.
  - Series, Limit, Residue, Sum, Product and DSolve suites pass.

## Trager: non-elementarity certified over the field of one residue (v0.6086)

- **Trace-functional certificates** (`traceCertificate`, `traceCertificateAt`,
  `traceDivisorTest` in `src/internal/trager/Pipeline.m`).
  - **The theorem.** Trager's criterion goes through a Q-basis of the residues and
    so through the field of all of them. A certificate needs less: if the integral
    is elementary, `Σ_i λ(α_i) Δ(α_i)` is torsion for every Q-linear λ on the span
    of the residues.

## Trager: reduced divisor-class arithmetic, Hensel-lifted residue powers, faster presentation (v0.6083)

- **Reduced divisor classes** (`src/internal/trager/Jacobian.m`, new). Torsion orders
  were found by testing M·delta through ideals that grow linearly with M; several
  integrals timed out. Classes are now kept reduced:
  - **Frame.** Centred at a root of G whose multiplicity is prime to m, so infinity
    is one totally ramified place P of degree 1, and orders at P are exact and
    additive.
  - **Representatives.** `[A - deg(A) P]` with A effective of degree at most the
    genus, found by Riemann–Roch from a Popov-reduced basis (`reducedRows`).
  - **Canonical keys.** L(D + sP) is one-dimensional at the least s, so the
    representative is unique.
  - **Order search.** Modulo the first prime p it is a linear search, then
    baby-step giant-step over the whole Weil range. Modulo q the order must be
    `n0 p^a q^b`, so a and b are found by multiplication, with no second search.
  - **Large orders in characteristic 0** (above 12, with a rational root of G):
    N·delta is formed by doubling and adding, and its generator is a short product.
- **Divisor specs** (`TorsionModP.m`): divisors are described independently of the
  frame, `{residues, coefficients, P or (x - c)_0}`. Per-orbit divisors with a
  rational root of G use `Delta(gamma) - kappa P` instead of
  `m Delta(gamma) - kappa (x - c)_0`. `placeIdealAt` gives P as a finite ideal in
  the working frame.
- **Ideals** (`Ideal.m`, `Normalize.m`):
  - `k Delta(alpha) = (A^k, Y - phi_k)`, with `phi_k` the Hensel lift of the branch
    value (`henselRoot`), and the inverse `(A^k, H_k)/A^k`. So N·delta is one
    product per residue, with no repeated squaring.
  - Ideal products use a Hermite form modulo the determinant (`hnfModDet`).
  - Repeated addends use verified two-generator forms (`twoGenerators`).
  - Over Q and GF(p), `idealReduce` removes common factors by gcd.
- **Verification and presentation:**
  - The log part is verified as a sum of logarithmic derivatives
    (`logSumResidual`), not a product.
  - The real form treats radicals as atoms in `Together` and samples sign changes in
    machine arithmetic. It is skipped above 1500 leaves and limited to 5 s.
  - In positive genus the complete Q-basis construction runs first; in genus 0,
    cubic and higher residue factors go one orbit at a time.
- **Results:**
  - **FriCAS stress test (360 integrands).** Trager has still 0 wrong answers,
    0 disagreements and 0 FriCAS-only cases. Timeouts fell from 15 to 10, and
    proofs of non-elementarity that FriCAS cannot confirm rose from 23 to 28. Median
    time is 0.031 s against FriCAS's 0.080 s, and the mean 2.5 s against 5.1 s.
  - **Cases where FriCAS is fast** now take 0.1–4.2 s, previously 2–15 s.
  - **Former timeouts.** Ten of them now finish with certificates, typically in
    6–110 s; the large primes those need come from splitting the full residue field.
- **Tests.** New suite `tests/test_trager_jacobian.c` (79 assertions). The existing
  suites were updated where the representation changed: good-prime records now carry
  the frame centre, and compact generators are multiplied out when small.

## Integrate: Trager's algorithm for simple radicals, and Kauers' log-part heuristic (v0.6082)

- **`Integrate[f, x, Method -> "Trager"]`**: Trager's 1984 algorithm for algebraic
  functions, restricted to a rational function of x and ONE radical `r(x)^(1/n)`
  over Q. It is a decision procedure. Either it returns an antiderivative, verified
  exactly in K(x)(Y) and then with `PossibleZeroQ[D[F, x] - f]`, or it proves the
  integral non-elementary (`Integrate::nonelem`). Otherwise it declines, unevaluated.
  - **Implementation.** Mathilda's own language in `src/internal/trager/`, 19 files,
    every function with a `::usage` docstring, loaded on first use.
  - **Origin.** Ported from the 4.6-era `packages/trager/`, without its Miller and
    Schultz engines, its Auto cascade, or its parametric mode, and reworked:
    - **Components.** The y^i components are integrated separately on their own
      absolutely irreducible curves ([T] Appendix A). Branch places then carry no
      residue, and residue divisors are exactly the ideals `(N Y - alpha E', Bp)`.
      This removes the package's branch-place hack and "lucky integer" search.
    - **Divisors.** Divisors are fractional ideals over Q, Q(theta) and GF(p): a
      Hermite form computed modulo the determinant, closed-form two-generator ideals
      for residue divisors and their inverses, and normalisation at infinity in
      Trager's integer formulation.
    - **Torsion.** The order of a residue divisor comes from its orders modulo two
      primes of good reduction ([T] Ch 6 §2–3), not a fixed `k ≤ 12` search.
    - **Galois orbits.** Residue fields beyond a small splitting field are handled
      one orbit at a time, over Q[Z]/(q): `m Delta(gamma) - kappa (x - c)_0`, with
      `RootSum` output. This is complete in genus 0. In higher genus the Q-basis
      construction is the fallback.
  - **Certificates** (each a theorem of [T]):
    - a pole at infinity left after Hermite reduction;
    - a nonzero remainder with no residues;
    - torsion orders modulo p and q that disagree;
    - N·delta not principal;
    - a nonzero holomorphic difference after the log part.
- **`Method -> "TragerKauers"`**: the same pipeline, with the logarithmic part
  found by Kauers' 2008 Groebner heuristic. It takes the least principal power of
  `<Y^m - G, Bp, N Y - Z E', q>` modulo the curve and q, and verifies the result
  exactly by traces over Q[Z]/(q). It declines where the heuristic does not close.
- **Method symbols.** `Integrate\`Trager[f, x]` and `Integrate\`TragerKauers[f, x]`
  return the raw `{"not elementary", reason}` / `{"failed", reason}` lists. Neither
  method is in the Automatic cascade. The Integrate docstring and
  `docs/spec/builtins/calculus.md` (`### Integrate\`Trager`) document both.
- **Newton–Leibniz** (`integrate_newton_leibniz.c`): an `ArcTan`/`ArcCot` of an
  x-dependent quotient now counts as a jump risk, so `F(b) - F(a)` is cross-checked
  numerically. `Integrate[1/Sqrt[1 - x^2], {x, -1/2, 1/2}, Method -> "Trager"]` was
  `-2 Pi/3` through the presented form `-ArcTan[Sqrt[1 - x^2]/x]`. Real forms now
  choose the ArcTan orientation whose denominator does not change sign, so it is
  `Pi/3`.
- **Tests.** Nine unit suites `tests/test_trager_*.c` (about 1,900 assertions: every
  package function, expectations derived independently), sharing
  `tests/trager_test_utils.h`. The nightly `trager_fricas_stress`
  (`tools/trager_fricas_stress.py`) compares against FriCAS on a 360-integrand
  corpus (`tests/data/trager_stress_corpus.m`): any wrong answer or disagreement is
  a hard failure, and the FriCAS-only count ratchets.
- **`LinearSolve` leak** (`src/linalg/linsolve.c`): the exact FLINT fast path
  never freed the solution array (16 bytes per call), because
  `build_solution_tensor` takes ownership of the entries but not of the array.
  `leaks --atExit` is now clean on LinearSolve and on the Trager methods.
- **Known limitations:**
  - a torsion order needing a large power over a residue field of degree 3 or more
    (e.g. `1/((x - 2)(x^3 + 1)^(1/3))`, order 21);
  - torsion searches for genus 2 or more on index 5 and 6 radicals over GF(p) — no
    divisor reduction yet, [T] Ch 6 §3;
  - radical compositums and algebraic coefficients are out of scope.

## Together / Cancel: the Modulus and Gaussian regressions of the factored form (v0.6076)

The v0.6075 shootout showed three families slower than v0.6072, all from
v0.6073's factored output: `tog-mod` up to 7.7x, `can-mod` 1.4x, `tog-gauss` up

| family | v0.6072 | v0.6076 |
|---|---|---|
| `tog-mod` | 0.15 / 0.34 / 0.54 ms | 0.17 / 0.32 / 0.51 ms |
| `can-mod` | 0.18 / 0.21 / 0.35 ms | 0.16 / 0.17 / 0.33 ms |
| `can-gauss` | 0.16 / 0.57 / 2.47 ms | 0.16 / 0.57 / 2.42 ms |
| `tog-gauss` | 0.20 / 0.96 / 0.38 ms | 0.23 / 1.04 / 0.50 ms |

The remaining `tog-gauss` cost is building the answer over a basis (the
reference engine takes 3.2 ms on `tog-gauss-3`).

- **Modulus, one variable: the factored form over Q, modulo p, with no
  factorisation.**
  - The v0.6073 form factored the whole denominator modulo `p`. That cost 5 ms
    on 60 summands modulo 2^61 - 1, and it was not the form of a reference
    engine either.
  - The pieces of the denominator as written (each factor of a product on its
    own) are recorded while the input is read. The answer's denominator is
    written over their gcd-free basis, refined by the gcd that cancelled.
  - A linear piece needs no gcd: two distinct ones are coprime, and `x + c`
    meets another piece exactly where that piece vanishes at `-c`.
  - A basis element a split produced is made squarefree. The numerator is
    monic with the constant in front.
  - A Cancel term with nothing to cancel and one piece keeps its form, the
    coefficients reduced.
  - The answer is built canonical and certified, as on Q. Multiplicities are
    found by `nmod_poly` division for a word-size `p`.
- **Parity of the form.** Of 28 one-variable probes against a reference
  engine (FullForm), 27 agree. The exception: `Together[(3 x + 2)/(2 x + 4), Modulus ->
  7]`, where the reference keeps a rational content `1/2 (2 + 3 x)/(2 + x)`.
  Several variables still factor the denominator modulo `p`, and a reference's
  normalisation there (which variable a numerator is made monic in) is left
  for later.
- **Numbers under Modulus.** `Together`, `Cancel` and `Apart` of a number give
  its residue: `Together[9, Modulus -> 7]` is `2`. Before, the call stayed
  unevaluated. A denominator that vanishes modulo `p` leaves the number as
  written.
- **Gaussian, factored form.**
  - A single denominator piece is declined before the pieces are converted
    again. A degree-65 Cancel paid a second conversion for nothing.
  - Pairs of basis candidates use the exact linear tests instead of a
    Euclidean gcd over Q(i).
  - A linear basis element's multiplicity is tested by evaluation before a
    division. `tog-gauss-3`: 1.34 -> 0.50 ms.
- **No abort on a huge degree.** `Together[1/(1 + x) + (x^(2^61 - 1) - x)/(x^2
  - 1)]` (with or without a Modulus) aborted with "FLINT memory exhausted":
  FLINT's gcds deflate to dense arrays. The engine now computes a degree bound
  of its input and declines above 2^22. Such a call stays unevaluated under
  Modulus, and goes to the other paths otherwise.

## Together / Cancel / Apart: a factored rational-function engine (v0.6072)

A review of `Together`, `Cancel` and `Apart`, with a five-engine shootout
(`benchmarks/98-rational-shootout`: Mathilda, a commercial reference engine, Maxima, SymPy,
PARI/GP; 98 cases in 26 families, every answer checked independently).

- **New engine** (`src/poly/ratfun.c`). A rational function over Q in symbols
  and kernels (`Log`, inverse trig, `E^(k u)`, `x[3]`) is converted into FLINT
  once, as `c * prod p_i^e_i` with each `p_i` primitive and normalised, and
  reduced there.
  - **Together** combines a sum through a gcd-free, squarefree basis of the
    denominator factors. The numerator comes from a product tree whose
    denominators are exponent vectors over the basis, so there are no exact
    divisions. One gcd against the squarefree denominator settles the
    cancellation. A sum with one fractional term needs no gcd at all.
  - **Cancel** keeps the factors. It splits numerator factors against
    denominator factors by gcd and leaves alone a term with nothing to cancel.
  - One variable runs on dense `fmpz_poly`.
- **Apart** factors the denominator with `fmpz_mpoly_factor`. It runs the CRT
  split and the P-adic digits in `fmpq_poly` over Q, and in `gr_poly` over
  `fmpz_mpoly_q` over Q(parameters). This replaces the symbolic `RowReduce`:
  `Apart[1/((x+a)(x+b)(x+c)(x+d)(x+f)), x]` went from 2.0 s to 1.6 ms, and six
  parameters from a 60 s timeout to 3 ms. The
  parametric forms are one fraction over the parameters per term:
  `Apart[x^3/(x - a), x]` is `a^2 + a x + x^2 + a^3/(-a + x)`.
- **Gaussian and number-field input.** One variable over Q(i) or one number
  field is reduced natively in K(x).
  - The K(x) arithmetic is lazy: one reduction at the end, not a gcd per
    operation.
  - Above degree 12, the gcd is the certified modular field gcd instead of the
    Euclidean one over `nf`:
    `Cancel[(x^2 - 2) A60/((x - Sqrt[2]) B60), Extension -> Automatic]` went
    from 3.8 s to 5 ms.
  - Bare `Complex` coefficients no longer make an `AlgebraicNumber` round trip
    through `ReplaceAll`.
  - The answer has integral Gaussian coefficients:
    `(-5 - 5 I)/(3 + I + 5 x)`, not `(-1 - I)/(3/5 + I/5 + x)`.
  - Apart over K factors the denominator with the Trager engine:
    `ap-gauss` went from 27 ms to 1.8 ms.
- **`Modulus -> p`** for all three heads, over GF(p): `fmpz_mod_poly` for one
  variable, `fmpz_mod_mpoly` otherwise. A composite modulus is `head::modp` and
  a modulus with an `Extension` is `head::mext`; in both cases the call stays
  unevaluated. `Modulus -> 0` and `Trig -> False` are accepted, and
  `Options[Together]` (also `Cancel`, `Apart`) now lists `Trig -> False`.
- **Output form** (superseded in v0.6073). The default form is unchanged: an expanded numerator over an
  expanded denominator. `MATHILDA_TOGETHER_FORM=factored` selects the
  factored form (the denominator as its basis factors, the content in front,
  Apart coefficients over a factored denominator). `MATHILDA_RATFUN=off`
  bypasses the engine, for A/B runs.
- **Docstrings rewritten** for all three heads (`?Together`, `?Cancel`,
  `?Apart`): every form, what counts as a variable, the sum and nesting
  behaviour, each option with its default and messages, and the threading.
- **Regressions caught while writing it.**
  - A monomial factor `x^3` kept whole was treated as coprime to `x`, which
    left `x^2` uncancelled.
  - The monomial split of `3x^3 - 2x^4` left a duplicate `2x - 3`, and the
    single-term pass looped on it.
- **Behaviour changes.** Cancel on a sum leaves a term with nothing to cancel
  as it is (`Cancel[x + 1/(1 - x^2)]` keeps `1/(1 - x^2)`). The Gruntz limit
  `E^(E^(E^(x + E^-x)))/E^(E^(E^x))` now evaluates to `Infinity` instead of
  abstaining.

## Factor review (v0.6061 – v0.6071)

A review of `Factor` and its family. It covers correctness, the options, a
FLINT fast path for every polynomial input, and a four-engine shootout
(`benchmarks/99-factor-shootout`: Mathilda, the reference, PARI/GP, Maxima, SymPy).
Each algorithm was stress-tested with a seeded driver: about 6,500 FLINT-path
cases, 3,400 pre-stage cases, 378 classical-path cases and 150 extension cases.

- **Wrong answers and crashes fixed.**
  - A univariate rational function went to the int64 Berlekamp–Zassenhaus
    path. It hung on `1/(x^43 - 1)`, crashed on `1/(x^6001 + x + 1)`, and left
    large-coefficient sides unfactored. Both sides now go to FLINT (v0.6061).
  - `PolynomialGCD` and `PolynomialLCM` took integer content in int64:
    `PolynomialLCM[7*10^18 x, 13 y]` came back with a wrapped negative
    coefficient (v0.6061).
  - `Rationalize[0.000001]` was `0`, so `Factor[x - 0.000001]` was `x`. A real
    of 2^63 or more sent the inexact round trip into infinite recursion
    (v0.6061).
  - A variable exponent of 2^31 or more was read through an `int`, so
    `Factor[x^(2^32 + 2) + x^2 - 1]` returned `(-1 + x)(1 + x)`. A variable
    exponent of 2^20 or more is now `Factor::lrgexp` (v0.6063).
  - An INT64_MIN coefficient printed as `--9223372036854775808` (v0.6064).
  - The classical (no-FLINT) univariate factoriser:
    - it was int64 throughout: undefined behaviour at 31 or more modular
      factors, overflow in equal-degree factoring (hangs) and in the
      leading-coefficient products;
    - it lifted to a fixed 10^15 bound;
    - its square-free GCDs were not primitive (`Factor[Expand[(x + 2)^40]]`).

    It now runs on GMP coefficients with a coefficient-bound lift, best-prime
    choice and exact recombination. On a 378-case corpus, UB reports went
    from 73 to 0 and under-factored or timed-out cases from 204 to 61, all
    of them multivariate (v0.6067).
- **Options** (v0.6062).
  - `Options[Factor]` is `{Extension -> None, GaussianIntegers -> False,
    Modulus -> 0, Trig -> False}`.
  - Options are validated, with `Factor::nonopt` and `::optx`; the first
    occurrence of an option wins, and `SetOptions` applies.
  - `Modulus -> p` factors over GF(p) for a prime of any size in any number of
    variables. Rational coefficients, rational functions, lists and relations
    all work, and products are never expanded over Q. The messages are
    `::modp`, `::bmod`, `::mext`, `::facim` and `::nmod`.
  - Options at their defaults no longer push an input onto the classical
    path: `Factor[x^99 - y^99, Extension -> None]` took over 100 s and now
    takes 4 ms.
  - FactorList, FactorSquareFree and FactorSquareFreeList validate their own
    options. FactorSquareFree accepts `Modulus`.
- **Fast entry** (v0.6063).
  - Products and powers factor base by base: `(x + 1)^500 (x - 1)^500` went
    from 1.44 s to 0.06 ms.
  - Plain polynomials go straight to FLINT.
  - `c x^k (x^m ± 1)` splits into cyclotomic polynomials: `x^1260 - 1` went
    from 23 s to under 1 ms.
  - The polynomial is read into FLINT in one pass. Monomials are pushed and
    sorted once, where re-merging the growing sum was quadratic (20k terms:
    1.1 s to 2 ms).
  - Factor signs follow the lexicographic order of the canonical variable
    order: `Factor[X^2 - a^2]` is `-((a - X)(a + X))`.
- **Kernels** (v0.6065). `E^x`, `2^x`, `(-1)^n` and positive fractional
  powers (`x^(1/3)`, `Sqrt[2]`) are generators of their own:
  `Factor[E^x y - E^x]` is `E^x (-1 + y)`.
- **Family conventions** (v0.6066).
  - FactorList lists a rational numerical factor as `{p, 1}, {q, -1}`.
  - FactorList and FactorSquareFreeList are no longer `Listable`.
  - FactorTermsList's content is rational and signed: `{3/4, 2 + x}`,
    `{-6, x^2 + 2 x y}`; for `0` it gives `{1, 0}`.
  - FactorTerms takes `Modulus`.
  - FactorSquareFree runs through FLINT, 65–340× faster than Yun on
    expressions, and its signs and monomial split match FactorList's.
  - Docstrings for the whole family are rewritten.
- **Univariate `Modulus`** (v0.6068) with a word-size prime uses
  `nmod_poly_factor`.
- **Number fields** (v0.6069).
  - `Extension` and `GaussianIntegers -> True` work in any number of
    variables. A new engine, `src/poly/flint_trager.c`, implements Trager's
    method: shifted norm via `fmpq_mpoly_resultant`, the norm factored by
    `fmpq_mpoly_factor`, and factors recovered with the certified modular
    field GCD, the whole answer checked by exact division. Example:
    `Factor[x^2 - 2 y^2, Extension -> Sqrt[2]]` is
    `-((Sqrt[2] y - x)(Sqrt[2] y + x))`.
  - A degree-40 product over Q(2^(1/6)) went from 87.8 s to 0.39 s.
  - A univariate polynomial with Gaussian coefficients factors over Q(i) by
    default.
  - `Extension -> Automatic` takes the whole tower.
  - Extension coefficients may be big rationals.
- **Cancel / Together over Q(i)** (v0.6070). A Gaussian fraction is reduced
  over the number field Q(I), not with `I` as a free variable:
  `Cancel[(x^2 + 1)/(x - I)]` is `I + x`. This unblocked DSolve, whose
  verification stalled on an unreduced degree-8 Gaussian quotient.
- **Extensions by pieces** (v0.6071): a polynomial over Q is factored over Q
  first and each irreducible factor over the field separately
  (`x^120 - 1` over Q(I): 129 ms -> 14 ms).
- **Shootout** (`benchmarks/99-factor-shootout/REPORT.md`). On 79 cases in 17

  family except univariate `Modulus -> 2` at degree 1250. That case takes

## Module locals: reclaimed during long evaluations, names freed (v0.6058)

Follow-ups to v0.6056, listed as outstanding in `SYMBOL_TABLE_IMPROVEMENTS.md`.

- **Escaped locals no longer wait for the statement to end.** An escaped local
  (returned, stored, captured by a closure) used to be queued and retried only
  when the outermost evaluation finished. A `Do` that stores and drops a
  `Module` result or a closure per iteration therefore kept every local until
  the loop ended.
  - `evaluate()` now also sweeps the queue in a nested evaluation once it holds
    `g_temp_sweep_at` entries: 4096, then twice the survivors of the last
    sweep. Each held local is re-walked an amortised O(1) times.

## Module locals are reclaimed (v0.6056)

- **The bug.** `Module` registered each local `Temporary` but never removed
  one. Every call left a symbol and its value behind for the whole session:
  548 bytes per call, and +111,005 symbols (+44 MB) after one issue #112
  trace, where the reference keeps +11.
- **Other constructs are clean.** Measured per call against the reference:
  `Block`, `With`, `Function` (including nested and capture-avoiding forms),
  `Table`, `Sum`, rules and `Compile` all stay at 0 symbols and 0 bytes.
  `Unique[]` keeps its symbols in both systems, as it should.
- **How it works** (`src/symtab.c`, "Module-local reclamation").
  - **Counting.** A local made from a never-interned name is counted: each live
    node naming it caches its `SymbolDef` and is counted in `temp_nodes`.
  - **The reclaim test.** It is reclaimed when a walk of its own rule lists
    finds every such node, each through refcount-1 parents, so self-references
    like `x$1 = {x$1}` do not keep it alive.
  - **When.** At its `Module`'s exit. An escaped local (returned, captured by a
    closure, rule or UpValue) is queued and retried when the outermost
    evaluation ends, once something was queued or a queued local lost a node.
  - **What is kept.** Name strings are kept, so no raw pointer can dangle.
  - **Kill switch.** `MATHILDA_NO_TEMP_RECLAIM=1`.
- **Results.**
  - +9 symbols after the trace.
  - `Module` costs 50 B per call (the kept names), down from 548.
  - The trace is faster too: generation 3.2 → 2.9 s, `MaxMemoryUsed[]`
    154 → 128 MB.
- **Checks.**
  - Escape semantics match the reference: closures, rules, UpValues, `Throw`,
    `Reap`, recursion, nesting, and a cleared `Temporary`.
  - Clean under AddressSanitizer: the trace, the Expand corpus, and the
    scoping corpus.
  - An audit found no long-lived `SymbolDef*` holder that could reach a freed
    local, and sweeps run only at the top level for that reason.
  - Pinned in `modular_tests`.

## Sum enumerates finite ranges; per-call overhead (v0.6055, issue #112)

- **Wrong answers in Sum.** A finite numeric range was summed in closed form
  first, which evaluates the body at a symbolic index. That is wrong for a body
  that takes the index apart:
  - `Sum[Length[Delete[{2, 3, 4}, m]], {m, 1, 3}]` was `9`; it is `6`.

  - Removing it took generation from 4.7 s to 3.1–3.2 s. The skeleton's base

  - The integer index also advances by direct addition instead of an

    before `strcmp`.
  - The table grows by doubling instead of a fixed 65535 buckets. `Module`
    mints a symbol per local per call, and a run leaves about 10^5 of them, so
    the chains had become long.
  - `disasm`'s kernel-name lookup takes the alphabetically first alias,
    independent of bucket order.
  - The skeleton is 12% faster and at parity with the reference
    (0.65 s against 0.71 s).
- **Not done here.** A real redesign — reclaiming `Module` locals (the

- **Result.** Issue #112 at n = 14: generation 4.7 s → 3.1–3.5 s, `TimeUsed[]`
  5.1 s → 3.5 s.

## Expand: opaque kernels as FLINT generators (v0.6054, issue #112 follow-up)

- **What changed.** `flint_expand_polynomial_kernels` expands a polynomial
  whose generators include opaque kernels in packed `fmpq_mpoly` arithmetic.
  Before, FLINT's expander accepted symbols only, so `f[1]`, `g[x[i], x[j]]`,
  `Sin[x]` or `Log[y]` sent the whole expansion to term-by-term distribution.
  - Kernels are found through a hash table (`expr_hash` + `expr_eq`); the GCD
    abstraction's linear generator scan would be quadratic here.
  - Monomial summands are pushed and combined once, not added one at a time.
  - Each generator renders back as a shared copy of its kernel node, and the
    result is evaluated.
- **Results.**

  | Expand | before | after |
  |---|---|---|
  | `(f[1] + ... + f[6])^8` | 78 ms | 4.9 ms |
  | `(x + Sin[x] + g[y] + 1)^12` | 26 ms | 1.4 ms |
  | `(1 + Log[x] + Log[y])^15 (Log[x] - 2)` | 6.0 ms | 0.4 ms |

- **Exactness.** Expand never looks inside a kernel, and the result is
  evaluated, so kernels that interact under `Times` come out exactly as
  before: `(Sin[x] + Csc[x])^3` is `3 Csc[x] + Csc[x]^3 + 3 Sin[x] + Sin[x]^3`.
  - **Excluded** are kernels whose multiplication has its own semantics, which
    collecting `k k` into `k^2` before evaluation would bypass:
    - System heads without `NumericFunction` (`List`, `Interval`,
      `SeriesData`, `ConditionalExpression`, `DirectedInfinity`,
      `AlgebraicNumber`, `Root`, ...);
    - heads with UpValues (`g /: g[a_] g[b_] := ...` fires on `g[1] g[1]` but
      not on `g[1]^2`);
    - user rules for `Plus`/`Times`/`Power`.
  - **Checked.** 190 expansions (43 hand-picked interaction cases and 150
    random kernel polynomials) are byte-identical to v0.6053.
- **When it is used.** Only when sums are multiplied together (a power of a
  sum, or two or more sum factors). Distributing one kernel over an
  already-expanded sum, which is every step of the issue #112 Dirac trace, is
  linear and stays classical. FLINT's lex-ordered output would have to be
  fully re-sorted there, and taking the path unconditionally cost that
  benchmark 25% (4.7 s → 5.8 s).

## Symbolic evaluation: INERT fixed points, adaptive Orderless sort, Times/Plus/Expand fast paths (v0.6053, issue #112)

The Dirac-trace benchmark from issue #112 (n = 14, 135135 terms), run on one
machine, v0.6052 → v0.6053:

| | v0.6052 | v0.6053 |
|---|---|---|
| trace generation | 18.5 s | 4.5 s |
| `Cases[res, _g, Infinity] // Union // Length` | 0.84 s | 0.33 s |
| `TimeUsed[]` | 19.8 s | 4.8 s |
| `MaxMemoryUsed[]` | 224 MB | 174 MB |

(v0.6049 was 24.1 s / 3.62 s / 462 MB.)

- **INERT fixed points (eval.c).** Every OwnValue binding (an iterator, a
  `Module` local) bumped the eval clock and so invalidated every cached fixed
  point. Only literal GROUND data survived. The recursion's 10^4-10^5-term
  symbolic sums were therefore re-canonicalised after every binding: `Sum` over
  13 stored pieces took 12 s against 2.3 s for `Plus @@`, and `Do` reading a
  stored 135135-term list never finished.
  - **What is INERT.** A fixed point built from numbers, symbols with no value
    or rules, and calls of `Plus`/`Times`/`Power`, a ground head, or a
    rule-free user head. It carries a second flag bit (bit 62).
  - **What invalidates it.** Each symbol it relied on is flagged
    `inert_observed`. A binding or DownValue for a flagged symbol, or for one
    of those builtin heads, advances `g_inert_epoch`. So does every other
    (conservative) clock bump, attribute change, `Clear`/`Remove`, and
    `Block` restore.
  - **Effect.** Reading a stored 2002-term symbolic sum 200 times in a `Do`
    loop went from 0.90 s to 42 µs. Fifteen invalidation scenarios (values,
    DownValues, UpValues, attributes, `Times`/`Power` rules, iterators, `Block`,
    `Module`, DownValue-stored results) match the reference, and are pinned in
    `eval_timestamps_tests`.
- **Builtins certify their own fixed points.** The evaluator ran `Plus` and
  `Times` a second time on their own result, only to see it unchanged.
  - `builtin_plus` now stamps its result (`eval_mark_fixed_point`) when every
    term is a machine integer, a rule-free symbol, or an evaluated
    `Times`/`Power`/user-headed call, and no rule can fire.
  - The new `Times` fast path does the same.
  - `Plus` also re-emits an unmerged term's own evaluated node, rather than an
    unevaluated `coeff * base` rebuild.
- **Adaptive Orderless sort.** `expr_orderless_sort` detects ascending runs
  and merges them pairwise, O(n log k) for k runs. A sum of k canonical sums is
  k sorted runs. `expr_compare` gains exact fast paths for two machine integers
  and for two calls with the same non-arithmetic head.
- **`Times` fast path.** A product of machine-integer coefficients and
  distinct opaque factors (non-System, rule-free heads or symbols) is its
  folded coefficient followed by the sorted factors. It skips the collector's
  passes, none of which can fire on such a product.
  `MATHILDA_TIMES_FAST_CHECK=1` runs both paths and aborts on a mismatch.
- **Expand.**
  - An already-expanded product or sum (stamped, with nothing to distribute)
    is returned as is. Before, every term was multiplied back together factor
    by factor.
  - An allocation-free term-count bound skips the kernel-degree estimate when
    it already fits.
  - The field expander's scan declines at the first opaque node.
  - Head dispatch compares interned pointers.
- **Rule application.** A rule with no bindings (`res[14] = ...`) returns its
  right-hand side without walking it: 20 ms → 0.4 µs per read of a 10^4-term
  value.
- **Cases `_h`** collects a match without the matcher or a `MatchEnv`.
- **`intern_symbol`** recognises an already-canonical pointer through a small
  pointer cache. Re-hashing `SYM_*` names was about 10% of the workload.
- **Block fix (wrong answer).** `Block` restored values and rules behind the
  evaluator's back, so a fixed point stamped inside it survived:
  - `x = 5; Block[{x}, x + y]` gave `x + y`; now `5 + y`.
  - `Block[{f}, f[3]]` with `f[a_] := a^2` gave `f[3]`; now `9`.
  - `test_rule_dispatch` had pinned the stale `{99, lf[2]}`. 13.2 and 14 both
    give `{99, 21}`.
- **Sort bug caught in development, not shipped.** The run detector's
  early-out first treated a short unsorted input as sorted. It is covered by
  a 4000-case ordering stress check.

## Factor over Gaussian coefficients: 63 s to 26 ms (v0.6051, issue #114)

FLINT's `fmpq_mpoly_factor` declines `Complex` coefficients, so a
multivariate polynomial with `I` in it went to the classical factoriser. On
the issue's 28-term, 30-kernel irreducible polynomial that took 63 s.
- **Degree-one irreducibility.** `factor_degree_one` now concludes P is
  irreducible when P is degree 1 in some variable v with
  gcd(coefficient of v, constant term) = 1 (Gauss's lemma). Before, it moved on
  to the next variable and then fell into the variable-specialisation split.
  The shortcut is gated on every variable being a genuine indeterminate:

  path on the regression set.

## Simplify: log fusion is sound, and fuses rational multiples (v0.6049)

SimpLogRules' fuser rewrites `Sum c_i Log[a_i]` as `Log[Prod a_i^c_i]`.
**Wrong answers removed.**
- **The unsound gate.** Without provable positivity, the fuser accepted any
  result that "collapsed to a small constant". The two forms differ by
  `2 Pi I k`, so that gate was unsound:
  - `Simplify[Log[x] + Log[1/x]]` was 0, which is false on the negative axis
    (`Log[-2] + Log[-1/2] = 2 Pi I`).
  - `Simplify[Log[-x] + Log[-1/x], x > 0]` was 0; the value is `2 Pi I`.
  - `Log[x^2 - y^2] - Log[x - y] - Log[x + y]` was 0; it is `-2 Pi I` at
    `x = -2, y = 1`.
- **The new rule.** Without positivity the fuser fuses only a numeric
  constant, and only when a 30-digit evaluation shows the difference is 0,
  which decides `k` exactly.

**Rational multiples.** When every coefficient is an integer multiple `r_i` of
the first, the fuser builds `c Log[Prod a_i^r_i]`. Evaluation never merges
`Sqrt[a] Sqrt[b]`, so the old form could not cancel: `1/2 Log[1 - x] +
1/2 Log[(1 + x)/(1 - x)]` is now `1/2 Log[1 + x]` under `0 < x < 1`. A
negative common factor is flipped, so `Log[2 x] - Log[x]` stays `Log[2]`.

**Positivity of Log arguments.**
- Now also proved through the reduction entailment and Refine, so compound
  arguments such as `1 - x` and `(1 + x)/(1 - x)` on a box count.
- `prov_pos` derives `Tan[u] > 0` and `Cot[u] > 0` from same-signed `Sin[u]`
  and `Cos[u]`.

**Region identities.** A sampled constant such as `ArcTanh[1/4] -
1/2 Log[5/3]` (the sample `x = 1/4`) is certified through `TrigToExp`, but
only when every resulting Log argument is a positive real. `ArcTan`'s complex
logs would otherwise "prove" its Pi-valued addition residue is 0. So
`Simplify[ArcTanh[x] - Log[(1 + x)/(1 - x)]/2, 0 < x < 1/2]` is 0. Before,
only the symmetric interval `-1 < x < 1` worked, because its sample `x = 0`
gives 0 directly.

## Limit: a negative constant times a divergent exponential is -Infinity (v0.6046)

`Limit[-E^x, x -> Infinity]` was **Infinity**. So were `-E^E^x` and
`-2 x^x`. `Limit[E^(-E^x), x -> Infinity]` was Infinity rather than 0, because
its exponent is such a product.
- **Cause.** The log reduction for a power product (`layer5_log_reduction`)
  took the Log of *every* factor. A constant `-1` contributed `Log[-1] = I Pi`
  beside a divergent log, and `Infinity + I Pi` exponentiated to `Infinity`.
- **Fix.** The constant factors now stay out of the log and multiply the
  result afterwards. An infinite result takes its direction from `Sign` of
  the constant, which also folds `Sqrt[2 Pi] Infinity` from Gamma's Stirling
  form. A constant of unknown sign gives no answer from this layer.
- **Found by** `gruntz_tests`. Thesis example 8.8 is right under
  `Method -> "Gruntz"`, but the Automatic cascade answered Infinity first.
  `limit_tests` now pins these cases.

## Limit's Assumptions option reaches Series, Simplify and the zero tests (v0.6044)

## Sum: Gosper antidifferences are certified (v0.6041)

`Sum[Binomial[3, k] x^k, {k, 0, 3}]` was **0**, and `{k, 0, 2}` gave `-x^3`. A
finite sum tries the closed-form cascade before enumerating, and
`Sum`Gosper` returned a false antidifference.
- **Cause.** Gosper's key equation is solved by `SolveAlways`, which solves
  for *every* symbol except the summation index. For `Binomial[3, k] x^k` it

- **Found by** `expand_tests`: `Expand[(x + 2)^60] === Sum[Binomial[60, k] x^k

## Root's canonical order no longer depends on LAPACK roundoff (v0.6040)

## Integrate: an exponential of a trig function gets a correct tower (v0.6038)

  `ClassifyPrime` on a constant does not terminate. `TowerSpecialPlaces` now

  with a real parameter, as in `E^(a Tan[x])`.

## A failing rule on a wide sum no longer tries every subset ; v0.6032

Matching a sum against `c1_. Cosh[a_] + c2_. Cosh[b_] + r___ /; cond`, the
negative-argument rule TrigReduce applies at the end, took time exponential in
the number of terms whenever the condition failed: 0.04 s at 8 terms, 10 s at
16. Two independent subset scans in the Flat matcher caused it, and each was
2ⁿ on its own:

- **The Optional-coefficient element.** For a Flat head, an element may take a
  group `Plus[t1, ..., tk]` of the operands, and the matcher drops the k > 1
  spans only when the element's head can never match such a group.
  `Times[c1_., Cosh[a_]]` kept them, because Times is OneIdentity with an
  Optional argument, and through defaults that can absorb a group
  (`Power[y_, n_.]` takes `2 x` as `y`). But once the optionals take their
  defaults, what is left must match the group. Two or more required arguments
  keep the Times head, and one required call on a fixed head that is not
  OneIdentity (`Cosh[a_]`) cannot equal a Plus. Those spans are now skipped.
- **The leftover retry.** When the whole-expression match fails, ReplaceAll
  retries a Flat head with the pattern augmented by a trailing
  `$FlatRest$___`, so that `a + b + c /. a + b -> w` gives `c + w`. With an
  `r___` already in the pattern, `r` was no longer last, so it ran through
  every subset of the operands. For an Orderless head this retry can never
  succeed when the pattern has an untyped, untested `___`/`__` whose name
  appears nowhere else in the pattern and in no guard of the replacement. Any
  augmented match becomes a plain match by giving the leftovers to `r`, and
  nothing reads `r` to reject that. The retry is skipped in that case. A
  condition that does read `r` (`a + r___ /; Length[{r}] == 2`) still gets it.

The TrigReduce call takes 5 ms, down from 20.6 s. `DSolve` of the forced
system `{x' == 2x + 4y + 3E^t, y' == 5x - y - t^2}` (§2.2.10-924) went from
0.3 s to 50 s when v0.591 made `-(u + v)` distribute: the
variation-of-parameters Simplify then handed TrigReduce a 16-term Cosh/Sinh
sum with `Sqrt[89]` coefficients. It now takes 1 s, and
`dsolve_corpus_2_2_10` passes again. Results are unchanged: the matcher, rule,
pattern and trig suites and the 311-case match stress corpus pass, and a probe
set of flat-leftover and OneIdentity-default rewrites gives the same output as
before. New test: `test_match_flat_orderless_no_subset_blowup` (30 terms).

## N[expr, p] keeps its p-digit promise under cancellation ; v0.6031

`N[expr, p]` used to evaluate once, at a fixed working precision (p digits plus
64 guard bits), and reported p digits whether cancellation had destroyed them
or not. At `p = 20`, `N[Cos[10^-20] - 1]` came back as an exact `0.0`; the
true value is `-5*10^-41`. At `p = 32` it had 11 correct digits out of 32. The
exact zeros `E^(I Pi/4) - (-1)^(1/4)` and `Sin[Pi/7]^2 + Cos[Pi/7]^2 - 1` came
back as `10^-52`- and `10^-60`-size values, and which precisions showed the
noise looked random. Now:

- MPFR addition and subtraction record the worst cancellation, i.e. the leading
  bits a sum lost (`note_cancellation`). When that exceeds the guard headroom,
  `N` re-evaluates with 64 more bits, until two successive results agree to p
  digits, up to `p + $MaxExtraPrecision` digits.
- At the limit, a part still shrinking is the residue of an exact cancellation
  and becomes `0`. Any other part that never settled gives `N::meprec`.
- A higher precision that comes back worse (`Overflow[]`) stops the climb. That
  is what exposed the BarnesG overflow fixed in v0.6030.

Only the `N` builtin's `N[expr, p]` adapts. Internal working-precision
numericalization evaluates once, and an expression that cancels nothing pays
nothing: `dsolve_corpus_2_2_51` went from 144 s to 134 s. New tests:
`test_n_adaptive_precision` and `test_barnesg_arbitrary_precision`, and
`numeric_tests` passes again. The full suite shows no regressions, and
`fit_tests`, `integer_assume_simplify_tests`, `numeric_tests` and
`sf_machine_error_tests` now pass.

## BarnesG at arbitrary precision goes through arb ; v0.6030

`N[BarnesG[37/10], 80]` was `Overflow[]`, and at 200 digits it did not finish.
The numeric kernel uses a fixed 12-term asymptotic series. To reach D digits it
shifts the argument by `R ~ 10^((D+4)/24)` and divides out R Gamma factors:
about 50 of them at 30 digits and about 3200 at 80. At 80 digits,
`Exp[(Z^2/2) Log[Z]] ~ e^(4*10^7)` came back `Overflow[]`, after the cost had
grown 50-fold. Above machine precision, `BarnesG` now uses arb's
`acb_barnes_g` (`flint_num_barnesg`), which is rigorous at any precision:
80 digits take 0.6 ms and 200 digits 1.7 ms. The series remains the fallback
and the machine path, so the machine-precision surfaces still agree bit for
bit. The 75- and 200-digit values agree, and
`N[BarnesG[x + 2] - x Gamma[x]^2 BarnesG[x] /. x -> 17/10, 30]` is `0.0` in
1 ms, down from 1.15 s.

## Erf of a huge complex argument keeps its imaginary part ; v0.6029

`Erf[1.*^300 + 1.*^300 I]` was the Real `1.0`. From first principles,
`erf z = 1 - erfc z`, with `erfc z ~ e^(-z^2)/(z Sqrt[Pi])`. At this `z`,
`|e^(-z^2)| = 1`, so `erfc z` has magnitude about `4*10^-301`, and `erf z` is
1 plus a complex number of that size, which fits in a double. For `|z|^2 > 400`,
arb's `erf` is accurate relative to `|erf z| ~ 1`, so the imaginary part came
back as the midpoint 0. `Erf` now computes `1 - erfc z` with `erfc` from arb.
`erfc` carries both parts componentwise and is never close to 1 here, so
nothing cancels. For `Re z < 0` it uses oddness instead. When arb's 53-bit
`erfc` is itself `[0 +- r]` (as at `10^300 (1 + i)`), `erfc` is retried at a
higher precision, as `Erfc[]` already did. Result:
`Erf[1.*^300 + 1.*^300 I] = 1.0 - 8.18*^-302 I`, which is exactly `1 - Erfc`
of the same argument, and `Erf[-z] = -Erf[z]`. The imaginary part is now kept
from `10^10` up to `10^300`. Values whose imaginary part is below the double
range, such as `Erf[1.*^3 + 20. I]` (`Im ~ 10^-434125`), are still a correctly
rounded `1.0`. The test that expected `Erf` to stay unevaluated now asserts
the derived properties.

## N: the inexact-contagion flag leaves NumericSpec ; v0.6028

`Fit[{{0,1},{1,0},{3,2},{5,4}}, {1, x, x^2}, x, WorkingPrecision -> 24]`
returned the exact fit `135/199 - 53/199 x + 38/199 x^2` in `fit_tests` at

## Simplify / Refine / Reduce: E^(I x) is not positive, 2 n is not even, for real x, n ; v0.6027

For real (not integer) `n`, `Simplify[Arg[Exp[I Pi (2 n + 1/2)]] - Pi/2,
Element[n, Reals]]` returned `0`. That is false: at `n = 0.3` the expression is
`-4.398`. Three separate unsound steps, each fixed at its source:

- **Positivity** (`prov_pos`): "a positive base to any power is positive"
  treated `E^((2 I) n Pi)` as positive, so `Arg[I E^(2 I n Pi)]` became `Pi/2`.
  It now requires a real exponent (`p^q = Exp[q Log[p]]` is positive only for
  real `q`), as the `Exp[...]` branch already did.
- **Reduce over the Reals**: the univariate sign diagram assumes a real-valued
  function. It sampled `E^(I x)` at `x = 0`, saw `1 > 0` and no real root, and
  answered `True` for `Reduce[E^(I x) > 0, x, Reals]` (the truth is
  `x == 2 Pi k`). `Refine` uses that entailment, so `Refine[Arg[E^(I x)],
  Element[x, Reals]]` became `0`. An ordering inequality with a visibly
  complex side now becomes its real meaning, `Im[l - r] == 0 && Re[l - r] > 0`
  (through `ComplexExpand`), before any engine sees it. If that does not
  settle, the call stays unevaluated. `Reduce[I > 0, x, Reals]` is `False`.
- **Parity of an exponent** (`ivp_term_decompose`): an even literal coefficient
  returned "vanishes mod 2" before the other factors were shown to be integers.
  So `(-1)^(2 n)`, reached as `E^(2 I n Pi)` through the one ungated branch of
  the root-of-unity rule, became `1` for real `n`. Every factor is now checked
  first.

`Simplify[E^(2 I n Pi), Element[n, Integers]]` is still `1`, `E^(I n Pi)` is
still `(-1)^n`, and `E^(2 I n Pi + x)` is still `E^x`. New tests:
`test_complex_valued_inequalities` (test_reduce.c) and seven soundness and
integer checks in test_integer_assume_simplify.c. Full suite: no regressions,
and `integer_assume_simplify_tests` passes again.

## Dialog ; v0.6025

## Definitions are ordered by pattern generality ; v0.6022

  `Complex[-1, 2]` is `False` (it was `True`), and `Complex[1, -2]` is `True`.
- **FeynCalc:** this fixes 2 more `SUNSimplify` failures.
  `colorFactor[a] /; SyntacticNegativeQ[a] :> -colorFactor[ExpandAll[-a]]`
  never fired on a negative sum, so the colour factor stayed unexpanded.
- **Mathilda's own caller is unaffected.** `MinusSignQ` in the special-function
  integrator already tests a sum's first term itself.

## OptionsPattern in any position ; v0.6011

- **`OptionsPattern[]` now matches when it is not the last pattern element.**
  It takes a leading run of option arguments, longest first and down to none,
  and leaves the rest to the later elements, as in the reference. Before, it
  took every remaining argument and failed on any that was not an option, so
  `MatchQ[t[x], t[OptionsPattern[], a_]]` was `False`.
- **The `Orderless` case.** On an `Orderless` head the slot sorts first, so
  `SetAttributes[s, Orderless]; s[x, y] /. s[args__, OptionsPattern[]] :> ...`
  never matched. That was a silent wrong value.
- **FeynCalc:** this was 10 of the 17 `SUNSimplify` failures. `SUND` is
  `Orderless`, and both its "unique coloured objects" step and its own
  DownValues are written this way.

## A condition does not reorder a definition ; v0.6010

- **DownValue order no longer promotes a conditioned rule.**
  - Each condition layer used to add to a rule's specificity score, so
    `f[p[a1_, b1_]] := "gen" /; True` was tried before an earlier
    `f[p[a_, a_]] := "spec"`.
  - A rule written `rhs /; c1 /; c2` jumped ahead of ones with a single
    condition.
  - In both cases a different rule fired, with no message: a wrong value.
- **Conditions and pattern tests (`x_?test`) are now neutral, as in the
  reference, wherever they sit in the pattern.** Between two rules that are
  the same once the guards are removed, the guarded one still goes first:
  `g[x_] := 1; g[x_ /; x > 0] := 2` tries the guard first.
- **FeynCalc:** this was all 28 `FromGFAD` failures. `fromGFAD`'s eikonal rule
  `... /; c1 /; c2` had moved ahead of the square-completion rules.

## Rational and Complex numbers are atoms to rules and searches ; v0.6009

- **Replacement rules no longer reach inside a number** (a wrong value until
  now). This covers `ReplaceAll`, `ReplaceRepeated` and `Replace` at any level:
  - `{1/2} /. 2 -> 3` was `{1/3}`, and `{1/2} /. 1 -> 5` was `{5/2}`.
  - `{1/2} /. Rational -> f` was `{f[1, 2]}`, and `{I} /. Complex -> h` was
    `{h[0, 1]}`.
  - All of these now stay as they are, as in the reference. A rule for the
    whole number (`Rational[a_, b_] -> ...`) still applies.
- **`Cases`, `Count`, `Position` and `DeleteCases` treat a number as an atom.**
  `Count[{1/2}, 2, Infinity]` is `0` and `Position[{I}, Complex]` is `{}`.
  `DeleteCases[{1/2}, 2, Infinity]` used to corrupt the number into
  `Rational[1]`.
- **`FreeQ` and `MemberQ` now see a number's head at the number's own level**
  when heads are included. `FreeQ[I x, Complex]` and `FreeQ[f[1/2], Rational]`
  are `False`, and `MemberQ[f[1/2], Rational, {1}, Heads -> True]` is `True`.
  `FreeQ` keeps `Heads -> True` with an explicit level spec.
- **FeynCalc:** its `MomentumCombine` gates its complex-momentum step on
  `FreeQ[expr, Complex]`. All 16 `FCLoopSwitchEtaSign` failures were this.
- **`Limit` rejects a number as its variable**, with `Limit::ivar`, as the
  reference does. `Block[{x}, x = 5; Limit[Sin[x]/x, x -> 0]]` used to answer
  `0`, and then `1` once `/.` stopped rewriting inside `1/5`.
- **Exposed: a test of real-form Risch output was vacuous.** The check
  `FreeQ[g, Complex]` behind it never saw complex numbers, so it always passed.
  It now shows that `1/(3 + 5 Cos[x])` still integrates to complex-argument
  logs. That case is asserted for correctness only until the real
  reconstruction handles it.

## Times: numeric powers over a coprime basis ; v0.6008

- **When an exponent is symbolic, `Times` now writes numeric powers whose
  bases share a factor over one coprime basis**, as the reference does.
  - `4^a 6^b` is `2^(2 a + b) 3^b`, and the coefficient is absorbed:
    `12 2^n` is `3 2^(2 + n)` and `4^a/2` is `2^(-1 + 2 a)`.
  - Equal exponents merge first (`2^a 8^a` is `16^a`), and
    `(2/3)^a (9/4)^b` is `(2/3)^(a - 2 b)`.
  - The basis comes from gcds alone, so FeynCalc's products of huge prime
    powers collapse with no factoring.
  - All 16 probes match the reference.
- **The O3 split now reads bignum rationals.** Its sign test had looked only at
  `int64` rationals, so `(p/N^4)^a` was not split when `N^4` was a bignum.

## Simplify: exponent normal form and a cross-term coprime basis ; v0.6007

- **Equal exponents and arguments, written differently, now cancel.**
  `Simplify[a^(3 (-1 + e)) - a^(-3 + 3 e)]` and
  `Simplify[g[2 (1 + e)] - g[2 + 2 e]]` are `0`, as in the reference. Before
  and after the search, two compound exponents (or ordinary-head arguments) in
  the same place that agree after `Expand` both take the factored expansion.
  An exponent whose expansion is strictly smaller is expanded:
  `Simplify[la^2 (la^2)^(e - 1), la > 0]` is `la^(2 e)`, where it was
  `la^(2 + 2 (-1 + e))`.
- **Numeric powers across the whole expression are written over one coprime
  basis** (gcds only), with each basis power held as an opaque generator while
  the search runs. Terms that are one power family in different clothes then
  cancel. FeynCalc's `FactorList2` check, a sum of products of random-prime
  powers, is now `0`, and `FactorList2` ID6 now passes.

## Powers with a symbolic exponent as polynomial generators ; v0.6006

- **`Variables`, `Factor` and `FactorList` read `b^(n + k t)` as
  `b^n (b^t)^k`**, as the reference does (`src/poly/expsplit.c`).
  - `Variables[x^(1 + e) + x^(2 + e)]` is `{x, x^e}`.
  - `Factor[x^(2 + e) - x^e]` is `(-1 + x) x^e (1 + x)`.
  - `FactorList[(x + y)^(-3 + 3 e)]` is `{{1, 1}, {(x + y)^e, 3}, {x + y, -3}}`.
  - Each power was previously one opaque atom: `Factor` and `FactorList`
    left such sums unfactored, and `Variables` listed `x^(1 + e)` itself.
- **How it works.** Each `b^t` becomes a private symbol, the builtin runs on
  the rewritten input, and the generators go back in (`K^k -> b^(k t)`).
  - An integer coefficient of `t` is the generator's degree. A non-integer one
    keeps its term whole (`x^(e/2)`).
  - A numeric base is a constant to `Variables`, as there.
- **Results.**
  - The 14 new expectations are identical to the reference's output.
  - FeynCalc's `FactorList2` ID7, ID8 and ID11 now pass.
  - ID5, ID6 and ID10 still need `Simplify` to normalise exponents
    (`a^(3 (-1 + e)) - a^(-3 + 3 e)`).
