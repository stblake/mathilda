# Changelog: week of 2026-11-02 (Mon) – 2026-11-08 (Sun)

## N[expr, n] certifies its digits under cancellation (v0.6283)

- **Wrong answers fixed.** `N[E^1000 (E^(1/1000 - E^-1000) - E^(1/1000)), 30]`
  returned 0 (two exact-zero passes counted as agreement), a 1-bit last step
  "confirmed" rounding noise (18 good digits reported as 30), and
  `N[(1 + 10^-40)^(10^40) - E, 10]` returned 3.5575743793 silently. A pass is now
  accepted only if it agrees to n digits with a run at least 32 bits lower and
  every cancelling sum kept at least n bits; steps jump by the measured loss; at
  `$MaxExtraPrecision` an unsettled result is returned at the digits it can claim
  with `N::meprec` (an `Overflow[]` too). A non-cancelling N costs one pass.

## E^z at arbitrary precision is exp(z), correctly rounded (v0.6282)

- **Wrong digits fixed.** The interpreter had no direct rule for `E^z` with an
  MPFR z: the mixed exact/inexact retry computed `N[E, p]^z`, multiplying E's
  rounding error by z, one ulp off the correctly rounded exp(z) in about half of
  all inputs (1263 of 2000 at 30 digits, 1965 for complex), and no longer
  bit-identical to the compiled path (which lowers `E^x` to exp since v0.6261).
  `E^z` now uses the same MPFR exp as `Exp[z]` (real and complex), so
  interpreter, `Exp` and `Compile` agree bit for bit.

## Integrate: logs of quadratics, Möbius log kernels, products of two logs (v0.6281)

- The PolyLog ladder splits the log of a real polynomial without real roots
  into linear logs (split at each partial-fraction pole), accepts logs of
  (a x + b)/(c x + d) oriented off the PolyLog cut, and reduces products of two
  logs by L_i L_j = (L_i^2 + L_j^2 - (L_i - L_j)^2)/2.
  `Integrate[Log[1 + x^2]/(1 + x^2), {x, 0, 1}] = Pi Log[2]/2 - Catalan` (55 ms) and
  `Integrate[Log[1 - x] Log[1 + x]/x, {x, 0, 1}] = -5 Zeta[3]/8` (99 ms); both were
  unevaluated.

## FunctionExpand of PolyLog[2, z] by the standard identity maps; Li3 conjugate pairs (v0.6280)

- `PolyLog[2, z]` closes when one of the standard maps (1 - z, 1/z, ...) sends z to a
  root of unity, 1/2 or 2 (each candidate checked to 30 digits). The definite-
  integral final pass closes Li3(z) + Li3(conj z) with Re z = 1/2 or Re 1/z = 1/2
  (Li3(1 + I) + Li3(1 - I) = Pi^2 Log[2]/16 + 35 Zeta[3]/32) and splits
  `Log[1 + I]`-type values so conjugate pieces cancel.

## Series of PolyLog where its argument vanishes (v0.6279)

- `Series[PolyLog[s, u], ...]` at a point where u = 0 used the defining series
  instead of Taylor via D (0/0), so endpoint limits such as
  `Log[1 - x] PolyLog[2, 1 - x]` at x = 1 evaluate.

## PolyLog at complex arbitrary-precision arguments through arb (v0.6278)

- Off the cut [1, ∞), an arbitrary-precision `PolyLog[s, z]` at complex z goes
  through arb's `acb_polylog` (~1 ms) instead of an expansion that called
  `mpfr_zeta` per term (0.3–0.6 s per call): a 30-digit NIntegrate of a PolyLog
  integrand 3.7 s -> 84 ms.

## FindRoot: the overflow rerun stops polishing at double resolution (v0.6277)

- A machine solve escalated to MPFR (|f| past the double range) stops once the
  iterate is resolved to a double.

## RiemannSiegelZ and RiemannSiegelTheta (v0.6276)

- **New:** `RiemannSiegelZ[t]` and `RiemannSiegelTheta[t]`, machine and arbitrary
  precision through arb's Hardy Z and theta (with the LogGamma/Zeta definitions as
  the fallback without FLINT); `RiemannSiegelTheta[0] = 0`,
  `RiemannSiegelZ[0] = Zeta[1/2]`; real for real t; unary packed/NDArray kernels
  and `Compile[]` share the machine helper. The 10th zero of Z is
  49.773832477672302182 (50 digits in 18 ms).

## JacobiP (v0.6275)

- **New:** `JacobiP[n, a, b, x]`. Exact polynomials from the sum form (valid for a
  negative integer a), symbolic parameters expanded to degree 40, numeric values
  by the three-term recurrence (machine helper shared by the scalar path, the
  packed/NDArray kernel and `Compile[]`; MPFR real or complex), a non-integer
  degree or a + b a negative integer <= -2 through `Hypergeometric2F1`; derivative
  rule; `Listable`, `NumericFunction`, `Protected`.

## FindRoot: Newton stops only when both the residual and the step meet the goal (v0.6274)

- **Wrong digits fixed.** Newton stopped when either the residual or the step test
  passed: a start one step from the root returned after that step
  (`FindRoot[ChebyshevT[2001, x], {x, 0.9999997}]` gave 1 - x = 3.07990e-7, true
  3.081169277e-7), and `FindRoot[Sin[x], {x, 3.}]` gave Pi to 8 digits. Both tests
  must now hold at the accepted point; when only the step test passes, Newton
  keeps stepping (up to 8 steps) until the residual passes or the steps reach
  noise. The machine finite-difference step shrinks with Newton's steps;
  `StepMonitor` sees the new iterate.

## ChebyshevT of high degree by its closed form (v0.6273)

- Above degree 1000 the machine helper uses Cos[n ArcCos[x]] (Cosh[n ArcCosh[|x|]]
  with the sign for |x| > 1), within ~3.7× of the error of rounding x itself; the
  MPFR path uses the same closed form above degree 64 (the machine path declined
  and every evaluation took the MPFR recurrence).

## LegendreP of an exact degree at an inexact point by its recurrence (v0.6272)

- **Wrong answer fixed.** `LegendreP[1000, x]` at an arbitrary-precision x
  substituted x into the expanded polynomial (coefficients ~1e600), so
  `LegendreP[1000, 0.999997`50]` was 2.4e329 after 2 s and FindRoot stalled at
  its start. The three-term recurrence now runs in MPFR (real or complex) with
  guard bits: 50 digits in 0.7 ms; the holdout's 50-digit root 36 s -> 8.5 ms.

## NIntegrate: complex-valued integrands compile in the complex MPFR domain (v0.6271)

- A complex-valued integrand of a real variable compiles at arbitrary precision:
  the ISSAC '97 problem 4 contour, `Method -> "Trapezoidal"` at 400 digits,
  3.8 s -> 1.7 s on the same host.

## NIntegrate: the stall stop no longer cuts a converging oscillatory integral (v0.6270)

- **Wrong answer fixed** (introduced two versions earlier): the stall stop cut
  `NIntegrate[Cos[1000 Sin[x]], ..., WorkingPrecision -> 40]` early (0.3669 for
  0.0779). It now fires only once the stalled difference is far below the integral.

## NIntegrate: A(x) Sin/Cos[c x^n] in the phase variable (v0.6269)

- **Wrong answers fixed.** An integrand `A(x) Sin[c x^n]` / `A(x) Cos[c x^n]` is
  integrated in t = c x^n, an ordinary half-line Fourier integral:
  `NIntegrate[Sin[x^3], {x, 0, Infinity}, WorkingPrecision -> 40]` (was -1.6e619,
  true 0.4465...) and `Sin[1/x]` on [0, 1] (machine 0.504166, true
  0.5040670619; 70 s at 40 digits, now 0.05 s).

## NIntegrate: endpoint singularities, option values, non-numeric integrands, stalls (v0.6268)

- **Wrong answers fixed.** `NIntegrate[x^(-999/1000) E^-x, {x, 0, 1}]` gave 131
  (machine) and 274 (40 digits) for 999.2042906713882: a strong endpoint power
  |x - a|^p, -1 < p < -1/2, is now mapped away by a substitution (machine cases
  whose mass lies below the double range go to the MPFR path).
- **Option values are evaluated:** `i = 1; NIntegrate[f, {y, 0, 1}, PrecisionGoal -> i]`
  stayed unevaluated (NIntegrate is HoldAll).
- **A non-numeric integrand** stays unevaluated with `NIntegrate::inumr` (it
  returned 0.0).
- **16-digit values at `WorkingPrecision -> 25`** no longer spin: the driver
  stops once it stops improving and issues `NIntegrate::precw`.

## PolyLog of integer order near z = 1 (v0.6267)

- `PolyLog[n, z]`, integer n, 1/2 < z < 1, uses a real series about z = 1:
  `PolyLog[3, z]` at 80 digits near 1 from 2–27 ms to 0.18 ms. A z that rounds to
  the double 1.0 is judged on its exact value (it took the complex path and
  returned a spurious `+ 0. I`).

## NIntegrate: boundary layers and the escalation path (v0.6266)

- MPFR tanh-sinh levels reuse earlier samples and accept a level on a
  quadratic-convergence estimate; an integrand climbing steeply into one endpoint
  is split and each piece integrated by Clenshaw–Curtis; once a machine sample
  overflows, the machine pass stops and the MPFR rerun starts at once (it spent
  ~11000 machine samples first). ISSAC '97 problem 2 on the same host: machine
  80 -> 8.6 ms, 30 digits 113 -> 9.1 ms, 50 digits 805 -> 81 ms, 100 digits
  3067 -> 316 ms (1150 samples, was 10575).

## MPFR powers and ProductLog: faster per-sample evaluation (v0.6265)

- A real `x^y` at arbitrary precision with a positive base and non-integer
  exponent is `exp(y log x)` with guard bits (`mpfr_pow` cost 2.6× as much).
- Real `ProductLog` at arbitrary precision: a precision-tripling Halley
  iteration seeded by the machine value; it now compiles on the MPFR path.

## Arbitrary-precision reals print the digits their precision carries (v0.6264)

- One formatter for arbitrary-precision reals, shared by the printer and
  `TeXForm`. The digit count is floor(bits log10 2), which gives back `n`
  exactly for `N[x, n]`, with trailing zeros kept: `N[1, 30]` prints
  `1.00000000000000000000000000000` (was `1.0`), `N[1/4, 20]` prints
  `0.25000000000000000000`. Layout is positional when -5 <= e < 6 and e < n,
  otherwise `d.ddd e+XX` in the machine-real style (`TeXForm`:
  `\times 10^{XX}`): `N[10^50, 30]` is `1.00...0e+50`, `123.`2` is `1.2e+02`,
  `12.`2` is `12.`. Machine reals are unchanged.
- An arbitrary-precision zero prints `0.` up to accuracy 5 and `0.e-a` beyond
  (`N[0, 30]` is `0.e-30`); its InputForm is `0``a.`.
- `InputForm`/`FullForm` write the shortest digits that identify the stored
  binary value, a precision mark checked to read back to the same number of
  bits, and `*^e` outside -5..5 (`N[10^50, 30]` is
  `1.0000000000000000000000000000004`30.*^50`), so `ToExpression` of the
  string gives back the same value and precision.
- Parser: `0``30` had an extra leading digit of accuracy (31.006, now 30.103,
  as `N[0, 30]`), and a `*^` exponent after an arbitrary-precision mantissa was
  rounded three times; it is now applied to the decimal text in one rounding,
  so `3.355`2.*^-61` is the number it spells.

## NSum: logarithmically slow tails (v0.6263)

- **Wrong answers fixed.** With a log factor the Euler–Maclaurin tail decays like
  1/Log[X] (or 1/Log[Log[X]]) for every X a double holds: machine
  `NSum[1/(n Log[n]^2), {n, 2, Infinity}]` gave 2.10831 (true 2.109743), and at
  `WorkingPrecision -> 30` it and `1/(n Log[n] Log[Log[n]]^2)` did not finish in
  120 s. The tail integral is redone after x = e^t or x = e^(e^t) (the summand
  sampled in MPFR so x reaches 2^(2^30)) until two estimates agree; their
  difference is the error estimate. Now 4 ms machine, ~0.2 s at 30 digits.

## NSum: oscillating and alternating summands summed by frequency (v0.6262)

- **Wrong answers fixed.** Automatic `NSum` sent trigonometric summands to
  Euler–Maclaurin, which fails when the continuous extension oscillates:
  machine `NSum[Sin[n]/n, {n, 1, Infinity}]` gave 1.11244 (true (Pi - 1)/2 =
  1.0708), `Cos[n]/Sqrt[n]` gave -0.391 (true -0.194), and a zero first term
  hid the alternation of `(-1)^n Log[n]/n`. A single-iterator infinite sum is
  now split by frequency (TrigToExp, Expand, factors b^(c n + d)): non-oscillating
  parts go to the usual dispatch, alternating parts to Cohen–Villegas–Zagier, and
  other frequencies to the Abel–Plana formula rotated onto the imaginary axis;
  the oscillating part is computed from two starting points whose difference is
  the error estimate (`NSum::accgl` when it is large).

## Compile (arbitrary precision): E^x as exp(x), b^(p/2) as Sqrt[b]^p (v0.6261)

- At MPFR the compiled `E^x` was a general power (a log by AGM plus an exp per
  evaluation) and `b^(p/2)` likewise; they now lower to exp and to an integer
  power of `Sqrt[b]`, exact principal-branch identities, bit-identical to the
  interpreter. ~60% of a 30-digit compiled-sampling profile was the general power.

## NDSolve into a singular endpoint at WorkingPrecision (v0.6260)

- **Wrong answer fixed.** A 30-digit solve of the Gauss hypergeometric equation
  up to its singular point x = 1 gave y[1] = 2.96e113: the step-size floor was
  judged at the guard-padded precision, so steps reached 1 - 1e-46 and rounded
  onto 1 with derivatives near 10^1000. The floor now uses the returned precision
  (the solution stops ~1e-27 short of 1) and extrapolation falls back to the end
  node's own Taylor polynomial when the last interval's interpolant disagrees with
  it beyond that polynomial's error estimate: y(1) = 2F1(1/3, 1/2; 2; 1) to 23
  digits at 30-digit working precision.
- `Interpolation` compares high-precision domain ends exactly (they were
  rounded to double), and the high-precision evaluation path issues
  `InterpolatingFunction::dmval` outside the data, so `Check` sees extrapolation.

## NDSolve: Abs in the arbitrary-precision Taylor integrator (v0.6259)

- `Abs[a]` is a times its sign up to the next zero of a; a step that would cross
  it is cut to end on the zero (bisection to working precision), and the node
  there stores the derivatives of the side being left. A non-analytic power at
  the zero (`Abs[y]^(3/2)`) is crossed by a few adaptive DOPRI5 steps. Lane–Emden
  n = 3 and n = 3/2 at 30 digits: 1.7 s -> 30 ms and 4.3 s -> 0.11 s (they fell
  back to DOPRI5, and ran out of steps at 50 digits).

## NDSolve: Runge–Kutta and BDF at WorkingPrecision start from data at that precision (v0.6258)

- **Wrong digits fixed.** The MPFR Runge–Kutta and BDF integrators re-read the
  start point, initial values and end point from doubles (`y[0] == 1/3` entered
  at 16 digits) and used the double DOPRI5 tableau (exact to ~1e-17). They now
  take all three at the working precision, land the last step exactly on the
  end, and round an exact-fraction tableau once: Lane–Emden n = 1 at 30 digits
  3.14159265358979326 -> 3.1415926535897932384678.

## NRoots: real coefficients at their exact binary value (v0.6257)

- A polynomial with machine or arbitrary-precision coefficients is taken at the
  coefficients' exact binary values (no decimal round trip) before root finding.

## NSolve: every solution of a polynomial system (v0.6256)

- **Wrong answer fixed.** Machine `NSolve` dropped valid solutions: a candidate
  from an eigenvector of the multiplication matrix was discarded when its
  absolute residual exceeded 1e-6 (ill-conditioned eigenvectors from a
  small-integer linear form, large coordinates, and points where every term
  vanishes such as Katsura-5's u0 = 1): Katsura-5 gave 24 of 32 solutions. Each
  candidate is now Newton-refined on the original equations and judged by a
  scale-aware relative residual; an attempt that loses a candidate is redone with
  a fresh ~30-bit random form at twice the precision, and clustered eigenvalues
  on one point count as a multiple solution. The quotient ring comes straight
  from the FLINT Gröbner engine; Katsura-5 566 -> 91 ms.

## GroebnerBasis with CoefficientDomain -> InexactNumbers by numeric FGLM (v0.6255)

- The lex basis comes from the degree-reverse-lex basis by FGLM with the linear
  algebra in MPFR (checked by agreement at two precisions, exact route as the
  fallback), not by converting an exact rational basis: ISSAC '97 problem 6
  70 -> 18 ms at the same load; coefficients match the monic exact basis to 1e-14
  (machine) and 1e-38 (40 digits).

## NRoots / NSolve: an adaptive-precision engine for high-degree polynomials (v0.6254)

- Each root gets its own evaluation precision, raised until a rounding-error
  bound proves it to the target; real roots are found largest first by
  Laguerre's method, then Aberth iteration finishes the rest. FLINT squarefree
  decomposition; rational coefficients have their denominators cleared.
  `NSolve[LaguerreL[1000, x] == 0, x, WorkingPrecision -> 50]`: > 600 s -> 4–9 s,
  all 1000 roots real, the largest correct to 50 digits;
  `NSolve[N[Expand[LaguerreL[200, x]]] == 0, x]` 0.7 s (was minutes).

## Binomial with word-sized arguments through mpz_bin_uiui (v0.6253)

- `Binomial[n, k]` with n fitting a machine word (negative-argument extensions
  included) uses `mpz_bin_uiui` (~1 µs) instead of `mpz_bin_ui` (~7 µs for
  `Binomial[355, 150]`): the exact 256 × 256 inverse Hilbert matrix 2.2 -> 0.9 s.

## Eigenvalues of a nonsymmetric arbitrary-precision matrix (v0.6252)

- Hessenberg reduction on arb midpoints with fused dot products (threaded rows
  for large steps); eigenvalue-only Francis sweeps touch the active block only;
  a subdiagonal below u‖A‖_F (the input's own rounding level) deflates. The
  ISSAC '97 problem 9 Nyström matrix at 20 digits: 83 -> 13 sweeps, 0.29 -> 0.04 s;
  the whole Nyström solve 3.9 s -> 0.32 s on the same host. Polynomial root
  finding keeps the purely relative deflation test.

## Outer of a pure-substitution function over numeric lists: compiled once (v0.6251)

- `Outer[f, l1, l2, ...]` with flat machine-real (or one-precision MPFR) lists
  and f a `Function[{x, ...}, body]` or a single unconditional `f[x_, ...] := body`
  compiles the body once and runs it per cell (interpreter fallback per declined
  cell), bit-identical to the interpreter: 64 × 64 `Outer[K, x, x]` with
  K = E^(x + y + x^2 + y^2 + x y + x^2 y^2), 40 -> 1.3 ms machine, 260 -> 47 ms at 20 digits.

## Dot: exact zeros stay on the fused arbitrary-precision path (v0.6250)

- An exact 0 among MPFR entries made the fused dot product decline, so
  `m . DiagonalMatrix[w]` at 20 digits fell back to n^3 symbolic Times/Plus.
  An exact 0 now reads as an MPFR zero at its partner's precision (the value and
  precision `Times[0, x]` gives): 64 × 64 at 20 digits 1.43 s -> 7 ms.

## Limit: a branch-point value re-derived per side (v0.6249)

- **Wrong answer fixed.** `Limit[2 ArcTanh[1 - 2 z] - Log[1 - z], z -> 1, Direction -> "FromBelow"]`
  gave 2 I Pi (true 0): with the side unknown, Series expands an ArcTanh or Log
  branch point on one side only. A value that is Indeterminate, or finite and
  non-real, read off a series whose coefficients still contain the variable, is
  now re-derived with the side assumed; the two-sided limit is then 0 too.

## Limit: high-order cancellation by leading terms; oscillatory gate; linearization fix (v0.6248)

- A leading-term layer builds c t^p of products, integer powers and
  compositions F(G(x)) directly (composition only when G vanishes at the point,
  F's leading coefficient is provably nonzero and its exponent an integer; any
  undecided zero test declines), with a truncated Series only where a sum
  cancels: ISSAC '97 problem 7, `Limit[f[g[x]]/g[f[x]], x -> 0]` = 3060352455.672…
  exactly in ~0.3 s (ran past 120 s and returned unevaluated).
- The oscillatory layer runs only when an oscillating argument diverges at the
  point (it rewrote problem 7 into exponentials at 20 s per pass).
- **Wrong answer fixed:** small-argument linearization (Sin[g] -> g) is applied
  only to multiplicative factors; inside a sum it made
  `Limit[(Sin[x]^2 + Cos[x]^2 - 1)/x^5, x -> 0]` ComplexInfinity.

## Residue: one exact expansion for a rational function over Q (v0.6247)

- At a rational point, the residue of a rational function over Q is the
  (z - z0)^-1 coefficient of a single exact expansion, without the repeated
  cross-check expansions: `Residue[p/x^3001, {x, 0}]` (ISSAC '97 problem 4)
  176 s -> ~0.24 s; a pole of order 200 at 1 of `1/((x - 1)^200 (x + 2)^300)`, 25 ms.

## Series: an exact rational arm over Q through FLINT (v0.6246)

- When every coefficient is rational, series multiplication, inversion, integer
  powers and composition run on FLINT polynomials truncated at the order, not
  through the evaluator: ISSAC '97 problem 4, `Series[p, {x, 0, 3000}]` of a
  product of polynomial powers, 29.2 s -> ~0.1–0.2 s; problem 7's nested
  Sin/Tan/Sinh/Tanh compositions to order 25, ~2 s -> ~30 ms each. An expanded
  polynomial at 0 is read off as its coefficient list; a rational function over
  Q no longer goes through `Apart` first.

## Series: a truncated inversion no longer claims coefficients it did not compute (v0.6245)

- **Wrong answer fixed.** Series inversion stopped after 64 terms but kept the
  requested order, so the missing coefficients read as 0: the x^80 coefficient
  of `1/(1 - x - x^2)` came out 0 instead of `Fibonacci[81]`. Numeric coefficients
  now get the full order; a symbolic series that is cut short moves its O-term
  down to the last computed term.

## FunctionInterpolation (v0.6244)

- **New:** `FunctionInterpolation[expr, {x, xmin, xmax}, ...]` (and grids in
  several dimensions) samples expr adaptively, the variables bound to numbers at
  each sample (an inner `NIntegrate` works), and returns an `InterpolatingFunction`.
  Options `InterpolationOrder`, `InterpolationPoints`, `InterpolationPrecision`,
  `AccuracyGoal`, `PrecisionGoal`, `MaxRecursion`; `SetOptions` applies.
- `InterpolatingFunction` is `NHoldAll`: `N`/`SetPrecision` no longer rewrite its
  internal data (it stopped evaluating). A machine table queried at an
  arbitrary-precision point returns its machine value (was unevaluated).

## GaussianQuadratureWeights and GaussianQuadratureError (v0.6243)

- **New:** `GaussianQuadratureWeights[n, a, b]` and `[n, a, b, prec]`, the
  n-point Gauss–Legendre rule on [a, b] as {x, w} pairs with increasing nodes,
  at machine or `prec`-digit precision (Newton on the Legendre recurrence from
  Tricomi guesses, half the zeros mirrored; MPFR with 32 + 3 log2(n) guard bits;
  each weight from the last Newton step). n = 1000 at machine precision in 4.4 ms.
- **New:** `GaussianQuadratureError[n, f, a, b]`, the rule's error constant.

## Arbitrary-precision dense linear algebra: fused dot products, threaded (v0.6242)

- `Dot` of MPFR vectors and matrices computes each output element as one fused
  dot product (`arb_approx_dot`, falling back to `mpfr_dot`), threaded over
  rows, instead of evaluator `Times`/`Plus` per term: 256 × 256 by a vector at
  800 digits 0.35 s -> 0.02 s.
- `LUDecomposition` of a real MPFR matrix: every entry one fused dot product,
  threaded per step, same pivots (~4.5× faster: 500 digits, 256 × 256, 11 s ->
  2.5 s on the same host); square MPFR `LinearSolve[m, b]` uses it (7.7–18 s -> 1.9 s).
- `Eigenvalues`: threaded tridiagonal reduction; the QR sweep 16 -> 10 products
  per rotation without allocation. ISSAC '97 problem 1 (`Eigenvalues` of
  `N[HilbertMatrix[256], 800]`): 16.1 s -> ~6.5 s.

## LinearSolve at high precision; LUBackSubstitution (v0.6241)

- **Wrong answer fixed.** `LinearSolve[N[HilbertMatrix[256], 500]]` reported
  `LinearSolve::sing1` and its `LinearSolveFunction` returned garbage: the
  singularity test used `MatrixRank`, which rounded MPFR entries to doubles
  (1e-389 underflowed) and judged pivots against machine epsilon
  (`MatrixRank[N[HilbertMatrix[40], 100]]` gave 21). An MPFR matrix is now
  factored once by an MPFR LU, cached in the `LinearSolveFunction` (each
  application two triangular solves), with zero pivots judged relative to the
  working precision; `MatrixRank` of an MPFR matrix eliminates at its own precision.
- `Conjugate` of an arbitrary-precision or big-integer number past ~1.8e308 no
  longer stays unevaluated.
- **New: `LUBackSubstitution[{lu, p, c}, b]`** solves m.x == b from
  `LUDecomposition[m]`, for a vector or matrix b, at machine precision (packed
  buffers in and out), arbitrary precision and exactly, real or complex
  (`::matsq`, `::lslc`, `::singlu`).

## Precision and Accuracy of a list or matrix: one number (v0.6240)

- `Precision` and `Accuracy` were `Listable`, so `Precision[matrix]` returned a
  matrix. They now return a single value, the minimum over the expression's
  numbers (exact parts count as `Infinity`). A packed array answers from its
  dtype (an integer buffer is exact); a machine `SparseArray` is
  `MachinePrecision` (was `Infinity`). Both are packed-aware.

## FunctionExpand: PolyLog[2, 2] and PolyLog[3, 2]; tidier definite integrals (v0.6239)

- `FunctionExpand[PolyLog[2, 2]] = Pi^2/4 - I Pi Log[2]`,
  `FunctionExpand[PolyLog[3, 2]] = 7 Zeta[3]/8 + Pi^2 Log[2]/4 - I Pi Log[2]^2/2`
  (plain evaluation leaves them).
- The final pass of a definite integral rewrites `Log[1/n]` as `-Log[n]` and
  expands, keeping the result only when strictly smaller.

## Integrate: complex definite values are not divergences (v0.6238)

- Newton–Leibniz reported `Integrate::idiv` for every complex value of a
  real-looking integrand, which is wrong when the integrand is itself complex
  inside the interval: `Integrate[x PolyLog[2, x], {x, 0, 2}]`,
  `Integrate[Sqrt[x - 1], {x, 0, 2}] = 2/3 + 2/3 I`,
  `Integrate[Log[x], {x, -1, 1}] = -2 + I Pi`. The integrand is sampled at
  interior points; a complex value is accepted after a complex NIntegrate check.

## Integrate: rational × PolyLog[n, Möbius or monomial argument] (v0.6237)

## Issue #126, second follow-up: negative bases in Times, power content and quotient terms in Simplify (v0.6225)

Three deficiencies reported against v0.6175, and the causes behind them.

- **`Simplify[(-2)^x/2]` stayed as it was; the reference gives `(-1)^x 2^(-1 + x)`.** That form comes from the reference's *evaluation* of `Times`, not from Simplify. `(-2)^x/2 - (-1)^x 2^(x - 1)` evaluates to `0` there, and in Mathilda it did not. The coprime-basis pass in `src/times.c` (O4) now admits negative rational bases through their magnitude. `(-n)^e = (-1)^e n^e` holds for every complex `e`, because `arg(-1) + arg(n) = Pi` lies in `(-Pi, Pi]`. The sign goes back onto a basis element of the power's own value whose exponent is still `e`, or else into one `(-1)^(...)`. The same pass also follows the reference's merge order more closely: same-base powers add exponents, then equal-exponent values multiply, and the basis is taken of what remains. So `6^x 4^x 9` is `8^x 3^(2 + x)`, as in the reference (was `2^(3 x) 3^(2 + x)`). A coefficient equal to a negative base merges only when that power is the first (smallest base) to share a prime with it, as the reference's canonical order gives it. Details and cases are in `docs/spec/builtins/arithmetic.md`.
- **`Simplify[(4^eps (a + b)^eps)/(4 a + 4 b)^eps] === 1` was False.** Factor and FactorTerms do not run on expressions with non-integer powers, and Times only splits a power of an explicit product, so the content of `4 a + 4 b` was invisible inside the power. A new Simplify transform, `PowerContent`, writes `(c u)^e` as `c^e u^e` for the positive rational content `c` of a sum, which holds for every `u` and `e`. The quotient then evaluates to `1`. `Simplify[Sqrt[4 a + 4 b]]` is `2 Sqrt[a + b]`.
- **`((4 - 4 eps) y)/(-1 + y^2) + z` was not simplified term by term** (open since v0.6175). The rational pipeline now offers each quotient term of a sum simplified on its own, taken at no worse score while the input is still the best.

Found along the way:
- `Simplify[(-4 a - 4 b)^eps/(a + b)^eps]` returned a *costlier* form (SimplifyCount 23 from 20). The bottom-up pass simplified the base to `-4 (a + b)`, and the rebuilt product evaluated to `4^eps (-a - b)^eps`. When evaluation alone lifts a rebuilt node above the original, the original node now goes on. A child rewrite that is costlier by design (a special-function canonical form) raises the unevaluated rebuild too, and is kept. A blanket "never worse than the input" check was tried and rejected: it undid those deliberate forms (the Bessel recurrence) and broke DiffUnderInt.
- The split signs reach Simplify as `(-1)^(3 + n)`. A second transform, `NegativeBasePower`, writes `c^(k + r)` as `c^k c^r` for a negative rational `c` and an exact `k`, taking ties as the reference does: `(-1)^(2 + n) a` → `(-1)^n a`, `-((-4)^(-1 + x) 3^x)` → `(-3)^x 4^(-1 + x)`.

Measured on two new random corpora, against the reference:
- 299 negative-base products (evaluation only): identical results went from 104 to 291.
- 685 Simplify inputs (products, powers of sums, quotients, sums with quotient terms): identical results went from 348 to 587.

On the Simplify corpus, 49 outputs score higher than before. In 44 of them the new output *is* the reference's result: evaluation now splits `(-2)^x 6` the way the reference does. Of the other five, three are `(-n)^(x/2)` factors, where Mathilda writes `(2 I)^x` and the reference writes `I^x 2^x`. That difference predates this change. The remaining two need a sign cancelled between two negative powers with opposite exponents (`(-1)^x/(-3)^x` is `3^-x`): the reference's Simplify does this and its evaluation does not.

Not yet: a negative base under a rational exponent (`(-2)^(1/3)/2`, which the reference writes `(-1)^(1/3) 2^(-2/3)`), and Gaussian-integer coefficients.

## Adamchik–Marichev's Meijer G integration algorithm completed (v0.6223)

Adamchik and Marichev (ISSAC '90) describe one integration algorithm: write each factor as a Meijer G function, check convergence, apply the two-G theorem, and reduce the resulting G. `Integrate[..., Method -> "Roach"]` already covered the definite part. These changes add the missing pieces, all in `src/internal/RoachIntegrate.m`.

- **Formula (26).** One G times a power, integrated from 0 to y, is a single G function. This now gives two things:
  - Definite integrals with a symbolic endpoint, conditioned on 0 ≤ a ≤ b. This includes the paper's eq. (27).
  - Antiderivatives: the new indefinite `Method -> "Roach"`, which is also the last stage of the indefinite cascade. Every antiderivative is checked by differentiation, at points with x > 0 and x < 0.
- **Table entries.**
  - pFq, 0F1, 1F1, 2F1 (eq. 17), `HypergeometricU`, and a raw `MeijerG`.
  - Products `J_μ J_ν` and `K_μ K_ν` of one argument.
  - The square of any G factor is treated as two G functions: `∫₀^∞ K₀² = π²/4`.
  - When a term has more than two factors, two of one argument are fused into one G.
  - Nested powers of x are read as one power.
- **Early automatic slot.** Integrands that Roach fully covers and that contain a higher transcendental function now try Roach before Newton–Leibniz and DiffUnderInt.
  - The paper's §5 demo took more than 60 s and now takes 0.9 s, giving the paper's result.
  - Its `p = v + 2` case gives `2^(b−1) Γ(b) J_(b+v)(c)/c^b`.
- **Output tidying.**
  - G results have their Gamma and pFq parameters put over one denominator.
  - The coefficient in front of a G or pFq that stays is simplified, with that function held fixed.
- **FunctionExpand changes (C, `meijer_g.c`, `functionexpand.c`, `hrep_core.c`).**
  - G^{2,2}_{2,2} is rewritten in terms of 2F1(1 − z), for the log case and unit argument.
  - pFq parameters are put through `Together` before reduction.
  - Bug fix: hrep's tidy could turn a valid pFq closed form into `Indeterminate`. It now keeps the untidied form in that case.
- **Integrate docstring.** It now lists `Method -> "Roach"`, which it never did before.
- **Tests.** New `test_adamchik_marichev` in `integrate_roach_tests`. The Roach corpus went from 44 to 54 CORRECT, still 0 WRONG; the baseline was updated.
- **Not implemented:** the classical integral transforms (Laplace, Fourier, Hankel, Mellin, Stieltjes, Hilbert), fractional integrals, and three-G integrals.

## Integrate: Cauchy principal values by Roach's PV theorem (v0.6217)

- **The PV kernel is a G function.** Under `PrincipalValue -> True` the kernel `1/(1 - x)` is `Pi G^{1,1}_{2,2}(x | 0, 1/2; 0, 1/2)`, whose Mellin transform is `Pi Cot[Pi w]`. The one- and two-G integration theorems therefore give principal values.

  - A G that lands at unit argument in a log case is summed as its Mellin–Barnes residues.
  - Every answer is checked against a symmetric-excision PV quadrature.
- **Examples:**

## Elliptic definite integrals (v0.6211)

## NIntegrate: an interior zero of the integrand is not the end of its tail (v0.6210)

- The double-exponential rules (`denint.c`, `dequad.c`) decided where to stop summing from single sample values. A squared Bessel or trigonometric factor touches zero at every root, so a sample near a root is tiny and the next one large. The rules read that as "the tail has decayed, now noise is growing" and stopped. Everything after the root was dropped, and the truncated sum was still reported as converged, with no warning.
  - `NIntegrate[Exp[-x] BesselJ[0, x]^2, {x, 0, Infinity}]` gave 0.636355; the value is 0.642638.

- **Sweep.** 46 such integrals at machine precision, WP 20 and WP 30, against 40-digit references: 103 of 138 results were wrong before, and none is now beyond its precision goal. Many high-precision runs are 10–100× faster.

## Integrate[..., Method -> "Roach"]: definite integrals by Meijer G functions (v0.6206)

- **`Method -> "Roach"`** (definite, any real interval) implements Kelly Roach's integration by Meijer G functions (*Meijer G Function Representations*, ISSAC '97 §5; *Solving Integrals with the Quantum Computer Algebra System*, 2008). The code is in `src/internal/RoachIntegrate.m`.
  1. The interval is mapped to (0, ∞); a finite interval gets the indicator θ(1 − y).
  2. Linearity is applied.
  3. Every factor becomes a G function by the PBM §8.4 table.
  4. One G uses the Mellin transform. Two G use the convolution, with Gauss multiplication bringing the arguments to a common power.
  5. Validity comes from the full PBM 2.24.1 condition set, decided atom by atom under the assumptions. An undecided condition gives a `ConditionalExpression`.
  6. The G result goes through `FunctionExpand`: the Formula algorithm of v0.6203 and v0.6205.
  - **Fallbacks:** `t = E^-x`, `t = Tan[x]`, and integration by parts for an inverse function.
- **Numeric check.** Every answer is checked at a FindInstance point of the assumptions, against `NIntegrate` or a split-interval `NIntegrate`.
- **Roach 2008, pp. 3–4.** 17 of the 23 non-elliptic integrals close and verify, among them:
  - `∫₀^∞ e^(−αx) J₀(βx)² dx = 2K(−4β²/α²)/(πα)`;
  - `∫₀^∞ (Si(ax) − π/2) e^(−bx) dx = (ArcTan[a/b] − π/2)/b`;
  - `∫₀^∞ x e^(−nx)/(eˣ + 1) dx = (ζ(2, (1+n)/2) − ζ(2, 1 + n/2))/4`;
  - `∫₀¹ ArcSin[x] x^(2n−1) dx = (π − √π Γ(n + 1/2)/Γ(n + 1))/(4n)`.

  The seven elliptic integrals are a separate stage, still to come.
- **Hygiene.** The package is read under `$ContextPath = {"System`"}` in `Roach`Private``. An earlier draft read in `Global``, where its pattern variables `a_`, `b_` were the user's own `a` and `b` and captured the integrand's parameters.

## The Meijer G walk; Struve L certificates (v0.6205)

- **`FunctionExpand[MeijerG[...]]`** now runs the G version of Roach's walk (*Meijer G Function Representations*, ISSAC '97) before the Slater expansion. The log case, with integer-spaced b's, previously stayed MeijerG and now reduces:
  - `MeijerG[{{}, {1}}, {{0, 0}, {}}, z]` = `Gamma[0, z]`;
  - `MeijerG[{{}, {-1/2}}, {{0, 0}, {-1/2}}, z]` = `BesselY[0, 2 Sqrt[z]]`;
  - `MeijerG[{{}, {1}}, {{0, 0}, {1/2}}, z]` = `-2 CosIntegral[2 Sqrt[z]]/Sqrt[Pi]`.
- **The module.** G lives in the module of its ODE, `[(−1)^(p−m−n) z Π(θ − a_j + 1) − Π(θ − b_k)] G = 0`.
  - The four first-order shifts come from the Γ recurrences of the Mellin–Barnes integrand.
  - The inverses are one polynomial division of the ODE each, with remainders `Π(b_k − a_j)` and `Π(a_j − b_k)`.
  - The certificates are the PBM §8.4 table read backwards: J, K, Y, Struve H, Si, Ci, Erfc, Γ(a, z), Log(1+z), ArcTan and EllipticK, with one- and two-parameter families.
- **Struve L certificates:**
  - the family 1F2(1; 3/2, ν+3/2);
  - the integral of I₀, 1F2(1/2; 1, 3/2).

  A third certificate, transcribed with a sign error, was caught by the certificate-against-series test before it shipped.
- **Order reduction.** Integer orders of `BesselI`, `BesselJ` and `StruveL` reduce to 0 and 1 by the three-term recurrences (StruveL's is inhomogeneous). Roach's gallery `1F2(3/2; 5/2, 5; z)` now prints in I₀, I₁, L₀, L₁ exactly as published.

## Roach's Formula algorithm: HypergeometricPFQ in closed form (v0.6203)

- **`FunctionExpand[HypergeometricPFQ[...]]`** and the diagnostic head `` HypergeometricPFQ`Representation `` write a pFq in closed form with Kelly Roach's Formula algorithm (*Hypergeometric Function Representations*, ISSAC '96). The code is in `src/special_functions/hrep_core.c` and `hrep_certs.c`, and the design is in `docs/spec/builtins/special-functions.md` (*Formula representations*).
  - `FunctionExpand[Hypergeometric2F1[-3/2, -1/2, 1/2, z]]` = `1/2 Sqrt[1-z] (2+z) + 3/2 Sqrt[z] ArcSin[Sqrt[z]]`, Roach's §2 example.
  - `FunctionExpand[HypergeometricPFQ[{-3/2}, {-1/2, 1/2}, z]]` = `(1+2z) Cosh[2 Sqrt[z]] + Sqrt[z] Sinh[2 Sqrt[z]] - 4 z^(3/2) SinhIntegral[2 Sqrt[z]]`.
- **The module.** A pFq is a coefficient vector in Q(params)(w) on the companion basis `[f0, θ f0, ...]` of a lookup certificate F(a0; b0) = f0.
  - The shift operators are `1 + θ/a_i` and `1 + θ/(b_i − 1)`.
  - Each inverse is one polynomial division of the ODE by the shift's linear factor, and the remainder is Roach's definedness condition.
  - The walk follows Roach's ordering rule, and every step is checked as it is taken.
  - The arithmetic is native FLINT `fmpz_mpoly_q`.
  - A certificate is just `(a0, b0, f0)`; the derivative matrix is derived from the ODE, never entered by hand.
- **Beyond the table:**
  - the derivative formula;
  - certificates generated by the integral formula, each checked against the series before use;
  - the Lerch Φ formula, with range reduction and the roots-of-unity theorems (the real root for a negative argument);
  - Euler, Pfaff and Kummer transformations;
  - one- and two-parameter family certificates;
  - at z = 1, −1 and 1/2, the Gauss, Kummer, Gauss-second, Bailey, Dixon, Watson and Whipple theorems, plus 3F2(a, b, 1; c, 2; 1) with its digamma limit, then a one-sided limit. The engine never returns `Indeterminate`.
- **Roach 2008, p. 6:** all eleven pFq values close. Examples:
  - `3F2(−15/4, 1, 1; −7/2, 2; 1)` = `(2252 + 315π + 630 Log[2])/665`;
  - `3F2(−11/4, −5/2, −5/2; −7/2, 1/4; 1)` = `355 Gamma[1/4]^2/(56 Sqrt[2π])`.
- **ISSAC '96 gallery:** every entry that does not need Struve L, Kelvin or the PFD duplication formula closes, mostly in Roach's printed form. Examples: 3F2(−1/2, 1/2, 1; 3/2, 5/2) in Li₂(±√z); 2F3(1, 3; 2, 4, 5; −z) in J₀, J₁ of 2√z.
- **Through FunctionExpand.** A MeijerG's Slater terms and the pFq left in a definite integral's result now go through the engine. `∫₀^∞ J₁(bx) J₁(cx)/x² dx` gives Roach's K/E answer.
- **Probe region.** The engine's internal Integrate, Limit and check messages do not reach the user or trip an enclosing `Check`.
- Tests in `tests/test_hrep.c`, including every certificate against its series.

## Two wrong elliptic integrals and an unbounded Limit recursion (v0.6197)

Found while measuring Kelly Roach's *Solving Integrals with the Quantum Computer Algebra System* (2008) against Mathilda.

- **Wrong answers, now unevaluated.** `∫₂^∞ dz/((z+14)√((z−1)((z−13)²+81)))` returned 0.042180, but the value is 0.035523. `∫_{−∞}^0 dz/((18−z)√((1−z)((16−z)²+64)))` returned 0.034659, but the value is 0.016075. The true values contain `EllipticK`; both had come back as an elementary `ArcTan`.

## Complex Schur and eigenvectors guarded against LAPACK 3.12.1's blocked-QR regression (v0.6208)

- **What was wrong:** on the Ubuntu CI runner, `SchurDecomposition` and `Eigenvectors` / `Eigensystem` of a complex machine matrix of order >= 75 were wrong. The Schur form had `Q T Q^H != A` (residual 0.12 on a random 91 x 91), and the eigenvector residuals were 0.7; the eigenvalues were right.
  - A standalone C program calling the system `zgees_` reproduced it with no Mathilda code involved, on both the reference LAPACK and OpenBLAS: the residual was 1.6e-14 at n = 64 and 0.11 at n = 91.
  - This is LAPACK 3.12.1's regression in the complex blocked multishift QR, the `zlaqr0` path at n >= 75 (Reference-LAPACK issue #1160, introduced by PR #1020).

## Arbitrary-precision real Schur exact to working precision for rank-deficient matrices; sparse ::luc threshold; oracle in ctest (v0.6198; committed as b185eb3d under 0.6197, renumbered for a tag collision)

## Experimental`FeynCalc`DiracTr, a native Dirac trace; Set skips its rhs walk for a fixed point (v0.6189)

- **`` Experimental`FeynCalc`DiracTr[a1, ..., an] ``** (new, `src/experimental/feyncalc/diractr.c`) is a C version of Rolf Mertig's recursive `diracTr` from Woxi issue #2142. It returns exactly what that definition returns (SameQ, term order included): `4 Σ sign(M) Π g[ai, aj]` over the (n-1)!! perfect matchings. `DiracTr[]` is `4` and an odd n gives `0`.
  - **No evaluator calls.** The n(n-1)/2 nodes `g[ai, aj]` are built once and shared by refcount. The matchings are enumerated as integer rows.
    - With distinct arguments, the terms are put in `Plus` order by an LSD radix sort on factor ranks, with the last factor most significant and no comparisons. The rows move with the sort, two factor positions per pass.
    - The tree is built and stamped bottom-up. One `Times` verdict is proven and copied to the other T - 1 terms with the new `eval_mark_fixed_point_like`.
  - **Repeated arguments.** Equal factors fold into powers, equal terms add, and zeros drop out. Order comes from `expr_compare`.
  - **Rules on g.** If `g` has rules or attributes, an argument has UpValues, or `Plus`/`Times`/`Power` have user rules, the raw sum is returned inside `Expand`, unstamped, so the evaluator applies the rules.
  - More than 2^32 terms gives `DiracTr::toobig`.
  - **Measured at n = 14** (135135 terms, on a loaded machine): the C kernel takes 26–40 ms. The `.m` recursion takes 2.9–3.9 s here and 9.0 s in the reference kernel. The issue reports FORM at 0.58 s. At n = 16 (2027025 terms) the C kernel takes 0.5–0.7 s.
- **`x = rhs`** (`eval.c`) skips its O(tree) "does rhs mention x?" walk when rhs is a compound node that is still a reusable fixed point after the binding. In that case `evaluate(rhs)` would return rhs unchanged. An INERT stamp that relied on `x` is invalidated by the binding itself, so `y = y + 1` and `x = x` behave as before. `r = <DiracTr result>` had spent as long walking the 2M-term value at n = 16 as building it.
- **Benchmark `123-native-vs-m`** (new, group G) times one computation written twice, the `.m` code and its native twin, with a `C === .m` check per size. It carries the issue's FORM program as `medium.frm`.
  - The C rows decide presence before the symbol is parsed. Parsing a statement interns the name, so the harness's run-time `Names[]` test would report "present" in a system without the builtin.
- Tests in `tests/test_experimental_diractr.c`: SameQ against the reference for n ≤ 12, non-canonical and compound arguments, repeated-index cases and a seeded random sweep, the n = 14 counts, the stamp under later definitions, the rules fallback, the size guard, and the Set edge cases. All 601 unit suites pass, and valgrind's leak summary is identical with and without DiracTr calls.

## SeparablePolynomialDecomposition, and Simplify tries it after Factor (v0.6177)

- **New function, written in Mathilda code.** `SeparablePolynomialDecomposition[p, S, T]` (`src/internal/simp/SeparablePolynomialDecomposition.m`, a package loaded at startup) writes `p` as a minimal-rank sum of products `B[S] Q[T]`. ## Matcher enumeration order agrees with the reference (v0.6173)

- The matches of one level are enumerated in three nested loops, as in the reference. Outermost are the Optional defaults. Next is the element-to-slot assignment, with slots in canonical order and leftover-takers last. Innermost are each slot's internal alternatives, in slot order. Before, a slot with internal alternatives (a nested `Orderless` call such as `Aa[ii_, jj_]`, a nested split, `Alternatives`, an inner Optional) put its alternatives outside the later slots' element choices. Now such a slot is deferred: the later slots choose their elements first, and the deferred matches run once the level is assigned. Deferral happens only where it changes the order, so a slot that matches its element in at most one way is matched in place as before. Before a deferral the chain is probed once, so an element that cannot match is still pruned early. See "Enumeration order" in `src/match.c` and `docs/spec/builtins/pattern-matching.md`.
- An Optional leftover-taker (`r_.` in a product) is moved behind the fixed slots like `r_`. `ReplaceList[s[1] s[2] s[3], r_. s[a_] :> {r, a}]` now lists `a = 1, 2, 3`, the reverse of before. It is also much faster: `ReplaceList[p10, r_. s[a_] :> a]` takes 3 ms instead of 100 ms.
- FeynCalc: the `pc /: pc[L[z_], L[x_]] f_[a__] := ... /; !FreeQ[f[a], L[z]]` shape now rewrites the factor the reference rewrites.
- Not changed, recorded: inside an `Orderless` product, a `/;` in one slot sees the earlier slots' bindings, while the reference sees none. `Replace[a + b + c, a + b -> x]` is still `c + x` (the reference gives `a + b + c`), because `rule_try` in `src/replace.c` falls back to Flat-leftover matching for `Replace` too.

## MathieuS and MathieuSPrime: the odd Mathieu function for all complex a, q, z (v0.6166)

**New builtins** (`src/special_functions/mathieu.c`; attributes `Listable`, `NHoldFirst`, `NumericFunction`, `Protected`). `MathieuS[a, q, z]` is the odd solution of `y'' + (a - 2 q Cos[2 z]) y = 0`, and `MathieuSPrime` is its z-derivative.

- **One plan, two sums.** `MathieuS` is `Sum[c_k Sin[(nu + 2k) z]]` with MathieuC's coefficients, so `MathieuC + I MathieuS` is the Floquet solution. That holds in the reference too: in stability bands its ratio `F(z+Pi)/F(z)` is `E^(I Pi nu)` to 30 digits. The `(a, q)` plan cache is shared, and every machine surface (scalar, packed/NDArray n-ary kernel, `Compile[]`) calls `sf_machine_mathieu_fn(odd, deriv, ...)`. The arb kernel takes the same `odd` flag.
- **Characteristic values.**
  - At `b_n`: `se_n` with the DLMF normalization; matches the reference and scipy `mathieu_sem`.
  - At `a_n`: `MathieuS` is `0`, the limit of the Floquet normalization; no periodic odd solution exists. The reference returns a value that does not satisfy the equation (residual about -4.5).
  - Symmetrically, `MathieuC` at `b_n` is now an exact `0`; it was ~1e-16 noise.
- **Integer-`nu` gluing.** At a characteristic value only the upper half of the coefficient vector is computed: continued fractions down to the fold centre `-n/2`, and a forward recurrence from the centre up to the dominant index. The `a_n`/`b_n` type is the one whose halves agree. The lower half is the exact mirror.
  - This fixes a failure at `b_{2j}(q)`, where the centre coefficient is exactly zero and the old two-sided continued fraction divided by it: `MathieuC[2.0994604454866654, 5., 0.7]`, `b_2(5)`, was unevaluated.
  - Inverse iteration now runs at the unsnapped `nu`, because the Floquet matrix at an exact characteristic value is singular.
- **Band edges.** Near an edge, `nu` is ill-conditioned (`g'` is small), so Newton reaches a noise floor of about `eps/|g'|` and never meets a 4e-16 step test. Newton now accepts once steps are below 1e-9 and stop shrinking. `MathieuC[2.1, 5., 0.7]` was unevaluated before this change.
- **Fixes to MathieuC found on the way.**

## MathieuC and MathieuCPrime: the even Mathieu function for all complex a, q, z (v0.6165)

**New builtins** (`src/special_functions/mathieu.c`; attributes `Listable`, `NHoldFirst`, `NumericFunction`, `Protected`). `MathieuC[a, q, z]` is the even solution of `y'' + (a - 2 q Cos[2 z]) y = 0`, and `MathieuCPrime` is its z-derivative. `MathieuCPrime` ships alongside because the derivative of `MathieuC`, and therefore every Series of it, is written in terms of it.

- **Normalization.** The Floquet form `Sum[c_k Cos[(nu + 2k) z]]`:
  - generic `nu`: `Sum[c_k^2] = 1`, with `Re(c_0) > 0`;
  - characteristic values (integer `nu`): DLMF;
  - `q = 0`: `Cos[Sqrt[a] z]`, and `1/Sqrt[2]` at `a = 0`.

  This matches the reference wherever `nu` is real, i.e. in stability bands and at every characteristic value (checked against 25-digit reference values and `scipy.special.mathieu_cem`). In instability bands and for complex `a` or `q`, the reference's normalization (`Sum[|c_k|^2] = 1` with a phase rule that could not be recovered from probes) is non-analytic. Mathilda keeps the analytic convention. The two differ by a z-independent constant there, and the tests check that constancy.
- **Machine kernel** (`src/special_functions/sf_machine_mathieu.c`). One cached plan per `(a, q)`:
  - `nu` from a pole-free Hill determinant (the sinc factor divided out at `a = (2k)^2`, the polynomially convergent tail in closed form);
  - `nu` then refined by Newton on the continued-fraction consistency function;
  - the dominant index from inverse iteration;
  - coefficients from two glued continued fractions.

  Each point is then a centred Horner sum. Complex `z` trims its window per point. A cancellation guard declines when the largest term exceeds the result by 10^4, so `MathieuC[-1, 2, 5. I]` is answered by the ball kernel; the reference returns ~1e45 there for a true value of ~1e-3. The kernel is shared bit-for-bit by the scalar path, the packed / NDArray n-ary map (parallel, with a thread-local plan cache), and `Compile[]` (scalar and array, real and complex).
- **Arbitrary precision** (`src/special_functions/mathieu_arb.c`, `flint_num_mathieu` in the ladder of `flint_num_bridge.c`):
  - Miller sweeps with small-integer recurrence coefficients;
  - precision-doubling Newton with a half-precision derivative;
  - a first-order final correction instead of a last full pass;
  - a per-index precision profile.

  Clenshaw and the Miller sweeps run on midpoints with a rigorous bound: their ball radii grow like `1.5^N`, which cost 950 of 33,000 bits before the change. Measured: 500 digits in 1.5 ms (reference 16 ms), 10^4 digits in 0.40 s (reference 0.70 s).
- **Symbolic.**
  - `D` rules in `src/calculus/deriv.c`; `d/da` and `d/dq` stay inert.
  - Series at 0 and at a generic point follow from the derivative rules.
  - Exact values: `q = 0`, `a = q = 0`, and `MathieuCPrime[a, q, 0] = 0`.
  - Parity in `z`; `MathieuC::argrx` for a wrong argument count.
- **Benchmark** (`benchmarks/11-special-functions`):
  - `MathieuC[2, 1, v]` over 10^6 points: 15 ms;
  - `ce_2(., 5)` over 10^6 points: 17 ms, against scipy 920 ms;
  - 10^4 digits: 0.39 s.
- **`Attributes` order** (`src/attr.c`). `NHoldFirst`/`NHoldRest`/`NHoldAll` now sort before `NumericFunction`, as in the reference (alphabetical): `{Listable, NHoldFirst, NumericFunction, Protected}`.

## Issue #126: Simplify takes Factor's form on a tie when it refines a product (v0.6164)

**The report.** `Simplify[(4 - 4 eps) y]` returned the input (`LeafCount` 7), where the reference gives `-4 (-1 + eps) y` (`LeafCount` 6).

**Cause.** The issue suggested a missing recursive `Collect`, but `Collect` was not the problem. The polynomial pipeline already generated `-4 (-1 + eps) y` from `Factor`, but both forms have `SimplifyCount` 8, and candidates replaced the best only on a strict improvement, so the input won the tie. The reference takes the factored form even under `ComplexityFunction -> (1 &)`, so on a tie it prefers the factorisation.

**Fix** (`src/simp/simp_search.c`, `simp_pipeline_polynomial`; `src/simp/simp_builtins.c`):
- `Factor`'s result wins a tie when the input is a product and the factorisation changed (content or factors, compared up to sign by `factor_is_sign_rearrangement`). A sign-only rewrite (`(5 - 3 z)^2 x z` → `x z (-5 + 3 z)^2`) and a tie with a sum input still need a strict win; the latter keeps the final `simp_lift_common_factor` polish effective (`(1 - eps) eps - 4 eps z (4 + z)` → `eps (1 - eps - 4 z (4 + z))`, 15, not Factor's 17).
- `factored_tie_candidate`: a negative content moves its sign into one Plus factor when strictly cheaper (`-y (-5 + 3 y)` → `(5 - 3 y) y`). The magnitude of the content is never folded back in.
- `canon_negate_pairs(input)` is an ordinary candidate in the pipeline. The final `canon_negate_pairs` polish is now score-gated (`<=`): it turned `-2 (-2 + eps) (-2 + x)` (11) into `-2 (2 - eps) (2 - x)` (15) once Factor's forms reached it.

- The special-function engine (`src/simp/simp_sf_engine.c`) now runs one bottom-up pass over its result, where it used to run a single top-level dispatch (bottom-up was trig-only). The terms the engine introduces must meet the input's already-simplified subterms in one spelling. `Simplify[(a/3 - 1)^2]` is now `(-3 + a)^2/9`, as in the reference, so the Hurwitz recurrence's `((-1 + a/3)^2)^(-s/2)` cancelled the input's `((-3 + a)^2/9)^(-s/2)` only after that pass (`simp_sf_zeta_tests`).
- `Simplify[Covariance[{1, Pi}, {E, 2}]]` is `-1/2 (-2 + E) (-1 + Pi)` (13), as in the reference; the old `-1/2 (2 - E) (1 - Pi)` (17) came from the unconditional sign-pair flip.

**Measured.** A random corpus of 400 polynomial products and sums: outputs identical to the reference 308 → 355; no output more complex than before (19 now strictly simpler). The remaining differences are ties or forms cheaper than the reference's. Tests: `test_simplify_factor_tie_issue126` in `tests/test_simplify.c`.

## Series keeps the branch phase of a fractional power / Log of a vanishing base; Limit[Sqrt[x^2]/x, x -> 0, Direction -> 1] is -1 (v0.6162)

**The bug.** `Limit[Sqrt[x^2]/x, x -> 0, Direction -> 1]` returned `1`; the answer is `-1`. The two-sided limit also returned `1`; the answer is `Indeterminate`. The cause was in Series. `so_pow_expr` wrote `(a0 t^r)^alpha` as `a0^alpha t^(r alpha)`, taking the principal root of the leading monomial, so `Series[Sqrt[x^2], {x, 0, 2}]` came out as `x + O[x]^3`. The Log handler did the same with `Log[a0 t^r] = Log[a0] + r Log[t]`, so `Log[x^2]` became `2 Log[x]` and `Log[-x]` became `I Pi + Log[x]`. Every one of these is wrong for `x < 0`.

- **Series** (`src/calculus/series.c`, `so_pow_lead_coef` / `so_log_lead_const`).
  - The leading coefficient now carries the phase `(a0 t^r)^alpha / t^(r alpha)` (or `Log[a0 t^r]`), in the reference system's form: `SeriesData[x, 0, {Sqrt[x^2]/x}, 1, ...]`.
  - Exact splits still happen. A positive factor comes out (`Sqrt[2 x + x^2]`, `Log[2 x]`). The phase is `1` for `-1 < r <= 1` with no sign (`Sqrt[x]`, `Sqrt[Sin[x]]`).
  - A new `SeriesCtx.tsign` records the known side of `x0`. It is `+1` at the `+Infinity` remap, where the output is byte-for-byte as before, and otherwise comes from an `x > x0` / `x < x0` assumption. With a known side the phase reduces to its value at `t = +-1`.
  - This replaces the old even-leading-power sign flip, which only applied under `x < 0`.
- **Limit** (`src/calculus/limit.c`, `layer2_series`).
  - When the plain expansion is stuck on an `x`-dependent coefficient at a finite point, a one-sided limit re-expands with the side as an assumption. A two-sided limit probes both sides: equal sides give the value, different sides give `Indeterminate`.
  - If only one side resolves, the full one-sided cascade settles the other. This covers `Log[x^2] - 2 Log[x]`, which is identically 0 for `x > 0`.
  - The retry runs only on the stuck path, so ordinary expansions pay nothing.
- **Gruntz** (`src/calculus/gruntz.c`, `series_in_w`). The mrv variable `w` is positive by construction, so its four expansions now say so: `Assumptions -> w > 0 && $Assumptions`. Without this, `Sqrt[x^2 + x] - Sqrt[x^2 + 1]` at `Infinity` met a symbolic phase and was left unevaluated.
- **Series assumption cleanup is keyed on assumed symbols.** The coefficient cleanup that runs under `Assumptions` used to rewrite every coefficient. It now skips coefficients that are numeric, or that mention no symbol occurring in any assumption fact.
  - This was more than cosmetic. Under `x > 0`, `Sqrt[E^(4 c)]` became `E^(2 c)`, which is false for complex `c`. A Limit side probe exposed it: DSolve's IVP fit for §2.2.31-3049 then fitted a complex `C[1]` to a spurious `Sqrt[-...]` branch.

- **ArcTan / ArcCot / ArcTanh / ArcCoth half-Pi constants** (`so_half_pi_branch_const`). Found by the 50-case stress set: `Limit[ArcTan[1/x] Sqrt[x^2]/x, x -> 0, Direction -> 1]` was `-Pi/2`, and the answer is `Pi/2`.

The old code read it from `lead` alone: `ArcTan[1/x]` was `Pi/2 - x` even under `x < 0`. ArcTanh and ArcCoth always used `+I Pi/2`, which is wrong for `x > 0` (`ArcTanh[1000.] = 0.001 - I Pi/2`).

## DSolve`RiemannP and DSolve`RationalSubstitution: three-point Fuchsian equations and t = x^k / inversion (v0.6156)

## DSolve`ChangeOfVariable: scaled trig, exponential and hyperbolic coefficients (v0.6149)

## A number inside a kernel is not a coefficient; CoefficientArrays with no variables (v0.6141)

- **`Factor`** (GitHub #121; `rationalize.c` `internal_args_contain_inexact_coefficient`). The exact polynomial heads rationalise inexact input, work exactly and numericalise the answer back. The trigger looked for an inexact number anywhere in the input, including inside an opaque kernel. So `Factor[f[1.] x[1] + f[1.]]` came back as `f[1.] (1.0 + x[1])`, with every exact integer turned machine.
  - The trigger now counts only coefficients. It walks the same structural heads as the kernel-preserving numericalise (`Plus`, `Times`, `Power`, lists, rules, relations and connectives). A numeric quantity such as `Sin[2.]` is still a coefficient. Any other compound is an indeterminate.

## Parametric Solve and Gröbner bases over Q(params), Solve without variables, PossibleZeroQ on inert calls (v0.6127)

FeynCalc's momentum-shift and topology-mapping finders (`FCLoopFindMomentumShifts`, `FCLoopFindTopologyMappings`) solve `lhs^2 == rhs^2` systems in the shifted loop momenta, with the momenta as parameters. Bisecting them turned up six kernel causes.

- **`Solve[eqns]` and `Solve[eqns, {}]`** (`solve/solve.c`) solve for every variable the system mentions. A variable is a maximal non-numeric subexpression not built by a numeric function, sorted canonically: `Solve[{f[a] + b == 1, b == 2}, {}]` is `{{b -> 2, f[a] -> -1}}`. A system with no variable is `{{}}` or `{}`, so `Solve[{}, {}]` is `{{}}` (it was `Solve::ivar`).
- **A wrong answer fixed: `GroebnerBasis[..., CoefficientDomain -> RationalFunctions]`** (`poly/groebner.c`, `poly/groebnerbasis.c`).
  - The reduction over Q(params) was a fraction-free pseudo-division. It scaled the remainder by the reducer's leading coefficient but not the part already retired, and it stripped content from one half only, so the "reduced" polynomial left the ideal. On the 5-equation shift system that produced a parameter-only generator: a false unit ideal, so Solve saw no solution.
  - The reduction now runs on FLINT rational functions (`fmpz_mpoly_q`), each generator monic. Denominators are cleared and the content in Z[params] is divided out at the end, so `Q x2 - p1 Q` is reported as `-p1 + x2`, as in the reference. The old routine stays as the fallback without FLINT.
  - The ring basis now uses a block order: lex on the main variables, then degree-reverse-lex on the parameters. A 4-unknown, 4-parameter system went from over a minute under full lex to 0.1 s. This also fixed a use-after-free: the weight matrix was freed before the reduction that orders by it.
- **No parameter cap in Solve's nonlinear-system path** (`solve/solvenlsys.c`). It declined above three parameters, so a routing system in four momenta stayed unevaluated (memory: no arbitrary caps). Back-substituted roots are `Cancel`-ed on the parametric path: `(p1 p4 q1 + p4^2 q1 + p4 q1^2)/(p1 q1 + p4 q1 + q1^2)` is `p4`.
- **Univariate Solve factors symbolic polynomials at every degree** (`poly/solvepoly.c`). Previously only quadratics were factored. A quartic `(d^2 - q1^2)(d^2 - (2 l2 + q1)^2)`, expanded, went to the biquadratic formula and came back as nested radicals; it now gives `d -> +-q1, +-(2 l2 + q1)`, as in the reference.
- **A wrong answer fixed: `PossibleZeroQ[f[a]]` was `True`** (`zero_test.c`).
  - The sampler substitutes numbers for symbols only, so an inert call stayed symbolic, the test ended UNKNOWN, and the builtin answered `True`. FeynCalc therefore accepted the shift `l1 -> l1` between two different propagators.
  - The builtin now abstracts each distinct maximal call to a fresh symbol, in the expression and its assumptions. A call is abstracted when its head is not a symbol, or names neither a builtin nor a `NumericFunction`. So `PossibleZeroQ[f[a]]` and `PossibleZeroQ[C[1] - 1]` are `False`, and `PossibleZeroQ[f[a] - f[a]]` is `True`, all as in the reference.
  - `zero_test_decide`, which the solvers use internally, is unchanged.
  - Three unit tests asserted the old answer and now check what they meant to check: the zero-test trip-wire expects the reference's `False`, a Solve check substitutes its integer `C[2]`, and the PowerSeries residual is tested through `Normal`.
- **`AssociationThread[keys -> scalar]`** (`assoc.c`) gives the scalar to every key. Lists of different lengths emit `AssociationThread::idim`. FeynCalc's preferred-topology filter builds its set this way, so every mapping was being filtered out.
- **Measured.**
  - The FeynCalc pass set is 13,257, up 40 and a superset of the last run.
  - All 48 `FCLoopFindMomentumShifts`, `FCLoopFindTopologyMappings` and `FCLoopApplyTopologyMappings` tests pass. `FCCompareNumbers` ID2, ID3 and ID12 and `FourSeries` ID10–12 pass as a side effect.

## Fixed points survive Block, Dispatch and held arguments; UpValues keyed on their outer head; Det memoises minors (v0.6124)

Three bisections of FeynCalc cliffs (Chisholm, FCFADiracChainJoin, FCFeynmanPrepare, PaVeReduce) found three kernel causes.

- **A certified "no rule fires" verdict survives a Block** (`eval.c`, `core.c`, `modular.c`, `symtab.h`). FeynCalc's `DiracChain` rule calls `FCI` in its condition. `FCI` uses `Block`, `Dispatch` and assignments to its Block locals. Every failed dispatch was therefore tainted, so an evaluated chain re-ran the 100 µs condition whenever it was wrapped again. In Chisholm that meant 18 times as many `FCI` calls as the reference. Four changes, each sound on its own:
  - `Dispatch`, `With` and `Block` are on the `eval_pure` list. Block saves and restores what it scopes; its body's reads are recorded as for any expression.
  - **Frame-scoped bindings.** A symbol scoped by a Block entered inside an open dispatch frame carries that frame's depth `s` (`SymbolDef.dep_scoped`).
    - A clock bump for such a symbol is discounted by the frames at depth `s` or less, which enclose the Block. Their certificate does not depend on it: the Block puts the symbol back before they end.
    - A `Set`/`SetDelayed` to it taints only frames nested inside the Block (`g_dep_taint_above`).
    - Reading it records no dependency for those frames. Recording it had made the next Block's binding of `FCI`'s locals advance the INERT epoch: 18,545 global invalidations in the FCFADiracChainJoin tests, now 497.
  - An argument held by its head no longer enters an INERT verdict, so `lhs :> DiracGamma[7]` and `Hold[...]` can be fixed points. Before, `FCI`'s rule lists carried only exact-clock stamps, and reusing them tainted every frame that read them.
  - `MATHILDA_DISPATCH_STATS` now also prints why frames were tainted.
- **UpValues are skipped by the pattern's own head** (`symtab.c`, `Rule.outer_head`). `SPD`'s UpValues are `SPD[a_, b_] = c_`, so they can only match a `Set`. They were tried inside every `Times`, `Plus` and `Power` holding an `SPD`: 210k failed matches in 31 `PaVeReduce` calls. The UpValue filter also uses the per-slot shape keys now.
- **Symbolic `Det` memoises minors** (`linalg/det.c`). FCFeynmanPrepare's 10×10 block-diagonal check went from 1.8–4 s to 0.6 ms (the reference takes 1.1 ms).
- **Tests.**
  - `eval_timestamps_tests` gains 8 scenarios for frame-scoped bindings and held arguments, agreeing with `MATHILDA_NO_DEP_CERT=1` and with v0.6120.
  - `dispatch_slot_keys_tests` gains the UpValue cases, with reference values.
  - The `Det` results were checked against the permutation formula.
- **Measured.**
  - FCFADiracChainJoin-ID5 went from 1.01 s to 0.39 s (the reference takes 0.16 s); ID12 from 0.41 s to 0.058 s (reference 0.071 s).
  - FeynCalc corpus geometric mean went from 1.296x to **1.443x**, and total compute from 206 s to 188 s (the reference takes 272 s).

## Dense linear algebra: closing the gaps to scipy (v0.6221)

The five benchmark cases that were furthest behind scipy/sympy, re-measured
on the same machine (seconds, Mathilda → after, against the scipy/sympy call):

| Case | Before | After | scipy / sympy |
|---|---|---|---|
| `CholeskyDecomposition`, 400 x 400 complex Hermitian | 0.10 | 0.016 | 0.006 |
| `MatrixFunction[Cos, m]`, 100 x 100 | unevaluated | 0.015 | 0.006 (`cosm`) |
| `Eigenvalues`, 250 x 250 complex Hermitian | 0.086 | 0.011 | 0.015 (`eigvalsh`) |
| `Eigenvectors`, 250 x 250 complex Hermitian | 0.11 | 0.038 | 0.042 (`eigh`) |
| `Adjugate`, 200 x 200 | 0.0185 | 0.0017 | 0.0026 (`det * inv`) |
| `CharacteristicPolynomial`, 40 x 40 integer | 1.25 | 0.002 | 0.22 (sympy) |

- **Complex results stay packed.** `na_finish` boxed every complex
  machine result (Cholesky factors, eigenvectors, Schur factors, Inverse, ...)
  into a nested List of `Complex[...]`. The comment justifying that dated from
  before complex packing (v0.402). Boxing a 400 x 400 complex factor cost about
  95% of `CholeskyDecomposition`. Complex factors are now complex packed
  arrays, as the reference returns them.
- **SameQ of packed complex arrays.** `SameQ` and `UnsameQ` were not
  packed-aware. The gate unpacked the buffer, and the materialised
  `Complex[1., 0.]` was compared against the Real `1.`, so
  `ToPackedArray[{1., 2. I}] === {1., 2. I}` was False; the reference gives
  True. Both heads are now on `AWARE`. `expr_eq` already compares a buffer
  against a boxed list without materialising, and now treats a zero-imaginary
  complex element as its Real; the packed hash does the same. The element
  still displays as `1. + 0. I`, as in the reference.
- **`(I/2) * packed` evaluated.** The NDArray scalar broadcast decoded
  `Complex[re, im]` only with Integer/Real parts, so `Complex[0, 1/2]` times
  a packed array stayed unevaluated. Its parts now decode like a real scalar
  (Rational, BigInt, MPFR).
- **MatrixFunction on a clustered spectrum** (wrong answer: unevaluated).
  The Schur–Parlett atom now uses the Higham–Davies truncation bound in place
  of "at least L terms". The derivative cache of `f` grows without the old cap
  at order 64.
- **Hermitian eigenproblems through `zheevd`.** Packed and boxed complex
  Hermitian input both use divide-and-conquer `zheevd`, with an `O(n^2)`
  self-check like the one guarding `zgees` / `zgeev`. Packed Hermitian input
  previously went to general `zgeev`, which was slower and returned complex
  eigenvalues with roundoff imaginary parts. They are Reals now, as in the
  reference. A `|lambda|` tie keeps LAPACK's ascending order, as the reference
  does: `Eigenvalues[N[{{0, -I}, {I, 0}}]]` is `{-1., 1.}` (was `{1., -1.}`).
- **Adjugate:** `det(m) m^-1` from one LU when `rcond >= sqrt(u)`; the SVD
  form is kept for near-singular input.
- **CharacteristicPolynomial:** an all-rational matrix goes to FLINT
  `fmpq_mat_charpoly`.
